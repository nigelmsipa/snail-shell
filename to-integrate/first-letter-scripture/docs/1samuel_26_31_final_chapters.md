# 1 Samuel 26-31: Final Fugitive Years and Saul's Death

## Overview
David spares Saul second time—patience for God's timing. Defects to Philistines—faith failure. Witch of Endor—Saul's final sin. Providence protects David from fighting Israel. Ziklag recovery—restoration. Saul's death at Gilboa—tragic end. Central theme: God's timing vindicated, David ready for throne after wilderness preparation.

---

## Chapter 26: Second Sparing

#### 26:1-12 — Spear and Water Jar
**Lore:** "Then the Ziphites came to Saul at Gibeah, saying, 'Is not David hiding himself on the hill of Hachilah?'" (26:1). Ziphites betray again! "So Saul arose and went down to the wilderness of Ziph, with three thousand chosen men of Israel, to seek David" (26:2). "And David saw that Saul had come after him into the wilderness... Then David rose and came to the place where Saul had encamped" (26:3-5). David scouts. "So David and Abishai went to the army by night. And there lay Saul sleeping within the encampment, with his spear stuck in the ground at his head, and Abner and the army lay around him" (26:7). Sneaks into sleeping camp! "Then Abishai said to David, 'God has given your enemy into your hand this day. Now please let me pin him to the earth with one stroke of the spear, and I will not strike him twice'" (26:8). Abishai urges killing. "But David said to Abishai, 'Do not destroy him, for who can put out his hand against the LORD's anointed and be guiltless?... The LORD forbid that I should put out my hand against the LORD's anointed'" (26:9-11). **Refuses again!** "So David took the spear and the jar of water from Saul's head, and they went away" (26:12). Takes proof—could have killed, chose not to.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Patience repeats*

#### 26:13-25 — Final Confrontation
**Lore:** "Then David went over to the other side and stood far off on the top of the hill, with a great space between them. And David called to the army, and to Abner the son of Ner, saying, 'Will you not answer, Abner?'" (26:13-14). Confronts from distance. "And David said to Abner, 'Are you not a man? Who is like you in Israel? Why then have you not kept watch over your lord the king? For one of the people came in to destroy the king your lord. This thing that you have done is not good'" (26:15-16). Rebukes Abner. "And Saul recognized David's voice and said, 'Is this your voice, my son David?' And David said, 'It is my voice, my lord, O king.' And he said, 'Why does my lord pursue after his servant? For what have I done? What evil is on my hands?'" (26:17-18). Saul recognizes voice. "Then Saul said, 'I have sinned. Return, my son David, for I will no more do you harm, because my life was precious in your eyes this day. Behold, I have acted foolishly, and have made a great mistake'" (26:21). Saul admits sin—but David doesn't trust. "And David answered and said, 'Here is the spear, O king! Let one of the young men come over and take it... The LORD rewards every man for his righteousness and his faithfulness... I would not put out my hand against the LORD's anointed'" (26:22-23). Won't force way to throne. "Then Saul said to David, 'Blessed be you, my son David! You will do many things and will succeed in them.' So David went his way, and Saul returned to his place" (26:25). Final parting—won't meet again.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Final separation*

## Chapter 27: Philistine Defection

#### 27:1-12 — Failure of Faith
**Lore:** "Then David said in his heart, 'Now I shall perish one day by the hand of Saul. There is nothing better for me than that I should escape to the land of the Philistines. Then Saul will despair of seeking me any longer within the borders of Israel, and I shall escape out of his hand'" (27:1). **FAITH FAILS**—despairs! "Grievous mistake"—acts without seeking counsel of LORD. "So David arose and went over, he and the six hundred men who were with him, to Achish the son of Maoch, king of Gath" (27:2). Defects to Philistines! "And David lived with Achish at Gath, he and his men, every man with his household... And David said to Achish, 'If I have found favor in your eyes, let a place be given me in one of the country towns, that I may dwell there... So that day Achish gave him Ziklag. Therefore Ziklag has belonged to the kings of Judah to this day. And the number of the days that David lived in the country of the Philistines was a year and four months" (27:3-7). 16 months among enemies. "Now David and his men went up and made raids against the Geshurites, the Girzites, and the Amalekites, for these were the inhabitants of the land from of old... And David would strike the land and would leave neither man nor woman alive, but would take away the sheep, the oxen, the donkeys, the camels, and the garments, and come back to Achish" (27:8-9). Deceives Achish—raids Israel's enemies while claiming to raid Israel's allies. "And Achish trusted David, thinking, 'He has made himself an utter stench to his people Israel; therefore he shall always be my servant'" (27:12). Achish deceived. David's double life—compromised position.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Faith's failure*

## Chapter 28: The Witch

#### 28:1-7 — God Silent
**Lore:** "In those days the Philistines gathered their forces for war, to fight against Israel. And Achish said to David, 'Understand that you and your men are to go out with me in the army'" (28:1). **TERRIBLE POSITION**—must fight Israel? "Now Samuel had died, and all Israel had mourned for him and buried him in Ramah, his own city. And Saul had put the mediums and the necromancers out of the land" (28:3). Samuel dead, Saul previously expelled occult practitioners. "When Saul saw the army of the Philistines, he was afraid, and his heart trembled greatly. And when Saul inquired of the LORD, the LORD did not answer him, either by dreams, or by Urim, or by prophets" (28:5-6). **GOD SILENT**—won't answer Saul! No dreams, Urim (stones), or prophets. "Then Saul said to his servants, 'Seek out for me a woman who is a medium, that I may go to her and inquire of her'" (28:7). Desperate—turns to occult.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Desperation*

#### 28:8-25 — Occult Consultation
**Lore:** "So Saul disguised himself and put on other garments and went, he and two men with him. And they came to the woman by night. And he said, 'Divine for me by a spirit and bring up for me whomever I shall name to you'" (28:8). Seeks medium. "But the woman said to him, 'Surely you know what Saul has done, how he has cut off the mediums and the necromancers from the land. Why then are you laying a trap for my life to bring about my death?'" (28:9). Fears trap. "But Saul swore to her by the LORD, 'As the LORD lives, no punishment shall come upon you for this thing'" (28:10). Swears by LORD while seeking occult! "Then the woman said, 'Whom shall I bring up for you?' He said, 'Bring up Samuel for me'" (28:11). Requests Samuel. "When the woman saw Samuel, she cried out with a loud voice... And she said to Saul, 'Why have you deceived me? You are Saul'" (28:12-13). Recognizes Saul. "And he said to her, 'Do not be afraid. What do you see?' And the woman said to Saul, 'I see a god coming up out of the earth... An old man is coming up, and he is wrapped in a robe'" (28:13-14). Apparition appears. NOT true Samuel resurrected—"angel of Satan assumes form" of Samuel. Demonic deception. "Then Samuel said to Saul, 'Why have you disturbed me by bringing me up?' Saul answered, 'I am in great distress, for the Philistines are warring against me, and God has turned away from me and answers me no more... Therefore I have summoned you to tell me what I shall do'" (28:15). Admits God abandoned him. "Samuel said... 'Why then do you ask me, since the LORD has turned from you and become your enemy? The LORD has done to you as he spoke by me, for the LORD has torn the kingdom out of your hand and given it to your neighbor, David... Because you did not obey the voice of the LORD and did not carry out his fierce wrath against Amalek, therefore the LORD has done this thing to you this day. Moreover, the LORD will give Israel also with you into the hand of the Philistines, and tomorrow you and your sons shall be with me'" (28:16-19). **PROPHECY OF DEATH**—Saul and sons die tomorrow! "Last act sealed that separation from God." "Made agreement with death, covenant with hell." Cup of iniquity full. "Then Saul fell at once full length on the ground, filled with fear because of the words of Samuel" (28:20). Devastated.
*Verses: 18 | Theme: Final sin*

## Chapter 29: Providence Protects

#### 29:1-11 — Philistine Lords Reject David
**Lore:** "Now the Philistines had gathered all their forces at Aphek. And the Israelites were encamped by the spring that is in Jezreel. As the lords of the Philistines were passing on by hundreds and by thousands, and David and his men were passing on in the rear with Achish, the commanders of the Philistines said, 'What are these Hebrews doing here?'" (29:1-3). David marches with Philistines against Israel—terrible position! "And the commanders of the Philistines said to him, 'Send the man back, that he may return to the place to which you have assigned him. He shall not go down with us to battle, lest in the battle he become an adversary to us. For how could this fellow reconcile himself to his lord? Would it not be with the heads of the men here? Is not this David, of whom they sing to one another in dances, "Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his ten thousands"?'" (29:4-5). Commanders object—remember the song! Fear David will turn. "Then Achish called David and said to him, 'As the LORD lives, you have been honest, and to me it seems right that you should march out and in with me in the campaign... Nevertheless, the lords do not approve of you. So go back now; and go peaceably, that you may not displease the lords of the Philistines'" (29:6-7). Dismisses David. "And David said to Achish, 'But what have I done?... Why may I not go and fight against the enemies of my lord the king?'" (29:8). Maintains charade—pretends to want to fight. "And Achish answered David and said, 'I know that you are as blameless in my sight as an angel of God. Nevertheless, the commanders of the Philistines have said, "He shall not go up with us to the battle"'" (29:9). "So David set out with his men early in the morning to return to the land of the Philistines. But the Philistines went up to Jezreel" (29:11). **GOD'S INVISIBLE HAND**—providence keeps David from sin of fighting Israel! Delivers him from dilemma his faithlessness created.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Providence delivers*

## Chapter 30: Restoration

#### 30:1-20 — Ziklag Recovery
**Lore:** "Now when David and his men came to Ziklag on the third day, the Amalekites had made a raid against the Negeb and against Ziklag. They had overcome Ziklag and burned it with fire and taken captive the women and all who were in it, both small and great... When David and his men came to the city, they found it burned with fire, and their wives and sons and daughters taken captive. Then David and the people who were with him raised their voices and wept until they had no more strength to weep" (30:1-4). **DISASTER**—families captured! "And David was greatly distressed, for the people spoke of stoning him, because all the people were bitter in soul, each for his sons and daughters. But David strengthened himself in the LORD his God" (30:6). **TURNING POINT**—"David strengthened himself in the LORD"! Returns to faith after Philistine failure. "And David said to Abiathar the priest... 'Please bring me the ephod.' So Abiathar brought the ephod to David. And David inquired of the LORD, 'Shall I pursue after this band? Shall I overtake them?' He answered him, 'Pursue, for you shall surely overtake and shall surely rescue'" (30:7-8). **Inquires of God**—back to seeking LORD's guidance! "So David set out, and the six hundred men who were with him... But David pursued, he and four hundred men. Two hundred stayed behind, who were too exhausted to cross the brook Besor" (30:9-10). Pursuit. Divine guidance leads to Egyptian servant who reveals Amalekite location (30:11-15). "And David struck them down from twilight until the evening of the next day, and not a man of them escaped, except four hundred young men, who mounted camels and fled. David recovered all that the Amalekites had taken, and David rescued his two wives. Nothing was missing, whether small or great, sons or daughters, spoil or anything that had been taken. David brought back all" (30:17-19). **COMPLETE RECOVERY**—nothing missing! "Prodigal son moment"—restoration after failure. Ready for throne.
*Verses: 20 | Theme: Restoration*

#### 30:21-31 — Generous Distribution
**Lore:** "Then David came to the two hundred men who had been too exhausted to follow David... And all the wicked and worthless fellows among the men who had gone with David said, 'Because they did not go with us, we will not give them any of the spoil that we have recovered, except that each man may lead away his wife and children, and depart'" (30:21-22). Selfish men want to exclude exhausted. "But David said, 'You shall not do so, my brothers, with what the LORD has given us. He has preserved us and given into our hand the band that came against us. Who would listen to you in this matter? For as his share is who goes down into the battle, so shall his share be who stays by the baggage. They shall share alike.' And he made it a statute and a rule for Israel from that day forward to this day" (30:23-25). **WISE RULING**—equal shares for all! Recognizes LORD's gift. Sends gifts to Judah towns (30:26-31)—building support for kingship.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Wise leadership*

## Chapter 31: Saul's Death

#### 31:1-13 — Tragic End at Gilboa
**Lore:** "Now the Philistines were fighting against Israel, and the men of Israel fled before the Philistines and fell slain on Mount Gilboa. And the Philistines overtook Saul and his sons, and the Philistines struck down Jonathan and Abinadab and Malchi-shua, the sons of Saul" (31:1-2). **Battle of Gilboa**—Israel routed. Jonathan killed! David's beloved friend dies. "The battle pressed hard against Saul, and the archers found him, and he was badly wounded by the archers. Then Saul said to his armor-bearer, 'Draw your sword, and thrust me through with it, lest these uncircumcised come and thrust me through, and mistreat me.' But his armor-bearer would not, for he feared greatly. Therefore Saul took his own sword and fell upon it" (31:3-4). **SAUL COMMITS SUICIDE**—refuses to be captured. Tragic end. "And when his armor-bearer saw that Saul was dead, he also fell upon his sword and died with him. Thus Saul died, and his three sons, and his armor-bearer, and all his men, on the same day together" (31:5-6). Complete disaster—Saul, sons, armor-bearer, men. "And when the men of Israel who were on the other side of the valley and those beyond the Jordan saw that the men of Israel had fled and that Saul and his sons were dead, they abandoned their cities and fled. And the Philistines came and lived in them" (31:7). Philistines occupy Israel. "The next day, when the Philistines came to strip the slain, they found Saul and his three sons fallen on Mount Gilboa. So they cut off his head and stripped off his armor and sent messengers throughout the land of the Philistines, to carry the good news to the house of their idols and to the people. They put his armor in the temple of Ashtaroth, and they fastened his body to the wall of Beth-shan" (31:8-10). Humiliation—body desecrated, armor in pagan temple. "But when the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead heard what the Philistines had done to Saul, all the valiant men arose and went all night and took the body of Saul and the bodies of his sons from the wall of Beth-shan, and they came to Jabesh and burned them there. And they took their bones and buried them under the tamarisk tree in Jabesh and fasted seven days" (31:11-13). **JABESH-GILEAD HONORS SAUL**—remembers his first victory (ch 11)! Risk lives to retrieve bodies, give honorable burial. Tragic end for "started out humble... ended up as enemy of God." Jealousy, presumption, disobedience, occult = downfall. Stage set for David's kingship (2 Samuel).
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Tragic end*
