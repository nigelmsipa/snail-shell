### Chapter 35: Return to Bethel; Rachel's Death

#### 35:1-15 — Purification and Covenant Renewal at Bethel
**Lore:** God tells Jacob: "Arise, go up to Bethel and dwell there. Make an altar there to the God who appeared to you when you fled from your brother Esau" (35:1). Return to the place of the ladder vision (ch 28). Jacob commands his household: "Put away the foreign gods that are among you and purify yourselves and change your garments" (35:2). They give Jacob all the foreign gods (including Rachel's teraphim from 31:19?) and their earrings; he buries them under the terebinth tree at Shechem (35:4). Moral cleansing before approaching God. Terror from God falls on surrounding cities—no one pursues them (35:5). Jacob comes to Bethel, builds an altar, calls it El-bethel ("God of Bethel") (35:7). Deborah, Rebekah's nurse, dies, buried under an oak (Allon-bacuth, "oak of weeping") (35:8). God appears to Jacob again, blesses him, reaffirms the name change: "Your name is Jacob; no longer shall your name be called Jacob, but Israel shall be your name" (35:10). God says, "I am God Almighty (El Shaddai): be fruitful and multiply. A nation and a company of nations shall come from you, and kings shall come from your own body. The land that I gave to Abraham and Isaac I will give to you, and I will give the land to your offspring after you" (35:11-12). Covenant reaffirmed. Jacob sets up a pillar of stone, pours oil and a drink offering on it (35:14).
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Covenant renewal*

#### 35:16-29 — Rachel Dies; Isaac Dies
**Lore:** They journey from Bethel. When there is still some distance to Ephrath (Bethlehem), Rachel goes into labor—hard labor (35:16). The midwife: "Do not fear, for you have another son" (35:17). As her soul is departing (for she is dying), she names him Ben-oni (בֶּן־אוֹנִי, "son of my sorrow"). But Jacob names him Benjamin (בִּנְיָמִין, "son of my right hand") (35:18). Rachel dies, is buried on the way to Ephrath. Jacob sets up a pillar over her tomb (35:19-20). Tragic—the beloved wife, mother of Joseph and Benjamin, dies giving birth. Jacob journeys on, pitches his tent beyond the tower of Eder (35:21). While Israel dwells in that land, Reuben (the firstborn) goes and lies with Bilhah, his father's concubine. Israel hears of it (35:22). This sin costs Reuben his birthright (49:3-4; 1 Chr 5:1). List of Jacob's twelve sons (35:23-26). Jacob comes to Isaac at Mamre (Hebron). Isaac lives 180 years, dies, "and his sons Esau and Jacob buried him" (35:28-29). The patriarchal line passes to the next generation.
*Verses: 14 | Theme: Death and succession*

### Chapter 36: Esau's Descendants (Edom)

#### 36:1-43 — The Generations of Esau
**Lore:** "These are the generations of Esau (that is, Edom)" (36:1). Genealogy of Esau's wives (Canaanite and Ishmaelite women), his sons, his settlement in Seir (36:2-8). Esau moves to Seir because the land could not support both him and Jacob (36:7). Lists Esau's descendants: chiefs, clans, kings (36:9-43). Edom becomes a nation southeast of Israel, perpetual rival. "These are the kings who reigned in the land of Edom, before any king reigned over the Israelites" (36:31)—Edomites have kings before Israel does. This genealogy closes out Esau's line, showing God's blessing on him (as promised in 27:39-40) but confirming he is outside the covenant line. Jacob/Israel is the chosen seed.
*Verses: 43 | Theme: The rejected line blessed but separate*

### Chapter 37: Joseph Sold into Slavery

#### 37:1-11 — Joseph's Dreams
**Lore:** Jacob dwells in Canaan (37:1). "Joseph, being seventeen years old, was pasturing the flock with his brothers...and Joseph brought a bad report of them to their father" (37:2). Tattletale. "Now Israel loved Joseph more than any other of his sons, because he was the son of his old age. And he made him a robe of many colors" (37:3). Favoritism (repeating Isaac/Esau pattern). Brothers see their father loves Joseph more, hate him, "could not speak peacefully to him" (37:4). Joseph dreams: "We were binding sheaves in the field, and behold, my sheaf arose and stood upright. And behold, your sheaves gathered around it and bowed down to my sheaf" (37:7). Brothers: "Are you indeed to reign over us?" They hate him even more (37:8). Second dream: "The sun, the moon, and eleven stars were bowing down to me" (37:9). Even Jacob rebukes him: "Shall I and your mother and your brothers indeed come to bow ourselves to the ground before you?" (37:10). Brothers are jealous; Jacob keeps the saying in mind (37:11). Dreams foreshadow Joseph's rise to power in Egypt.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Dreams of destiny*

#### 37:12-36 — Joseph Sold by His Brothers
**Lore:** Brothers pasture the flock at Shechem. Jacob sends Joseph to check on them. They see him from afar: "Here comes this dreamer. Come now, let us kill him and throw him into one of the pits. Then we will say that a fierce animal has devoured him, and we will see what will become of his dreams" (37:19-20). Murder plot. Reuben intervenes: "Let us not take his life...throw him into this pit here in the wilderness, but do not lay a hand on him" (37:21-22). Reuben plans to rescue him later. They strip Joseph of his robe, throw him into an empty pit (37:23-24). They sit down to eat. A caravan of Ishmaelites passes by, carrying spices to Egypt. Judah: "What profit is it if we kill our brother and conceal his blood? Come, let us sell him to the Ishmaelites, and let not our hand be upon him, for he is our brother, our own flesh" (37:26-27). They sell Joseph for twenty shekels of silver (37:28)—the price of a slave (Exod 21:32). Joseph is taken to Egypt. Reuben returns to the pit, finds Joseph gone, tears his clothes (37:29). Brothers slaughter a goat, dip Joseph's robe in blood, bring it to Jacob: "This we have found; please identify whether it is your son's robe or not" (37:32). Jacob recognizes it: "It is my son's robe. A fierce animal has devoured him. Joseph is without doubt torn to pieces" (37:33). Jacob tears his garments, mourns many days, refuses to be comforted: "I shall go down to Sheol to my son, mourning" (37:35). Meanwhile, "the Midianites had sold him in Egypt to Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh, the captain of the guard" (37:36). Joseph is a type of Christ: rejected by his brothers, sold for silver, suffers innocently.
*Verses: 25 | Theme: Rejection and betrayal*

### Chapter 38: Judah and Tamar

**📖 Chapter Note:** Genesis 38 interrupts the Joseph narrative to show Judah's moral failure, contrasting with Joseph's integrity in chapter 39. Yet God works through this scandal—Tamar's twins are in the Messianic line (Matt 1:3).

#### 38:1-30 — Tamar's Righteousness
**Lore:** (Condensed) Judah marries a Canaanite woman, has three sons: Er, Onan, Shelah (38:2-5). Er marries Tamar, but Er is wicked; the LORD puts him to death (38:7). Judah tells Onan to perform levirate duty (raise up offspring for his brother). Onan refuses, spills his seed; the LORD puts him to death too (38:9-10). Judah tells Tamar to wait for Shelah to grow up, but he doesn't give her to him (38:11-14). Tamar, seeing she's been deceived, disguises herself as a prostitute, sits by the roadside. Judah sees her, propositions her, doesn't recognize her (38:15-16). She asks for a pledge: his signet, cord, and staff (38:17-18). She conceives. Later, Judah tries to send payment; she's gone. Three months later, Judah is told, "Tamar your daughter-in-law has been immoral. Moreover, she is pregnant by immorality" (38:24). Judah: "Bring her out, and let her be burned" (38:24). As she is brought out, she sends word: "By the man to whom these belong, I am pregnant...Please identify whose these are, the signet and the cord and the staff" (38:25). Judah recognizes them: "She is more righteous than I, since I did not give her to my son Shelah" (38:26). She bears twins: Perez (פֶּרֶץ, "breach") and Zerah (זֶרַח, "scarlet") (38:28-30). Perez is in the line of David and Christ (Ruth 4:18-22; Matt 1:3). Tamar's bold action secures the Messianic line. Judah's repentance ("she is more righteous") marks a turning point in his character.
*Verses: 30 | Theme: Righteousness through scandal*

### Chapters 39-41: Joseph in Egypt

#### 39:1-23 — Potiphar's Wife; Prison
**Lore:** (Summary) Joseph serves Potiphar. "The LORD was with Joseph, and he became a successful man" (39:2). Potiphar puts Joseph in charge of his house (39:4-6). Potiphar's wife tries to seduce Joseph daily. Joseph refuses: "How then can I do this great wickedness and sin against God?" (39:9). She grabs his garment; he flees, leaving it in her hand (39:12). She falsely accuses him of attempted rape (39:14-18). Joseph is thrown in prison (39:19-20). "But the LORD was with Joseph and showed him steadfast love and gave him favor in the sight of the keeper of the prison" (39:21). Joseph is put in charge of all the prisoners (39:22-23). Integrity under siege—Joseph's moral strength contrasts with Judah's failure in chapter 38.
*Verses: 23 | Theme: Integrity despite injustice*

#### 40:1-23 — Dreams of the Cupbearer and Baker
**Lore:** (Summary) Pharaoh's cupbearer and baker offend him, are imprisoned (40:1-4). Both have dreams the same night (40:5). Joseph interprets: cupbearer will be restored in three days; baker will be executed in three days (40:12-19). It happens as Joseph said (40:20-22). Joseph asks the cupbearer, "Remember me when it is well with you" (40:14). But "the chief cupbearer did not remember Joseph, but forgot him" (40:23). Two more years pass (41:1).
*Verses: 23 | Theme: Forgotten in prison*

#### 41:1-57 — Pharaoh's Dreams; Joseph Exalted
**Lore:** (Summary) Two years later, Pharaoh dreams: seven fat cows devoured by seven thin cows; seven full ears of grain devoured by seven thin ears (41:1-7). No one can interpret (41:8). The cupbearer remembers Joseph (41:9-13). Pharaoh summons Joseph. Joseph: "It is not in me; God will give Pharaoh a favorable answer" (41:16). Joseph interprets: seven years of plenty, then seven years of famine (41:25-32). Advises storing grain during plenty (41:33-36). Pharaoh: "Can we find a man like this, in whom is the Spirit of God?" (41:38). Appoints Joseph second-in-command at age 30 (41:40-44). Joseph marries Asenath, daughter of an Egyptian priest; she bears Manasseh ("God has made me forget") and Ephraim ("God has made me fruitful") (41:50-52). Seven years of plenty come; Joseph stores grain. Famine begins. "All the earth came to Egypt to Joseph to buy grain" (41:57). From prison to palace—God's providence.
*Verses: 57 | Theme: Exaltation after humiliation*

### Chapters 42-45: Joseph and His Brothers

#### 42-44 — Testing the Brothers
**Lore:** (Summary) Jacob sends ten sons to Egypt to buy grain; Benjamin stays home (42:1-4). They bow before Joseph (fulfilling his dreams); he recognizes them, they don't recognize him (42:6-8). He accuses them of being spies (42:9-14). Keeps Simeon hostage, sends them home with grain and their money returned (42:19-25). They realize, "In truth we are guilty concerning our brother...therefore this distress has come upon us" (42:21). Second journey with Benjamin (43:1-15). Joseph seats them by age, gives Benjamin five times the portions (43:33-34). Plants his silver cup in Benjamin's sack (44:1-2). Cup found; Judah offers himself as a slave instead of Benjamin (44:18-34)—Judah's transformation complete. He who sold Joseph now offers himself for Benjamin.
*Theme: Guilt, repentance, transformation*

#### 45:1-28 — Joseph Reveals Himself
**Lore:** Joseph can no longer control himself. "Make everyone go out from me" (45:1). He weeps loudly. "I am Joseph! Is my father still alive?" (45:3). Brothers are dismayed, terrified. Joseph: "Do not be distressed or angry with yourselves that you sold me here, for God sent me before you to preserve life...So it was not you who sent me here, but God" (45:5, 8). Key theology: divine providence overrides human evil. "God meant it for good" (theme echoed in 50:20). Joseph kisses his brothers, weeps; they talk with him (45:15). Pharaoh hears, tells Joseph to bring his family to Egypt (45:16-20). Jacob hears Joseph is alive: "It is enough; Joseph my son is still alive. I will go and see him before I die" (45:28). Reunion ahead.
*Verses: 28 | Theme: Providence revealed*

### Chapters 46-47: Israel in Egypt

#### 46:1-34 — Jacob Goes to Egypt
**Lore:** Israel journeys with all he has to Egypt. At Beersheba, offers sacrifices. God speaks in visions: "I am God, the God of your father. Do not be afraid to go down to Egypt, for there I will make you into a great nation. I myself will go down with you to Egypt, and I will also bring you up again" (46:3-4). List of Jacob's descendants: seventy persons (46:27). Jacob sends Judah ahead to Joseph; they meet in Goshen. Joseph falls on Jacob's neck, weeps (46:29). Jacob: "Now let me die, since I have seen your face and know that you are still alive" (46:30).
*Theme: God goes with them to Egypt*

#### 47:1-31 — Settling in Goshen
**Lore:** Joseph presents five brothers to Pharaoh (47:2). Jacob blesses Pharaoh (47:7, 10). Joseph settles family in Goshen, provides food (47:11-12). Joseph administers famine relief; Egyptians sell land to Pharaoh, become servants (47:13-26). Israel multiplies in Goshen (47:27). Jacob makes Joseph swear to bury him in Canaan, not Egypt (47:29-31).
*Theme: Sojourners in Egypt*

### Chapters 48-50: Jacob's Death and Joseph's Final Years

#### 48:1-22 — Jacob Blesses Ephraim and Manasseh
**Lore:** Jacob is ill. Joseph brings his two sons. Jacob adopts them as his own: "Ephraim and Manasseh shall be mine, as Reuben and Simeon are" (48:5). Jacob crosses his hands, putting his right hand on Ephraim (younger) and left on Manasseh (older) (48:14). Joseph tries to correct him; Jacob insists: "His younger brother shall be greater than he" (48:19). Blessing pronounced (48:15-16, 20). The pattern continues—younger over older.
*Theme: Blessing the next generation*

#### 49:1-28 — Jacob Blesses the Twelve Tribes
**Lore:** Jacob calls his sons: "Gather yourselves together, that I may tell you what shall happen to you in days to come" (49:1). Prophetic blessings over each:
- Reuben: Lost preeminence due to sin with Bilhah (49:3-4)
- Simeon and Levi: Scattered due to violence at Shechem (49:5-7)
- **Judah: "The scepter shall not depart from Judah...until tribute comes to him" (49:10)—Messianic prophecy**
- Joseph: "Blessings of heaven above...blessings of the everlasting hills" (49:25-26)
- Benjamin: "A ravenous wolf" (49:27)
*Theme: Tribal destinies prophesied*

#### 49:29-50:14 — Jacob's Death and Burial
**Lore:** Jacob commands: "Bury me with my fathers in the cave...at Machpelah" (49:29-32). Jacob dies (49:33). Joseph weeps, embalms him (40 days), Egypt mourns 70 days (50:2-3). Joseph asks Pharaoh for permission to bury Jacob in Canaan (50:4-6). Great procession—officials of Egypt, Joseph's household, brothers (50:7-9). They bury Jacob at Machpelah (50:13). Return to Egypt.
*Theme: Buried in the Promised Land*

#### 50:15-21 — Joseph Reassures His Brothers
**Lore:** After Jacob dies, brothers fear Joseph will take revenge (50:15). They send word (possibly lying): "Your father gave this command before he died: 'Say to Joseph, "Please forgive..."'" (50:16-17). Joseph weeps. Brothers come, fall before him: "Behold, we are your servants" (50:18). Joseph: "Do not fear, for am I in the place of God? **As for you, you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good, to bring it about that many people should be kept alive, as they are today**" (50:19-20). The Bible's clearest statement of divine providence. Joseph comforts them, speaks kindly, provides for them (50:21).
*Theme: God meant it for good*

#### 50:22-26 — Joseph's Death
**Lore:** Joseph lives 110 years, sees Ephraim's great-grandchildren (50:22-23). Before dying, he says: "I am about to die, but God will visit you and bring you up out of this land to the land that he swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob" (50:24). Makes them swear to carry his bones up from Egypt (fulfilled in Exod 13:19; Josh 24:32). Joseph dies, is embalmed, put in a coffin in Egypt (50:26). Genesis ends with Israel in Egypt, awaiting the Exodus. The promise remains; the story continues.
*Theme: Faith in the promise*
