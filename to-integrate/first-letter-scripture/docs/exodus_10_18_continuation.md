# Exodus 10-18: Plagues 8-10, Passover, Red Sea, Wilderness Journey

### Chapter 10: Plagues Eight and Nine

#### 10:1-20 — Eighth Plague: Locusts
**Lore:** God tells Moses: "Go in to Pharaoh, for I have hardened his heart and the heart of his servants, that I may show these signs of mine among them, and that you may tell in the hearing of your son and of your grandson how I have dealt harshly with the Egyptians and what signs I have done among them, that you may know that I am the LORD" (10:1-2). The plagues have pedagogical purpose—Israel must remember and teach future generations. Moses warns: "Tomorrow I will bring locusts (אַרְבֶּה, arbeh) into your country, and they shall cover the face of the land, so that no one can see the land" (10:4-5). Pharaoh's servants plead: "How long shall this man be a snare to us? Let the men go...Do you not yet understand that Egypt is ruined?" (10:7). Pharaoh summons Moses, offers compromise: "Go, serve the LORD your God. But which ones are to go?" (10:8). Moses: "We will go with our young and our old. We will go with our sons and daughters and with our flocks and herds, for we must hold a feast to the LORD" (10:9). Pharaoh refuses: only the men may go (10:10-11). Moses and Aaron are driven out. Moses stretches his staff; an east wind brings locusts that cover the land, eating every plant the hail left (10:13-15). "Not a green thing remained, neither tree nor plant of the field, through all the land of Egypt" (10:15). Total devastation. Pharaoh hastily calls Moses: "I have sinned against the LORD your God, and against you. Now therefore, forgive my sin, please, only this once, and plead with the LORD your God only to remove this death from me" (10:16-17). Moses prays; God sends a west wind that drives the locusts into the Red Sea (10:19). "But the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he did not let the people of Israel go" (10:20). The locust plague echoes Joel 1-2, where locusts symbolize divine judgment and precede the Day of the LORD.
*Verses: 20 | Theme: Total devastation*

#### 10:21-29 — Ninth Plague: Darkness
**Lore:** God tells Moses: "Stretch out your hand toward heaven, that there may be darkness over the land of Egypt, a darkness to be felt" (10:21). Moses does so. "There was thick darkness (חֹשֶׁךְ, choshek) in all the land of Egypt three days. They did not see one another, nor did anyone rise from his place for three days, but all the people of Israel had light where they lived" (10:22-23). Palpable, oppressive darkness—not mere night but supernatural gloom. Egypt worshiped Ra, the sun god. God extinguishes their deity. The darkness prefigures the ninth hour darkness at Christ's crucifixion (Matt 27:45). Pharaoh summons Moses: "Go, serve the LORD; your little ones also may go with you; only let your flocks and your herds remain behind" (10:24). Another compromise. Moses refuses: "You must also let us have sacrifices and burnt offerings, that we may sacrifice to the LORD our God. Our livestock also must go with us; not a hoof shall be left behind" (10:25-26). Pharaoh explodes in rage: "Get away from me; take care never to see my face again, for on the day you see my face you shall die" (10:28). Moses: "As you say! I will not see your face again" (10:29). The final confrontation. Next: the tenth plague, which will break Pharaoh completely.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Darkness over Egypt*

### Chapter 11: The Tenth Plague Announced

#### 11:1-10 — Death of the Firstborn Foretold
**Lore:** God tells Moses: "Yet one plague more I will bring upon Pharaoh and upon Egypt. Afterward he will let you go from here. When he lets you go, he will drive you away completely" (11:1). Israel is to ask Egyptians for silver and gold (11:2)—the promised plunder (3:21-22). "And the LORD gave the people favor in the sight of the Egyptians. Moreover, the man Moses was very great in the land of Egypt, in the sight of Pharaoh's servants and in the sight of the people" (11:3). Moses, once a fugitive, is now revered—divine vindication. Moses announces to Pharaoh: "Thus says the LORD: 'About midnight I will go out in the midst of Egypt, and every firstborn in the land of Egypt shall die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sits on his throne, even to the firstborn of the slave girl who is behind the handmill, and all the firstborn of the cattle. There shall be a great cry throughout all the land of Egypt, such as there has never been, nor ever will be again'" (11:4-6). Universal judgment—no social class spared. "But not a dog shall growl against any of the people of Israel, either man or beast, that you may know that the LORD makes a distinction between Egypt and Israel" (11:7). Perfect protection for God's people. Moses leaves Pharaoh "in hot anger" (11:8). "But the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he did not let the people of Israel go out of his land" (11:10). The die is cast. One final plague will accomplish what nine could not.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: The final warning*

### Chapter 12: Passover and the Exodus

#### 12:1-13 — The Passover Instituted
**Lore:** God speaks to Moses and Aaron in Egypt: "This month shall be for you the beginning of months. It shall be the first month of the year for you" (12:2). Nisan (March-April) becomes the new covenant calendar start—redemption resets time. Instructions: On the tenth day, each household takes a lamb (שֶׂה, seh, "lamb" or "kid"), without blemish, a year old (12:3-5). "You shall keep it until the fourteenth day of this month, when the whole assembly of the congregation of Israel shall kill their lambs at twilight" (12:6). Four days of inspection—prefiguring Christ's public ministry before Passover week (John 12:1). "Then they shall take some of the blood and put it on the two doorposts and the lintel of the houses in which they eat it" (12:7). Blood applied externally—visible faith. "They shall eat the flesh that night, roasted on the fire; with unleavened bread and bitter herbs they shall eat it" (12:8). Hasty meal, prepared for departure. "In this manner you shall eat it: with your belt fastened, your sandals on your feet, and your staff in your hand. And you shall eat it in haste. It is the LORD's Passover (פֶּסַח, pesach)" (12:11). Pesach means "to pass over, to skip." God explains: "For I will pass through the land of Egypt that night, and I will strike all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, both man and beast; and on all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgments: I am the LORD. The blood shall be a sign for you, on the houses where you are. And when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and no plague will befall you to destroy you, when I strike the land of Egypt" (12:12-13). The blood saves—not Israel's merit, but substitutionary atonement. The lamb dies so the firstborn lives. Typology: 1 Corinthians 5:7, "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed." John the Baptist: "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!" (John 1:29). The Passover is the gospel in symbolic action. Ellen White: "In the Passover, God desired His people to review annually His wonderful works in their deliverance from Egyptian bondage...Thus they were to keep alive in all hearts a grateful sense of His care and mercy" (Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 539).
*Verses: 13 | Theme: The blood on the door*

#### 12:14-28 — Feast of Unleavened Bread
**Lore:** "This day shall be for you a memorial day, and you shall keep it as a feast to the LORD; throughout your generations, as a statute forever, you shall keep it as a feast" (12:14). Passover becomes an eternal ordinance. Seven days of unleavened bread (מַצָּה, matzah) follow (12:15-20). Leaven (חָמֵץ, chametz, yeast) symbolizes sin (1 Cor 5:6-8). Removal of leaven = purging of sin. Instructions are detailed: no leaven in houses, violators cut off from Israel (12:15, 19). Moses assembles elders, commands them: "Go and select lambs for yourselves according to your clans, and kill the Passover lamb. Take a bunch of hyssop (אֵזוֹב, ezov, a cleansing plant, cf. Ps 51:7) and dip it in the blood that is in the basin, and touch the lintel and the two doorposts with the blood that is in the basin. None of you shall go out of the door of his house until the morning" (12:21-22). Safety inside the blood-marked house. "For the LORD will pass through to strike the Egyptians, and when he sees the blood on the lintel and on the two doorposts, the LORD will pass over the door and will not allow the destroyer (הַמַּשְׁחִית, hamashchit, "the destroyer," possibly the destroying angel) to enter your houses to strike you" (12:23). The people worship (12:27-28).
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Memorial forever*

#### 12:29-36 — The Tenth Plague: Death of the Firstborn
**Lore:** "At midnight the LORD struck down all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sat on his throne to the firstborn of the captive who was in the dungeon, and all the firstborn of the livestock" (12:29). Universal death. "And Pharaoh rose up in the night, he and all his servants and all the Egyptians. And there was a great cry in Egypt, for there was not a house where someone was not dead" (12:30). Unimaginable grief. Pharaoh summons Moses and Aaron by night: "Up, go out from among my people, both you and the people of Israel; and go, serve the LORD, as you have said. Take your flocks and your herds, as you have said, and be gone, and bless me also!" (12:31-32). Pharaoh, who asked "Who is the LORD?" (5:2), now begs for blessing. Total reversal. "The Egyptians were urgent with the people to send them out of the land in haste. For they said, 'We shall all be dead'" (12:33). Panic-driven expulsion. Israel asks for silver, gold, and clothing; God gives them favor, and they plunder Egypt (12:35-36)—back wages for slavery. Egypt's wealth will fund the Tabernacle (25:1-9).
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Pharaoh broken*

#### 12:37-42 — The Exodus Begins
**Lore:** "And the people of Israel journeyed from Rameses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand men on foot, besides women and children" (12:37). Estimates: 2-3 million total (including women, children, mixed multitude). "A mixed multitude (עֵרֶב רַב, erev rav) also went up with them, and very much livestock, both flocks and herds" (12:38). Non-Israelites join—Gentiles grafted in early. They bake unleavened cakes, "for they were thrust out of Egypt and could not wait, nor had they prepared any provisions for themselves" (12:39). Israel's sojourn in Egypt: 430 years (12:40-41). God keeps exact time. "It was a night of watching by the LORD, to bring them out of the land of Egypt; so this same night is a night of watching kept to the LORD by all the people of Israel throughout their generations" (12:42). Passover night is sacred vigil, remembering deliverance.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: The great exodus*

#### 12:43-51 — Passover Regulations
**Lore:** Additional laws: No foreigner may eat Passover (12:43). Servants and sojourners may participate if circumcised (12:44-49). "It shall be eaten in one house; you shall not take any of the flesh outside the house, and you shall not break any of its bones" (12:46). Unbroken bones prefigure Christ: "Not one of his bones will be broken" (John 19:36, citing Ps 34:20). Passover is covenant meal—only covenant members partake. One law for native and sojourner (12:49)—equality before God.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: One law for all*

### Chapter 13: Consecration and Guidance

#### 13:1-16 — Consecration of the Firstborn
**Lore:** God commands: "Consecrate to me all the firstborn. Whatever is the first to open the womb among the people of Israel, both of man and of beast, is mine" (13:2). The tenth plague redeemed Israel's firstborn; now they belong to God. Firstborn sons must be redeemed (13:13, 15)—later substituted by Levites (Num 3:11-13). Unleavened bread festival reaffirmed (13:3-10). "And it shall be to you as a sign on your hand and as a memorial between your eyes, that the law of the LORD may be in your mouth" (13:9). This verse inspires Jewish tefillin (phylacteries). The Passover is to be taught to children: "It is because of what the LORD did for me when I came out of Egypt" (13:8). Generational transmission of faith. Firstborn animals: donkeys redeemed with a lamb; unredeemed donkeys' necks broken (13:13). "And when in time to come your son asks you, 'What does this mean?' you shall say to him, 'By a strong hand the LORD brought us out of Egypt, from the house of slavery'" (13:14). Catechetical method—questions and answers preserve truth.
*Verses: 16 | Theme: Remember and teach*

#### 13:17-22 — God Leads by Cloud and Fire
**Lore:** "When Pharaoh let the people go, God did not lead them by way of the land of the Philistines, although that was near. For God said, 'Lest the people change their minds when they see war and return to Egypt'" (13:17). Shortest route avoided—Israel not ready for battle. "But God led the people around by the way of the wilderness toward the Red Sea" (13:18). Longer, harder path for spiritual formation. "Moses took the bones of Joseph with him, for Joseph had made the sons of Israel solemnly swear, saying, 'God will surely visit you, and you shall carry up my bones with you from here'" (13:19, fulfilling Gen 50:25). Joseph's faith in the Promised Land honored. "And the LORD went before them by day in a pillar of cloud to lead them along the way, and by night in a pillar of fire to give them light, that they might travel by day and by night. The pillar of cloud by day and the pillar of fire by night did not depart from before the people" (13:21-22). Shekinah glory—God's visible presence. The pillar guides, protects, and marks God's dwelling with Israel. It foreshadows Christ: "I am the light of the world" (John 8:12).
*Verses: 6 | Theme: God goes before us*

### Chapter 14: The Red Sea Deliverance

**📖 Structural Note:** Exodus 14 is a chiasm (inverted parallelism) with verses 19-20 at the center—God's Angel and pillar moving between Israel and Egypt. The structure emphasizes that YHWH Himself fights for Israel (Ministry Magazine, "Salvation and Deliverance").

#### 14:1-14 — Trapped by the Sea
**Lore:** God tells Moses: "Tell the people of Israel to turn back and encamp in front of Pi-hahiroth, between Migdol and the sea, in front of Baal-zephon; you shall encamp facing it, by the sea" (14:2). Strategic positioning—God orchestrates apparent trap. "For Pharaoh will say of the people of Israel, 'They are wandering in the land; the wilderness has shut them in'" (14:3). God hardens Pharaoh's heart one last time; Pharaoh pursues with 600 chariots and all Egyptian cavalry (14:5-9). Israel sees Egypt approaching and panics: "Is it because there are no graves in Egypt that you have taken us away to die in the wilderness? What have you done to us in bringing us out of Egypt?" (14:11). Sarcasm born of fear. "It would have been better for us to serve the Egyptians than to die in the wilderness" (14:12). First of many grumbling episodes. Moses responds: "Fear not, stand firm, and see the salvation of the LORD, which he will work for you today. For the Egyptians whom you see today, you shall never see again. The LORD will fight for you, and you have only to be silent" (14:13-14). Stand firm, be silent—trust God to act. This is faith's posture in crisis.
*Verses: 14 | Theme: The LORD will fight for you*

#### 14:15-31 — The Sea Divided
**Lore:** God tells Moses: "Why do you cry to me? Tell the people of Israel to go forward. Lift up your staff, and stretch out your hand over the sea and divide it, that the people of Israel may go through the sea on dry ground" (14:15-16). Faith requires action—stand firm (v13), but also move forward (v15). "And I will harden the hearts of the Egyptians so that they shall go in after them, and I will get glory over Pharaoh and all his host, his chariots, and his horsemen. And the Egyptians shall know that I am the LORD" (14:17-18). Glory through judgment. **Chiasm center (14:19-20):** "Then the angel of God who was going before the host of Israel moved and went behind them, and the pillar of cloud moved from before them and stood behind them, coming between the host of Egypt and the host of Israel. And there was the cloud and the darkness. And it lit up the night without one coming near the other all night" (14:19-20). God Himself interposes—the Angel of the LORD (a Christophany, pre-incarnate Christ) physically protects Israel. The pillar is both light (for Israel) and darkness (for Egypt)—same presence, different experience based on relationship. "Then Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and the LORD drove the sea back by a strong east wind all night and made the sea dry land, and the waters were divided" (14:21). Natural means (wind) + supernatural power (God's command). "And the people of Israel went into the midst of the sea on dry ground, the waters being a wall to them on their right hand and on their left" (14:22). Walls of water—impossible, miraculous. The Egyptians pursue (14:23). "And in the morning watch the LORD in the pillar of fire and of cloud looked down on the Egyptian forces and threw the Egyptian forces into a panic, clogging their chariot wheels" (14:24-25). God fights from the pillar. Egyptians: "Let us flee from before Israel, for the LORD fights for them against the Egyptians" (14:25). Too late. God tells Moses: "Stretch out your hand over the sea, that the water may come back upon the Egyptians" (14:26). Moses does; the sea returns at dawn, drowning Pharaoh's army (14:27-28). "Not one of them remained" (14:28). Total destruction. "But the people of Israel walked on dry ground through the sea, the waters being a wall to them on their right hand and on their left. Thus the LORD saved Israel that day from the hand of the Egyptians, and Israel saw the Egyptians dead on the seashore. Israel saw the great power that the LORD used against the Egyptians, so the people feared the LORD, and they believed in the LORD and in his servant Moses" (14:29-31). Faith born from seeing God's salvation. Typology: 1 Corinthians 10:1-2, "Our fathers were all under the cloud, and all passed through the sea, and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea." The Red Sea is baptism—death to the old life (Egypt), resurrection to new life (freedom).
*Verses: 17 | Theme: Baptism into freedom*

### Chapter 15: Song of Deliverance

#### 15:1-21 — The Song of the Sea
**Lore:** "Then Moses and the people of Israel sang this song to the LORD, saying, 'I will sing to the LORD, for he has triumphed gloriously; the horse and his rider he has thrown into the sea'" (15:1). First song in Scripture—worship follows deliverance. Key verses: "The LORD is my strength and my song, and he has become my salvation; this is my God, and I will praise him, my father's God, and I will exalt him" (15:2). Personal relationship: "my God." "The LORD is a man of war; the LORD is his name" (15:3). Divine warrior theme. The song recounts the sea crossing (15:4-12), then looks forward: "You will bring them in and plant them on your own mountain, the place, O LORD, which you have made for your abode, the sanctuary, O LORD, which your hands have established" (15:17). Prophetic vision of Zion and the Temple. "The LORD will reign forever and ever" (15:18). Eternal kingship. Miriam, Moses' sister, leads the women with timbrels and dancing: "Sing to the LORD, for he has triumphed gloriously; the horse and his rider he has thrown into the sea" (15:21). Women's chorus—worship includes all. This song is later called the "Song of Moses" and echoed in Revelation 15:3, where the redeemed sing "the song of Moses...and the song of the Lamb."
*Verses: 21 | Theme: Worship after deliverance*

### Chapters 15-17: Wilderness Testing

#### 15:22-27 — Marah: Bitter Waters Made Sweet
**Lore:** Israel journeys three days into the wilderness of Shur without finding water (15:22). At Marah (מָרָה, "bitter"), they find water but cannot drink it—it's bitter (15:23). "And the people grumbled (לוּן, lun, "murmured, complained") against Moses, saying, 'What shall we drink?'" (15:24). First grumbling episode post-deliverance. Moses cries to the LORD; God shows him a tree. "He threw it into the water, and the water became sweet" (15:25). The tree transforms death to life—typology of the cross. "There the LORD made for them a statute and a rule, and there he tested them, saying, 'If you will diligently listen to the voice of the LORD your God, and do that which is right in his eyes, and give ear to his commandments and keep all his statutes, I will put none of the diseases on you that I put on the Egyptians, for I am the LORD, your healer'" (15:26). Covenant condition: obedience brings health. God's name: יְהוָה רֹפְאֶךָ (YHWH Rophe'eka, "the LORD your healer"). They camp at Elim—12 springs, 70 palm trees (15:27). Provision follows testing.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: The LORD our healer*

#### 16:1-36 — Manna and Quail
**Lore:** (Condensed due to length—full treatment would be extensive.) Israel reaches the wilderness of Sin (between Elim and Sinai). "The whole congregation of the people of Israel grumbled against Moses and Aaron in the wilderness, and the people of Israel said to them, 'Would that we had died by the hand of the LORD in the land of Egypt, when we sat by the meat pots and ate bread to the full, for you have brought us out into this wilderness to kill this whole assembly with hunger'" (16:2-3). Selective memory—slavery now seems better than hunger. God promises bread from heaven (16:4). Each morning, manna (מָן, man, "What is it?") appears like frost (16:14-15). "It was like coriander seed, white, and the taste of it was like wafers made with honey" (16:31). Instructions: gather daily (one omer per person); on the sixth day, gather double for Sabbath (16:22-26). "This is what the LORD has commanded: 'Tomorrow is a day of solemn rest, a holy Sabbath to the LORD'" (16:23). Sabbath observance begins before Sinai. Some violate, gathering on Sabbath; God rebukes: "How long will you refuse to keep my commandments?" (16:28). Manna tests obedience. Typology: John 6:31-35, Jesus says, "Your fathers ate the manna in the wilderness, and they died...I am the bread of life; whoever comes to me shall not hunger." Jesus is the true manna. An omer of manna is kept in a jar before the testimony (16:33-34), later placed in the Ark (Heb 9:4). Israel eats manna 40 years until entering Canaan (16:35).
*Verses: 36 | Theme: Daily bread from heaven*

#### 17:1-7 — Water from the Rock
**Lore:** At Rephidim, no water. "The people quarreled with Moses and said, 'Give us water to drink.' And Moses said to them, 'Why do you quarrel with me? Why do you test the LORD?'" (17:2). Grumbling escalates to near-rebellion: "Why did you bring us up out of Egypt, to kill us and our children and our livestock with thirst?" (17:3). Moses cries to God: "What shall I do with this people? They are almost ready to stone me" (17:4). God commands: "Take in your hand the staff with which you struck the Nile...Behold, I will stand before you there on the rock at Horeb, and you shall strike the rock, and water shall come out of it, and the people will drink" (17:5-6). Moses strikes the rock; water flows. The place is named Massah (מַסָּה, "testing") and Meribah (מְרִיבָה, "quarreling") because Israel tested God, saying, "Is the LORD among us or not?" (17:7). Typology: 1 Corinthians 10:4, "They drank from the spiritual Rock that followed them, and the Rock was Christ." Christ is the Rock; struck once (crucified), He gives living water (John 7:37-39). Later, Moses will wrongly strike the rock a second time (Num 20:11), forfeiting entry to Canaan—Christ is crucified once for all (Heb 9:28).
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Christ the Rock*

#### 17:8-16 — Victory over Amalek
**Lore:** "Then Amalek came and fought with Israel at Rephidim" (17:8). Amalek (descendants of Esau, Gen 36:12) attacks unprovoked. Moses tells Joshua: "Choose for us men, and go out and fight with Amalek. Tomorrow I will stand on the top of the hill with the staff of God in my hand" (17:9). First mention of Joshua (יְהוֹשֻׁעַ, Yehoshua, "YHWH is salvation"—Greek: Ἰησοῦς, Jesus). Joshua fights; Moses holds up the staff. "Whenever Moses held up his hand, Israel prevailed, and whenever he lowered his hand, Amalek prevailed" (17:11). Aaron and Hur support Moses' arms; Israel wins (17:12-13). The battle is won through intercession—Moses' raised hands symbolize dependence on God. "Then the LORD said to Moses, 'Write this as a memorial in a book and recite it in the ears of Joshua, that I will utterly blot out the memory of Amalek from under heaven'" (17:14). Amalek is doomed (fulfilled in 1 Sam 15; Esther 3-9). Moses builds an altar: "The LORD is my banner (יְהוָה נִסִּי, YHWH Nissi)" (17:15). God is our rallying point in battle. "A hand upon the throne of the LORD! The LORD will have war with Amalek from generation to generation" (17:16). Amalek represents opposition to God's people—spiritual warfare theme.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: The LORD our banner*

### Chapter 18: Jethro's Counsel

#### 18:1-12 — Jethro Visits Moses
**Lore:** Jethro, Moses' father-in-law (priest of Midian), hears of Israel's deliverance and brings Zipporah and Moses' two sons to meet him (18:1-6). Moses recounts all God has done (18:7-8). "Jethro rejoiced for all the good that the LORD had done to Israel, in that he had delivered them out of the hand of the Egyptians. Jethro said, 'Blessed be the LORD, who has delivered you out of the hand of the Egyptians and out of the hand of Pharaoh...Now I know that the LORD is greater than all gods, because in this affair they dealt arrogantly with the people'" (18:9-11). Gentile priest acknowledges YHWH's supremacy. Jethro offers burnt offerings and sacrifices; Aaron and elders eat bread with him before God (18:12). Inclusive worship—Gentiles welcomed.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Gentiles bless the LORD*

#### 18:13-27 — Delegation of Authority
**Lore:** Moses sits as judge from morning to evening, resolving disputes (18:13-16). Jethro observes: "What you are doing is not good. You and the people with you will certainly wear yourselves out, for the thing is too heavy for you. You are not able to do it alone" (18:17-18). Wise counsel—one person cannot do everything. Jethro advises: Represent the people before God (18:19), teach them statutes and laws (18:20), appoint capable, God-fearing men as leaders over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens (18:21). "Let them judge the people at all times. Every great matter they shall bring to you, but any small matter they shall decide themselves. So it will be easier for you, and they will bear the burden with you" (18:22). Delegation and subsidiarity—wisdom principle. Moses heeds Jethro's advice (18:24-26). Later, God Himself will command similar structure (Num 11:16-17). Jethro departs to his own land (18:27). Themes: wisdom from unexpected sources, shared leadership, sustainable ministry.
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Shared leadership*
