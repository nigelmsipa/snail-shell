# Exodus 24-31: Covenant Ratification and Tabernacle Blueprints

### Chapter 24: Blood of the Covenant

#### 24:1-11 — The Covenant Meal
**Lore:** God commands Moses: "Come up to the LORD, you and Aaron, Nadab, and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel, and worship from afar. Moses alone shall come near to the LORD, but the others shall not come near, and the people shall not come up with him" (24:1-2). Graduated access to God—Moses closest, then Aaron and sons, then elders, then people. Moses descends, tells Israel all God's words and rules. "And all the people answered with one voice and said, 'All the words that the LORD has spoken we will do'" (24:3). Second unanimous consent. Moses writes everything down—"the Book of the Covenant" (24:4, 7). He builds an altar, sets up twelve pillars for the twelve tribes. Young men offer burnt offerings and peace offerings (24:5). "And Moses took half of the blood and put it in basins, and half of the blood he threw against the altar" (24:6). Blood on altar = God's side of covenant. Moses reads the Book of the Covenant aloud. People respond: "All that the LORD has spoken we will do, and we will be obedient" (24:7). Third affirmation. "And Moses took the blood and threw it on the people and said, 'Behold the blood of the covenant that the LORD has made with you in accordance with all these words'" (24:8). Blood on people = human side. Covenant ratified by blood. Hebrews 9:18-20: "Therefore not even the first covenant was inaugurated without blood." Jesus at Last Supper: "This cup that is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood" (Luke 22:20). Blood seals covenants. Then Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and the seventy elders go up. "And they saw the God of Israel. There was under his feet as it were a pavement of sapphire stone, like the very heaven for clearness. And he did not lay his hand on the chief men of the people of Israel; they beheld God, and ate and drank" (24:10-11). Stunning theophany—they see God and live! Eating before God = covenant fellowship meal. Sapphire pavement = heaven's glory touching earth.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Blood seals the covenant*

#### 24:12-18 — Forty Days on Sinai
**Lore:** God calls Moses higher: "Come up to me on the mountain and wait there, that I may give you the tablets of stone, with the law and the commandment, which I have written for their instruction" (24:12). Stone tablets written by God's finger. Moses and Joshua ascend (24:13). Moses tells elders: wait here until we return; Aaron and Hur will judge disputes (24:14). "Then Moses went up on the mountain, and the cloud covered the mountain. The glory of the LORD dwelt on Mount Sinai, and the cloud covered it six days. And on the seventh day he called to Moses out of the midst of the cloud. Now the appearance of the glory of the LORD was like a devouring fire on the top of the mountain in the sight of the people of Israel. Moses entered the cloud and went up on the mountain. And Moses was on the mountain forty days and forty nights" (24:15-18). Forty days = testing period (Gen 7:12; 1 Kings 19:8; Matt 4:2). God's glory appears as consuming fire—terrifying yet Moses enters. What follows (chapters 25-31) are God's instructions during this 40-day encounter.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Glory like consuming fire*

### Chapters 25-31: The Tabernacle Blueprint

**📖 Structural Note:** Exodus 25-31 contains God's detailed instructions for the Tabernacle, given to Moses on Sinai. The order moves from inside out (Most Holy Place → Holy Place → Courtyard) and from most sacred (Ark) to least (Courtyard altar). Chapters 35-40 reverse this, building from outside in, showing movement from man's work toward God's presence. The Tabernacle (מִשְׁכָּן, mishkan, "dwelling") is where YHWH dwells among His people—the climax of Exodus.

#### 25:1-9 — Offerings for the Sanctuary
**Lore:** God says, "Speak to the people of Israel, that they take for me a contribution (תְּרוּמָה, terumah, "heave offering, gift"). From every man whose heart moves him you shall receive the contribution for me" (25:2). Freewill giving—God doesn't coerce. Materials listed: gold, silver, bronze, blue/purple/scarlet yarn, fine linen, goat hair, ram skins, goatskins, acacia wood, oil, spices, onyx stones, gems (25:3-7). These are the plundered treasures from Egypt (12:35-36)—Egypt's wealth now funds God's dwelling. "And let them make me a sanctuary (מִקְדָּשׁ, miqdash), that I may dwell (שָׁכַנְתִּי, shakhanti) in their midst. Exactly as I show you concerning the pattern (תַּבְנִית, tabnit) of the tabernacle, and of all its furniture, so you shall make it" (25:8-9). God's goal: to dwell among His people. The Tabernacle is patterned after a heavenly reality (Heb 8:5, 9:23-24). Later, Christ "tabernacled" among us (John 1:14, σκηνόω, skenoo, "pitched his tent").
*Verses: 9 | Theme: God desires to dwell with us*

#### 25:10-22 — The Ark of the Covenant
**Lore:** First item: the Ark (אֲרוֹן, aron), most sacred object. Dimensions: 2.5 cubits long, 1.5 cubits wide, 1.5 cubits high (about 45"x27"x27"). Acacia wood overlaid with pure gold inside and out (25:10-11). Gold rings at corners for carrying poles (25:12-15). "And you shall put into the ark the testimony (עֵדֻת, edut) that I shall give you" (25:16)—the stone tablets. The lid: "mercy seat" (כַּפֹּרֶת, kapporet, from כָּפַר, kaphar, "to atone, cover"). Made of pure gold, same dimensions as top of ark (25:17). Two cherubim (כְּרֻבִים, keruvim) of hammered gold on each end, facing each other, wings spread upward covering the mercy seat (25:18-20). "There I will meet with you, and from above the mercy seat, from between the two cherubim that are on the ark of the testimony, I will speak with you about all that I will give you in commandment for the people of Israel" (25:22). God's throne on earth—He sits enthroned between the cherubim (1 Sam 4:4; Ps 80:1, 99:1). The mercy seat is where blood is sprinkled on Yom Kippur (Lev 16:14-15)—atonement covers the law. Typology: Christ is our mercy seat (ἱλαστήριον, hilasterion, Rom 3:25). The law (tablets) condemns; the mercy seat (blood of Christ) atones.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: God's throne of mercy*

#### 25:23-30 — The Table of Bread
**Lore:** Table of acacia wood overlaid with gold: 2 cubits long, 1 cubit wide, 1.5 cubits high (36"x18"x27") (25:23-24). Gold molding, rings, poles for carrying (25:25-28). Plates, dishes, pitchers, bowls of pure gold (25:29). "And you shall set the bread of the Presence (לֶחֶם הַפָּנִים, lechem ha-panim, "bread of the face") on the table before me regularly" (25:30). Twelve loaves (one per tribe) placed on table each Sabbath, eaten by priests (Lev 24:5-9). Symbolizes God's provision and Israel's dedication. Jesus: "I am the bread of life" (John 6:35). The table represents fellowship and covenant communion.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Bread of God's presence*

#### 25:31-40 — The Golden Lampstand
**Lore:** The menorah (מְנוֹרָה): pure hammered gold, one piece, no seams (25:31). Six branches from central shaft (seven lamps total). Almond blossom design—cups, calyxes, flowers (25:32-36). "And you shall make seven lamps for it. And the lamps shall be set up so as to give light on the space in front of it" (25:37). Light shines forward—illuminating the Holy Place. Fueled with pure olive oil (27:20). Tended morning and evening by priests (30:7-8; Lev 24:2-4). Symbolism: God is light (1 John 1:5); Israel is light to nations (Isa 42:6). Jesus: "I am the light of the world" (John 8:12). Revelation shows seven lampstands = seven churches (Rev 1:20). One talent of gold (75 pounds) for the lampstand and accessories (25:39). "And see that you make them after the pattern for them, which is being shown you on the mountain" (25:40). Heavenly pattern revealed to Moses.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Light of God's presence*

### Chapter 26: The Tabernacle Structure

#### 26:1-37 — Curtains, Frames, and Veil
**Lore:** (Condensed) The Tabernacle proper consists of curtains and wooden frames forming a tent. Inner curtains: fine linen with blue, purple, scarlet, cherubim woven in (26:1-6). Outer curtains: goat hair (26:7-13). Coverings: ram skins dyed red, goatskins (26:14). Wooden frames (acacia overlaid with gold) form walls: 20 frames south, 20 north, 6 west plus 2 corners (26:15-30). Five crossbars hold frames together (26:26-29). Interior divided by a veil (פָּרֹכֶת, parokhet): blue, purple, scarlet, cherubim embroidered (26:31). The veil hangs on four gold-overlaid pillars, separating the Holy Place from the Most Holy Place (26:33). "And you shall put the mercy seat on the ark of the testimony in the Most Holy Place" (26:34). Ark behind the veil. Table and lampstand in Holy Place (26:35). Outer screen for entrance: embroidered curtain on five pillars (26:36-37). Structure: Courtyard → Holy Place (table, lampstand, incense altar) → Veil → Most Holy Place (Ark). Only high priest enters Most Holy once yearly (Yom Kippur). At Christ's death, the veil tears top to bottom (Matt 27:51)—access to God opened permanently (Heb 10:19-20).
*Verses: 37 | Theme: The veil separates holy from most holy*

### Chapter 27: The Altar and Courtyard

#### 27:1-8 — The Bronze Altar
**Lore:** Altar of burnt offering: acacia wood overlaid with bronze, 5 cubits square, 3 cubits high (7.5'x7.5'x4.5') (27:1-2). Horns at four corners (27:2). Bronze utensils: pots, shovels, basins, forks, firepans (27:3). Bronze grating, rings, poles (27:4-7). Hollow, made of boards (27:8). This altar stands in the courtyard, first thing encountered entering Tabernacle—sacrifice precedes worship. All burnt offerings, grain offerings, sin offerings offered here. Blood sprinkled on horns (Lev 4). Perpetual fire (Lev 6:12-13). Typology: Christ crucified (Heb 13:10-12). The altar represents atonement—without shedding of blood, no forgiveness (Heb 9:22).
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Bronze altar of atonement*

#### 27:9-19 — The Courtyard
**Lore:** Courtyard (חָצֵר, chatser): 100 cubits long (150'), 50 cubits wide (75'), 5 cubits high (7.5'). Perimeter: linen hangings on bronze pillars with silver hooks and bands (27:9-18). Gate on east side: 20 cubits wide, embroidered curtain of blue, purple, scarlet (27:16). All utensils and tent pegs: bronze (27:19). The courtyard separates sacred space from common space. Israel camps around it; only priests enter.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Sacred boundaries*

#### 27:20-21 — Oil for the Lamp
**Lore:** Command: "You shall command the people of Israel that they bring to you pure beaten olive oil for the light, that a lamp may regularly be set up to burn. In the tent of meeting, outside the veil that is before the testimony, Aaron and his sons shall tend it from evening to morning before the LORD. It shall be a statute forever to be observed throughout their generations by the people of Israel" (27:20-21). The menorah never goes out—continual light symbolizes God's perpetual presence. Pure oil = Holy Spirit (Zech 4:1-6). Jesus: "Let your light shine before others" (Matt 5:16).
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Perpetual light*

### Chapter 28: The Priestly Garments

#### 28:1-43 — Aaron's Holy Garments
**Lore:** (Condensed) God commands: "Bring near to you Aaron your brother, and his sons with him, from among the people of Israel, to serve me as priests" (28:1). Priestly garments: "for glory and for beauty" (28:2, 40). Components: (1) **Ephod**: gold, blue, purple, scarlet, fine linen (28:5-14). Two onyx stones on shoulder pieces with names of Israel's twelve tribes engraved—six names per stone (28:9-12). Aaron bears Israel's names before God. (2) **Breastpiece**: square, doubled, same materials as ephod (28:15-30). Twelve precious stones set in gold, each engraved with a tribe's name (28:17-21). "So Aaron shall bear the names of the sons of Israel in the breastpiece of judgment on his heart, when he goes into the Holy Place, to bring them to regular remembrance before the LORD" (28:29). Israel on his shoulders (strength) and heart (love). The Urim and Thummim (אוּרִים וְתֻמִּים, "lights and perfections") placed in breastpiece—means of divine guidance (28:30). Exact nature unknown—possibly sacred lots. (3) **Robe of the ephod**: all blue, with hem decorated with pomegranates (blue, purple, scarlet) and golden bells alternating (28:31-35). "And it shall be on Aaron when he ministers, and its sound shall be heard when he goes into the Holy Place before the LORD, and when he comes out, so that he does not die" (28:35). Bells announce his movements—reverent approach to God. (4) **Golden plate**: engraved "Holy to the LORD" (קֹדֶשׁ לַיהוָה, qodesh la-YHWH), fastened to turban (28:36-38). Aaron bears iniquity of holy things. (5) **Tunic, sash, turban** of fine linen (28:39). (6) **Undergarments** of linen to cover nakedness (28:42-43). All garments emphasize holiness and mediation. Typology: Christ our High Priest (Heb 4:14-16). Believers are priests (1 Pet 2:9; Rev 1:6).
*Verses: 43 | Theme: Garments of glory and beauty*

### Chapter 29: Consecration of Priests

#### 29:1-46 — The Ordination Ritual
**Lore:** (Condensed) Seven-day consecration ceremony for Aaron and sons (29:35). Procedure: (1) Wash with water (29:4). (2) Dress in priestly garments (29:5-6). (3) Anoint with oil (29:7). (4) Sacrifices: sin offering (bull) (29:10-14), burnt offering (ram) (29:15-18), ordination offering (ram) (29:19-28). Blood applied to right ear, thumb, big toe of priests (29:20)—sanctifying hearing, action, walk. Blood sprinkled on garments with oil (29:21). Wave offering of ram parts and bread before the LORD (29:24-26). Priests eat consecrated portions (29:31-33). Daily offerings: two lambs, morning and evening, with grain and drink offerings (29:38-41). Perpetual offering "throughout your generations at the entrance of the tent of meeting before the LORD, where I will meet with you, to speak to you there. There I will meet with the people of Israel, and it shall be sanctified by my glory" (29:42-43). God's promise: "I will dwell among the people of Israel and will be their God. And they shall know that I am the LORD their God, who brought them out of the land of Egypt that I might dwell among them. I am the LORD their God" (29:45-46). The purpose of the Exodus: God dwelling with His redeemed people.
*Verses: 46 | Theme: God dwells with the redeemed*

### Chapter 30: Incense, Census, Bronze Laver

#### 30:1-10 — The Altar of Incense
**Lore:** Gold altar for burning incense: 1 cubit square, 2 cubits high (18"x18"x36"), horns, gold molding (30:1-3). Rings and poles for carrying (30:4-5). Placed "before the veil that is above the ark of the testimony, before the mercy seat" (30:6)—in Holy Place, directly in front of the veil. Aaron burns fragrant incense morning and evening when tending lamps (30:7-8). "A regular incense offering before the LORD throughout your generations" (30:8). Sacred incense only—no unauthorized incense, grain, burnt, or drink offerings on this altar (30:9). Once yearly, Aaron makes atonement on its horns with blood (Yom Kippur) (30:10). Incense symbolizes prayer rising to God (Ps 141:2; Rev 5:8, 8:3-4). Sweet fragrance represents intercession.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Incense of prayer*

#### 30:11-16 — The Census Tax
**Lore:** When taking census, each man pays a ransom (כֹּפֶר, kopher, "atonement price") to avoid plague (30:12). Half-shekel per person, rich and poor alike (30:13-15). "The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less, than the half shekel, when you give the LORD's contribution to make atonement for your lives" (30:15). Equality before God—life's value not determined by wealth. Money funds the Tabernacle service (30:16). Ransom principle: every life requires atonement. Jesus: "The Son of Man came...to give his life as a ransom (λύτρον, lytron) for many" (Mark 10:45).
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Ransomed lives*

#### 30:17-21 — The Bronze Laver
**Lore:** Bronze basin for washing, placed between tent of meeting and altar (30:18). Aaron and sons must wash hands and feet before entering tent or approaching altar, "so that they may not die" (30:20-21). Ritual purity required for service. Water cleanses from defilement. Typology: baptism (Titus 3:5); Christ washing disciples' feet (John 13:1-17); "the washing of water with the word" (Eph 5:26).
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Washed before service*

#### 30:22-38 — Anointing Oil and Incense
**Lore:** Holy anointing oil recipe: myrrh, cinnamon, cane, cassia, olive oil (30:23-25). For anointing Tabernacle, furnishings, and priests (30:26-30). "It shall not be poured on the body of an ordinary person, and you shall make no other like it in composition. It is holy, and it shall be holy to you" (30:32). Misuse punishable by being cut off (30:33). Sacred incense recipe: stacte, onycha, galbanum, frankincense (30:34). Beaten fine, placed before the testimony (30:36). "You shall not make any in the same composition for yourselves. It shall be for you holy to the LORD. Whoever makes any like it to use as perfume shall be cut off from his people" (30:37-38). God's holiness demands exclusive worship—no counterfeits.
*Verses: 17 | Theme: Sacred and set apart*

### Chapter 31: Bezalel, Hur, and the Sabbath Sign

#### 31:1-11 — The Spirit-Filled Craftsmen
**Lore:** God tells Moses: "See, I have called by name Bezalel (בְּצַלְאֵל, "in the shadow of God") the son of Uri, son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah, and I have filled him with the Spirit of God, with ability (חָכְמָה, chokmah, "wisdom") and intelligence and knowledge and all craftsmanship, to devise artistic designs, to work in gold, silver, and bronze, in cutting stones for setting, and in carving wood, to work in every craft" (31:2-5). First person in Scripture said to be filled with the Spirit—for building God's house. God's Spirit empowers artistic skill. Oholiab (אָהֳלִיאָב, "Father's tent") of Dan appointed to assist (31:6). "And I have given to all able men ability, that they may make all that I have commanded you" (31:6). God distributes gifts for corporate worship. All furnishings listed (31:7-11). Building the Tabernacle is Spirit-led work.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Spirit-empowered artistry*

#### 31:12-18 — The Sabbath Sign
**Lore:** God says, "Above all (אַךְ, akh, "surely, however, only") you shall keep my Sabbaths, for this is a sign between me and you throughout your generations, that you may know that I, the LORD, sanctify you. You shall keep the Sabbath, because it is holy for you. Everyone who profanes it shall be put to death. Whoever does any work on it, that soul shall be cut off from among his people. Six days shall work be done, but the seventh day is a Sabbath of solemn rest, holy to the LORD. Whoever does any work on the Sabbath day shall be put to death. Therefore the people of Israel shall keep the Sabbath, observing the Sabbath throughout their generations, as a covenant forever. It is a sign forever between me and the people of Israel that in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and on the seventh day he rested and was refreshed" (31:13-17). Sabbath is the covenant sign—more important than Tabernacle building. Work stops for Sabbath. Sabbath recalls creation (Gen 2:2-3) and redemption (Deut 5:15). Jesus: "The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath. So the Son of Man is lord even of the Sabbath" (Mark 2:27-28). Sabbath points to rest in Christ (Heb 4:9-10). "And he gave to Moses, when he had finished speaking with him on Mount Sinai, the two tablets of the testimony, tablets of stone, written with the finger of God" (31:18). Conclusion of 40 days—Moses receives the tablets. But while Moses is on the mountain, disaster unfolds below...
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Sabbath, the covenant sign*
