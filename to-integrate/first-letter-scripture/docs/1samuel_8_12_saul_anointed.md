# 1 Samuel 8-12: Saul's Anointing

## Overview
Israel demands king "like all nations"—rejects God's direct kingship. Saul chosen: impressive appearance, initially humble, Spirit-empowered. Early success at Jabesh-gilead shows God's favor. Samuel's farewell warns: monarchy conditional on obedience. Central theme: people's choice (appearance-based) vs. God's accommodation.

---

## Chapter 8: Israel Demands a King

#### 8:1-9 — The Elders' Request
**Lore:** "When Samuel became old, he made his sons judges over Israel... Yet his sons did not walk in his ways but turned aside after gain. They took bribes and perverted justice" (8:1, 3). Samuel's sons fail—echoes Eli! "Then all the elders of Israel gathered together and came to Samuel at Ramah and said to him, 'Behold, you are old and your sons do not walk in your ways. Now appoint for us a king to judge us like all the nations'" (8:4-5). **DEMAND FOR KING**—"like all nations." Want visible military leader. Legitimate concern but wrong solution. "And the LORD said to Samuel, 'Obey the voice of the people in all that they say to you, for they have not rejected you, but they have rejected me from being king over them'" (8:7). **KEY THEOLOGY**—rejection of theocracy! God grants request but doesn't approve.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Rejecting divine kingship*

#### 8:10-22 — Warning and Insistence
**Lore:** "So Samuel told all the words of the LORD to the people... 'These will be the ways of the king who will reign over you: he will take your sons... daughters... fields... vineyards'" (8:10-14). Lists king's oppression. "And in that day you will cry out because of your king, whom you have chosen for yourselves, but the LORD will not answer you" (8:18). Future regret. "But the people refused to obey the voice of Samuel. And they said, 'No! But there shall be a king over us, that we also may be like all the nations'" (8:19-20). Persist despite warning. Dissatisfied longing for worldly power. God grants—will work through their choice.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Demanding conformity*

## Chapters 9-10: Saul's Selection

#### 9:1-10 — Lost Donkeys Lead to Prophet
**Lore:** "There was a man of Benjamin whose name was Kish... And he had a son whose name was Saul, a handsome young man. There was not a man among the people of Israel more handsome than he. From his shoulders upward he was taller than any of the people" (9:1-2). **SAUL** (שָׁאוּל) = "asked for"—name matches request! Impressive appearance—what people wanted. "Now the donkeys of Kish, Saul's father, were lost" (9:3). Mundane errand. God's providence through ordinary. Servant suggests consulting prophet (9:5-6). Perfect timing—divine appointment!
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Providence guides*

#### 9:11-21 — Samuel Awaits God's Anointed  
**Lore:** "Now the day before Saul came, the LORD had revealed to Samuel: 'Tomorrow about this time I will send to you a man from the land of Benjamin, and you shall anoint him to be prince over my people Israel'" (9:15-16). **GOD PREPARED SAMUEL** day before! "Prince" (נָגִיד, nagid) not yet "king." "When Samuel saw Saul, the LORD told him, 'Here is the man... He it is who shall restrain my people'" (9:17). Divine confirmation. "Saul answered, 'Am I not a Benjaminite, from the least of the tribes of Israel? And is not my clan the humblest?'" (9:21). **INITIAL HUMILITY**—good sign. Samuel honors Saul at feast (9:22-24).
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Divine appointment*

#### 10:1-8 — Private Anointing and Three Signs
**Lore:** "Then Samuel took a flask of oil and poured it on his head and kissed him and said, 'Has not the LORD anointed you to be prince over his people Israel?'" (10:1). **PRIVATE ANOINTING**. Samuel gives **THREE SIGNS** (10:2-6): donkeys found, men give bread, meet prophets. "Then you shall go down before me to Gilgal... Seven days you shall wait, until I come to you" (10:8). **CRITICAL INSTRUCTION**—violated in ch 13!
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Anointing confirmed*

#### 10:9-16 — Spirit Transformation
**Lore:** "When he turned his back to leave Samuel, God gave him another heart. And all these signs came to pass that day" (10:9). "Another heart" (לֵב אַחֵר) = transformed. "When they came to Gibeah... the Spirit of God rushed upon him, and he prophesied among them" (10:10). **SPIRIT COMES**—public spectacle. "Is Saul also among the prophets?" becomes proverb. Saul doesn't reveal kingship to uncle (10:15-16)—humility or fear?
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Spirit empowerment*

#### 10:17-27 — Public Selection by Lot
**Lore:** Samuel rehearses rebellion before selection (10:17-19). "Then Samuel brought all the tribes of Israel near, and the tribe of Benjamin was taken by lot... and Saul the son of Kish was taken by lot" (10:20-21). **SELECTION BY LOT**—three-stage narrowing. "But when they sought him, he could not be found... 'Behold, he has hidden himself among the baggage'" (10:22). **HIDES AMONG SUPPLIES**! "And Samuel said to all the people, 'Do you see him whom the LORD has chosen? There is none like him among all the people.' And all the people shouted, 'Long live the king!'" (10:24). "Then Samuel told the people the rights and duties of the kingship, and he wrote them in a book and laid it up before the LORD" (10:25). **CONSTITUTIONAL MONARCHY**—king under God's law! Division—supporters vs. rejecters (10:26-27). Saul wisely quiet.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Public confirmation*

## Chapter 11: First Victory

#### 11:1-11 — Jabesh-Gilead Deliverance
**Lore:** "Then Nahash the Ammonite went up and besieged Jabesh-gilead... 'On this condition I will make a treaty with you, that I gouge out all your right eyes'" (11:1-2). **Nahash** (נָחָשׁ) = "serpent." Humiliating terms. "Now, behold, Saul was coming from the field behind the oxen... And the Spirit of God rushed upon Saul when he heard these words, and his anger was greatly kindled" (11:5-6). **SPIRIT EMPOWERMENT**—still farming (humble). Righteous anger. Saul at spiritual peak. "He took a yoke of oxen and cut them in pieces and sent them throughout all the territory of Israel... 'Whoever does not come out after Saul and Samuel, so shall it be done to his oxen!' Then the dread of the LORD fell upon the people" (11:7). Dramatic call. 330,000 respond (11:8)! "And the next day Saul put the people in three companies... and struck down the Ammonites until the heat of the day. And those who survived were scattered, so that no two of them were left together" (11:11). Dawn surprise attack. Complete rout.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Spirit-filled victory*

#### 11:12-15 — Magnanimity and Renewal
**Lore:** "Then the people said to Samuel, 'Who is it that said, "Shall Saul reign over us?" Bring the men, that we may put them to death.' But Saul said, 'Not a man shall be put to death this day, for today the LORD has accomplished salvation in Israel'" (11:12-13). **SAUL'S MAGNANIMITY**—refuses revenge! Gives God glory. Perfect theology. Saul at his absolute best. "Then Samuel said... 'Come, let us go to Gilgal and there renew the kingdom... and there they made Saul king before the LORD in Gilgal. There they sacrificed peace offerings before the LORD, and there Saul and all the men of Israel rejoiced greatly'" (11:14-15). Formal coronation at Gilgal (Joshua's site). This is Saul's finest hour.
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Humble victory*

## Chapter 12: Samuel's Farewell

#### 12:1-12 — Vindication and Historical Review
**Lore:** "And Samuel said to all Israel... 'Here I am; testify against me before the LORD and before his anointed. Whose ox have I taken? Or whose donkey have I taken? Or whom have I defrauded?'" (12:1-3). **SAMUEL'S VINDICATION**—challenges accusation. "They said, 'You have not defrauded us or oppressed us'" (12:4). Confirm **strict integrity**. Historical review (12:6-11): cycles of rebellion and deliverance. "And when you saw that Nahash the king of the Ammonites came against you, you said to me, 'No, but a king shall reign over us,' when the LORD your God was your king" (12:12). Rebukes motive—God was already king!
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Faithful vindication*

#### 12:13-25 — Conditional Covenant and Thunder Sign
**Lore:** "If you will fear the LORD and serve him and obey his voice and not rebel against the commandment of the LORD, and if both you and the king who reigns over you will follow the LORD your God, it will be well. But if you will not obey... then the hand of the LORD will be against you and your king" (12:14-15). **CONDITIONAL COVENANT**—key! Both king and people accountable. "Is it not wheat harvest today? I will call upon the LORD, that he may send thunder and rain" (12:17). Wheat harvest = dry season. Rain impossible. "So Samuel called upon the LORD, and the LORD sent thunder and rain that day" (12:18). **MIRACLE**—instant answer! People confess: "we have added to all our sins this evil, to ask for ourselves a king" (12:19). "Moreover, as for me, far be it from me that I should sin against the LORD by ceasing to pray for you, and I will instruct you in the good and the right way... But if you still do wickedly, you shall be swept away, both you and your king'" (12:23-25). **SAMUEL'S COMMITMENT**—will intercede and teach. Final warning: obedience or destruction.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Obedience required*
