# Leviticus 25-27: Jubilee, Blessings/Curses, Vows (Properly Divided)

## Part VII: Sabbath Economics and Covenant Consequences (Chapters 25-27)

### Chapter 25: Sabbath Year and Jubilee

#### 25:1-7 — Land Sabbath Every Seventh Year
**Lore:** "The LORD spoke to Moses on Mount Sinai, saying, 'Speak to the people of Israel and say to them, When you come into the land that I give you, the land shall keep a Sabbath to the LORD'" (25:1-2). Even the land observes Sabbath! Six years sow and harvest, seventh year = Sabbath of solemn rest for land (25:3-4). No organized harvesting; voluntary growth may be eaten by all (25:5-7). Tests faith: will you trust God to provide? Declares God as ultimate owner. Prevents soil exhaustion, protects poor. Disobedience → exile (26:34-35; fulfilled 2 Chr 36:21).
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Land rest*

#### 25:8-17 — The Fiftieth Year: Jubilee
**Lore:** "You shall count seven weeks of years, seven times seven years, so that the time of the seven weeks of years shall give you forty-nine years. Then you shall sound the loud trumpet on the tenth day of the seventh month. On the Day of Atonement you shall sound the trumpet throughout all your land. And you shall consecrate the fiftieth year, and proclaim liberty (דְּרוֹר, deror, "release") throughout the land to all its inhabitants. It shall be a jubilee (יוֹבֵל, yovel, "ram's horn") for you" (25:8-10). Most radical economic law in Scripture! After 49 years, 50th = Jubilee. Proclaimed on Yom Kippur with trumpets. Land returns to original families, slaves freed, debts canceled. Isaiah 61:1-2 echoes this. Jesus reads it in Nazareth synagogue (Luke 4:18-21): "Today this scripture has been fulfilled." Jesus = ultimate Jubilee—spiritual liberation. "In this year of jubilee each of you shall return to his property" (25:13). Land sales actually leases till Jubilee. "You shall not wrong one another, but you shall fear your God" (25:17). Economic fairness rooted in reverence.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Liberty proclaimed*

#### 25:18-22 — Trust God's Provision in Sabbath Year
**Lore:** "Therefore you shall do my statutes and keep my rules and perform them, and then you will dwell in the land securely" (25:18-19). Obedience = security. "And if you say, 'What shall we eat in the seventh year, if we may not sow or gather in our crop?' I will command my blessing on you in the sixth year, so that it will produce a crop sufficient for three years" (25:20-21). Supernatural provision promised! Sixth year sustains through Sabbath year and eighth year planting/harvest. Manna-like faith required (Ex 16). God tests trust.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Supernatural provision*

#### 25:23-34 — Land Redemption and Levitical Cities
**Lore:** "The land shall not be sold in perpetuity, for the land is mine. For you are strangers and sojourners with me" (25:23). Ultimate ownership: God's. Humans = tenants. If poverty forces land sale, kinsman may redeem it (25:25). If no redeemer, seller may buy back calculating years to Jubilee (25:26-27). If unable, returns at Jubilee (25:28). Houses in walled cities: 1 year redemption period, then permanent to buyer (25:29-30). Village houses treated like fields—redeemable anytime, return at Jubilee (25:31). Levitical cities: Levites may redeem anytime; return at Jubilee (25:32-33). Pasturelands never sold (25:34). Kinsman-redeemer (גֹּאֵל, goel) foreshadows Christ (Ruth 3-4; Job 19:25; Isa 59:20). Peter: "ransomed...with the precious blood of Christ" (1 Pet 1:18-19).
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Redemption laws*

#### 25:35-43 — No Interest on Loans, Treat Servants Kindly
**Lore:** "If your brother becomes poor and cannot maintain himself with you, you shall support him as though he were a stranger and a sojourner" (25:35). Care for poor mandatory. "Take no interest (נֶשֶׁךְ, neshekh, "usury, bite") from him or profit, but fear your God, that your brother may live beside you" (25:36-37). No charging interest to fellow Israelites—compassion-motivated economics. "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt to give you the land of Canaan, and to be your God" (25:38). Redemption memory grounds ethics. If Israelite sells himself: don't treat as slave, but as hired worker until Jubilee, then free with children (25:39-41). "For they are my servants, whom I brought out of the land of Egypt; they shall not be sold as slaves. You shall not rule over him ruthlessly but shall fear your God" (25:42-43). Israelites belong to God; can't be enslaved by humans.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Compassionate economics*

#### 25:44-55 — Foreign Slaves and Redemption Rights
**Lore:** May buy slaves from surrounding nations; they're permanent property, inherited (25:44-46). "But over your brothers the people of Israel you shall not rule, one over another ruthlessly" (25:46). Distinction: foreigners may be enslaved; Israelites may not. If Israelite sells himself to foreigner in Israel, kinsman or he himself may redeem (25:47-49). Calculate years to Jubilee, prorate redemption price (25:50-52). If not redeemed, free at Jubilee (25:54). "For it is to me that the people of Israel are servants. They are my servants whom I brought out of the land of Egypt: I am the LORD your God" (25:55). Israelites serve God alone. Extraordinary covenant with creation and community. Foreshadows messianic liberty (Isa 61). Did Israel observe Jubilee? No clear biblical evidence. Principle remains: God owns all; we're stewards.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Ultimate belonging*

### Chapter 26: Blessings and Curses

#### 26:1-13 — Blessings for Obedience
**Lore:** "You shall not make idols for yourselves or erect an image or pillar...You shall keep my Sabbaths and reverence my sanctuary: I am the LORD" (26:1-2). Frame: no idolatry, keep Sabbaths, revere sanctuary. "If you walk in my statutes and observe my commandments and do them, then I will give you your rains in their season, and the land shall yield its increase" (26:3-4). Agricultural abundance. "Your threshing shall last to the time of the grape harvest, and the grape harvest shall last to the time for sowing...you shall eat your bread to the full and dwell in your land securely" (26:5). Continuous harvest—supernatural. "I will give peace (שָׁלוֹם) in the land...I will remove harmful beasts...the sword shall not go through your land" (26:6). Victory over enemies (26:7-8). "I will turn to you and make you fruitful and multiply you" (26:9). So much food you discard old stock (26:10)! "I will make my dwelling (מִשְׁכָּן, mishkan, "tabernacle") among you, and my soul shall not abhor you. And I will walk among you and will be your God, and you shall be my people" (26:11-12). **Ultimate blessing: God's presence.** Echoes Ex 29:45-46. "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, that you should not be their slaves. And I have broken the bars of your yoke and made you walk erect" (26:13). Dignity restored.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Abundant blessings*

#### 26:14-26 — Escalating Curses: Terror, Drought, Wild Beasts, War
**Lore:** "But if you will not listen to me and will not do all these commandments...I will do this to you..." (26:14-16). Five cycles of escalating judgment. **First cycle** (26:14-17): Terror, disease, fever, enemies prevail. "You shall sow your seed in vain, for your enemies shall eat it" (26:16). Flee when none pursue. **Second cycle** (26:18-20): "If in spite of this you will not listen to me, then I will discipline you again sevenfold" (26:18). Sky like iron, earth like bronze—drought. Strength spent in vain; no yield. **Third cycle** (26:21-22): "If you walk contrary to me and will not listen...I will let loose the wild beasts against you, which shall bereave you of your children and destroy your livestock" (26:21-22). Roads desolate. **Fourth cycle** (26:23-26): "If by this discipline you are not turned to me but walk contrary to me, then I also will walk contrary to you, and I myself will strike you sevenfold for your sins" (26:23-24). God Himself fights against them. Pestilence, siege, famine—bread rationed by weight (26:26). Escalating severity designed to provoke repentance.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Escalating discipline*

#### 26:27-39 — Final Cycle: Cannibalism, Exile, Desolation
**Lore:** "If in spite of all this you will not listen to me, but walk contrary to me, then I will walk contrary to you in fury, and I myself will discipline you sevenfold for your sins. You shall eat the flesh of your sons, and you shall eat the flesh of your daughters" (26:27-29). Cannibalism during siege—fulfilled in 2 Kgs 6:28-29, Lam 4:10. Destruction of high places, corpses piled on idol ruins (26:30). Cities waste, sanctuaries desolate (26:31). Land devastated, enemies appalled (26:32). "And I will scatter you among the nations, and I will unsheathe the sword after you, and your land shall be a desolation, and your cities shall be a waste. Then the land shall enjoy its Sabbaths as long as it lies desolate, while you are in your enemies' land; then the land shall rest, and enjoy its Sabbaths" (26:33-34). Exile. The land finally gets its rest—forcibly. Fulfilled in Babylonian exile (2 Chr 36:21). Survivors live in terror: "the sound of a driven leaf shall put them to flight" (26:36). Stumble over one another fleeing imaginary enemies. "And you shall perish among the nations, and the land of your enemies shall eat you up" (26:38). Assimilation and death. "Those of you who are left shall rot away in your enemies' lands because of their iniquity" (26:39). Generational consequences.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Exile and desolation*

#### 26:40-46 — Hope: Confession and Restoration
**Lore:** "But if they confess their iniquity and the iniquity of their fathers in their treachery that they committed against me, and also in walking contrary to me, so that I walked contrary to them and brought them into the land of their enemies—if then their uncircumcised heart is humbled and they make amends for their iniquity, then I will remember my covenant with Jacob, and I will remember my covenant with Isaac and my covenant with Abraham, and I will remember the land" (26:40-42). After dire judgment, **promise of restoration!** God will remember the covenant. "But for their sake I will remember the covenant with their forefathers, whom I brought out of the land of Egypt in the sight of the nations, that I might be their God: I am the LORD" (26:45). Grace triumphs. Even in exile, God doesn't abandon. "These are the statutes and rules and laws that the LORD made between himself and the people of Israel through Moses on Mount Sinai" (26:46). Conclusion of Holiness Code. Deut 28-30 parallels. Exilic prophets reflect on this. Christ fulfills blessings and bears curses (Gal 3:13-14).
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Repentance restores*

### Chapter 27: Vows and Dedication

#### 27:1-13 — Valuation of Persons and Animals
**Lore:** "If anyone makes a special vow (נֶדֶר, neder) to the LORD involving the valuation of persons" (27:2). Sliding scale by age/gender: Males 20-60: 50 shekels. Females 20-60: 30 shekels. Males 5-20: 20. Females 5-20: 10. Males 1 mo-5 yrs: 5. Females 1 mo-5 yrs: 3. Males 60+: 15. Females 60+: 10 (27:3-7). If too poor, priest assesses ability (27:8). Valuations = economic/labor capacity, not intrinsic worth. Clean animal vowed = holy, no substitution (27:9-10). Unclean animal: priest assesses, add 20% to redeem (27:11-13). Vow dedicates to sanctuary; price redeems.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Vow valuations*

#### 27:14-25 — Houses, Fields, and Redemption
**Lore:** Dedicate house: priest assesses, add 20% to redeem (27:14-15). Inherited land: valuation based on seed required (homer barley = 50 shekels). If dedicated at Jubilee start, full value; mid-cycle, prorated to Jubilee (27:16-18). Add 20% to redeem; otherwise sanctuary property permanently (27:19-20). If not redeemed and sold, at Jubilee goes to priests as "most holy" (27:21). Purchased land dedicated: valuation to Jubilee, returns to original owner (27:22-24). All valuations in sanctuary shekels (20 gerahs per shekel, 27:25). Every detail regulated to honor God.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Property dedication*

#### 27:26-34 — Firstborns, Devoted Things, Tithes
**Lore:** "But a firstborn of animals, which as a firstborn belongs to the LORD, no man may dedicate; whether ox or sheep, it is the LORD's" (27:26). Firstborn already God's (Ex 13:2); can't vow. Unclean firstborn may be redeemed at valuation + 20%, or sold (27:27). "But no devoted thing (חֵרֶם, cherem, "devoted to destruction") that a man devotes to the LORD...shall be sold or redeemed; every devoted thing is most holy to the LORD. No one devoted, who is to be devoted for destruction from mankind, shall be ransomed; he shall surely be put to death" (27:28-29). Cherem = irrevocably consecrated, often for destruction (Jericho, Josh 6:17). Once devoted, no redemption. "Every tithe (מַעֲשֵׂר, ma'aser, "tenth") of the land, whether of the seed of the land or of the fruit of the trees, is the LORD's; it is holy to the LORD" (27:30). To redeem tithe, add 20% (27:31). Every tenth animal passing under rod is holy—no substitution (27:32-33). **Closing:** "These are the commandments that the LORD commanded Moses for the people of Israel on Mount Sinai" (27:34). Final statement. Symmetry: book opens "the LORD called" (1:1), closes with "commandments...on Mount Sinai" (27:34). Everything—life, possessions, produce—ultimately belongs to God. Typology: We're "bought with a price" (1 Cor 6:20). Christ is our redemption (Eph 1:7). We vow ourselves to God through Christ, who paid the ultimate price.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: All belongs to God*
