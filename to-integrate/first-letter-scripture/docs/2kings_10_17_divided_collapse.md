# 2 Kings 10-17: The Divided Kingdom's Collapse

## Overview
Jehu's bloody purge of Baal worship establishes a "Jehu religion"—zeal without heart. Both kingdoms decline through assassination, superficial reform, and persistent idolatry, culminating in Israel's fall to Assyria. Central themes: unsafe zeal, preserved Davidic line, posthumous miracles, unheeded mercy, theological explanation for exile.

---

## Chapter 10: The Jehu Religion

#### 10:1-11 — Seventy Sons Slaughtered
**Lore:** "And Ahab had seventy sons in Samaria. And Jehu wrote letters, and sent to Samaria, unto the rulers of Jezreel, to the elders, and to them that brought up Ahab's children, saying... take ye the best and meetest of your master's sons, and set him on his father's throne, and fight for your master's house" (10:1-3). **Challenge**—defend Ahab's house or submit? "And they were exceedingly afraid, and said, Behold, two kings stood not before him: how then shall we stand?... We are thy servants, and will do all that thou shalt bid us; we will not make any king: do thou that which is good in thine eyes" (10:4-5). **Total capitulation**. "Then he wrote a letter the second time to them, saying, If ye be mine, and if ye will hearken unto my voice, take ye the heads of the men your master's sons, and come to me to Jezreel by to morrow this time" (10:6). **Demands heads**—forces elders to execute princes\! "And it came to pass, when the letter came to them, that they took the king's sons, and slew seventy persons, and put their heads in baskets, and sent him them to Jezreel" (10:7). **70 heads in baskets**—gruesome\! Fulfills Ahab's doom (1 Kings 21:21). "And he slew all that remained of the house of Ahab in Jezreel, and all his great men, and his kinsfolks, and his priests, until he left him none remaining" (10:11). **Complete extermination**—divine mandate executed brutally.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Dynasty destroyed*

#### 10:12-17 — Jehu's Further Bloodshed
**Lore:** "And he arose and departed, and came to Samaria. And as he was at the shearing house in the way, Jehu met with the brethren of Ahaziah king of Judah, and said, Who are ye? And they answered, We are the brethren of Ahaziah; and we go down to salute the children of the king and the children of the queen" (10:12-13). **42 relatives of Ahaziah** traveling—unaware of coup\! "And he said, Take them alive. And they took them alive, and slew them at the pit of the shearing house, even two and forty men; neither left he any of them" (10:14). **Mass execution**—Judah's princes killed\! Beyond divine mandate (only Ahab's house targeted). Shows Jehu's excess. "And when he was departed thence, he lighted on Jehonadab the son of Rechab coming to meet him: and he saluted him, and said to him, Is thine heart right, as my heart is with thy heart? And Jehonadab answered, It is. If it be, give me thine hand. And he gave him his hand; and he took him up to him into the chariot. And he said, Come with me, and see my zeal for the LORD. So they made him ride in his chariot" (10:15-16). **Alliance with Jehonadab**—Rechabite zealot (descendants refuse wine, live in tents, Jer 35). Jehu boasts: "see my **zeal** (קִנְאָה, qin'ah) for the LORD." Yet zeal ≠ obedience. "And when he came to Samaria, he slew all that remained unto Ahab in Samaria, till he had destroyed him, according to the saying of the LORD, which he spake to Elijah" (10:17). Purge complete.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Excessive zeal*

#### 10:18-28 — Baal Worship Eradicated
**Lore:** "And Jehu gathered all the people together, and said unto them, Ahab served Baal a little; but Jehu shall serve him much" (10:18). **Deception**—pretends to be Baal worshiper\! "Now therefore call unto me all the prophets of Baal, all his servants, and all his priests; let none be wanting: for I have a great sacrifice to do to Baal; whosoever shall be wanting, he shall not live. But Jehu did it in subtilty, to the intent that he might destroy the worshippers of Baal" (10:19). **Trap set**—summons all Baal devotees. "And Jehu sent through all Israel: and all the worshippers of Baal came, so that there was not a man left that came not. And they came into the house of Baal; and the house of Baal was full from one end to another" (10:21). Baal's temple packed. "And he said unto them, Search, and look that there be here with you none of the servants of the LORD, but the worshippers of Baal only" (10:23). Ensures only Baal worshipers present—won't kill Yah worshipers\! "And it came to pass, as soon as he had made an end of offering the burnt offering, that Jehu said to the guard and to the captains, Go in, and slay them; let none come forth. And they smote them with the edge of the sword... And they brought forth the images out of the house of Baal, and burned them. And they brake down the image of Baal, and brake down the house of Baal, and made it a draught house unto this day" (10:25-27). **Total destruction**—Baal worshipers killed, images burned, temple made **latrine** (ultimate desecration)\! Baal worship ended in Israel.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Baal destroyed*

#### 10:29-36 — Jehu's Incomplete Reform
**Lore:** "Thus Jehu destroyed Baal out of Israel. Howbeit from the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin, Jehu departed not from after them, to wit, the golden calves that were in Bethel, and that were in Dan" (10:28-29). **FATAL FLAW**—destroys Baal but keeps **golden calves**\! "The sins of Jeroboam" = original sin of Northern Kingdom (1 Kings 12:28-30). Political idolatry (prevents pilgrimage to Jerusalem) remains. "And the LORD said unto Jehu, Because thou hast done well in executing that which is right in mine eyes, and hast done unto the house of Ahab according to all that was in mine heart, thy children of the fourth generation shall sit on the throne of Israel" (10:30). **Reward**—4 generations promised (longest dynasty in Northern Kingdom: Jehu, Jehoahaz, Joash, Jeroboam II, Zechariah). "But Jehu took no heed to walk in the law of the LORD God of Israel with all his heart: for he departed not from the sins of Jeroboam, which made Israel to sin" (10:31). **"JEHU RELIGION"**—violent zeal without heart loyalty\! Obedience in judgment, disobedience in worship. Shows **unsafe religion**—will "never bind hearts together." "In those days the LORD began to cut Israel short: and Hazael smote them in all the coasts of Israel" (10:32). **Territory lost immediately**—Hazael conquers Transjordan (10:33). Judgment for incomplete reform. Jehu reigns 28 years, dies (10:34-36).
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Zeal without heart*

## Chapter 11: The Preserved Line

#### 11:1-12 — Athaliah's Coup and Joash Hidden
**Lore:** "And when Athaliah the mother of Ahaziah saw that her son was dead, she arose and destroyed all the seed royal" (11:1). **ATHALIAH** (עֲתַלְיָה)—daughter of Ahab and Jezebel, married into Judah's royal line, mother of Ahaziah. When son killed by Jehu (9:27), she **massacres entire Davidic line** to seize throne\! Only woman to rule Judah, usurper. **Satanic attempt to destroy Messianic line**\! "But Jehosheba, the daughter of king Joram, sister of Ahaziah, took Joash the son of Ahaziah, and stole him from among the king's sons which were slain; and they hid him, even him and his nurse, in the bedchamber from Athaliah, so that he was not slain" (11:2). **JEHOSHEBA**—king's daughter, priest's wife (2 Chr 22:11), rescues infant Joash\! Hides him in temple 6 years while Athaliah reigns (11:3). **God preserves Davidic covenant** (2 Sam 7) through one faithful woman\! Type of Jesus preserved from Herod's slaughter (Matt 2:13-16). "And the seventh year Jehoiada sent and fetched the rulers over hundreds, with the captains and the guard, and brought them to him into the house of the LORD, and made a covenant with them... And he showed them the king's son" (11:4-5). **JEHOIADA THE PRIEST**—high priest, Jehosheba's husband, orchestrates restoration\! Reveals Joash after 6 years. "And he brought forth the king's son, and put the crown upon him, and gave him the testimony; and they made him king, and anointed him; and they clapped their hands, and said, God save the king" (11:12). **7-year-old king crowned**\! Davidic line restored.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Line preserved*

#### 11:13-21 — Athaliah Executed, Baal Destroyed
**Lore:** "And when Athaliah heard the noise of the guard and of the people, she came to the people into the temple of the LORD. And when she looked, behold, the king stood by a pillar, as the manner was, and the princes and the trumpeters by the king, and all the people of the land rejoiced, and blew with trumpets: and Athaliah rent her clothes, and cried, Treason, Treason" (11:13-14). Athaliah discovers coup—screams "Treason\!" (ironic, she's the usurper). "But Jehoiada the priest commanded the captains of the hundreds, the officers of the host, and said unto them, Have her forth without the ranges: and him that followeth her kill with the sword. For the priest had said, Let her not be slain in the house of the LORD. And they laid hands on her; and she went by the way by the which the horses came into the king's house: and there was she slain" (11:15-16). **Executed outside temple**—not defiled with her blood. Jezebel's line ends. "And Jehoiada made a covenant between the LORD and the king and the people, that they should be the LORD's people; between the king also and the people" (11:17). **Covenant renewal**—triple covenant (God-king, God-people, king-people). "And all the people of the land went into the house of Baal, and brake it down; his altars and his images brake they in pieces thoroughly, and slew Mattan the priest of Baal before the altars" (11:18). **Baal worship purged from Judah**—priest of Baal killed, temple destroyed. "And he took the rulers over hundreds, and the captains, and the guard, and all the people of the land; and they brought down the king from the house of the LORD... And he sat on the throne of the kings. And all the people of the land rejoiced, and the city was in quiet: and they slew Athaliah with the sword beside the king's house" (11:19-20). Joy\! Rightful king reigns. **Joash was seven years old when he began to reign** (11:21).
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Usurper slain*

## Chapter 12: Superficial Reform

#### 12:1-16 — Joash Repairs the Temple
**Lore:** "In the seventh year of Jehu Jehoash began to reign; and forty years reigned he in Jerusalem... And Jehoash did that which was right in the sight of the LORD all his days wherein Jehoiada the priest instructed him" (12:1-2). **JOASH** (יוֹאָשׁ) = "Yah has given"—reigns 40 years (835-796 BC). "Did right... **wherein Jehoiada instructed him**"—dependent faith\! Good only while mentor alive. **2 Chr 24:17-22 reveals**: after Jehoiada's death, Joash abandons Yah, worships idols, murders Zechariah (Jehoiada's son, prophet) in temple court. Jesus references this (Matt 23:35). "But the high places were not taken away: the people still sacrificed and burnt incense in the high places" (12:3). **Incomplete reform**—high places remain (unauthorized worship sites). "And Jehoash said to the priests, All the money of the dedicated things that is brought into the house of the LORD... let the priests take it to them, every man of his acquaintance: and let them repair the breaches of the house, wheresoever any breach shall be found" (12:4-5). Temple in disrepair—Joash commands priests to fix it. "But it was so, that in the three and twentieth year of king Jehoash the priests had not repaired the breaches of the house" (12:6). **23 years pass, nothing done**\! Priests failed/corrupt. "Then king Jehoash called for Jehoiada the priest... and said unto them, Why repair ye not the breaches of the house?... And the priests consented to receive no more money of the people, neither to repair the breaches of the house" (12:7-8). New system needed. "But Jehoiada the priest took a chest, and bored a hole in the lid of it, and set it beside the altar... and the priests that kept the door put therein all the money that was brought into the house of the LORD" (12:9). **Collection box invented**—transparent system\! Money counted, given directly to workmen, not priests (12:10-12). "Howbeit there were not made for the house of the LORD bowls of silver, snuffers, basons, trumpets, any vessels of gold, or vessels of silver, of the money that was brought into the house of the LORD: But they gave that to the workmen, and repaired therewith the house of the LORD" (12:13-14). Funds used for repairs only, not ornaments—integrity\! "Moreover they reckoned not with the men, into whose hand they delivered the money to be bestowed on workmen: for they dealt faithfully" (12:15). **Workmen trusted**—no audit needed. Good financial system, but spiritually shallow.
*Verses: 16 | Theme: Temple repaired*

#### 12:17-21 — Tribute and Assassination
**Lore:** "Then Hazael king of Syria went up, and fought against Gath, and took it: and Hazael set his face to go up to Jerusalem. And Jehoash king of Judah took all the hallowed things that Jehoshaphat, and Jehoram, and Ahaziah, his fathers, kings of Judah, had dedicated, and his own hallowed things, and all the gold that was found in the treasures of the house of the LORD, and in the king's house, and sent it to Hazael king of Syria: and he went away from Jerusalem" (12:17-18). **Tribute to Hazael**—Joash buys off Syrian invasion with temple treasures\! Faithlessness—doesn't trust God (contrast Hezekiah, ch 19). "And his servants arose, and made a conspiracy, and slew Joash in the house of Millo, which goeth down to Silla. For Jozachar the son of Shimeath, and Jehozabad the son of Shomer, his servants, smote him, and he died" (12:20-21). **Assassinated by servants**—2 Chr 24:25 reveals: killed for murdering Zechariah\! Buried in Jerusalem but "not in the sepulchres of the kings"—dishonorable burial. **Superficial reform collapses**—without heart change, external repairs meaningless.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Faithless end*

## Chapter 13: The Posthumous Miracle

#### 13:1-9 — Jehoahaz's Oppression
**Lore:** "In the three and twentieth year of Joash the son of Ahaziah king of Judah Jehoahaz the son of Jehu began to reign over Israel in Samaria, and reigned seventeen years. And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD, and followed the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, which made Israel to sin; he departed not therefrom" (13:1-2). **JEHOAHAZ** (יְהוֹאָחָז) = "Yah has grasped"—Jehu's son, continues idolatry. "And the anger of the LORD was kindled against Israel, and he delivered them into the hand of Hazael king of Syria, and into the hand of Ben-hadad the son of Hazael, all their days" (13:3). **Syrian oppression**—Hazael dominates Israel (fulfills 8:12). "And Jehoahaz besought the LORD, and the LORD hearkened unto him: for he saw the oppression of Israel, because the king of Syria oppressed them. (And the LORD gave Israel a saviour, so that they went out from under the hand of the Syrians: and the children of Israel dwelt in their tents, as beforetime.)" (13:4-5). **God grants deliverer** despite unrepentance\! Divine mercy. "Nevertheless they departed not from the sins of the house of Jeroboam, who made Israel sin, but walked therein: and there remained the grove also in Samaria" (13:6). **No reform**—Asherah pole stands, idolatry continues. "Neither did he leave of the people to Jehoahaz but fifty horsemen, and ten chariots, and ten thousand footmen; for the king of Syria had destroyed them, and had made them like the dust by threshing" (13:7). Military decimated—from powerful army to token force\! Judgment for persistent idolatry.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Oppression despite mercy*

#### 13:10-19 — Elisha's Deathbed Prophecy
**Lore:** "In the thirty and seventh year of Joash king of Judah began Jehoash the son of Jehoahaz to reign over Israel in Samaria, and reigned sixteen years. And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD; he departed not from all the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin: but he walked therein" (13:10-11). **JEHOASH/JOASH** (same name as Judah's king, confusing\!)—continues idolatry. "Now Elisha was fallen sick of his sickness whereof he died. And Joash the king of Israel came down unto him, and wept over his face, and said, O my father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof" (13:14). **Elisha dying**—Joash uses same words Elisha spoke at Elijah's ascension (2:12)\! Recognizes prophet as Israel's true defense. "And Elisha said unto him, Take bow and arrows. And he took unto him bow and arrows. And he said to the king of Israel, Put thine hand upon the bow. And he put his hand upon it: and Elisha put his hands upon the king's hands. And he said, Open the window eastward. And he opened it. Then Elisha said, Shoot. And he shot. And he said, The arrow of the LORD's deliverance, and the arrow of deliverance from Syria: for thou shalt smite the Syrians in Aphek, till thou have consumed them" (13:15-17). **Prophetic sign**—arrow shot eastward (toward Syria) = deliverance promised\! "And he said, Take the arrows. And he took them. And he said unto the king of Israel, Smite upon the ground. And he smote thrice, and stayed. And the man of God was wroth with him, and said, Thou shouldest have smitten five or six times; then hadst thou smitten Syria till thou hadst consumed it: whereas now thou shalt smite Syria but thrice" (13:18-19). **Joash's halfheartedness judged**—strikes ground only 3 times (lack of zeal/faith). Elisha angry—limited victories result (defeats Syria 3 times only, 13:25). **Test of faith intensity**—partial obedience = partial success.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Limited faith*

#### 13:20-25 — Elisha's Posthumous Miracle
**Lore:** "And Elisha died, and they buried him. And the bands of the Moabites invaded the land at the coming in of the year. And it came to pass, as they were burying a man, that, behold, they spied a band of men; and they cast the man into the sepulchre of Elisha: and when the man was let down, and touched the bones of Elisha, he revived, and stood up on his feet" (13:20-21). **POSTHUMOUS MIRACLE**—dead man touches Elisha's bones, **revives**\! Demonstrates: (1) God's power outlives prophet, (2) Elisha's "double portion" confirmed (Elijah raised 1 dead, Elisha 2—one after his own death\!), (3) Even dead, faithful prophets mediate life. "But Hazael king of Syria oppressed Israel all the days of Jehoahaz. And the LORD was gracious unto them, and had compassion on them, and had respect unto them, because of his covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and would not destroy them, neither cast he them from his presence as yet" (13:22-23). **Divine grace**—God preserves Israel not for their merit but **covenant faithfulness**\! "So Hazael king of Syria died; and Ben-hadad his son reigned in his stead. And Jehoash the son of Jehoahaz took again out of the hand of Ben-hadad the son of Hazael the cities, which he had taken out of the hand of Jehoahaz his father by war. Three times did Joash beat him, and recovered the cities of Israel" (13:24-25). **Prophecy fulfilled**—3 victories, no more. Elisha's word stands even after death.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Posthumous power*

## Chapters 14-15: Unheeded Mercy and Chaos

#### 14:1-14 — Amaziah's Foolish Challenge
**Lore:** "In the second year of Joash son of Jehoahaz king of Israel reigned Amaziah the son of Joash king of Judah... And he did that which was right in the sight of the LORD, yet not like David his father: he did according to all things as Joash his father did" (14:1-3). **AMAZIAH** (אֲמַצְיָהוּ) = "Yah is mighty"—good but not great, follows Joash's superficial pattern. "Howbeit the high places were not taken away: as yet the people did sacrifice and burnt incense on the high places" (14:4). Persistent problem. "And it came to pass, as soon as the kingdom was confirmed in his hand, that he slew his servants which had slain the king his father. But the children of the murderers he slew not: according unto that which is written in the book of the law of Moses, wherein the LORD commanded, saying, The fathers shall not be put to death for the children, nor the children be put to death for the fathers; but every man shall be put to death for his own sin" (14:5-6). **Obeys Mosaic law** (Deut 24:16)—doesn't execute murderers' children. Shows legal reform. "He slew of Edom in the valley of salt ten thousand, and took Selah by war, and called the name of it Joktheel unto this day" (14:7). Victory over Edom. **Pride follows**. "Then Amaziah sent messengers to Jehoash, the son of Jehoahaz son of Jehu, king of Israel, saying, Come, let us look one another in the face" (14:8). **Challenges Israel to battle**\! Foolish provocation. "And Jehoash the king of Israel sent to Amaziah king of Judah, saying, The thistle that was in Lebanon sent to the cedar that was in Lebanon, saying, Give thy daughter to my son to wife: and there passed by a wild beast that was in Lebanon, and trode down the thistle" (14:9). **Parable**—Amaziah = thistle, Israel = cedar. Warning: you'll be crushed. "Thou hast indeed smitten Edom, and thine heart hath lifted thee up: glory of this, and tarry at home: for why shouldest thou meddle to thy hurt, that thou shouldest fall, even thou, and Judah with thee?" (14:10). Wise counsel—don't fight\! "But Amaziah would not hear" (14:11). **Pride refuses**. Israel defeats Judah, breaks down Jerusalem's wall 400 cubits, plunders temple and palace (14:12-14). **Judah humiliated**—internal Jewish warfare\! Tragedy of divided kingdom.
*Verses: 14 | Theme: Foolish pride*

#### 14:15-29 — Jeroboam II's Unheeded Mercy
**Lore:** "And Jehoash slept with his fathers, and was buried in Samaria with the kings of Israel; and Jeroboam his son reigned in his stead" (14:16). **JEROBOAM II** (same name as founder, ominous)—Israel's most successful king territorially. "In the fifteenth year of Amaziah the son of Joash king of Judah Jeroboam the son of Joash king of Israel began to reign in Samaria, and reigned forty and one years. And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD: he departed not from all the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin" (14:23-24). **41-year reign** (longest in Israel), yet **evil**—golden calves remain\! "He restored the coast of Israel from the entering of Hamath unto the sea of the plain, according to the word of the LORD God of Israel, which he spake by the hand of his servant Jonah the son of Amittai, the prophet, which was of Gath-hepher" (14:25). **Greatest territorial expansion** since Solomon\! **Jonah prophesied it** (same Jonah who later goes to Nineveh). "For the LORD saw the affliction of Israel, that it was very bitter: for there was not any shut up, nor any left, nor any helper for Israel. And the LORD said not that he would blot out the name of Israel from under heaven: but he saved them by the hand of Jeroboam the son of Joash" (14:26-27). **UNHEEDED MERCY**—God's final gracious act for Northern Kingdom\! Sees affliction, grants prosperity despite wickedness. "Had not said... blot out"—God delays judgment, gives chance to repent. **Yet no repentance comes**. Prosperity without reform = wasted grace. Amos and Hosea prophesy during this time—warn of coming doom ignored amid wealth. **Tragic squandered opportunity**. Amaziah assassinated (14:19-20), Jeroboam dies (14:29). Stage set for rapid collapse.
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Wasted mercy*

#### 15:1-7 — Azariah's Leprosy
**Lore:** "In the twenty and seventh year of Jeroboam king of Israel began Azariah son of Amaziah king of Judah to reign... And he did that which was right in the sight of the LORD, according to all that his father Amaziah had done; Save that the high places were not removed: the people sacrificed and burnt incense still on the high places" (15:1-4). **AZARIAH/UZZIAH** (same person, 2 Chr 26)—good king but high places remain. "And the LORD smote the king, so that he was a leper unto the day of his death, and dwelt in a several house. And Jotham the king's son was over the house, judging the people of the land" (15:5). **Struck with leprosy**—2 Chr 26:16-21 explains: pride led him to burn incense in temple (priestly duty), God judged. Lived isolated, son co-reigned. Warning against overstepping ordained roles.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Pride judged*

#### 15:8-31 — Israel's Political Chaos
**Lore:** Rapid succession of assassinations begins. **Zechariah** (son of Jeroboam II) reigns 6 months, "did evil" (15:8-9), assassinated by **Shallum** (15:10)—ends Jehu's dynasty (4 generations as prophesied, 10:30). **Shallum** reigns 1 month, killed by **Menahem** (15:13-14). **Menahem** reigns 10 years, brutally violent: "Then Menahem smote Tiphsah, and all that were therein, and the coasts thereof from Tirzah: because they opened not to him, therefore he smote it; and all the women therein that were with child he ripped up" (15:16). **Horrific atrocity**—rips open pregnant women\! "Did evil" (15:18). "And Pul the king of Assyria came against the land: and Menahem gave Pul a thousand talents of silver, that his hand might be with him to confirm the kingdom in his hand" (15:19). **ASSYRIA ARRIVES**—first mention of empire that will destroy Israel\! **Pul = Tiglath-pileser III**, begins Assyrian expansion. Menahem pays tribute. **Pekahiah** (son of Menahem) reigns 2 years, "did evil" (15:23-24), assassinated by **Pekah** his captain (15:25). **Pekah** reigns 20 years, "did evil" (15:27-28). "In the days of Pekah king of Israel came Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria, and took Ijon, and Abel-beth-maacah, and Janoah, and Kedesh, and Hazor, and Gilead, and Galilee, all the land of Naphtali, and carried them away to Assyria" (15:29). **FIRST DEPORTATION**—northern tribes exiled\! **Hoshea** assassinates Pekah, becomes last king (15:30). **In 40 years: 6 kings, 4 assassinated**—complete political chaos\! Judgment accelerates.
*Verses: 24 | Theme: Anarchy reigns*

## Chapters 16-17: Apostasy and Exile

#### 15:32–16:9 — Ahaz's Apostasy
**Lore:** Brief interlude: **Jotham** of Judah reigns 16 years, "did right," but high places remain (15:32-38). His son **AHAZ** becomes Judah's worst king yet. "Twenty years old was Ahaz when he began to reign... And he walked in the way of the kings of Israel, yea, and made his son to pass through the fire, according to the abominations of the heathen, whom the LORD cast out from before the children of Israel" (16:2-3). **CHILD SACRIFICE**—burns his son to Molech\! Unprecedented wickedness in Judah. "And he sacrificed and burnt incense in the high places, and on the hills, and under every green tree" (16:4). Idolatry everywhere. Rezin of Syria and Pekah of Israel attack Judah (16:5)—"Syro-Ephraimite War." "So Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria, saying, I am thy servant and thy son: come up, and save me out of the hand of the king of Syria, and out of the hand of the king of Israel, which rise up against me. And Ahaz took the silver and gold that was found in the house of the LORD, and in the treasures of the king's house, and sent it for a present to the king of Assyria" (16:7-8). **Ahaz invites Assyria**—makes Judah vassal, empties temple\! Isaiah opposes (Isa 7), but Ahaz refuses faith. "And the king of Assyria hearkened unto him: for the king of Assyria went up against Damascus, and took it, and carried the people of it captive to Kir, and slew Rezin" (16:9). Assyria conquers Damascus. **Fatal alliance**—Judah now Assyrian puppet.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Child sacrifice*

#### 16:10-20 — Pagan Altar Replaces God's
**Lore:** "And king Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria, and saw an altar that was at Damascus: and king Ahaz sent to Urijah the priest the fashion of the altar, and the pattern of it, according to all the workmanship thereof. And Urijah the priest built an altar according to all that king Ahaz had sent from Damascus" (16:10-11). **REPLACES GOD'S ALTAR**—Ahaz sees pagan altar in Damascus, copies it, installs in Jerusalem temple\! Urijah the priest (weak, compliant) obeys. "And when the king was come from Damascus, the king saw the altar: and the king approached to the altar, and offered thereon. And he offered his burnt offering and his meat offering, and poured his drink offering, and sprinkled the blood of his peace offerings, upon the altar. And he brought also the brasen altar, which was before the LORD, from the forefront of the house, from between the altar and the house of the LORD, and put it on the north side of the altar" (16:12-14). **God's bronze altar demoted**—new pagan altar takes central place\! Ahaz uses it for sacrifices. "And king Ahaz commanded Urijah the priest, saying, Upon the great altar burn the morning burnt offering, and the evening meat offering, and the king's burnt sacrifice, and his meat offering, with the burnt offering of all the people of the land, and their meat offering, and their drink offerings; and sprinkle upon it all the blood of the burnt offering, and all the blood of the sacrifice: and the brasen altar shall be for me to enquire by" (16:15). **Pagan altar for regular worship, bronze altar for divination**—complete inversion\! Ahaz also cuts up temple furnishings, closes temple doors (16:17-18, 2 Chr 28:24). **Destroys worship system**. Most apostate king yet in Judah.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Altar replaced*

#### 17:1-6 — The Fall of Samaria
**Lore:** "In the twelfth year of Ahaz king of Judah began Hoshea the son of Elah to reign in Samaria over Israel nine years. And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD, but not as the kings of Israel that were before him" (17:1-2). **HOSHEA**—last king of Israel, slightly better (relatively) but still evil. "Against him came up Shalmaneser king of Assyria; and Hoshea became his servant, and gave him presents" (17:3). **Tributary to Assyria**. "And the king of Assyria found conspiracy in Hoshea: for he had sent messengers to So king of Egypt, and brought no present to the king of Assyria, as he had done year by year: therefore the king of Assyria shut him up, and bound him in prison" (17:4). **Hoshea rebels**—seeks Egyptian help, stops tribute. **Fatal mistake**. "Then the king of Assyria came up throughout all the land, and went up to Samaria, and besieged it three years. In the ninth year of Hoshea the king of Assyria took Samaria, and carried Israel away into Assyria, and placed them in Halah and in Habor by the river of Gozan, and in the cities of the Medes" (17:5-6). **722 BC—NORTHERN KINGDOM FALLS**\! After 3-year siege, Samaria conquered. **Ten tribes deported to Assyria**—never return as nation. End of Israel. 200+ years from Jeroboam's golden calves to exile. **Covenant curses fulfilled** (Lev 26, Deut 28).
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Kingdom falls*

#### 17:7-23 — Theological Explanation
**Lore:** "For so it was, that the children of Israel had sinned against the LORD their God, which had brought them up out of the land of Egypt, from under the hand of Pharaoh king of Egypt, and had feared other gods" (17:7). **ROOT CAUSE**: forgot Exodus deliverance, worshiped false gods. "And walked in the statutes of the heathen, whom the LORD cast out from before the children of Israel, and of the kings of Israel, which they had made" (17:8). Became like Canaanites God expelled\! "And the children of Israel did secretly those things that were not right against the LORD their God, and they built them high places in all their cities, from the tower of the watchmen to the fenced city. And they set them up images and groves in every high hill, and under every green tree" (17:9-10). Secret sins, ubiquitous idolatry. "And there they burnt incense in all the high places, as did the heathen whom the LORD carried away before them; and wrought wicked things to provoke the LORD to anger" (17:11). Incense to false gods. "Yet the LORD testified against Israel, and against Judah, by all the prophets, and by all the seers, saying, Turn ye from your evil ways, and keep my commandments and my statutes, according to all the law which I commanded your fathers, and which I sent to you by my servants the prophets. Notwithstanding they would not hear, but hardened their necks, like to the neck of their fathers, that did not believe in the LORD their God" (17:13-14). **God sent prophets repeatedly**—Elijah, Elisha, Amos, Hosea, Micah. **Israel refused**. "And they rejected his statutes, and his covenant that he made with their fathers, and his testimonies which he testified against them; and they followed vanity, and became vain, and went after the heathen that were round about them... And they left all the commandments of the LORD their God, and made them molten images, even two calves, and made a grove, and worshipped all the host of heaven, and served Baal. And they caused their sons and their daughters to pass through the fire, and used divination and enchantments, and sold themselves to do evil in the sight of the LORD, to provoke him to anger" (17:15-17). **Complete apostasy**—golden calves, Asherah, astrology, Baal, child sacrifice, witchcraft. "Therefore the LORD was very angry with Israel, and removed them out of his sight: there was none left but the tribe of Judah only" (17:18). **Exile = divine judgment**. "And the LORD rejected all the seed of Israel, and afflicted them, and delivered them into the hand of spoilers, until he had cast them out of his sight" (17:20). Final verdict: **rejected, cast out**. "(For he rent Israel from the house of David; and they made Jeroboam the son of Nebat king: and Jeroboam drave Israel from following the LORD, and made them sin a great sin. For the children of Israel walked in all the sins of Jeroboam which he did; they departed not from them; Until the LORD removed Israel out of his sight, as he had said by all his servants the prophets. So was Israel carried away out of their own land to Assyria unto this day.)" (17:21-23). **Root sin: Jeroboam's golden calves**—original sin never abandoned, led inexorably to exile. **Warning to Judah**.
*Verses: 17 | Theme: Covenant broken*

#### 17:24-41 — Syncretistic Samaritans
**Lore:** "And the king of Assyria brought men from Babylon, and from Cuthah, and from Ava, and from Hamath, and from Sepharvaim, and placed them in the cities of Samaria instead of the children of Israel: and they possessed Samaria, and dwelt in the cities thereof" (17:24). **Assyrian resettlement policy**—replace Israelites with foreign peoples. **Origin of Samaritans**. "And so it was at the beginning of their dwelling there, that they feared not the LORD: therefore the LORD sent lions among them, which slew some of them" (17:25). Lions attack—judgment on pagan settlers. "Wherefore they spake to the king of Assyria, saying, The nations which thou hast removed, and placed in the cities of Samaria, know not the manner of the God of the land: therefore he hath sent lions among them" (17:26). Interpret as local deity problem. "Then the king of Assyria commanded, saying, Carry thither one of the priests whom ye brought from thence; and let them go and dwell there, and let him teach them the manner of the God of the land. Then one of the priests whom they had carried away from Samaria came and dwelt in Bethel, and taught them how they should fear the LORD" (17:27-28). Priest sent to teach Yah-worship. "Howbeit every nation made gods of their own, and put them in the houses of the high places which the Samaritans had made, every nation in their cities wherein they dwelt" (17:29). **Syncretism**—worship Yah + their gods\! "They feared the LORD, and served their own gods... So these nations feared the LORD, and served their graven images, both their children, and their children's children: as did their fathers, so do they unto this day" (17:33, 41). **Hybrid religion**—Jew-Samaritan hostility for centuries rooted here. Explains New Testament tensions (John 4).
*Verses: 18 | Theme: Syncretism begins*
