# Exodus 19-40: Sinai Covenant, Law, and God's Dwelling

## Part II: Covenant & Worship (Exodus 19-40)

### Chapter 19: The Covenant Offer at Sinai

#### 19:1-8 — A Kingdom of Priests
**Lore:** "On the third new moon after the people of Israel had gone out of the land of Egypt, on that day they came into the wilderness of Sinai" (19:1). Three months from Passover—arrival at Sinai, the mountain of God (same mountain as the burning bush, 3:1). Israel camps before the mountain (19:2). Moses goes up to God. "The LORD called to him out of the mountain, saying, 'Thus you shall say to the house of Jacob, and tell the people of Israel: "You yourselves have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I bore you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself"'" (19:3-4). Tender imagery—God carried them like an eagle carries eaglets (cf. Deut 32:11). "Now therefore, if you will indeed obey my voice and keep my covenant, you shall be my treasured possession (סְגֻלָּה, segullah, "special treasure") among all peoples, for all the earth is mine; and you shall be to me a kingdom of priests (מַמְלֶכֶת כֹּהֲנִים, mamlekhet kohanim) and a holy nation" (19:5-6). This is the Sinai covenant offer—conditional ("if you obey"), relational ("my treasured possession"), missional ("kingdom of priests"). Peter applies this to the church: "You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his own possession" (1 Pet 2:9). "And all the people answered together and said, 'All that the LORD has spoken we will do'" (19:8). Unanimous consent—Israel accepts the covenant.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Kingdom of priests*

#### 19:9-15 — Preparation for Theophany
**Lore:** God tells Moses: "Behold, I am coming to you in a thick cloud, that the people may hear when I speak with you, and may also believe you forever" (19:9). Public revelation—not just Moses' word, but direct divine speech. Moses is to consecrate the people: "Let them wash their garments and be ready for the third day. For on the third day the LORD will come down on Mount Sinai in the sight of all the people" (19:10-11). Boundaries are set: "Take care not to go up into the mountain or touch the edge of it. Whoever touches the mountain shall be put to death" (19:12). God's holiness is dangerous—unapproachable without invitation. Even animals touching the mountain must die (19:13). Sexual abstinence required (19:15)—total consecration. Preparation for encountering the Holy One.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Consecration before holiness*

#### 19:16-25 — The Mountain Quakes
**Lore:** "On the morning of the third day there were thunders and lightnings and a thick cloud on the mountain and a very loud trumpet blast, so that all the people in the camp trembled" (19:16). Theophany begins—nature convulses. "Mount Sinai was wrapped in smoke because the LORD had descended on it in fire. The smoke of it went up like the smoke of a kiln, and the whole mountain trembled greatly" (19:18). Fire, smoke, earthquake—terrifying manifestation of God's presence. "And as the sound of the trumpet grew louder and louder, Moses spoke, and God answered him in thunder" (19:19). Audible divine voice. God descends to the top of Sinai, calls Moses up (19:20). He warns again: "Go down and warn the people, lest they break through to the LORD to look and many of them perish. Also let the priests who come near to the LORD consecrate themselves, lest the LORD break out against them" (19:21-22). Even priests risk death if unholy. Moses protests: the people can't come up, boundaries are set (19:23). God insists: warn them again (19:24-25). Repetition underscores danger—God's holiness is not casual. This scene prepares for the giving of the Law.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: The terror of holiness*

### Chapter 20: The Ten Commandments

#### 20:1-17 — The Decalogue
**Lore:** "And God spoke all these words, saying..." (20:1). Direct divine speech to all Israel—not mediated through Moses yet. The Ten Commandments (עֲשֶׂרֶת הַדִּבְּרוֹת, aseret ha-dibrot, "ten words") form the covenant core. Structure: Two tablets—vertical duties (love God, commands 1-4) and horizontal duties (love neighbor, commands 5-10).

**1. No other gods** (20:2-3): "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery. You shall have no other gods before me" (עַל־פָּנָי, al-panai, "before my face, in my presence"). Exclusive worship—YHWH alone. Redemption precedes command—relationship before rules.

**2. No idols** (20:4-6): "You shall not make for yourself a carved image (פֶּסֶל, pesel), or any likeness of anything that is in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath, or that is in the water under the earth. You shall not bow down to them or serve them, for I the LORD your God am a jealous God (אֵל קַנָּא, El qanna), visiting the iniquity of the fathers on the children to the third and the fourth generation of those who hate me, but showing steadfast love (חֶסֶד, chesed) to thousands of those who love me and keep my commandments." Prohibition against representing God visually—God is spirit (John 4:24). "Jealous" (qanna) = zealous for exclusive devotion, like a husband for his wife. Consequences across generations for idolatry; mercy extends to thousands for faithfulness. Asymmetry: judgment limited, mercy abundant.

**3. No misuse of God's name** (20:7): "You shall not take the name of the LORD your God in vain (לַשָּׁוְא, lashav, "emptiness, worthlessness"), for the LORD will not hold him guiltless who takes his name in vain." Not just profanity but empty oaths, false claims to speak for God, hypocrisy. God's name represents His character—misusing it profanes Him.

**4. Remember the Sabbath** (20:8-11): "Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy. Six days you shall labor, and do all your work, but the seventh day is a Sabbath to the LORD your God. On it you shall not do any work...For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that is in them, and rested on the seventh day. Therefore the LORD blessed the Sabbath day and made it holy." Creation Sabbath—rooted in Genesis 2:2-3. Sabbath is sign of the covenant (31:13, 16-17). The fourth commandment is the longest, emphasizing its importance. SDA perspective: Sabbath (Saturday, 7th day) remains binding, not replaced by Sunday. Ellen White: "The Sabbath is God's memorial of creation, a sign between Him and His people forever" (Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 307).

**5. Honor parents** (20:12): "Honor your father and your mother, that your days may be long in the land that the LORD your God is giving you." First command with promise—long life. Transition from vertical (God) to horizontal (people). Parents represent God's authority; honoring them teaches submission to God. Paul cites this as "the first commandment with a promise" (Eph 6:2).

**6. No murder** (20:13): "You shall not murder" (לֹא תִרְצָח, lo tirtzach). Specific verb: ratzach = unlawful killing (murder), not all killing (war, capital punishment use different verbs). Protects image of God (Gen 9:6). Jesus expands: anger and contempt violate this (Matt 5:21-22).

**7. No adultery** (20:14): "You shall not commit adultery" (לֹא תִנְאָף, lo tin'af). Protects covenant faithfulness in marriage. Adultery = covenant breaking. Jesus expands: lust violates this (Matt 5:27-28).

**8. No stealing** (20:15): "You shall not steal" (לֹא תִגְנֹב, lo tignov). Protects property and economic justice. Theft violates trust and community.

**9. No false witness** (20:16): "You shall not bear false witness (עֵד שֶׁקֶר, ed sheqer, "lying testimony") against your neighbor." Protects truth and reputation. False testimony in court can destroy lives (see Naboth, 1 Kings 21).

**10. No coveting** (20:17): "You shall not covet (לֹא תַחְמֹד, lo tachmod) your neighbor's house; you shall not covet your neighbor's wife, or his male servant, or his female servant, or his ox, or his donkey, or anything that is your neighbor's." Internal desire, not just external action—coveting is the root of theft, adultery, murder. Paul identifies covetousness as idolatry (Col 3:5). This command exposes the heart; outward obedience is insufficient.

**Lore Summary:** The Decalogue is not arbitrary rules but gospel promises—these are the terms by which redeemed people live in covenant relationship with God and each other. They are inscribed "by the finger of God" (31:18; Deut 9:10). Jesus summarizes them: love God (commands 1-4), love neighbor (commands 5-10) (Matt 22:37-40). The Law reveals sin (Rom 7:7) and points to Christ (Gal 3:24).
*Verses: 17 | Theme: The ten words*

#### 20:18-21 — The People's Fear
**Lore:** "Now when all the people saw the thunder and the flashes of lightning and the sound of the trumpet and the mountain smoking, the people were afraid and trembled, and they stood far off and said to Moses, 'You speak to us, and we will listen; but do not let God speak to us, lest we die'" (20:18-19). Israel cannot bear direct encounter with God—they request mediation. Moses: "Do not fear, for God has come to test you, that the fear of him may be before you, that you may not sin" (20:20). Paradox: don't fear (terror), but fear (reverence). "The people stood far off, while Moses drew near to the thick darkness where God was" (20:21). Moses the mediator—prefigures Christ, the ultimate mediator (1 Tim 2:5; Heb 8:6).
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Mediation required*

### Chapters 21-23: The Book of the Covenant

**📖 Chapter Note:** Exodus 21-23 is called the "Book of the Covenant" (סֵפֶר הַבְּרִית, sefer habrit, 24:7). These are case laws (casuistic law) applying the Decalogue to specific situations. They cover slavery, violence, property, social justice, festivals, and Sabbath.

#### 20:22-26 — Altar Laws
**Lore:** Transition from Decalogue to covenant code. God commands: "You shall not make gods of silver to be with me, nor shall you make for yourselves gods of gold" (20:23). Reiteration of second commandment. "An altar of earth you shall make for me and sacrifice on it your burnt offerings and your peace offerings, your sheep and your oxen...And if you make me an altar of stone, you shall not build it of hewn stones, for if you wield your tool on it you profane it" (20:24-25). Simple, unhewn stones—no human artistry to distract from God. "And you shall not go up by steps to my altar, that your nakedness be not exposed on it" (20:26). Modesty in worship.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Simplicity in worship*

#### 21:1-11 — Laws on Slavery
**Lore:** "When you buy a Hebrew slave, he shall serve six years, and in the seventh he shall go out free, for nothing" (21:2). Debt slavery limited to six years—Sabbath principle applied to servitude. Release in the seventh year prefigures Jubilee (Lev 25). If the slave came in single, he leaves single; if married, his wife leaves with him (21:3). If the master gave him a wife, she and children remain (21:4)—harsh, but reflects ancient Near Eastern norms. If the slave loves his master and chooses to stay, his ear is pierced with an awl at the doorpost, marking permanent service (21:5-6). Female slaves have different regulations—if taken as a wife, she must be treated fairly; if not, she goes free (21:7-11). These laws regulate, not endorse, slavery—they limit abuse and protect dignity. Typology: pierced ear = willing servant (Ps 40:6-8, quoted in Heb 10:5-7 about Christ).
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Limits on servitude*

#### 21:12-36 — Laws on Violence and Restitution
**Lore:** (Condensed) Murder: "Whoever strikes a man so that he dies shall be put to death" (21:12). Capital punishment for murder. Accidental killing: "If he did not lie in wait for him, but God let him fall into his hand, then I will appoint for you a place to which he may flee" (21:13). Cities of refuge (Num 35; Deut 19). Premeditated murder: no sanctuary, even at God's altar (21:14). Striking or cursing parents: death penalty (21:15, 17)—echoing fifth commandment. Kidnapping: death (21:16). Assault causing injury: "eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot, burn for burn, wound for wound, stripe for stripe" (21:24-25). Lex talionis ("law of retaliation")—not personal vengeance but proportional justice administered by courts. Jesus reinterprets: "Do not resist the one who is evil. But if anyone slaps you on the right cheek, turn to him the other also" (Matt 5:38-39)—moving from legal justice to personal mercy. Ox goring: owner liable if ox known to gore (21:28-32). Property damage: restitution required (21:33-36). These laws protect life, limb, and property, establishing justice and community order.
*Verses: 25 | Theme: Proportional justice*

#### 22:1-15 — Laws on Property
**Lore:** (Condensed) Theft of livestock: restitution of five oxen for one ox, four sheep for one sheep (22:1). If thief cannot pay, he is sold as slave (22:3). Breaking and entering at night: killing the thief is justified; in daylight, it's murder (22:2-3)—protecting homeowner while valuing life. Damage to another's field: restitution (22:5). Fire spreading: culpability (22:6). Safekeeping: if entrusted property is stolen, oaths before God determine liability (22:7-13). Borrowed property damaged: owner compensated unless it was rented (wear and tear expected) (22:14-15). These laws establish economic fairness and trust.
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Economic justice*

#### 22:16-31 — Social and Ritual Laws
**Lore:** (Condensed) Seduction of a virgin: bride-price paid, marriage required (22:16-17). Sorcery: "You shall not permit a sorceress to live" (22:18)—death penalty for witchcraft (cf. Deut 18:10-12). Bestiality: death (22:19). Idolatry: "Whoever sacrifices to any god, other than the LORD alone, shall be devoted to destruction" (22:20). Protection of vulnerable: "You shall not wrong a sojourner or oppress him, for you were sojourners in the land of Egypt. You shall not mistreat any widow or fatherless child" (22:21-22). Israel's memory of suffering should produce compassion. God's warning: "If you do mistreat them, and they cry out to me, I will surely hear their cry, and my wrath will burn, and I will kill you with the sword" (22:23-24). God defends the defenseless. Loans: "If you lend money to any of my people with you who is poor, you shall not be like a moneylender to him, and you shall not exact interest from him" (22:25). No usury among covenant members. If a cloak is taken as pledge, return it by sundown—"for that is his only covering...in what else shall he sleep?" (22:26-27). Compassionate economics. Reverence: "You shall not revile God, nor curse a ruler of your people" (22:28). Firstfruits and firstborn belong to God (22:29-30). Holiness: "You shall not eat any flesh that is torn by beasts in the field" (22:31). Dietary purity.
*Verses: 16 | Theme: Justice for the vulnerable*

#### 23:1-9 — Justice and Impartiality
**Lore:** "You shall not spread a false report. You shall not join hands with a wicked man to be a malicious witness" (23:1). Ninth commandment applied. "You shall not fall in with the many to do evil, nor shall you bear witness in a lawsuit, siding with the many, so as to pervert justice" (23:2). Majority doesn't determine morality—truth over popularity. "nor shall you be partial to a poor man in his lawsuit" (23:3). Justice is blind—no favoritism, even for the poor. "If you meet your enemy's ox or his donkey going astray, you shall bring it back to him. If you see the donkey of one who hates you lying down under its burden, you shall refrain from leaving him with it; you shall rescue it with him" (23:4-5). Love for enemies applied practically (cf. Matt 5:44). "You shall not pervert the justice due to your poor in his lawsuit" (23:6). Protect the poor from legal abuse. "Keep far from a false charge, and do not kill the innocent and righteous, for I will not acquit the wicked" (23:7). God's justice is ultimate. "And you shall take no bribe, for a bribe blinds the clear-sighted and subverts the cause of those who are in the right" (23:8). Corruption condemned. "You shall not oppress a sojourner. You know the heart of a sojourner, for you were sojourners in the land of Egypt" (23:9). Empathy rooted in experience.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Impartial justice*

#### 23:10-19 — Sabbath, Festivals, Firstfruits
**Lore:** Sabbath year: "For six years you shall sow your land and gather in its yield, but the seventh year you shall let it rest and lie fallow, that the poor of your people may eat; and what they leave the beasts of the field may eat" (23:10-11). Land Sabbath—rest for creation, provision for poor. Weekly Sabbath: "Six days you shall do your work, but on the seventh day you shall rest; that your ox and your donkey may have rest, and the son of your servant woman, and the alien, may be refreshed" (23:12). Sabbath benefits all, even animals and workers. Three annual festivals: (1) Feast of Unleavened Bread (Passover week) (23:15), (2) Feast of Harvest (Pentecost/Weeks, firstfruits of wheat) (23:16a), (3) Feast of Ingathering (Tabernacles/Booths, end of harvest) (23:16b). All males appear before the LORD three times yearly (23:17). Regulations: no leaven with sacrificial blood (23:18), bring best firstfruits to God's house (23:19a). "You shall not boil a young goat in its mother's milk" (23:19b). This command (repeated in 34:26; Deut 14:21) is the basis for kosher separation of meat and dairy. Exact meaning debated—possibly a Canaanite fertility ritual God forbids, or a compassion principle (don't use life-giving milk for death).
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Sabbath and feasts*

#### 23:20-33 — The Angel and the Promised Land
**Lore:** God promises: "Behold, I send an angel before you to guard you on the way and to bring you to the place that I have prepared. Pay careful attention to him and obey his voice; do not rebel against him, for he will not pardon your transgression, for my name is in him" (23:20-21). The Angel of the LORD (מַלְאָךְ יְהוָה, malakh YHWH)—likely a Christophany, pre-incarnate Christ. "My name is in him"—He bears God's authority. "But if you carefully obey his voice and do all that I say, then I will be an enemy to your enemies and an adversary to your adversaries" (23:22). Conditional blessing. The angel will lead Israel into Canaan: "I will blot out" the Amorites, Hittites, Perizzites, Canaanites, Hivites, Jebusites (23:23). Command: "You shall not bow down to their gods nor serve them, nor do as they do, but you shall utterly overthrow them and break their pillars in pieces" (23:24). Total separation from idolatry. Promise: "You shall serve the LORD your God, and he will bless your bread and your water, and I will take sickness away from among you. None shall miscarry or be barren in your land; I will fulfill the number of your days" (23:25-26). Health, fertility, longevity—covenant blessings. God will send terror, confusion, and hornets ahead of Israel (23:27-28)—psychological and natural warfare. Gradual conquest: "Little by little I will drive them out from before you, until you have increased and possess the land" (23:30). God's wisdom—too fast and wild animals would overrun the land. Boundaries: Red Sea to the Sea of the Philistines (Mediterranean), wilderness to the Euphrates (23:31). Warning: "You shall make no covenant with them and their gods. They shall not dwell in your land, lest they make you sin against me; for if you serve their gods, it will surely be a snare to you" (23:32-33). Idolatry is contagious—separation required.
*Verses: 14 | Theme: The angel leads*
