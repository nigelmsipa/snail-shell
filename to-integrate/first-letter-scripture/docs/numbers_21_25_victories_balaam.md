# Numbers 21-25: Victories and Balaam's Oracles

## Part IX: Military Victories Begin (Chapter 21)

### Chapter 21: Bronze Serpent and First Conquests

#### 21:1-3 — Victory at Hormah
**Lore:** "When the Canaanite, the king of Arad, who lived in the Negeb, heard that Israel was coming by the way of Atharim, he fought against Israel, and took some of them captive" (21:1). First military test. "And Israel vowed a vow to the LORD and said, 'If you will indeed give this people into my hand, then I will devote their cities to destruction'" (21:2). Vow of חֵרֶם (cherem), complete destruction. "And the LORD heeded the voice of Israel and gave over the Canaanites, and they devoted them and their cities to destruction. So the name of the place was called Hormah (חָרְמָה, 'destruction')" (21:3). First victory! New generation shows faith. Contrast to ch 14 defeat at same place. Faith + obedience = victory.
*Verses: 3 | Theme: First victory*

#### 21:4-9 — Fiery Serpents and Bronze Serpent
**Lore:** Journey from Mount Hor around Edom (21:4). "And the people became impatient on the way. And the people spoke against God and against Moses, 'Why have you brought us up out of Egypt to die in the wilderness? For there is no food and no water, and we loathe this worthless food (manna)'" (21:4-5). Grumbling again! "Then the LORD sent fiery serpents (נְחָשִׁים הַשְּׂרָפִים, nechashim haseraphim, 'burning snakes') among the people, and they bit the people, so that many people of Israel died" (21:6). Judgment. "And the people came to Moses and said, 'We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD and against you. Pray to the LORD, that he take away the serpents from us.' So Moses prayed for the people" (21:7). Confession! "And the LORD said to Moses, 'Make a fiery serpent and set it on a pole, and everyone who is bitten, when he sees it, shall live.' So Moses made a bronze serpent (נְחַשׁ נְחֹשֶׁת, nechash nechoshet) and set it on a pole. And if a serpent bit anyone, he would look at the bronze serpent and live" (21:8-9). Paradox: snake = curse, but bronze snake = healing. Look in faith → live. **Jesus references this:** "And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in him may have eternal life" (John 3:14-15). Cross = place of curse becoming blessing. Look to Christ in faith → live eternally. SDA emphasis: sanctuary/cross = central focus for salvation.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Look and live*

#### 21:10-20 — Journey Northward with Song
**Lore:** Journey itinerary: Oboth, Iye-abarim, Zered valley, beyond Arnon (Moabite border, 21:10-13). "Wherefore it is said in the Book of the Wars of the LORD, 'Waheb in Suphah, and the valleys of the Arnon'" (21:14). Ancient poetry source, now lost. More stops: Beer ("well," 21:16). "Then Israel sang this song: 'Spring up, O well!—Sing to it!—the well that the princes made, that the nobles of the people dug, with the scepter and with their staffs'" (21:17-18). Song of the well! Joy replacing complaint. From Mattanah to Nahaliel to Bamoth to valley in Moab (21:18-20). Names trace God's provision. Songs mark victories. Exodus 15: song after Red Sea. Judges 5: Deborah's song. Psalms = Israel's hymnbook. Worship follows deliverance.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Songs of journey*

#### 21:21-32 — Defeat of Sihon, King of Amorites
**Lore:** Israel sends messengers to Sihon, king of Heshbon: "Let me pass through your land. We will not turn aside into field or vineyard. We will not drink the water of the wells. We will go by the King's Highway until we have passed through your territory" (21:21-22). Same request as to Edom. "But Sihon would not allow Israel to pass through his territory. He gathered all his people together and went out against Israel to the wilderness and came to Jahaz and fought against Israel" (21:23). Hostile response. "And Israel defeated him with the edge of the sword and took possession of his land from the Arnon to the Jabbok, as far as to the Ammonites, for the border of the Ammonites was strong" (21:24). Total victory! Israel takes all Amorite cities, settles in Heshbon (21:25). Song inserted (21:27-30): "Come to Heshbon, let it be built...For fire came out from Heshbon...it devoured Ar of Moab." Victory poem. Israel captures villages, drives out Amorites (21:31-32). East of Jordan now Israelite territory. Foreshadows Joshua's conquest.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Sihon defeated*

#### 21:33-35 — Defeat of Og, King of Bashan
**Lore:** "Then they turned and went up by the way to Bashan. And Og the king of Bashan came out against them, he and all his people, to battle at Edrei" (21:33). Another giant king (cf. Deut 3:11—Og's bed 9 cubits/13.5 feet long!). "But the LORD said to Moses, 'Do not fear him, for I have given him into your hand, and all his people, and his land. And you shall do to him as you did to Sihon king of the Amorites, who lived at Heshbon'" (21:34). God promises victory. "So they defeated him and his sons and all his people, until he had no survivor left. And they possessed his land" (21:35). Complete conquest. Two major kingdoms fall. Transjordan secured. Psalm 135:10-11: "who struck many nations and killed mighty kings, Sihon, king of the Amorites, and Og, king of Bashan." God fights for Israel. New generation = obedient, victorious. Faith brings conquest.
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Og defeated*

## Part X: Balaam's Oracles (Chapters 22-24)

### Chapter 22: Balak Summons Balaam

#### 22:1-14 — Balak Hires Balaam to Curse Israel
**Lore:** "Then the people of Israel set out and camped in the plains of Moab beyond the Jordan at Jericho" (22:1). Arrival at Canaan's doorstep! "And Balak the son of Zippor saw all that Israel had done to the Amorites. And Moab was in great dread of the people, because they were many. Moab was overcome with fear of the people of Israel" (22:2-3). Terror spreads. Balak (Moab's king) tells Midian elders: "This horde will now lick up all that is around us, as the ox licks up the grass of the field" (22:4). Sends messengers to Balaam son of Beor at Pethor (Mesopotamia) with diviner's fees: "Behold, a people has come out of Egypt. They cover the face of the earth, and they are dwelling opposite me. Come now, curse this people for me, since they are too mighty for me. Perhaps I shall be able to defeat them and drive them from the land, for I know that he whom you bless is blessed, and he whom you curse is cursed'" (22:5-6). Balaam = pagan prophet/diviner with real spiritual power. God speaks to Balaam: "Who are these men with you?" (22:9). Balaam explains (22:10-11). God: "You shall not go with them. You shall not curse the people, for they are blessed" (22:12). Balaam refuses messengers (22:13-14). God protects His people even through pagan prophet!
*Verses: 14 | Theme: Israel cannot be cursed*

#### 22:15-21 — Balaam's Greed, God Permits Him to Go
**Lore:** Balak sends more numerous, honorable princes with greater reward (22:15-17). Balaam: "Though Balak were to give me his house full of silver and gold, I could not go beyond the word of the LORD my God to do less or more" (22:18). Sounds pious! "Please stay here tonight also, that I may know what more the LORD will say to me" (22:19). Testing God. "And God came to Balaam at night and said to him, 'If the men have come to call you, rise, go with them; but only do what I tell you'" (22:20). Conditional permission. "So Balaam rose in the morning and saddled his donkey and went with the princes of Moab" (22:21). 2 Peter 2:15-16 condemns Balaam as loving "gain from wrongdoing." Jude 11: "Balaam's error" = greed. Revelation 2:14: "teaching of Balaam"—causing Israel to sin. Balaam technically obeys but heart craves reward. Dangerous mixture: spiritual gifts + love of money.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Greed masked as piety*

#### 22:22-35 — Balaam's Donkey Speaks
**Lore:** "But God's anger was kindled because he went, and the angel of the LORD took his stand in the way as his adversary" (22:22). God angry Balaam went even though He permitted it—Balaam's heart wrong. Angel blocks path with sword; donkey sees angel, turns aside (22:23). Balaam beats donkey (22:23). Three times donkey sees angel, Balaam beats her (22:24-27). "Then the LORD opened the mouth of the donkey, and she said to Balaam, 'What have I done to you, that you have struck me these three times?'" (22:28). Talking donkey! Balaam converses normally: "Because you have made a fool of me. I wish I had a sword in my hand, for then I would kill you" (22:29). Donkey: "Am I not your donkey, on which you have ridden all your life long to this day? Is it my habit to treat you this way?" (22:30). Good point. "Then the LORD opened the eyes of Balaam, and he saw the angel of the LORD standing in the way, with his drawn sword in his hand. And he bowed down and fell on his face" (22:31). Angel: "Why have you struck your donkey these three times? Behold, I have come out to oppose you because your way is perverse before me. The donkey saw me and turned aside before me these three times. If she had not turned aside from me, surely just now I would have killed you and let her live" (22:32-33). Donkey saved Balaam's life! Balaam confesses sin, offers to return (22:34). Angel: "Go with the men, but speak only the word that I tell you" (22:35). Humorous yet sobering: even a donkey is more spiritually perceptive than greedy prophet. God uses any means—even animals—to accomplish His will (cf. Num 22:28; Acts 9:3-6, Jesus speaks to Saul).
*Verses: 14 | Theme: Donkey wiser than prophet*

#### 22:36-41 — Balak Receives Balaam
**Lore:** Balak hears Balaam coming, goes to Moabite border city to meet him (22:36). "And Balak said to Balaam, 'Did I not send to you to call you? Why did you not come to me? Am I not able to honor you?'" (22:37). Impatient for curse. Balaam: "Behold, I have come to you! Have I now any power of my own to speak anything? The word that God puts in my mouth, that must I speak" (22:38). Correct answer, finally. Balak sacrifices oxen and sheep, sends portions to Balaam (22:40). Next morning, brings Balaam to Bamoth-baal ("high places of Baal") to see edge of Israel's camp (22:41). Sets stage for oracles. Pagan king expects pagan blessing ritual to work. But Yahweh controls even pagan prophet's mouth.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: God controls words*

### Chapter 23: First and Second Oracles

#### 23:1-12 — First Oracle: Israel Cannot Be Cursed
**Lore:** Balaam instructs: build 7 altars, prepare 7 bulls, 7 rams (23:1). Balak obeys (23:2). Balaam: "Stand beside your burnt offering, and I will go. Perhaps the LORD will come to meet me, and whatever he shows me I will tell you" (23:3). Balaam goes to barren height; God meets him (23:4). Balaam reports sacrifice; God puts word in his mouth (23:5). Returns, Balak and princes waiting at offering (23:6). **First oracle** (23:7-10): "From Aram Balak has brought me, the king of Moab from the eastern mountains: 'Come, curse Jacob for me, and come, denounce Israel!' How can I curse whom God has not cursed? How can I denounce whom the LORD has not denounced? For from the top of the crags I see him, from the hills I behold him; behold, a people dwelling alone, and not counting itself among the nations! Who can count the dust of Jacob or number the fourth part of Israel? Let me die the death of the righteous, and let my end be like his!" Blessing instead of curse! Balak furious: "What have you done to me? I took you to curse my enemies, and behold, you have done nothing but bless them" (23:11). Balaam: "Must I not take care to speak what the LORD puts in my mouth?" (23:12). God's sovereignty: even hired to curse, prophet can only bless. Romans 8:31: "If God is for us, who can be against us?"
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Blessed people*

#### 23:13-26 — Second Oracle: God Does Not Lie
**Lore:** Balak tries again from different location: Zophim field on Pisgah top, where he can see another part of Israel (23:13-14). Same ritual: 7 altars, bull and ram on each (23:14). Balaam: "Stand here beside your burnt offering, while I meet the LORD over there" (23:15). God meets him, puts word in mouth (23:16). Balaam returns; Balak asks, "What has the LORD spoken?" (23:17). **Second oracle** (23:18-24): "Rise, Balak, and hear; give ear to me, O son of Zippor: God is not man, that he should lie, or a son of man, that he should change his mind. Has he said, and will he not do it? Or has he spoken, and will he not fulfill it? Behold, I received a command to bless: he has blessed, and I cannot revoke it. He has not beheld misfortune in Jacob, nor has he seen trouble in Israel. The LORD their God is with them, and the shout of a king is among them...There is no enchantment against Jacob, no divination against Israel...Behold, a people! As a lioness it rises up and as a lion it lifts itself; it does not lie down until it has devoured the prey and drunk the blood of the slain" (23:19-24). Even stronger blessing! God's immutability emphasized. Balak: "Do not curse them at all, and do not bless them at all" (23:25). Balaam: "Did I not tell you, 'All that the LORD says, that I must do'?" (23:26). Balaam helpless to disobey God.
*Verses: 14 | Theme: God unchangeable*
