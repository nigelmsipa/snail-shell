# Deuteronomy 1-11: Moses's First Speech - Remember and Obey

## Overview

**Hebrew Name:** דְּבָרִים (Devarim, "Words/These are the words")  
**English Name:** Deuteronomy (Greek: "Second Law")  
**Central Theme:** Love the LORD your God with all your heart  
**Structure:** Three speeches by Moses on Plains of Moab before entering Canaan  
**Goal:** 6-12 verse pericopes for optimal memorization

---

## Part I: Historical Prologue (Chapters 1-4)

### Chapter 1: Moses Recalls the Journey

#### 1:1-8 — Introduction: Moses Speaks on Plains of Moab
**Lore:** "These are the words that Moses spoke to all Israel beyond the Jordan in the wilderness" (1:1). Location: Moab, east of Jordan, opposite Jericho. Time: "the fortieth year, on the first day of the eleventh month" (1:3). Forty years after Exodus! "The LORD our God said to us in Horeb, 'You have stayed long enough at this mountain. Turn and take your journey...See, I have set the land before you. Go in and take possession of the land that the LORD swore to your fathers, to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, to give to them and to their offspring after them'" (1:6-8). God's command: move forward, possess the land. Deuteronomy = Moses's farewell sermons. Explains law, warns, blesses. Unlike Leviticus (priestly manual), Deuteronomy = pastoral, heartfelt. Moses won't enter but prepares people. Echoes ancient Near Eastern treaty structure: preamble, historical prologue, stipulations, blessings/curses. Covenant renewal for new generation.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Moses's farewell begins*

#### 1:9-18 — Appointing Leaders and Judges
**Lore:** "At that time I said to you, 'I am not able to bear you by myself. The LORD your God has multiplied you, and behold, you are today as numerous as the stars of heaven'" (1:9-10). Recalls Jethro's advice (Ex 18). Moses appointed wise leaders, commanders, judges (1:13-15). Charged them: "Hear the cases between your brothers, and judge righteously between a man and his brother or the alien who is with him. You shall not be partial in judgment. You shall hear the small and the great alike. You shall not be intimidated by anyone, for the judgment is God's" (1:16-17). Justice = God's domain. No favoritism. Fairness for all, including foreigners. Difficult cases brought to Moses (1:17-18). Echoes: James 2:1-9 (no partiality). Principle: Godly leadership requires humility, wisdom, impartiality.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Justice for all*

#### 1:19-33 — Israel's Refusal to Enter Canaan
**Lore:** "Then we set out from Horeb and went through all that great and terrifying wilderness that you saw, on the way to the hill country of the Amorites, as the LORD our God commanded us. And we came to Kadesh-barnea" (1:19). Moses sent 12 spies (1:22-23); they returned with good report (1:24-25). "Yet you would not go up, but rebelled against the command of the LORD your God. And you murmured in your tents and said, 'Because the LORD hated us he has brought us out of the land of Egypt, to give us into the hand of the Amorites, to destroy us'" (1:26-27). Accuses God of hatred! Moses: "Do not be in dread or afraid of them. The LORD your God who goes before you will himself fight for you" (1:29-30). Recalls Egypt's deliverance, wilderness provision (1:30-31). "Yet in spite of this word you did not believe the LORD your God" (1:32). Unbelief = root sin. Hebrews 3:19: "They were unable to enter because of unbelief."
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Rebellion at Kadesh*

#### 1:34-46 — God's Judgment: Forty Years of Wandering
**Lore:** "And the LORD heard your words and was angered, and he swore, 'Not one of these men of this evil generation shall see the good land that I swore to give to your fathers, except Caleb the son of Jephunneh. He shall see it, and to him and to his children I will give the land on which he has trodden, because he has wholly followed the LORD!'" (1:34-36). Joshua also spared (1:38). Moses himself barred (1:37). Entire generation doomed to die in wilderness (1:39-40). Then presumptuously tried to attack after God said no—defeated (1:41-44). "And you returned and wept before the LORD, but the LORD did not listen to your voice or give ear to you. So you remained at Kadesh many days" (1:45-46). Disobedience in both directions: refusing when God says go, going when God says stop. Consequence: 40 years delay. Warning against unbelief and presumption.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Consequences of unbelief*

### Chapter 2: Journey Through Edom and Moab

#### 2:1-15 — Peaceful Passage Through Edom and Moab
**Lore:** "Then we turned and journeyed into the wilderness in the direction of the Red Sea, as the LORD told me. And for many days we traveled around Mount Seir" (2:1). God commands: "You have been traveling around this mountain country long enough. Turn northward" (2:2-3). Pass through Edom (Esau's descendants); don't fight them, buy food/water (2:4-6). "For the LORD your God has blessed you in all the work of your hands. He knows your going through this great wilderness. These forty years the LORD your God has been with you. You have lacked nothing" (2:7). God's faithful provision. Similarly for Moab (Lot's descendants, 2:9). Ar belongs to Moabites (2:9, 18-19). Israel respects God-given boundaries. Emim and Rephaim (giants) previously there, displaced by Edomites/Moabites (2:10-12, 20-23). "And the time from our leaving Kadesh-barnea until we crossed the brook Zered was thirty-eight years, until the entire generation, that is, the men of war, had perished from the camp, as the LORD had sworn to them" (2:14). Judgment complete. New generation ready.
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Respecting kin nations*

#### 2:16-25 — Preparing to Conquer Sihon
**Lore:** "So as soon as all the men of war had perished and were dead from among the people, the LORD said to me, 'Today you are to cross the border of Moab at Ar'" (2:16-18). Don't harass Ammonites (2:19). Transition moment: old generation dead, conquest begins. "This day I will begin to put the dread and fear of you on the peoples who are under the whole heaven, who shall hear the report of you and shall tremble and be in anguish because of you" (2:25). God goes before, strikes terror. Joshua 2:9-11: Rahab confirms this. Spiritual warfare principle: God fights for His people, enemies already defeated in their hearts.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: God strikes terror*

#### 2:26-37 — Defeat of Sihon, King of Heshbon
**Lore:** Moses sends peace messengers to Sihon: "Let me pass through your land...I will go only by the road. I will turn aside neither to the right nor to the left. You shall sell me food for money, that I may eat, and give me water for money, that I may drink. Only let me pass through on foot" (2:27-29). Reasonable request! "But Sihon the king of Heshbon would not let us pass by him, for the LORD your God hardened his spirit and made his heart obstinate, that he might give him into your hand, as he is this day" (2:30). God hardens Sihon (like Pharaoh). "Then Sihon came out against us, he and all his people, to battle at Jahaz. And the LORD our God gave him over to us, and we defeated him and his sons and all his people" (2:32-33). Total victory. Took all cities, devoted to destruction (חֵרֶם, cherem, 2:34). "Not one was left to remain" (2:34). Plunder kept (2:35). "From Aroer...to Gilead, there was not a city too high for us. The LORD our God gave all into our hands" (2:36). First major conquest. Foreshadows Joshua's campaigns.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: First conquest victory*

### Chapter 3: Defeat of Og and Land Distribution

#### 3:1-11 — Og of Bashan Defeated
**Lore:** "Then we turned and went up the way to Bashan. And Og the king of Bashan came out against us, he and all his people, to battle at Edrei. But the LORD said to me, 'Do not fear him, for I have given him and all his people and his land into your hand'" (3:1-2). Giant king! "So the LORD our God gave into our hand Og also, the king of Bashan, and all his people, and we struck him down until he had no survivor left" (3:3). Took 60 fortified cities (3:4-5). Devoted all to destruction, kept livestock/spoil (3:6-7). "So we took the land at that time out of the hand of the two kings of the Amorites who were beyond the Jordan, from the Valley of the Arnon to Mount Hermon" (3:8). "(For only Og the king of Bashan was left of the remnant of the Rephaim. Behold, his bed was a bed of iron. Is it not in Rabbah of the Ammonites? Nine cubits was its length, and four cubits its breadth, according to the common cubit.)" (3:11). Og's bed = 13.5 feet long! Emphasizes his giant status. God defeats giants. David vs. Goliath echoes this. Ephesians 6:12: "We do not wrestle against flesh and blood."
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Giant king defeated*

#### 3:12-20 — Land Given to Reuben, Gad, Half-Manasseh
**Lore:** "When we took possession of this land at that time, I gave to the Reubenites and the Gadites the territory beginning at Aroer...and half the hill country of Gilead with its cities" (3:12-13). Transjordan tribes receive inheritance. Condition: "And I commanded you at that time, saying, 'The LORD your God has given you this land to possess. All your men of valor shall cross over armed before your brothers, the people of Israel. Only your wives, your little ones, and your livestock...shall remain in the cities that I have given you, until the LORD gives rest to your brothers, as to you, and they also occupy the land that the LORD your God gives them beyond the Jordan. Then each of you may return to his possession which I have given you'" (3:18-20). Must help conquer Canaan first, then return. Joshua 22: they fulfill this. Unity in conquest though geographically separated.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Transjordan inheritance*

#### 3:21-29 — Moses Forbidden to Enter, Joshua Commissioned
**Lore:** "And I commanded Joshua at that time, 'Your eyes have seen all that the LORD your God has done to these two kings. So will the LORD do to all the kingdoms into which you are crossing. You shall not fear them, for it is the LORD your God who fights for you'" (3:21-22). Encouragement to Joshua. "And I pleaded with the LORD at that time, saying, 'O Lord GOD, you have only begun to show your servant your greatness and your mighty hand...Please let me go over and see the good land beyond the Jordan, that good hill country and Lebanon'" (3:23-25). Moses's heart cry! "But the LORD was angry with me because of you and would not listen to me. And the LORD said to me, 'Enough from you; do not speak to me of this matter again. Go up to the top of Pisgah and lift up your eyes westward and northward and southward and eastward, and look at it with your eyes, for you shall not go over this Jordan. But charge Joshua, and encourage and strengthen him, for he shall go over at the head of this people, and he shall put them in possession of the land that you shall see'" (3:26-28). Heartbreaking! Moses sees but doesn't enter (consequence of Meribah, Num 20:12). Joshua takes over. Moses = type of law (brings to border); Joshua (יְהוֹשֻׁעַ, Yehoshua, "Yahweh saves" = Jesus in Greek) brings into rest (Heb 4:8). Law points to Promised Land; Christ brings us in.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Moses's plea denied*
