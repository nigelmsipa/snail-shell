# 1 Samuel 13-15: Saul's Rejection

## Overview
Saul's tragic decline through three tests. Presumptuous sacrifice (ch 13)—can't wait on God, blames Samuel. Rash oath (ch 14)—human invention nearly destroys hero Jonathan. Final test with Amalek (ch 15)—partial obedience, excuses, blames people. Samuel declares: "to obey is better than sacrifice." Saul definitively rejected. God seeks "man after his own heart." Central theme: presumption and disobedience lead to divine rejection. Key terms: שָׁמַע (shama = hear/obey), מָאַס (ma'as = reject), זָבַח (zavach = sacrifice).

## Chapter 13: The Presumptuous Sacrifice

#### 13:1-7 — Philistine Crisis and Panic
**Lore:** "Saul lived for one year and then became king, and when he had reigned for two years over Israel, Saul chose three thousand men of Israel" (13:1-2). Chronological note (Hebrew text uncertain). "Jonathan defeated the garrison of the Philistines that was at Geba, and the Philistines heard of it. And Saul blew the trumpet throughout all the land, saying, 'Let the Hebrews hear.' And all Israel heard it said that Saul had defeated the garrison of the Philistines, and also that Israel had become a stench to the Philistines" (13:3-4). Jonathan's victory provokes massive Philistine response. "Stench" (נִבְאַשׁ, niv'ash) = become odious. National crisis. "And the Philistines mustered to fight with Israel, thirty thousand chariots and six thousand horsemen and troops like the sand on the seashore in multitude. They came up and encamped in Michmash" (13:5). **OVERWHELMING FORCE**—massive army. Numbers possibly hyperbolic but emphasize Israel's disadvantage. "When the men of Israel saw that they were in trouble (for the people were hard pressed), the people hid themselves in caves and in holes and in rocks and in tombs and in cisterns, and some Hebrews crossed the fords of the Jordan to the land of Gad and Gilead. Saul was still at Gilgal, and all the people followed him trembling" (13:6-7). **PANIC**—people scatter, hide, desert. Army dissolving. Pressure mounting on Saul.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Crisis and fear*

#### 13:8-15 — Saul's Folly and Rejection
**Lore:** "He waited seven days, the time appointed by Samuel. But Samuel did not come to Gilgal, and the people were scattering from him" (13:8). **CRITICAL MOMENT**—recalls 10:8 instruction. Saul waiting for Samuel to offer sacrifice. Samuel delayed, army deserting. Extreme pressure. "So Saul said, 'Bring the burnt offering here to me, and the peace offerings.' And he offered the burnt offering" (13:9). **PRESUMPTUOUS ACT**—Saul offers sacrifice himself! Priestly duty, not king's role. "Forced himself" to do it—violated God's order. "As soon as he had finished offering the burnt offering, behold, Samuel came. And Saul went out to meet him and greet him" (13:10). Samuel arrives just after! Bad timing reveals Saul's impatience. "Samuel said, 'What have you done?' And Saul said, 'When I saw that the people were scattering from me, and that you did not come within the days appointed, and that the Philistines had mustered at Michmash, I said, "Now the Philistines will come down against me at Gilgal, and I have not sought the favor of the LORD." So I forced myself, and offered the burnt offering'" (13:11-12). **SELF-JUSTIFICATION**—blames: (1) people scattering, (2) Samuel's delay, (3) Philistine threat. "I forced myself" (וָאֶתְאַפַּק, va'et'apaq) = restrained myself then acted against conscience. Prioritized pragmatism over obedience. Failed to trust God's timing. "And Samuel said to Saul, 'You have done foolishly. You have not kept the command of the LORD your God, which he commanded you. For then the LORD would have established your kingdom over Israel forever. But now your kingdom shall not continue. The LORD has sought out a man after his own heart, and the LORD has commanded him to be prince over his people, because you have not kept what the LORD commanded you'" (13:13-14). **DEVASTATING JUDGMENT**—"You have done foolishly" (נִסְכַּלְתָּ, niskalta). Dynasty won't continue. God seeking "man after his own heart" (אִישׁ כִּלְבָבוֹ, ish kilvavo)—David foreshadowed! Saul proved "unworthy to be entrusted with sacred responsibilities" through lack of "implicit trust" in God. **Presumption and folly**—didn't wait, didn't obey orders, blamed others. First great test = failed. "And Samuel arose and went up from Gilgal. The rest of the people went up after Saul to meet the army; they went up from Gilgal to Gibeah of Benjamin. And Saul numbered the people who were present with him, about six hundred men" (13:15). Army reduced to 600—massive desertion.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Presumption judged*

#### 13:16-23 — Dire Military Situation
**Lore:** "And Saul and Jonathan his son and the people who were present with them stayed in Geba of Benjamin, but the Philistines encamped in Michmash" (13:16). Geographical setup for ch 14 battle. "Now there was no blacksmith to be found throughout all the land of Israel, for the Philistines said, 'Lest the Hebrews make themselves swords or spears.' But every one of the Israelites went down to the Philistines to sharpen his plowshare, his mattock, his axe, or his sickle" (13:19-20). **PHILISTINE MONOPOLY**—control metalworking, prevent weapons. Strategic oppression. "So on the day of the battle there was neither sword nor spear found in the hand of any of the people with Saul and Jonathan, but Saul and Jonathan his son had them" (13:22). Only Saul and Jonathan armed! Dire situation sets stage for Jonathan's faith-filled action (ch 14). God's deliverance must be miraculous.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Military disadvantage*

## Chapter 14: The Rash Oath

#### 14:1-15 — Jonathan's Faith-Filled Attack
**Lore:** "One day Jonathan the son of Saul said to the young man who carried his armor, 'Come, let us go over to the Philistine garrison on the other side.' But he did not tell his father" (14:1). Jonathan initiates bold plan without Saul's knowledge. "Jonathan said to the young man who carried his armor, 'Come, let us go over to the garrison of these uncircumcised. It may be that the LORD will work for us, for nothing can hinder the LORD from saving by many or by few'" (14:6). **FAITH DECLARATION**—"LORD will work for us... nothing can hinder." Like Gideon's 300. Faith in God's power, not numbers. "Uncircumcised" (עֲרֵלִים, arelim) = outside covenant, vulnerable to God's judgment. "And Jonathan said, 'Behold, we will cross over to the men, and we will show ourselves to them. If they say to us, "Wait until we come to you," then we will stand still... But if they say, "Come up to us," then we will go up, for the LORD has given them into our hand. And this shall be the sign to us'" (14:8-10). Sets up test—if Philistines invite up, take as divine sign. Like Gideon's fleece. "And the men of the garrison hailed Jonathan and his armor-bearer and said, 'Come up to us, and we will show you a thing.' And Jonathan said to his armor-bearer, 'Come up after me, for the LORD has given them into the hand of Israel'" (14:12). **SIGN CONFIRMED**—Philistines invite. Jonathan interprets as God's will. "Then Jonathan climbed up on his hands and feet, and his armor-bearer after him. And they fell before Jonathan, and his armor-bearer killed them after him. And that first strike, which Jonathan and his armor-bearer made, killed about twenty men" (14:13-14). Daring climb, surprise attack, kills 20. "And there was a panic in the camp, in the field, and among all the people. The garrison and even the raiders trembled, the earth quaked, and it became a very great panic" (14:15). **DIVINE PANIC** (מֶחֱרָדָה, mechradah)—God magnifies attack, causes earthquake, spreads terror. "God-wrought deliverance" through one man's faith.
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Faith initiates victory*

#### 14:16-23 — Confusion and Deliverance
**Lore:** "And the watchmen of Saul in Gibeah of Benjamin looked, and behold, the multitude was dispersing here and there" (14:16). Saul's scouts see chaos. "Then Saul said to the people who were with him, 'Count and see who has gone from us.' And when they had counted, behold, Jonathan and his armor-bearer were not there" (14:17). Discovers Jonathan missing. "So Saul said to Ahijah, 'Bring the ark of God here.' For the ark of God went at that time with the people of Israel" (14:18). Saul calls for ark/ephod to inquire of God. "Now while Saul was talking to the priest, the tumult in the camp of the Philistines increased more and more. So Saul said to the priest, 'Withdraw your hand'" (14:19). Stops inquiry—too impatient to wait for God's answer! Parallels ch 13 presumption. "Then Saul and all the people who were with him came to the battle, and behold, every Philistine's sword was against his fellow, and there was very great confusion" (14:20). Philistines fight each other—total chaos. "So the LORD saved Israel that day. And the battle passed beyond Beth-aven. And the men of Israel had been hard pressed that day" (14:23-24). **Clear divine intervention**—LORD gets credit.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Divine chaos*

#### 14:24-35 — Saul's Foolish Oath
**Lore:** "And the men of Israel had been hard pressed that day, so Saul had laid an oath on the people, saying, 'Cursed be the man who eats food until it is evening and I am avenged on my enemies.' So none of the people had tasted food" (14:24). **RASH OATH**—"man-invented test," not inspired by God! Forbids eating during battle—weakens army. "I am avenged" reveals selfish motive (personal vengeance vs. God's glory). "Now when all the people came to the forest, behold, there was honey on the ground. And when the people entered the forest, behold, the honey was dropping, but no one put his hand to his mouth, for the people feared the oath" (14:25-26). Natural provision (honey) but fear of oath prevents eating. "But Jonathan had not heard his father charge the people with the oath, so he put out the tip of the staff that was in his hand and dipped it in the honeycomb and put his hand to his mouth, and his eyes became bright" (14:27). Jonathan, unaware, eats honey—gets strength ("eyes became bright" = renewed vigor). "Then one of the people said, 'Your father strictly charged the people with an oath, saying, "Cursed be the man who eats food this day."' And the people were faint. Then Jonathan said, 'My father has troubled the land. See how my eyes have become bright because I tasted a little of this honey. How much better if the people had eaten freely today of the spoil of their enemies that they found. For now the defeat among the Philistines has not been great'" (14:28-30). **JONATHAN REBUKES FATHER'S FOLLY**—oath "troubled" (עָכַר, akhar) the land, prevented greater victory. Right theology. "And the people were very faint. The people pounced on the spoil and took sheep and oxen and calves and slaughtered them on the ground. And the people ate them with the blood" (14:31-32). Exhausted army commits sin—eating blood (Lev 17:10-14). Saul's oath led to this! "Then they told Saul, 'Behold, the people are sinning against the LORD by eating with the blood.' And he said, 'You have dealt treacherously; roll a great stone to me here'" (14:33). Saul addresses blood sin with proper slaughter. "Then Saul built an altar to the LORD; it was the first altar that he built to the LORD" (14:35). First altar—late in reign, reactive not proactive worship.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Rash oath consequences*

#### 14:36-46 — Would Execute Jonathan
**Lore:** "Then Saul said, 'Let us go down after the Philistines by night and plunder them until the morning light; let us not leave a man of them.' And they said, 'Do whatever seems good to you.' But the priest said, 'Let us draw near to God here.' And Saul inquired of God, 'Shall I go down after the Philistines? Will you give them into the hand of Israel?' But he did not answer him that day" (14:36-37). **GOD SILENT**—won't answer Saul. "And Saul said, 'Come here, all you leaders of the people, and know and see how this sin has arisen today. For as the LORD lives who saves Israel, though it be in Jonathan my son, he shall surely die.' But there was not a man among all the people who answered him" (14:38-39). Saul determines to find guilty party—would execute even Jonathan! Can't discern own guilt (rash oath = cause). "Cast the lot between me and my son Jonathan.' And Jonathan was taken" (14:42). Lot falls on Jonathan. "Then Saul said to Jonathan, 'Tell me what you have done.' And Jonathan told him, 'I tasted a little honey with the tip of the staff that was in my hand. Here I am; I will die.' And Saul said, 'God do so to me and more also; you shall surely die, Jonathan'" (14:43-44). **SAUL WOULD EXECUTE SON/HERO** over technicality! "Failing to discern" own guilt, blames innocent. Rigid legalism without mercy. "Then the people said to Saul, 'Shall Jonathan die, who has worked this great salvation in Israel? Far from it! As the LORD lives, there shall not one hair of his head fall to the ground, for he has worked with God this day.' So the people ransomed Jonathan, so that he did not die" (14:45). **PEOPLE INTERVENE**—recognize Jonathan's faithfulness, ransom him. Contrast: Jonathan's faith initiated victory, Saul's human invention nearly destroyed hero. "Then Saul went up from pursuing the Philistines, and the Philistines went to their own place" (14:46). Incomplete victory due to Saul's folly.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Legalism vs. mercy*

#### 14:47-52 — Summary of Saul's Reign
**Lore:** "When Saul had taken the kingship over Israel, he fought against all his enemies on every side, against Moab, the Ammonites, Edom, the kings of Zobah, and the Philistines. Wherever he turned he routed them" (14:47). Military successes listed—capable warrior. "And he did valiantly and struck the Amalekites and delivered Israel out of the hands of those who plundered them" (14:48). Ironic foreshadowing—Amalekite success mentioned before ch 15 failure! "Now the sons of Saul were Jonathan, Ishvi, and Malchi-shua. And the names of his two daughters were these: the name of the firstborn was Merab, and the name of the younger Michal. And the name of Saul's wife was Ahinoam the daughter of Ahimaaz. And the name of the commander of his army was Abner the son of Ner, Saul's uncle" (14:49-50). Family roster. "There was hard fighting against the Philistines all the days of Saul. And when Saul saw any strong man, or any valiant man, he attached him to himself" (14:52). Ongoing Philistine pressure, recruits warriors. Sets context for David (ch 16-17).
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Military summary*

## Chapter 15: The Definitive Rejection

#### 15:1-9 — The Amalek Command and Partial Obedience
**Lore:** "And Samuel said to Saul, 'The LORD sent me to anoint you king over his people Israel; now therefore listen to the words of the LORD. Thus says the LORD of hosts, "I have noted what Amalek did to Israel in opposing them on the way when they came up out of Egypt. Now go and strike Amalek and devote to destruction all that they have. Do not spare them, but kill both man and woman, child and infant, ox and sheep, camel and donkey"'" (15:1-3). **DIVINE COMMAND**—utterly destroy Amalekites (חֵרֶם, herem = ban/devotion to destruction). Historical context: Amalek attacked Israel's weak/elderly leaving Egypt (Exod 17:8-16; Deut 25:17-19). God's judgment pronounced. Final test of Saul's obedience. "So Saul summoned the people and numbered them in Telaim, two hundred thousand men on foot, and ten thousand men of Judah" (15:4). Massive army—210,000. "And Saul came to the city of Amalek and lay in wait in the valley. Then Saul said to the Kenites, 'Go, depart; go down from among the Amalekites, lest I destroy you with them. For you showed kindness to all the people of Israel when they came up out of Egypt.' So the Kenites departed from among the Amalekites" (15:5-6). Spares Kenites (descendants of Moses's father-in-law)—shows Saul can discriminate. Proper mercy here. "And Saul defeated the Amalekites from Havilah as far as Shur, which is east of Egypt. And he took Agag the king of the Amalekites alive and devoted to destruction all the people with the edge of the sword. But Saul and the people spared Agag and the best of the sheep and of the oxen and of the fattened calves and the lambs, and all that was good, and would not utterly destroy them. All that was despised and worthless they devoted to destruction" (15:7-9). **PARTIAL OBEDIENCE**—spared king Agag and best livestock. "All that was good" kept, "despised and worthless" destroyed. Selective obedience = disobedience. Valued plunder over command.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Partial obedience*

#### 15:10-23 — "To Obey Is Better Than Sacrifice"
**Lore:** "The word of the LORD came to Samuel: 'I regret that I have made Saul king, for he has turned back from following me and has not performed my commandments.' And Samuel was angry, and he cried to the LORD all night" (15:10-11). God's regret (נָחַם, nacham = relent/grieve). Samuel's anguish—prays all night for Saul. "And Samuel rose early to meet Saul in the morning. And it was told Samuel, 'Saul came to Carmel, and behold, he set up a monument for himself and turned and passed on and went down to Gilgal'" (15:12). **MONUMENT FOR HIMSELF** (יָד, yad = hand/monument)—pride, self-glory vs. God's glory. "And Samuel came to Saul, and Saul said to him, 'Blessed be you to the LORD. I have performed the commandment of the LORD.' And Samuel said, 'What then is this bleating of the sheep in my ears and the lowing of the oxen that I hear?'" (15:13-14). **CAUGHT IN DISOBEDIENCE**—audible evidence! "Bleating" (קוֹל, qol) of sheep exposes lie. "Saul said, 'They have brought them from the Amalekites, for the people spared the best of the sheep and of the oxen to sacrifice to the LORD your God, and the rest we have devoted to destruction'" (15:15). **EXCUSE**—blames people ("they brought"), claims religious motive ("sacrifice to the LORD your God"). Notice: "your God" not "our God"—distance from personal relationship. Cloak of "pretended righteousness." "And Samuel said to Saul, 'Stop! I will tell you what the LORD said to me this night'... 'Though you are little in your own eyes, are you not the head of the tribes of Israel? The LORD anointed you king over Israel. And the LORD sent you on a mission and said, "Go, devote to destruction the sinners, the Amalekites, and fight against them until they are consumed." Why then did you not obey the voice of the LORD? Why did you pounce on the spoil and do what was evil in the sight of the LORD?'" (15:16-19). Samuel's rebuke—contrasts former humility ("little in your own eyes," 9:21) with present disobedience. "Pounced" (עָבַט, avat) = swoop like bird of prey, greedy grab. "And Saul said to Samuel, 'I have obeyed the voice of the LORD. I have gone on the mission on which the LORD sent me. I have brought Agag the king of Amalek, and I have devoted the Amalekites to destruction. But the people took of the spoil, sheep and oxen, the best of the things devoted to destruction, to sacrifice to the LORD your God in Gilgal'" (15:20-21). **SELF-JUSTIFICATION**—insists he obeyed, blames people again. "And Samuel said, 'Has the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to listen than the fat of rams. For rebellion is as the sin of divination, and presumption is as iniquity and idolatry. Because you have rejected the word of the LORD, he has also rejected you from being king'" (15:22-23). **THEOLOGICAL CLIMAX**—"to obey is better than sacrifice" (שְׁמֹעַ מִזֶּבַח טוֹב). Ritual without obedience = worthless. Rebellion = divination (witchcraft), presumption = idolatry. Measure for measure: "you have rejected... he has also rejected you."
*Verses: 14 | Theme: Obedience over sacrifice*

#### 15:24-35 — Saul's Confession and Final Separation
**Lore:** "Then Saul said to Samuel, 'I have sinned, for I have transgressed the commandment of the LORD and your words, because I feared the people and obeyed their voice. Now therefore, please pardon my sin and return with me that I may bow before the LORD'" (15:24-25). **CONFESSION** but blames people ("feared the people"). Self-justification continues. "And Samuel said to Saul, 'I will not return with you. For you have rejected the word of the LORD, and the LORD has rejected you from being king over Israel.' As Samuel turned to go away, Saul seized the skirt of his robe, and it tore. And Samuel said to him, 'The LORD has torn the kingdom of Israel from you this day and has given it to a neighbor of yours, who is better than you. And also the Glory of Israel will not lie or have regret, for he is not a man, that he should have regret'" (15:26-29). Torn robe = torn kingdom. "Neighbor... better than you" = David. "Glory of Israel" (נֵצַח יִשְׂרָאֵל, netsach Yisrael) = Eternal One, won't change mind. "Then he said, 'I have sinned; yet honor me now before the elders of my people and before Israel, and return with me, that I may bow before the LORD your God.' So Samuel turned back after Saul, and Saul bowed before the LORD" (15:30-31). Second confession, still concerned with appearance ("honor me... before elders"). Samuel grants for sake of office. "Then Samuel said, 'Bring here to me Agag the king of the Amalekites.' And Agag came to him cheerfully. Agag said, 'Surely the bitterness of death is past.' And Samuel said, 'As your sword has made women childless, so shall your mother be childless among women.' And Samuel hacked Agag to pieces before the LORD in Gilgal" (15:32-33). **SAMUEL EXECUTES AGAG**—completes what Saul failed to do. Agag's false hope ("bitterness of death is past") crushed. Divine judgment fulfilled. "Then Samuel went to Ramah, and Saul went up to his house in Gibeah of Saul. And Samuel did not see Saul again until the day of his death, but Samuel grieved over Saul. And the LORD regretted that he had made Saul king over Israel" (15:34-35). **FINAL SEPARATION**—Samuel and Saul never meet again. Samuel grieves—loves the man despite judgment. LORD regrets—divine sorrow over failed king. Saul's stubbornness and self-justification make case "hopeless."
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Definitive rejection*

**1 SAMUEL 13-15 COMPLETE!**

**Pericopes: 12** (3 chapters = 4/chapter)

**Theme Summary:** Saul's three failures seal his rejection. Presumptuous sacrifice (ch 13)—can't wait on God, blames Samuel/circumstances. Rash oath (ch 14)—human invention nearly destroys hero Jonathan, rigid legalism. Final test with Amalek (ch 15)—partial obedience, excuses ("religious motive"), blames people. Samuel: "to obey is better than sacrifice." Rebellion = divination, presumption = idolatry. God seeks "man after his own heart." Saul started humble but ended as God's enemy through presumption, disobedience, self-justification. Kingdom torn away, given to "neighbor who is better."
