# Proverbs 22:17-24:22: PrMP 3 — The Thirty Sayings: The Words of the Wise

## Overview
**THE EGYPTIAN CONNECTION**—This collection (22:17-24:22, 68 verses) shows remarkable literary parallels to the Egyptian "Instruction of Amenemope" (c. 1100 BC), containing approximately 30 distinct units of wisdom (hence "thirty sayings"). **Superscription**: "Bow down thine ear, and hear the words of the wise" (22:17). **Literary shift**: Unlike the terse, antithetical couplets of chapters 10-22:16, this section features longer, multi-verse instructions with motivations ("for" clauses explaining reasons). **International wisdom**: Demonstrates Israel's engagement with broader Ancient Near Eastern sapiential traditions while reframing them theologically around YHWH. **Pedagogical method**: Direct address ("my son"), parental tone, practical counsel for young man entering adulthood—especially court life, economic dealings, and social relationships. **Structural marker**: 24:22 concludes with warning against revolt, forming natural boundary before 24:23 ("These also are sayings of the wise"). **Core themes**: Social justice (oppression of poor), table manners (dining with rulers), wealth/poverty ethics, boundary stones (property rights), diligence, parental honor. **Canonical purpose**: Bridge between Solomonic collection (10-22:16) and Hezekiah collection (25-29), demonstrating that Hebrew wisdom is both rooted in Israel's covenant faith AND conversant with international wisdom while subordinating it to fear of YHWH.

## Sayings 1-5: Foundation and Instruction (22:17-23)

#### 22:17-21 — Bow Down Your Ear and Hear the Words of the Wise; That Your Trust May Be in the LORD
**Lore:** **"Bow down thine ear, and hear the words of the wise, and apply thine heart unto my knowledge"** (22:17). **SUPERSCRIPTION + EXHORTATION**—"bow down ear" (הַט אָזְנְךָ, hat oznekha, incline ear) = attentive posture (4:20; 5:1; Ps 78:1). "Words of wise" (דִּבְרֵי חֲכָמִים, divrei chakhamim) = new collection marker (distinct from "proverbs of Solomon," 10:1; 25:1). "Apply heart to knowledge" (תָּשִׁית לִבְּךָ לְדַעְתִּי, tashit libekha leda'ti) = internalize, not just hear. "For it is a pleasant thing if thou keep them within thee; they shall withal be fitted in thy lips" (22:18). **INTERNALIZATION**—keep wisdom within (בְּבִטְנְךָ, bevitnekha, in belly/inner being). Result: "fitted in lips" (יִכֹּנוּ יַחְדָּו עַל־שְׂפָתֶיךָ, yikkonu yachdav al-sefatekha, established together on lips) = ready speech, eloquent articulation. Wisdom stored internally enables external expression. "That thy trust may be in the LORD, I have made known to thee this day, even to thee" (22:19). **PURPOSE STATEMENT**—goal = trust in LORD (בַּיהוָה, ba-YHWH). This subordinates all following counsel to covenant faith. Egyptian parallels lack this theocentric grounding. "Have not I written to thee excellent things in counsels and knowledge, that I might make thee know the certainty of the words of truth; that thou mightest answer the words of truth to them that send unto thee?" (22:20-21). **"THIRTY" REFERENCE**—"excellent things" (שָׁלִשִׁים, shalishim) most likely = "thirty things" (ESV, NIV), matching Egyptian Amenemope's 30 chapters. Purpose dual: (1) know truth with certainty, (2) give truthful answers to those who send (diplomatic/courtly responsibility, envoy role).
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Foundation*

#### 22:22-23 — Rob Not the Poor; For the LORD Will Plead Their Cause
**Lore:** **"Rob not the poor, because he is poor: neither oppress the afflicted in the gate"** (22:22). **SAYING 1: JUSTICE FOR POOR**—"rob not" (אַל־תִּגְזָל, al-tigzal, don't steal from). Poor (דָּל, dal, weak/helpless) = easy target (defenseless). "Oppress afflicted in gate" (תְּדַכֵּא עָנִי בַשָּׁעַר, tedakke ani vasha'ar) = crush vulnerable at city gate (where legal cases adjudicated, Ruth 4:1; Deut 21:19; Amos 5:12). Exploiting poor via corrupt courts. "For the LORD will plead their cause, and spoil the soul of those that spoiled them" (22:23). **DIVINE ADVOCATE**—"LORD pleads" (יְהוָה יָרִיב, YHWH yariv, legal term, advocate/contend). God = lawyer for defenseless (Ps 35:1; Jer 50:34; Mic 7:9; 1 Sam 25:39). "Spoil soul of those who spoiled" = exact retribution (measure-for-measure justice, qaba, plunder). Parallel in Amenemope 2:4-5, but here explicitly Yahwistic.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Justice*

## Sayings 6-10: Relationships and Boundaries (22:24-29)

#### 22:24-25 — Make No Friendship with an Angry Man; Lest You Learn His Ways
**Lore:** **"Make no friendship with an angry man; and with a furious man thou shalt not go"** (22:24). **SAYING 2: AVOID HOT-TEMPERED**—"make no friendship" (אַל־תִּתְרָעַ, al-titra', don't associate with). "Angry man" (בַּעַל אַף, ba'al aph, lit. "possessor of anger," habitual rage). "Furious man" (אִישׁ חֵמוֹת, ish chemot, man of wrath). "Lest thou learn his ways, and get a snare to thy soul" (22:25). **CONTAGION PRINCIPLE**—anger = learned behavior (13:20, "companion of fools destroyed"). Associates shape character (1 Cor 15:33, "bad company corrupts"). Befriending wrathful → acquiring wrath → soul-trap (self-destruction via imitation).
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Association*

#### 22:26-27 — Be Not One of Them That Strike Hands; Why Should He Take Away Your Bed from Under You?
**Lore:** "Be not thou one of them that strike hands, or of them that are sureties for debts" (22:26). **SAYING 3: CO-SIGNING WARNING** (repeated theme: 6:1-5; 11:15; 17:18; 20:16; 27:13). "Strike hands" (תֹּקְעֵי כָף, toq'ei khaph) = handshake sealing guarantee for another's loan. "If thou hast nothing to pay, why should he take away thy bed from under thee?" (22:27). **FINANCIAL RUIN**—creditor seizes possessions (Ex 22:26-27; Deut 24:12-13 allow taking cloak as pledge but must return at night). "Take bed from under you" = extreme poverty (losing even bedding). Rhetorical question: Why risk destitution for another's debt?
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Debt*

#### 22:28 — Remove Not the Ancient Landmark Which Your Fathers Have Set
**Lore:** **"Remove not the ancient landmark, which thy fathers have set"** (22:28). **SAYING 4: PROPERTY RIGHTS**—"remove not landmark" (אַל־תַּסֵּג גְּבוּל, al-taseg gevul, don't move boundary stone, repeated 23:10; Deut 19:14; 27:17; Job 24:2; Hos 5:10). Ancient (עוֹלָם, olam, eternal/long-standing) landmarks = stone markers establishing property lines. Moving them = land theft (expanding one's field by encroachment). "Which fathers set" = ancestral inheritance (Num 34:1-15; Josh 13-21, tribal allotments). Violating boundaries = violating covenant land distribution. Amenemope parallel (6:1-2), but Hebrew grounds in Torah land theology.
*Verses: 1 | Theme: Property*

#### 22:29 — Seest You a Man Diligent in His Business? He Shall Stand Before Kings
**Lore:** **"Seest thou a man diligent in his business? he shall stand before kings; he shall not stand before mean men"** (22:29). **SAYING 5: DILIGENCE → PROMOTION**—"diligent" (מָהִיר, mahir, skilled/expert, not just "busy" but excellent). "In his business" (בִּמְלַאכְתּוֹ, bimla'khto, in work/craft). "Stand before kings" (לִפְנֵי־מְלָכִים, lifnei-melakhim) = serve royalty (Gen 41:46, Joseph before Pharaoh; 1 Kgs 10:8, servants before Solomon; Dan 1:4-5, Daniel selected for king's court). Contrast: "not stand before obscure men" (בַּל־יִתְיַצֵּב לִפְנֵי חֲשֻׁכִּים, bal-yityatzev lifnei chashukkim, won't serve nobodies). Excellence brings upward mobility. Skill trumps social class.
*Verses: 1 | Theme: Diligence*

## Sayings 11-15: Rulers, Gluttony, and Wealth (23:1-8)

#### 23:1-3 — When You Sit to Eat with a Ruler, Consider Diligently What Is Before You
**Lore:** **"When thou sittest to eat with a ruler, consider diligently what is before thee"** (23:1). **SAYING 6: DINING WITH RULERS**—courtly etiquette. "Ruler" (מוֹשֵׁל, moshel, one with authority). "Consider diligently" (בִּין תָּבִין, bin tavin, understand carefully, intensive form) what's before you = food + political situation (not just appetite). "And put a knife to thy throat, if thou be a man given to appetite" (23:2). **SELF-RESTRAINT**—hyperbolic (not literal suicide). "Put knife to throat" (וְשַׂמְתָּ שַׂכִּין בְּלֹעֶךָ, vesamta sakkin belo'ekha) = restrain appetite as if life depends on it. "Be not desirous of his dainties: for they are deceitful meat" (23:3). **DECEPTIVE FOOD**—"dainties" (מַטְעַמּוֹת, mat'ammot, delicacies). "Deceitful meat" (לֶחֶם כְּזָבִים, lechem kezavim, bread of lies) = food given with ulterior motives (testing you, obligating you, bribing you). Political meal = trap. Eat carefully. Amenemope parallel (23:13-18), "Don't eat food in presence of official."
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Restraint*

#### 23:4-5 — Labour Not to Be Rich; Wilt You Set Your Eyes Upon That Which Is Not?
**Lore:** **"Labour not to be rich: cease from thine own wisdom"** (23:4). **SAYING 7: WEALTH PURSUIT WARNING**—"labor not" (אַל־תִּיגַע, al-tiga', don't weary yourself). "To be rich" (לְהַעֲשִׁיר, leha'ashir) as primary goal. "Cease from own wisdom" = stop relying on schemes to get wealthy (human cunning inadequate). "Wilt thou set thine eyes upon that which is not? for riches certainly make themselves wings; they fly away as an eagle toward heaven" (23:5). **WEALTH'S TRANSIENCE**—rhetorical question. Riches = illusion (Ps 39:6; 62:10; Eccl 5:10-15; 1 Tim 6:7, 17). "Make wings" (עֹשֶׂה יַעֲשֶׂה לּוֹ כְנָפַיִם, ose ya'ase lo kenafayim, surely makes for itself wings, emphatic) + "fly like eagle" = swift disappearance. Don't chase what vanishes.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Wealth*

#### 23:6-8 — Eat Not the Bread of Him That Has an Evil Eye; For as He Thinks in His Heart, So Is He
**Lore:** "Eat thou not the bread of him that hath an evil eye, neither desire thou his dainty meats" (23:6). **SAYING 8: STINGY HOST**—"evil eye" (עַיִן רָע, ayin ra', stingy/grudging, opposite of "good eye," 22:9). Don't accept hospitality from miser. "For as he thinketh in his heart, so is he: Eat and drink, saith he to thee; but his heart is not with thee" (23:7). **FAMOUS VERSE**—"as thinks in heart, so is he" (כִּי כְּמוֹ שָׁעַר בְּנַפְשׁוֹ כֶּן־הוּא, ki khemo sha'ar benáfsho ken-hu, lit. "as he calculates in his soul, so he is"). Inner thoughts = true self. He says "eat, drink" (hospitality words), but heart absent (calculating cost, resenting expense). "The morsel which thou hast eaten shalt thou vomit up, and lose thy sweet words" (23:8). **DISGUST RESULT**—you'll vomit food (regret accepting grudging hospitality). "Lose sweet words" = pleasant conversation wasted (soured by host's begrudging spirit).
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Generosity*

## Sayings 16-20: Fools, Discipline, and Envy (23:9-18)

#### 23:9 — Speak Not in the Ears of a Fool; For He Will Despise the Wisdom of Your Words
**Lore:** **"Speak not in the ears of a fool: for he will despise the wisdom of thy words"** (23:9). **SAYING 9: DON'T WASTE WISDOM ON FOOLS**—"speak not" (אַל־תְּדַבֵּר, al-tedaber, don't talk). Fool (כְּסִיל, kesil) won't appreciate. "Despise wisdom" (יָבוּז לְשֵׂכֶל, yavuz lesekhel, scorn insight). Pearls before swine (Matt 7:6). Save counsel for teachable (9:7-8; 26:4-5).
*Verses: 1 | Theme: Wisdom*

#### 23:10-11 — Remove Not the Old Landmark; For Their Redeemer Is Mighty
**Lore:** "Remove not the old landmark; and enter not into the fields of the fatherless" (23:10). **SAYING 10: PROTECT ORPHANS' LAND**—repeats 22:28 but adds orphan context. "Enter not fields of fatherless" (וּבִשְׂדֵי יְתוֹמִים אַל־תָּבֹא, uvisdei yetomim al-tavo') = don't encroach on orphans' property (land grab from defenseless). "For their redeemer is mighty; he shall plead their cause with thee" (23:11). **DIVINE GO'EL**—"redeemer" (גֹּאֵל, go'el, kinsman-redeemer, legal protector, Lev 25:25; Ruth 2:20; Job 19:25; Isa 47:4). God = advocate for orphans (Ps 68:5, "father of fatherless"; Deut 10:18; Jer 49:11). "Plead cause with you" (יָרִיב אֶת־רִיבָם אִתָּךְ, yariv et-rivam ittakh) = litigate against land thieves. Don't pick fight with God.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Justice*

#### 23:12 — Apply Your Heart unto Instruction and Your Ears to the Words of Knowledge
**Lore:** **"Apply thine heart unto instruction, and thine ears to the words of knowledge"** (23:12). **SAYING 11: TEACHABILITY**—"apply heart" (הָבִיאָה לַמּוּסָר לִבֶּךָ, havi'ah lammusar libekha, bring heart to discipline). Ears + heart both engaged (22:17, parallel opening). Transition verse (resumes parental exhortation tone).
*Verses: 1 | Theme: Instruction*

#### 23:13-14 — Withhold Not Correction from the Child; You Shall Beat Him with the Rod and Deliver His Soul from Hell
**Lore:** "Withhold not correction from the child: for if thou beatest him with the rod, he shall not die. Thou shalt beat him with the rod, and shalt deliver his soul from hell" (23:13-14). **SAYING 12: PARENTAL DISCIPLINE** (13:24; 19:18; 22:15; 29:15, 17; Heb 12:5-11). "Withhold not correction" (אַל־תִּמְנַע מִנַּעַר מוּסָר, al-timna minna'ar musar) = don't spare discipline. "Beat with rod" (שֵׁבֶט, shevet, physical correction, not abuse). "He won't die" = corrective discipline doesn't kill child (reassurance to merciful parent). Outcome: "deliver soul from Sheol" (שְׁאוֹל, she'ol, realm of dead). Discipline = salvation from death path (folly leads to Sheol, 22:15; discipline redirects to life).
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Discipline*

#### 23:15-16 — My Son, If Your Heart Be Wise, My Heart Shall Rejoice; My Reins Shall Rejoice When Your Lips Speak Right Things
**Lore:** "My son, if thine heart be wise, my heart shall rejoice, even mine. Yea, my reins shall rejoice, when thy lips speak right things" (23:15-16). **SAYING 13: PARENTAL JOY**—"if heart wise" = conditional (child chooses wisdom). "My heart rejoices" (יִשְׂמַח לִבִּי, yismach libbi) = father's gladness (10:1; 15:20; 27:11; 29:3). "Reins" (כִּלְיוֹתַי, kilyotai, kidneys, seat of emotions) rejoice. "Lips speak right things" (מֵישָׁרִים, meisharim, uprightness, integrity). Wise child = parent's greatest reward. Emotional investment in child's character.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Wisdom*

#### 23:17-18 — Let Not Your Heart Envy Sinners; Surely There Is an End, and Your Expectation Shall Not Be Cut Off
**Lore:** **"Let not thine heart envy sinners: but be thou in the fear of the LORD all the day long"** (23:17). **SAYING 14: NO ENVY OF WICKED**—"envy not sinners" (אַל־יְקַנֵּא לִבְּךָ בַּחַטָּאִים, al-yeqanne libekha bachatta'im, don't let heart be jealous of sinners). Wicked prosper temporarily (Ps 37:1; 73:3-12; Jer 12:1). Antidote: "fear of LORD all day" (continuous, not episodic). "For surely there is an end; and thine expectation shall not be cut off" (23:18). **ESCHATOLOGICAL HOPE**—"there is end" (יֵשׁ אַחֲרִית, yesh acharit, there's future/reward). Your hope (תִּקְוָה, tiqvah) won't be cut off (righteous vindicated eventually, wicked judged). Long-term perspective counters envy.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Fear*

## Sayings 21-25: Self-Control and Wisdom (23:19-28)

#### 23:19-21 — Hear, My Son, and Be Wise; Be Not Among Winebibbers and Riotous Eaters of Flesh
**Lore:** "Hear thou, my son, and be wise, and guide thine heart in the way. Be not among winebibbers; among riotous eaters of flesh" (23:19-20). **SAYING 15: AVOID EXCESS**—"guide heart in way" (אַשֵּׁר בַּדֶּרֶךְ לִבֶּךָ, asher badderekh libbeka, direct heart in path). "Winebibbers" (סֹבְאֵי יָיִן, sov'ei yayin, drunkards, heavy drinkers). "Riotous eaters of flesh" (זֹלְלֵי בָשָׂר, zolélei vasar, gluttons). Avoid partiers. "For the drunkard and the glutton shall come to poverty: and drowsiness shall clothe a man with rags" (23:21). **POVERTY CONSEQUENCE**—excess → destitution (20:1; 21:17). "Drowsiness" (נוּמָה, numah, excessive sleep from intoxication) clothes with rags (literal poverty garments, 6:9-11).
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Self-control*

#### 23:22 — Hearken unto Your Father That Begat You, and Despise Not Your Mother When She Is Old
**Lore:** **"Hearken unto thy father that begat thee, and despise not thy mother when she is old"** (23:22). **SAYING 16: PARENTAL HONOR** (Ex 20:12; Lev 19:3; 30:17; Matt 15:4-6). "Hearken to father" = obey. "Despise not mother when old" (בָּזוּז זִקְנָה, ki zaknah, when aged) = care for elderly parents (reverse of child-rearing). Lifecycle responsibility: parents raise children, children support parents in old age. Honor never expires.
*Verses: 1 | Theme: Honor*

#### 23:23 — Buy the Truth and Sell It Not; Also Wisdom, and Instruction, and Understanding
**Lore:** **"Buy the truth, and sell it not; also wisdom, and instruction, and understanding"** (23:23). **SAYING 17: ACQUIRE TRUTH**—"buy" (קְנֵה, qeneh, purchase/acquire, Isa 55:1, "buy without money"). Truth (אֱמֶת, emet), wisdom (חָכְמָה, chokhmah), instruction (מוּסָר, musar), understanding (בִּינָה, binah) = four-fold treasure. "Sell it not" = never relinquish for any price (Matt 13:44-46, pearl of great price; 16:26, "gain whole world, lose soul"). Truth priceless—spend everything to obtain, refuse all offers to abandon.
*Verses: 1 | Theme: Truth*

#### 23:24-25 — The Father of the Righteous Shall Greatly Rejoice; Let Your Father and Mother Be Glad
**Lore:** "The father of the righteous shall greatly rejoice: and he that begetteth a wise child shall have joy of him. Thy father and thy mother shall be glad, and she that bare thee shall rejoice" (23:24-25). **SAYING 18: PARENTS' JOY** (repeat of 23:15-16; 10:1; 15:20; 29:3). "Father of righteous" (אֲבִי צַדִּיק, avi tzaddiq) greatly rejoices (גִּיל יָגִיל, gil yagil, intensified joy). "Begetter of wise child" has joy (יִשְׂמַח־בּוֹ, yismach-bo, rejoice in him). "She who bore you" rejoices (תָּגֵל, tagel, glad). Righteous + wise child = dual parental reward. Repeated emphasis underscores importance.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Joy*

#### 23:26-28 — My Son, Give Me Your Heart; Let Not Your Heart Envy Sinners; For a Whore Is a Deep Ditch
**Lore:** **"My son, give me thine heart, and let thine eyes observe my ways"** (23:26). **SAYING 19: SURRENDER HEART**—"give me heart" (תְּנָה־בְנִי לִבְּךָ לִי, tenah-beni libbeka li, give, my son, your heart to me). Father (wisdom personified) requests total allegiance. "Let eyes observe my ways" = imitate lifestyle (not just knowledge, but embodiment). "For a whore is a deep ditch; and a strange woman is a narrow pit" (23:27). **SEXUAL TRAP** (2:16-19; 5:3-14; 6:24-35; 7:5-27; 22:14). "Deep ditch" (שׁוּחָה עֲמֻקָּה, shuchah amuqqah) + "narrow pit" (בְּאֵר צָרָה, be'er tzarah) = inescapable trap. "She also lieth in wait as for a prey, and increaseth the transgressors among men" (23:28). **PREDATOR IMAGE**—prostitute lies in wait (תֶּאֱרֹב, te'erov, ambushes like robber). "Increases transgressors" (בּוֹגְדִים, bog'dim, unfaithful ones) among men = multiplies adultery. Hunting metaphor (she's predator, men prey).
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Purity*

## Sayings 26-30: Wine, Wisdom, and Conclusion (23:29-24:22)

#### 23:29-35 — Who Has Woe? Who Has Sorrow? They That Tarry Long at the Wine
**Lore:** **"Who hath woe? who hath sorrow? who hath contentions? who hath babbling? who hath wounds without cause? who hath redness of eyes? They that tarry long at the wine; they that go to seek mixed wine"** (23:29-30). **SAYING 20: ALCOHOL'S DEVASTATION** (20:1). Six rhetorical questions: woe (אוֹי, oy), sorrow (אֲבוֹי, avoy), contentions (מִדְיָנִים, midyanim, strife), babbling (שִׂיחַ, siach, complaints), wounds without cause (פְּצָעִים חִנָּם, petza'im chinnam, unprovoked injuries from brawls), red eyes (חַכְלִילוּת עֵינָיִם, chakhlilut enayim, bloodshot). Answer: those who linger at wine. "Mixed wine" (מִמְסָךְ, mimsakh, spiced wine, stronger). "Look not thou upon the wine when it is red, when it giveth his colour in the cup, when it moveth itself aright" (23:31). **SEDUCTIVE APPEARANCE**—"red wine" (יִתְאַדָּם, yit'addam, becomes red), "gives color in cup" = visually appealing. "Moves aright" (יִתְהַלֵּךְ בְּמֵישָׁרִים, yithalekh bemeisharim, goes down smoothly). Temptation aesthetic. "At the last it biteth like a serpent, and stingeth like an adder" (23:32). **SERPENT METAPHOR**—initial pleasure, final pain. Snake bite (יִשָּׁךְ, yishakh) + adder sting (יַפְרִשׁ, yafrish, viper, poisonous). Alcohol = delayed venom. "Thine eyes shall behold strange women, and thine heart shall utter perverse things" (23:33). **INTOXICATION EFFECTS**—hallucinations ("strange women," זָרוֹת, zarot, could = foreign women OR strange visions). Heart speaks perversity (תַּהְפֻּכוֹת, tahpukhot, twisted things, loss of judgment). "Yea, thou shalt be as he that lieth down in the midst of the sea, or as he that lieth upon the top of a mast" (23:34). **DISORIENTATION**—drunk = lying in ocean's midst (drowning sensation) OR top of ship's mast (swaying, nauseated, dizzy). Ancient description of vertigo/nausea. "They have stricken me, shalt thou say, and I was not sick; they have beaten me, and I felt it not: when shall I awake? I will seek it yet again" (23:35). **ADDICTION**—drunk doesn't feel blows (numbness). Wakes up thinking "when can I drink again?" (cycle of addiction, craving despite consequences). Earliest biblical description of alcoholism.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Sobriety*

#### 24:1-2 — Be Not Envious of Evil Men; For Their Heart Studies Destruction
**Lore:** **"Be not thou envious against evil men, neither desire to be with them. For their heart studieth destruction, and their lips talk of mischief"** (24:1-2). **SAYING 21: NO ENVY OF WICKED** (23:17; Ps 37:1; 73:3). "Envious" (תְּקַנֵּא, teqanne', jealous). "Evil men" (אַנְשֵׁי־רָע, anshei-ra'). Don't desire their company. Reason: "heart studies destruction" (שֹׁד יֶהְגֶּה, shod yehgeh, meditates on violence). "Lips talk mischief" (עָמָל, amal, trouble). Inner + outer evil (plotting + speaking). Proximity = contamination.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Envy*

#### 24:3-4 — Through Wisdom Is a House Built; By Knowledge Shall the Chambers Be Filled
**Lore:** **"Through wisdom is an house builded; and by understanding it is established: And by knowledge shall the chambers be filled with all precious and pleasant riches"** (24:3-4). **SAYING 22: WISDOM BUILDS**—three-fold foundation: wisdom (חָכְמָה, chokhmah) builds house, understanding (תְּבוּנָה, tevunah) establishes, knowledge (דַּעַת, da'at) fills chambers. "Precious and pleasant riches" (הוֹן יָקָר וְנָעִים, hon yaqar vena'im, valuable and delightful wealth). House = household (family, estate, life, cf. 14:1). Not just construction, but thriving household requires wisdom foundation (9:1, Wisdom builds seven-pillar house).
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Wisdom*

#### 24:5-6 — A Wise Man Is Strong; By Wise Counsel You Shall Make Your War
**Lore:** "A wise man is strong; yea, a man of knowledge increaseth strength. For by wise counsel thou shalt make thy war: and in multitude of counsellors there is safety" (24:5-6). **SAYING 23: WISDOM = STRENGTH**—"wise man strong" (גֶּבֶר־חָכָם בַּכֹּחַ, gever-chakham bakoach, wise man in power). Parallels 21:22, wisdom conquers city. "Man of knowledge increases strength" (אִישׁ דַּעַת מְאַמֵּץ־כֹּחַ, ish da'at me'ammetz-koach, knowledge man strengthens power). Physical strength < strategic wisdom. "By wise counsel make war" (11:14; 15:22; 20:18). War requires strategy (תַּחְבֻּלוֹת, tachbulot, guidance). "Multitude of counselors = safety" (repeated principle). Collective wisdom > individual cleverness.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Strength*

#### 24:7 — Wisdom Is Too High for a Fool; He Opens Not His Mouth in the Gate
**Lore:** **"Wisdom is too high for a fool: he openeth not his mouth in the gate"** (24:7). **SAYING 24: FOOL EXCLUDED**—"too high" (רָאמוֹת, ra'mot, too lofty/inaccessible). Fool (אֱוִיל, ewil) can't reach wisdom (17:16, "price in hand but no heart"). "Opens not mouth in gate" = can't participate in legal/civic discussions (gate = court, 22:22). Fool disqualified from leadership. Silence = incapacity, not restraint.
*Verses: 1 | Theme: Folly*

#### 24:8-9 — He That Devises to Do Evil Shall Be Called a Mischievous Person; The Thought of Foolishness Is Sin
**Lore:** "He that deviseth to do evil shall be called a mischievous person. The thought of foolishness is sin: and the scorner is an abomination to men" (24:8-9). **SAYING 25: EVIL PLOTTER**—"devises evil" (מְזִמּוֹת, mezimmot, schemes/plots). "Mischievous person" (בַּעַל מְזִמּוֹת, ba'al mezimmot, master of schemes). "Thought of foolishness = sin" (thought precedes action, intent matters, Matt 5:28). "Scorner" (לֵץ, letz, mocker) = abomination to men (social outcast, not just God's enemy).
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Evil*

#### 24:10 — If You Faint in the Day of Adversity, Your Strength Is Small
**Lore:** **"If thou faint in the day of adversity, thy strength is small"** (24:10). **SAYING 26: PERSEVERANCE TEST**—"faint" (הִתְרַפִּיתָ, hitrapita, show yourself slack/weak). "Day of adversity" (יוֹם צָרָה, yom tzarah, trouble/distress). Trials reveal character (1 Pet 1:7, faith tested by fire). Small strength (צָר כֹּחֶךָ, tzar kochekha, narrow/limited your power) exposed when pressure applied. True strength measured by endurance, not appearance.
*Verses: 1 | Theme: Strength*

#### 24:11-12 — Deliver Them That Are Drawn unto Death; Does Not He That Ponders the Heart Consider It?
**Lore:** **"If thou forbear to deliver them that are drawn unto death, and those that are ready to be slain; If thou sayest, Behold, we knew it not; doth not he that pondereth the heart consider it? and he that keepeth thy soul, doth not he know it? and shall not he render to every man according to his works?"** (24:11-12). **SAYING 27: RESCUE OBLIGATION**—"drawn unto death" (לְקֻחִים לַמָּוֶת, lequchim lammavet, taken to death, possibly judicial murder, cf. Est 4:14). "Ready to be slain" (מָטִים לַהֶרֶג, matim lahereg, staggering to slaughter). Duty to intervene. "If you say, 'We didn't know'" = claiming ignorance. God's response: "Does not He who ponders heart consider?" (הֲלֹא־תֹכֵן לִבּוֹת הוּא־יָבִין, halo-tokhen libbót hu-yavin). God weighs hearts (21:2), knows your excuses false. "Keeper of soul knows" (נֹצֵר נַפְשְׁךָ, notzer nafshekha, guardian of your life). "Render to each according to works" (Ps 62:12; Jer 17:10; Matt 16:27; Rom 2:6; Rev 22:12). Accountability for inaction. Silence = complicity.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Justice*

#### 24:13-14 — Eat Honey, My Son, for It Is Good; So Shall the Knowledge of Wisdom Be unto Your Soul
**Lore:** "My son, eat thou honey, because it is good; and the honeycomb, which is sweet to thy taste: So shall the knowledge of wisdom be unto thy soul: when thou hast found it, then there shall be a reward, and thy expectation shall not be cut off" (24:13-14). **SAYING 28: WISDOM = HONEY**—honey (דְּבַשׁ, devash) metaphor for wisdom (Ps 19:10; 119:103). Sweetness to taste (Ezek 3:3, scroll sweet as honey). "Knowledge of wisdom to soul" = honey's sweetness. "When you find it" (מָצָאתָ, matzata, discover) there's "reward" (אַחֲרִית, acharit, future/end). "Expectation not cut off" (תִּקְוָתְךָ לֹא תִכָּרֵת, tiqvatekha lo tikaret, hope endures, 23:18). Wisdom satisfies + ensures future.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Wisdom*

#### 24:15-16 — Lay Not Wait Against the Dwelling of the Righteous; For a Just Man Falls Seven Times and Rises Up Again
**Lore:** **"Lay not wait, O wicked man, against the dwelling of the righteous; spoil not his resting place: For a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again: but the wicked shall fall into mischief"** (24:15-16). **SAYING 29: RIGHTEOUS RESILIENCE**—"lay not wait" (אַל־תֶּאֱרֹב, al-te'erov, don't ambush, 1:11, 18). Wicked attacks righteous dwelling (נְוֵה צַדִּיק, neveh tzaddiq, pasture/home of righteous). "Spoil resting place" (רִבְצוֹ, rivtzo, where lies down, family home). Reason: "Righteous falls seven times, rises again" (Ps 37:24; Mic 7:8; Prov 3:34). "Seven" (שֶׁבַע, sheva, completeness/repeated) = many times. Resilience through God's help (Ps 145:14). Contrast: wicked fall into mischief (בְּרָעָה, bera'ah, calamity) = permanent collapse. Futility of attacking righteous (God restores them).
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Resilience*

#### 24:17-18 — Rejoice Not When Your Enemy Falls; Lest the LORD See It and Turn Away His Wrath from Him
**Lore:** **"Rejoice not when thine enemy falleth, and let not thine heart be glad when he stumbleth: Lest the LORD see it, and it displease him, and he turn away his wrath from him"** (24:17-18). **SAYING 30: NO SCHADENFREUDE**—"rejoice not" (אַל־תִּשְׂמַח, al-tismach). Enemy's fall shouldn't bring joy (contrast Obad 12-14, Edom rejoiced at Judah's fall, condemned). "Let not heart be glad when he stumbles" (בְּכָשְׁלוֹ, bekhoshlo, in his stumbling). Reason: LORD sees (רָאָה, ra'ah) + "it displeases Him" (וְרַע בְּעֵינָיו, vera' be'einav, evil in His eyes). Result: God might "turn wrath from him" (enemy) to you (role reversal, Amenemope parallel). Gloating = pride, invites divine correction. Love enemies (Matt 5:44; Rom 12:20, "if enemy hungry, feed him").
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Mercy*

#### 24:19-20 — Fret Not Yourself Because of Evil Men; For There Shall Be No Reward to the Evil Man
**Lore:** "Fret not thyself because of evil men, neither be thou envious at the wicked; For there shall be no reward to the evil man; the candle of the wicked shall be put out" (24:19-20). **SAYING 31: NO FRET/ENVY**—(repeats 23:17; 24:1; Ps 37:1, 7). "Fret not" (אַל־תִּתְחַר, al-titchar, don't burn with anger/anxiety). Reason: "no reward" (אַחֲרִית, acharit, future) for wicked. "Candle put out" (נֵר רְשָׁעִים יִדְעָךְ, ner resha'im yid'akh, lamp extinguished, 13:9; 20:20; 24:20; Job 18:5-6; 21:17). Wicked's prosperity temporary, doom certain. Don't envy what's doomed.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Trust*

#### 24:21-22 — My Son, Fear the LORD and the King; For Their Calamity Shall Rise Suddenly
**Lore:** **"My son, fear thou the LORD and the king: and meddle not with them that are given to change: For their calamity shall rise suddenly; and who knoweth the ruin of them both?"** (24:21-22). **SAYING 32 + CONCLUSION**—"fear LORD and king" (יְהוָה וָמֶלֶךְ, YHWH va-melekh, dual authority, Rom 13:1-7; 1 Pet 2:17). "Meddle not with changers" (שׁוֹנִים, shonim, revolutionaries/rebels). Sedition forbidden. "Calamity rises suddenly" (פִּתְאֹם, pit'om, unexpected disaster). "Who knows ruin of them both?" (מִשְּׁנֵיהֶם, misheneihem) = LORD + king OR rebels + their followers. Ambiguity intentional: revolt against God or king brings swift, unknowable ruin (2 Pet 2:1, "swift destruction"). **COLLECTION BOUNDARY**—24:22 ends PrMP 3 (next verse: "These also are sayings of wise," new section).
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Authority*

**Structural Features**:
- **Egyptian Parallels**: Close literary relationship to Amenemope (30 sayings structure)
- **Longer Form**: Multi-verse instructions vs. couplets in PrMP 2
- **Direct Address**: "My son" personalizes teaching
- **Motivation Clauses**: "For..." explanations provide rationale

**Major Themes**:
- Social Justice (poor, orphans, boundary stones)
- Court Etiquette (dining with rulers, gifts)
- Wealth Ethics (contentment, generosity, labor)
- Family Relations (parental honor, joy, discipline)
- Self-Control (alcohol, gluttony, sexual purity)
- Political Loyalty (fear LORD + king, avoid rebels)

**Pedagogical Value**: Bridges ancient Near Eastern wisdom traditions with Yahwistic theology—"words of wise" grounded in "fear of LORD" (22:19), transforming international wisdom into covenant instruction for preparing young Israelite men for leadership, commerce, and righteous living.
