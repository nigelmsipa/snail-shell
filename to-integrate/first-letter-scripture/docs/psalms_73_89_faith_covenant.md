# Psalms 73-89: Faith & Covenant (Book 3)

## Overview
Book 3 (Psalms 73-89) is the theological center and darkest book of the Psalter. Dominated by Asaph's psalms (73-83) and the Sons of Korah (84-85, 87-88), this collection wrestles with profound suffering, the destruction of the Sanctuary, and the apparent failure of the Davidic covenant. It opens with the wisdom question of why the wicked prosper (Ps 73) and concludes with the anguished cry over God's rejection of His anointed king (Ps 89). Yet amid the darkness, flashes of hope emerge—the beauty of God's dwelling (Ps 84), God's faithfulness remembered (Ps 89:1-37). This book ends with the doxology: "Blessed be the LORD forever\! Amen and Amen" (89:52).

## Psalm 73: Surely God is Good to Israel

#### 73:1-14 — But As for Me, My Feet Had Almost Stumbled
**Lore:** "A Psalm of Asaph" (superscription). **ASAPH** begins Book 3 (Pss 73-83 all Asaph). **Wisdom psalm** on theodicy. "Truly God is good to Israel, to those who are pure in heart. But as for me, my feet had almost stumbled, my steps had nearly slipped. For I was envious of the arrogant when I saw the prosperity of the wicked" (73:1-3). **Central problem of Book 3**—why do wicked prosper? Almost lost faith. "For they have no pangs until death; their bodies are fat and sleek. They are not in trouble as others are; they are not stricken like the rest of mankind" (73:4-5). Wicked appear blessed—healthy, trouble-free. "Therefore pride is their necklace; violence covers them as a garment. Their eyes swell out through fatness; their hearts overflow with follies" (73:6-7). Pride, violence characterize them. "They scoff and speak with malice; loftily they threaten oppression. They set their mouths against the heavens, and their tongue struts through the earth. Therefore his people turn back to them, and find no fault in them" (73:8-10). Arrogant speech. Even God's people admire them. "And they say, 'How can God know? Is there knowledge in the Most High?' Behold, these are the wicked; always at ease, they increase in riches. All in vain have I kept my heart clean and washed my hands in innocence. For all the day long I have been stricken and rebuked every morning" (73:11-14). Wicked mock God's knowledge. Psalmist questions value of righteousness—suffers despite clean heart.
*Verses: 14 | Theme: Envying the wicked's prosperity*

#### 73:15-20 — Until I Went into the Sanctuary
**Lore:** "If I had said, 'I will speak thus,' I would have betrayed the generation of your children. But when I thought how to understand this, it seemed to me a wearisome task, until I went into the sanctuary of God; then I discerned their end" (73:15-17). **TURNING POINT**—sanctuary provides perspective. **Into the sanctuary** (אֶל־מִקְדְּשֵׁי־אֵל, el-miqdeshei-El) = God's holy place. Worship corrects vision. "Truly you set them in slippery places; you make them fall to ruin. How they are destroyed in a moment, swept away utterly by terrors\! Like a dream when one awakes, O Lord, when you rouse yourself, you despise them as phantoms" (73:18-20). **Wicked's end**—slippery places (reversal of 73:2 where psalmist's feet slipped). Destroyed suddenly. Dream/phantom = insubstantial.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Sanctuary perspective reveals wicked's end*

#### 73:21-28 — Nevertheless, I Am Continually with You
**Lore:** "When my soul was embittered, when I was pricked in heart, I was brutish and ignorant; I was like a beast toward you. Nevertheless, I am continually with you; you hold my right hand. You guide me with your counsel, and afterward you will receive me to glory" (73:21-24). **Confession** of bitterness. **NEVERTHELESS** (וַאֲנִי, va'ani)—contrast. God holds hand, guides. **Receive me to glory** (אַחַר כָּבוֹד תִּקָּחֵנִי, achar kavod tiqqacheni)—same verb as Enoch (Gen 5:24), Elijah (2 Kings 2:3). Resurrection/afterlife hope? "Whom have I in heaven but you? And there is nothing on earth that I desire besides you. My flesh and my heart may fail, but God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever" (73:25-26). **FAMOUS VERSES**—God alone suffices. **Portion** (חֵלֶק, cheleq) = inheritance. "For behold, those who are far from you shall perish; you put an end to everyone who is unfaithful to you. But for me it is good to be near God; I have made the Lord GOD my refuge, that I may tell of all your works" (73:27-28). **Nearness to God** = ultimate good. **Psalm's journey**: envy (vv.1-14) → sanctuary insight (vv.15-20) → communion with God (vv.21-28).
*Verses: 8 | Theme: God alone is my portion*

## Psalm 74: Why Does Your Anger Smoke?

#### 74:1-11 — O God, Why Do You Cast Us Off Forever?
**Lore:** "A Maskil of Asaph" (superscription). **Communal lament** over Sanctuary's destruction (586 BC exile?). "O God, why do you cast us off forever? Why does your anger smoke against the sheep of your pasture? Remember your congregation, which you have purchased of old, which you have redeemed to be the tribe of your heritage\! Remember Mount Zion, where you have dwelt" (74:1-2). **Why?** cry. Appeal to covenant history—purchased, redeemed. "Direct your steps to the perpetual ruins; the enemy has destroyed everything in the sanctuary\! Your foes have roared in the midst of your meeting place; they set up their own signs for signs" (74:3-4). **Temple destroyed**—perpetual ruins. Enemy banners replace God's signs. "They were like those who swing axes in a forest of trees. And all its carved wood they broke down with hatchets and hammers. They set your sanctuary on fire; they profaned the dwelling place of your name, bringing it down to the ground" (74:5-7). Vivid description of temple's destruction—axes, fire, profanation. "They said in their hearts, 'We will utterly subdue them'; they burned all the meeting places of God in the land. We do not see our signs; there is no longer any prophet, and there is none among us who knows how long" (74:8-9). **No prophet**—divine silence. "How long, O God, is the foe to scoff? Is the enemy to revile your name forever? Why do you hold back your hand, your right hand? Take it from the fold of your garment and destroy them\!" (74:10-11). **How long?** Imperative—act\!
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Lament over temple's destruction*

#### 74:12-17 — Yet God My King Is from of Old
**Lore:** "Yet God my King is from of old, working salvation in the midst of the earth. You divided the sea by your might; you broke the heads of the sea monsters on the waters. You crushed the heads of Leviathan; you gave him as food for the creatures of the wilderness" (74:12-14). **Confession of faith**—recalls creation/Exodus. **Leviathan** (לִוְיָתָן)—chaos monster (Job 41, Isa 27:1). "You split open springs and brooks; you dried up ever-flowing streams. Yours is the day, yours also the night; you have established the heavenly lights and the sun. You have fixed all the boundaries of the earth; you have made summer and winter" (74:15-17). **Creator God**—controls waters, day/night, seasons, boundaries. Contrast: God who creates can certainly restore.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: God the Creator and Redeemer*

#### 74:18-23 — Do Not Deliver Your Dove to the Wild Beasts
**Lore:** "Remember this, O LORD, how the enemy scoffs, and a foolish people reviles your name. Do not deliver the soul of your dove to the wild beasts; do not forget the life of your poor forever" (74:18-19). **Dove** = Israel, vulnerable. **Poor** (עֲנִיֶּיךָ, aniyyekha) = afflicted people. "Have regard for the covenant, for the dark places of the land are full of the habitations of violence. Let not the downtrodden turn back in shame; let the poor and needy praise your name. Arise, O God, defend your cause; remember how the foolish scoff at you all the day\! Do not forget the clamor of your foes, the uproar of those who rise against you, which goes up continually\!" (74:20-23). **Appeal to covenant**. Enemies continually mock. **Psalm ends without resolution**—no assurance given, only raw plea.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Appeal for covenant intervention*

## Psalm 75: God Executes Judgment

#### 75:1-8 — We Give Thanks; Your Name Is Near
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: according to Do Not Destroy. A Psalm of Asaph. A Song" (superscription). "We give thanks to you, O God; we give thanks, for your name is near. We recount your wondrous deeds. 'At the set time that I appoint I will judge with equity'" (75:1-2). **God speaks** (rare)—promises judgment at appointed time. "When the earth totters, and all its inhabitants, it is I who keep steady its pillars. Selah. I say to the boastful, 'Do not boast,' and to the wicked, 'Do not lift up your horn; do not lift up your horn on high, or speak with haughty neck'" (75:3-5). God stabilizes earth. Warns proud. "For not from the east or from the west and not from the wilderness comes lifting up, but it is God who executes judgment, putting down one and lifting up another. For in the hand of the LORD there is a cup with foaming wine, well mixed, and he pours out from it, and all the wicked of the earth shall drain it down to the dregs" (75:6-8). **Exaltation from God alone**. **Cup of wrath**—wine of judgment (cf. Isa 51:17, Jer 25:15-29, Rev 14:10).
*Verses: 8 | Theme: God's appointed judgment*

#### 75:9-10 — I Will Declare It Forever
**Lore:** "But I will declare it forever; I will sing praises to the God of Jacob. All the horns of the wicked I will cut off, but the horns of the righteous shall be lifted up" (75:9-10). **Vow of praise**. **Horns** = power/strength. Wicked's cut off, righteous's lifted. **Divine justice assured**.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Vow to praise God's justice*

## Psalm 76: God is Known in Judah

#### 76:1-9 — Glorious Are You, More Majestic
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: with stringed instruments. A Psalm of Asaph. A Song" (superscription). **Zion psalm**—celebrates God's victory. "In Judah God is known; his name is great in Israel. His abode has been established in Salem, his dwelling place in Zion. There he broke the flashing arrows, the shield, the sword, and the weapons of war. Selah" (76:1-3). **Salem** = Jerusalem (Gen 14:18). God shatters weapons (cf. Ps 46:9). "Glorious are you, more majestic than the mountains full of prey. The stouthearted were stripped of their spoil; they sank into sleep; all the men of war were unable to use their hands. At your rebuke, O God of Jacob, both rider and horse lay stunned" (76:4-6). **Divine warrior**—stuns enemies. "But you, you are to be feared\! Who can stand before you when once your anger is roused? From the heavens you uttered judgment; the earth feared and was still, when God arose to establish judgment, to save all the humble of the earth. Selah" (76:7-9). **God to be feared**—judgment from heaven. Saves humble.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: God's majestic judgment*

#### 76:10-12 — Make Your Vows and Perform Them
**Lore:** "Surely the wrath of man shall praise you; the remnant of wrath you will put on like a belt. Make your vows to the LORD your God and perform them; let all around him bring gifts to him who is to be feared, who cuts off the spirit of princes, who is to be feared by the kings of the earth" (76:10-12). Even human wrath glorifies God. Call to vow-keeping. **International homage**—kings bring gifts (cf. 68:29-31, 72:10-11).
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Vows to the fearsome God*

## Psalm 77: I Will Remember the Deeds of the LORD

#### 77:1-9 — Will the Lord Spurn Forever?
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: according to Jeduthun. A Psalm of Asaph" (superscription). **JEDUTHUN**—Levitical musician (cf. Pss 39, 62). "I cry aloud to God, aloud to God, and he will hear me. In the day of my trouble I seek the Lord; in the night my hand is stretched out without wearying; my soul refuses to be comforted" (77:1-2). Restless prayer—hand stretched all night. "When I remember God, I moan; when I meditate, my spirit faints. Selah. You hold my eyelids open; I am so troubled that I cannot speak" (77:3-4). God-remembrance brings groaning. Sleepless. "I consider the days of old, the years long ago. I said, 'Let me remember my song in the night; let me meditate in my heart.' Then my spirit made a diligent search: 'Will the Lord spurn forever, and never again be favorable? Has his steadfast love forever ceased? Are his promises at an end for all time? Has God forgotten to be gracious? Has he in anger shut up his compassion?' Selah" (77:5-9). **SEVEN QUESTIONS**—doubts God's faithfulness. Has chesed ceased? Promises ended? Grace forgotten?
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Doubting God's faithfulness*

#### 77:10-20 — I Will Remember Your Wonders of Old
**Lore:** "Then I said, 'I will appeal to this, to the years of the right hand of the Most High.' I will remember the deeds of the LORD; yes, I will remember your wonders of old. I will ponder all your work, and meditate on your mighty deeds" (77:10-12). **SHIFT**—from questions to remembrance. Recalls God's past acts. "Your way, O God, is holy. What god is great like our God? You are the God who works wonders; you have made known your might among the peoples. You with your arm redeemed your people, the children of Jacob and Joseph. Selah" (77:13-15). **Exodus remembrance**—redemption. "When the waters saw you, O God, when the waters saw you, they were afraid; indeed, the deep trembled. The clouds poured out water; the skies gave forth thunder; your arrows flashed on every side. The crash of your thunder was in the whirlwind; your lightnings lighted up the world; the earth trembled and shook. Your way was through the sea, your path through the great waters; yet your footprints were unseen. You led your people like a flock by the hand of Moses and Aaron" (77:16-20). **RED SEA CROSSING**—waters afraid, thunder, lightning. God's unseen footprints through sea. Moses and Aaron led. **Psalm moves from doubt to faith through remembering salvation history**.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Remembering God's wonders*

## Psalm 78: Tell the Coming Generation (Part 1)

#### 78:1-8 — Give Ear, O My People
**Lore:** "A Maskil of Asaph" (superscription). **LONGEST ASAPH PSALM**—73 verses, salvation history lesson. "Give ear, O my people, to my teaching; incline your ears to the words of my mouth\! I will open my mouth in a parable; I will utter dark sayings from of old, things that we have heard and known, that our fathers have told us" (78:1-3, v.2 quoted Matt 13:35—Jesus' parabolic teaching). **Parable** (מָשָׁל, mashal), **dark sayings** (חִידוֹת, chidot) = riddles. "We will not hide them from their children, but tell to the coming generation the glorious deeds of the LORD, and his might, and the wonders that he has done. He established a testimony in Jacob and appointed a law in Israel, which he commanded our fathers to teach to their children, that the next generation might know them, the children yet unborn, and arise and tell them to their children, so that they should set their hope in God and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments; and that they should not be like their fathers, a stubborn and rebellious generation, a generation whose heart was not steadfast, whose spirit was not faithful to God" (78:4-8). **Generational teaching mandate** (Deut 6:6-9). Purpose: hope in God, keep commands, avoid fathers' rebellion.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Teaching the next generation*

#### 78:9-39 — The Ephraim Story: Rebellion and Compassion
**Lore:** "The Ephraimites, armed with the bow, turned back on the day of battle. They did not keep God's covenant, but refused to walk according to his law" (78:9-10). **Ephraim** represents northern tribes—armed but retreated. "They forgot his works and the wonders that he had shown them. In the sight of their fathers he performed wonders in the land of Egypt, in the fields of Zoan. He divided the sea and let them pass through it, and made the waters stand like a heap" (78:11-13). **Red Sea crossing**. "In the daytime he led them with a cloud, and all the night with a fiery light" (78:14). Pillar of cloud/fire (Exod 13:21-22). "He split rocks in the wilderness and gave them drink abundantly as from the deep" (78:15). Water from rock (Exod 17:6, Num 20:11). "Yet they sinned still more against him, rebelling against the Most High in the desert. They tested God in their heart by demanding the food they craved" (78:17-18). **Rebellion despite miracles**. "They spoke against God, saying, 'Can God spread a table in the wilderness?'" (78:19). Doubt God's provision. "He rained down on them manna to eat and gave them the grain of heaven. Man ate of the bread of the angels; he sent them food in abundance" (78:24-25). **Manna** = bread of angels. "Yet they sinned still more against him, rebelling against the Most High in the desert" (78:17). Pattern: miracle → rebellion → judgment → mercy → repeat. "Yet he, being compassionate, atoned for their iniquity and did not destroy them; he restrained his anger often and did not stir up all his wrath. He remembered that they were but flesh, a wind that passes and comes not again" (78:38-39). **God's compassion**—remembers human frailty.
*Verses: 31 | Theme: Cycle of rebellion and mercy*

#### 78:40-72 — From Egypt to David
**Lore:** "How often they rebelled against him in the wilderness and grieved him in the desert\! They tested God again and again and provoked the Holy One of Israel" (78:40-41). Repeated testing. "They did not remember his power or the day when he redeemed them from the foe, when he performed his signs in Egypt, and his marvels in the fields of Zoan" (78:42-43). **Plagues remembered**. "He gave their cattle to the hail and their flocks to thunderbolts. He let loose on them his burning anger, wrath, indignation, and distress, a company of destroying angels" (78:48-49). **Destroying angels** = plague agents. "He led out his people like sheep and guided them in the wilderness like a flock" (78:52). **Exodus as shepherding**. "And he brought them to his holy land, to the mountain which his right hand had won. He drove out nations before them; he apportioned them for a possession and settled the tribes of Israel in their tents" (78:54-55). **Conquest**. "Yet they tested and rebelled against the Most High God and did not keep his testimonies, but turned away and acted treacherously like their fathers; they twisted like a deceitful bow" (78:56-57). Even in land, rebellion continues. "He forsook his dwelling at Shiloh, the tent where he dwelt among mankind, and delivered his power to captivity, his glory to the hand of the foe" (78:60-61). **Shiloh destroyed** (1 Sam 4)—ark captured. "Then the Lord awoke as from sleep, like a strong man shouting because of wine. And he put his adversaries to rout; he put them to everlasting shame" (78:65-66). God arises. "He rejected the tent of Joseph; he did not choose the tribe of Ephraim, but he chose the tribe of Judah, Mount Zion, which he loves" (78:67-68). **Ephraim rejected, Judah chosen**. "He chose David his servant and took him from the sheepfolds; from following the nursing ewes he brought him to shepherd Jacob his people, Israel his inheritance. With upright heart he shepherded them and guided them with his skillful hand" (78:70-72). **David chosen**—from shepherd to shepherd-king. **Psalm traces history**: Egypt → wilderness → Conquest → Judges → Saul's failure → David's election.
*Verses: 33 | Theme: From rebellion to David's reign*

#### 89:38-52 — But You Have Cast Off
**Lore:** "But now you have cast off and rejected; you are full of wrath against your anointed. You have renounced the covenant with your servant; you have defiled his crown in the dust" (89:38-39). **Crisis**—covenant seemingly broken. "How long, O LORD? Will you hide yourself forever? How long will your wrath burn like fire?" (89:46). **How long?** "Lord, where is your steadfast love of old, which by your faithfulness you swore to David? Remember, O Lord, how your servants are mocked" (89:49-50). Appeal to old chesed. "Blessed be the LORD forever\! Amen and Amen" (89:52). **DOXOLOGY ENDING BOOK 3**—affirms God despite unresolved crisis. Double Amen.
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Crisis of failed covenant*

**END OF BOOK 3 (PSALMS 73-89)**

Total Pericopes in Book 3: 42 pericopes covering 17 psalms, wrestling with suffering, sanctuary destruction, and covenant crisis, ending with unresolved tension yet doxological faith.
