# Esther 6-7: Reversal

## Overview
Act 3 of the Esther drama: The great reversal begins. King's insomnia leads to discovery of Mordecai's unrewarded service, Haman arrives to request Mordecai's execution but instead must honor him publicly, Haman rushed to Esther's second banquet where he is exposed as villain and hanged on his own gallows. Peak of ironic justice. Central themes: Hidden providence (divine insomnia), poetic justice, pride before fall, tables turned, God vindicates the faithful.

---

## Chapter 6: The Great Reversal

#### 6:1-6 — The Sleepless Night
**Lore:** "On that night the king could not sleep. And he gave orders to bring the book of memorable deeds, the chronicles, and they were read before the king" (6:1). **PIVOT POINT OF ENTIRE BOOK**—**"On that night"** (בַּלַּיְלָה הַהוּא, balaylah hahu) = the night between Esther's two banquets, the night Haman built gallows (5:14). **"The king could not sleep"** (נָדְדָה שְׁנַת הַמֶּלֶךְ, nadedah shenat hamelekh) = literally "the king's sleep fled." **HIDDEN PROVIDENCE MASTERPIECE**—God never mentioned, yet **divine insomnia** unmistakable. Why couldn't king sleep? No explanation given—**just happened**. But **timing = perfect**: Night before Haman plans to request Mordecai's execution (6:4), night before Esther's crucial banquet (7:1). **Coincidence? No—PROVIDENCE.** God keeps king awake to orchestrate reversal. **Proverbs 21:1**: "The king's heart is a stream of water in the hand of the LORD; he turns it wherever he will." God controls king's **sleep** too. King orders **"book of memorable deeds, the chronicles"** (סֵפֶר הַזִּכְרֹנוֹת דִּבְרֵי הַיָּמִים, sefer hazikhronot divrei hayamim) = official annals recording empire events. Why chronicles? Insomniacs read boring books—Persian state records = ancient Ambien. "And it was found written how Mordecai had told about Bigthana and Teresh, two of the king's eunuchs, who guarded the threshold, and who had sought to lay hands on King Ahasuerus" (6:2). **SPECIFIC PASSAGE READ**—not random entry, but **Mordecai's unrewarded service** (2:21-23, saved king from assassination five years earlier). **HIDDEN PROVIDENCE AGAIN**: Of all records, this one read. Chronicles = massive (empire's entire history)—what are odds? **God directed reading**. Mordecai's good deed archived but **forgotten**—until now. "And the king said, 'What honor or distinction has been bestowed on Mordecai for this?' The king's young men who attended him said, 'Nothing has been done for him'" (6:3). King's question reveals: **Mordecai never rewarded**. **Why not?** 1) Bureaucratic oversight. 2) Mordecai = Jew, low status. 3) God's **timing**—delayed reward for strategic moment. **Now = perfect time**: Haman plotted Mordecai's death (5:14); God planned Mordecai's honor. **IRONY #15**: King discovers unrewarded hero same night villain built gallows for him. **Ecclesiastes 3:11**: "He has made everything beautiful in its time." Mordecai's time = now. "And the king said, 'Who is in the court?' Now Haman had just entered the outer court of the king's palace to speak to the king about having Mordecai hanged on the gallows that he had prepared for him" (6:4). **PERFECT TIMING**—king asks "Who's here?" exact moment Haman arrives. **Why Haman there?** Executing plan (5:14): Get king's approval to hang Mordecai before second banquet. Haman = early riser, eager to kill. Enters outer court (חָצֵר, chatzer)—waiting area before throne room. **IRONY #16**: Haman comes to **request Mordecai's death**; king about to **request Mordecai's honor**. Two opposite intentions collide. "And the king's young men told him, 'Haman is there, standing in the court.' And the king said, 'Let him come in'" (6:5). King summons Haman—**divine trap closing**. Haman thinks: "Perfect! I'll ask now." King thinks: "Perfect! Haman will know how to honor someone." **Two agendas, one conversation—comedy of errors** (if not tragic for Haman). "So Haman came in, and the king said to him, 'What should be done to the man whom the king delights to honor?'" (6:6a). King's question—**assumes Haman's wisdom** re: honors. **Doesn't name Mordecai**—leaves Haman guessing. "And Haman said to himself, 'Whom would the king delight to honor more than me?'" (6:6b). **HAMAN'S FATAL ASSUMPTION**—narcissism blinds. **"More than me?"** Haman convinced he's king's favorite (evidence: queen's banquets, 5:12). **Doesn't consider anyone else**. **IRONY #17**: Haman designs **ultimate honor** thinking for himself; actually for enemy. **Pride = self-deception**. **Proverbs 16:18**: "Pride goes before destruction, and a haughty spirit before a fall." Haman's pride = free-fall begun.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Insomnia*

#### 6:7-11 — Thus Shall It Be Done to Mordecai the Jew
**Lore:** "And Haman said to the king, 'For the man whom the king delights to honor, let royal robes be brought, which the king has worn, and the horse that the king has ridden, and on whose head a royal crown is set'" (6:7-8). **HAMAN'S FANTASY HONOR**—three elements: 1) **Royal robes** (לְבוּשׁ מַלְכוּת, levush malkhut) = king's personal garments (wearing king's clothes = co-regency symbolism). 2) **King's horse** (סוּס, sus) = not just any horse, but **one king rode** (status symbol—Persian kings = famed horsemen). 3) **Royal crown** (כֶּתֶר מַלְכוּת, keter malkhut) on horse's head = coronet (not crown for man—that's treason—but horse's ornament). **Haman imagines** himself parading as quasi-king. "And let the robes and the horse be handed over to one of the king's most noble officials. Let them dress the man whom the king delights to honor, and let them lead him on the horse through the square of the city, proclaiming before him: 'Thus shall it be done to the man whom the king delights to honor'" (6:9). **Public spectacle**—noble official dresses honoree + leads horse through **city square** (רְחוֹב הָעִיר, rechov ha'ir, same place Mordecai mourned, 4:6). Herald proclaims: **"Thus shall it be done..."** (כָּכָה יֵעָשֶׂה לָאִישׁ, kakhah ye'aseh la'ish) = ritual formula, maximum visibility. **Haman designs own humiliation** without knowing. **IRONY #18**: Every detail Haman suggests (to exalt self) will be done—**but for Mordecai**. "Then the king said to Haman, 'Hurry; take the robes and the horse, as you have said, and do so to Mordecai the Jew, who sits at the king's gate. Leave out nothing that you have mentioned'" (6:10). **BOMBSHELL**—king reveals honoree: **"Mordecai the Jew"** (מָרְדֳּכַי הַיְּהוּדִי, Mordokhai hayehudi). Same phrase Haman used (5:13, "Mordecai the Jew sitting at king's gate"). King knows 1) Mordecai's name, 2) Mordecai's ethnicity, 3) Mordecai's location. **King values Mordecai**—calls him man "whom the king delights to honor" (6:6). **Haman's world collapses**—must honor man he hates, man whose execution he planned. **"Hurry"** (מַהֵר, maher) = urgent command, no delay. **"Leave out nothing"** (אַל־תַּפֵּל דָּבָר, al-tapel davar) = do **every detail** Haman suggested. **IRONY #19**: Haman's elaborate plan used **against him**. Must personally lead Mordecai through streets. **POETIC JUSTICE**—Haman = herald of enemy's triumph. **So Haman took the robes and the horse, and he dressed Mordecai and led him through the square of the city, proclaiming before him, 'Thus shall it be done to the man whom the king delights to honor'" (6:11). **HAMAN OBEYS**—no recorded protest. Must swallow pride, execute humiliating task. Dresses Mordecai in **king's robes** (imagined for self), leads **king's horse** (dreamed of riding), proclaims **royal honor** (coveted for self)—all for **hated Jew**. **City square**—public humiliation. Crowds see: Haman (prime minister) serving Mordecai (Jewish gatekeeper). **Reversal complete**: Haman exalted (3:1) → Haman humiliated (6:11). Mordecai condemned (3:13) → Mordecai honored (6:11). **Proverbs 29:23**: "One's pride will bring him low, but he who is lowly in spirit will obtain honor." Mordecai = lowly gatekeeper → honored. Haman = proud prince → humiliated. **HIDDEN PROVIDENCE VISIBLE**: No miracle, no angel, no voice from heaven—just **sleepless night** + **archived good deed** + **narcissist's assumption** = divine reversal. **God's invisible hand** orchestrates human events. **Lesson: God vindicates the faithful in His timing.** Mordecai waited five years (2:23 → 6:11); God rewarded publicly, perfectly. **Romans 12:19**: "Beloved, never avenge yourselves, but leave it to the wrath of God, for it is written, 'Vengeance is mine, I will repay, says the Lord.'" Mordecai didn't retaliate—God exalted him.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Reversal*

#### 6:12-14 — Haman Hurried to His House Mourning
**Lore:** "Then Mordecai returned to the king's gate. But Haman hurried to his house, mourning and with his head covered" (6:12). **CONTRAST**—Mordecai returns to **king's gate** (business as usual, humble despite honor). Haman **rushes home** (חָפוּי רֹאשׁ, chafui rosh = head covered) = **shame gesture** (2 Sam 15:30, David fleeing Absalom—head covered in grief). **IRONY #20**: Mordecai (just honored) = calm. Haman (just humiliated) = devastated. "And Haman told his wife Zeresh and all his friends everything that had happened to him" (6:13a). Confesses disaster to **Zeresh** (wife who advised gallows, 5:14) + friends (who agreed). Expects sympathy? Gets prophecy of doom. "Then his wise men and his wife Zeresh said to him, 'If Mordecai, before whom you have begun to fall, is of the Jewish people, you will not overcome him but will surely fall before him'" (6:13b). **PAGAN PROPHECY**—Zeresh + advisors (now called "wise men," חֲכָמָיו, chakhamav) **recognize Jewish chosenness**. **"If Mordecai... is of the Jewish people"** (מִזֶּרַע הַיְּהוּדִים, mizera hayehudim = "from the seed of the Jews")—ethnicity = decisive. **Why?** Pagans knew Jewish history: 1) **Egyptian plagues** (Ex 7-12, Egypt fell trying to destroy Israel). 2) **Red Sea** (Ex 14, Pharaoh's army drowned). 3) **Jericho, Ai, Canaan conquered** (Josh 6-12). 4) **Assyria destroyed** for threatening Jerusalem (2 Kings 19:35, 185,000 slain). **Pattern clear: Whoever attacks Jews loses.** **"You have begun to fall"** (הַחִלּוֹתָ לִנְפֹּל, hachilota linpol)—process started (public humiliation). **"You will not overcome him but will surely fall"** (לֹא־תוּכַל לוֹ כִּי־נָפוֹל תִּפּוֹל, lo-tukhal lo ki-nafol tipol)—emphatic Hebrew (infinitive + verb = "falling you will fall"). **Inevitable doom prophesied** by Haman's own family. **IRONY #21**: Haman's wife/friends (who encouraged gallows) now predict his destruction. Zeresh = Lady Macbeth (pushes husband to evil, then sees futility). **Theology**: Even pagans recognize **God's protection of Jews** (Abrahamic covenant, Gen 12:3, "I will bless those who bless you, and him who dishonors you I will curse"). Haman dishonored Jews → cursed. **While they were yet talking with him, the king's eunuchs arrived and hurried to bring Haman to the feast that Esther had prepared** (6:14). **TIME'S UP**—mid-conversation, eunuchs arrive. **"Hurried"** (וַיַּבְהִלוּ, vayavhilu) = rushed—Haman late (spent time parading Mordecai + mourning at home). Esther's **second banquet** (5:8) = now. Haman dragged to feast—**no time to process disaster, no escape**. **Stage set for final confrontation** (ch 7). Haman enters banquet **demoralized** (just honored enemy, just heard prophecy of doom)—perfect timing for Esther's accusation. **HIDDEN PROVIDENCE AGAIN**: Sleepless night (6:1) → Mordecai honored (6:11) → Haman humiliated (6:12) → doom prophesied (6:13) → **all before crucial banquet** (6:14). God orchestrates **sequence** to maximize Haman's vulnerability. **Esther will strike** when enemy weakest. **Lesson: God's timing = perfect.** Not too early (Haman still confident), not too late (Jews already killed)—**exactly right**.
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Prophecy*

## Chapter 7: Haman's Execution

#### 7:1-6 — This Wicked Haman
**Lore:** "So the king and Haman went in to feast with Queen Esther" (7:1). **SECOND BANQUET**—delayed revelation (5:8). Haman arrives shaken (6:12-13), king curious (5:6, "What is your request?"). **And as they were drinking wine the king said to Esther, 'What is your wish, Queen Esther? It shall be granted you. And what is your request? Even to the half of my kingdom, it shall be fulfilled'" (7:2). **THIRD TIME** king offers "half kingdom" (5:3, 5:6, 7:2). Repetition = narrative emphasis—king **eager** to grant Esther's request. Wine flowing—convivial atmosphere. Esther waited for **optimal moment**. "Then Queen Esther answered, 'If I have found favor in your sight, O king, and if it please the king, let my life be granted me for my wish, and my people for my request'" (7:3). **BOMBSHELL #1**: Esther requests **"my life... and my people"**—reveals **1) She's under threat**, **2) She has a people** (ethnic identity). King shocked—queen in danger? **What people?** Esther hasn't disclosed Jewish identity yet (2:10, 20). Suspense builds. "For we have been sold, I and my people, to be destroyed, to be killed, and to be annihilated" (7:4a). **THREE GENOCIDAL VERBS** (exact quote from 3:13—Esther read decree). **"We have been sold"** (נִמְכַּרְנוּ, nimkarnu)—betrayed for money (Haman's 10,000 talents, 3:9). Esther = target of genocide. **"If we had been sold merely as slaves, men and women, I would have been silent, for our affliction is not to be compared with the loss to the king"** (7:4b). Clarifies: **Not slavery (tolerable), but extermination (intolerable).** "Affliction not to be compared" = awkward phrase (possibly: "our trouble wouldn't justify bothering the king")—Esther minimizes **self**, maximizes **injustice**. King's **loss** (economic/political, losing productive subjects) mentioned—pragmatic appeal. "Then King Ahasuerus said to Queen Esther, 'Who is he, and where is he, who has dared to do this?'" (7:5). King enraged—**"Who dared?"** (מָלֵא לִבּוֹ, male libbo = "filled his heart," idiom for audacity). Someone plotted queen's death—**treason**. King doesn't yet connect: **He authorized decree** (3:10-11). Irony: King angry at unnamed villain = **himself** (signed decree). But Haman = architect, king = unwitting accomplice. "And Esther said, 'A foe and enemy! This wicked Haman!'" (7:6a). **BOMBSHELL #2**: Esther **names Haman**. **"Foe and enemy"** (אִישׁ צַר וְאוֹיֵב, ish tzar ve'oyev)—double epithet (adversary + hater). **"This wicked Haman"** (הָמָן הָרָע הַזֶּה, Haman hara hazeh)—emphatic demonstrative ("THIS evil Haman," pointing finger). **Accusation direct**: Man sitting at table = would-be murderer. **Then Haman was terrified before the king and the queen** (7:6b). **Haman's terror**—Hebrew נִבְעַת (niv'at) = terrified/dismayed. Knows he's exposed. Just hours earlier: honored by king (false security). Now: accused by queen, king's fury imminent. **Zeresh's prophecy** (6:13, "you will surely fall") = **fulfilling**. **IRONY #22**: Haman at **height of honor** (queen's exclusive guest) = **moment of doom** (accused of treason). **HIDDEN PROVIDENCE CLIMAX**: Esther positioned as queen (ch 2) → invited Haman to banquet (ch 5) → Haman weakened by humiliation (ch 6) → **now Esther strikes** (ch 7). **God's multi-year plan converges**. Orphan girl = instrument of justice. **Typology: Esther intercedes for her people before king, exposes enemy → Christ intercedes for His people before Father, defeats Satan** (Heb 7:25; Rev 12:10, Satan = accuser, Christ = defender). Esther's accusation of Haman = type of Christ's victory over evil.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Accusation*

#### 7:7-10 — They Hanged Haman on the Gallows
**Lore:** "And the king arose in his wrath from the wine-drinking and went into the palace garden, but Haman stayed to beg for his life from Queen Esther, for he saw that harm was determined against him by the king" (7:7). King storms out—**wrath** (חֵמָה, chemah) + wine = explosive rage. Goes to **palace garden** (בִּיתַן הַגִּנָּה, bitan haginnah) to cool off, process shock (his decree threatens his beloved queen). Haman **stays**, **begs Esther** for life—**role reversal**: Powerful prime minister → groveling before woman he condemned. **"Harm determined"** (כָּלְתָה אֵלָיו הָרָעָה, kaletah elav hara'ah) = evil/disaster finalized. Haman reads king's fury—**doom certain**. "And the king returned from the palace garden to the place where they were drinking wine, as Haman was falling on the couch where Esther was. And the king said, 'Will he even assault the queen in my presence, in my own house?'" (7:8a). **CATASTROPHIC MISUNDERSTANDING**—King returns, sees Haman **fallen on Esther's couch** (מִטָּה, mittah = reclining couch for dining). Haman **pleading** (fallen posture = supplication), but king interprets as **sexual assault** ("assault the queen," לִכְבּוֹשׁ, likhbosh = to rape/violate). **Persian protocol**: Touch queen = treason. **IRONY #23**: Haman begging for mercy → perceived as **attacking queen** → seals own fate. **Misunderstanding = final nail** in coffin. "As the word left the mouth of the king, they covered Haman's face" (7:8b). **Face covering** (חָפוּ אֶת־פְּנֵי הָמָן, chafu et-pnei Haman) = **execution ritual**. Persian custom: Condemned man's face covered before execution (cf. condemned criminals led to death hooded). **Instant sentencing**—no trial, no appeal. King's word = law (Medo-Persian irrevocability strikes again, 1:19). "Then Harbona, one of the eunuchs in attendance on the king, said, 'Moreover, the gallows that Haman has prepared for Mordecai, whose word saved the king, is standing at Haman's house, fifty cubits high'" (7:9). **HARBONA** (חַרְבוֹנָה, Charbonah, one of seven eunuchs from 1:10) **informs king**: 1) Haman built **gallows**. 2) Intended for **Mordecai** (whom king just honored, 6:11). 3) Mordecai = king's savior (2:22). 4) Gallows = **fifty cubits** (~75 feet—visible landmark). **IRONY #24**: Haman's gallows (built for Mordecai) **reported to king** by palace insider—**secret construction exposed**. Harbona = hero (or opportunist—reads political winds, switches sides). **"And the king said, 'Hang him on that'" (7:10a). **INSTANT JUSTICE**—king orders Haman **hanged on own gallows**. Hebrew תְּלֻהוּ עָלָיו (teluhu alav) = "hang him on it." **POETIC JUSTICE PERFECTED**: Haman's instrument of murder → instrument of execution. **IRONY #25**: Gallows built overnight (5:14) for Mordecai → Haman hanged same day. **Proverbs 26:27**: "Whoever digs a pit will fall into it, and a stone will come back on him who starts it rolling." **LITERAL FULFILLMENT**. **"So they hanged Haman on the gallows that he had prepared for Mordecai"** (7:10b). Execution immediate—servants hang Haman on **his own gallows**. **Highest irony**: 75-foot gallows intended for **maximum shame** of Mordecai = **maximum shame** of Haman. Entire city sees (gallows visible across Susa). **"Then the wrath of the king abated"** (7:10c). King's rage satisfied—justice served. Hebrew שָׁכָכָה (shakhakhah) = subsided/calmed. Haman dead, Esther safe, Mordecai vindicated. **BUT**: Genocidal decree (3:13) **still active** (irrevocable, 1:19). Jews still condemned to die Adar 13. **Chapter 7 = personal victory** (Haman executed), **not yet corporate deliverance** (Jews still threatened). **Chapter 8 = counter-decree** required. **HIDDEN PROVIDENCE COMPLETE FOR HAMAN**: God orchestrated every step: Vashti deposed (1:19) → Esther crowned (2:17) → Mordecai saved king (2:22) → Haman plotted genocide (3:13) → Esther positioned (5:4-8) → King's insomnia (6:1) → Mordecai honored (6:11) → Haman humiliated (6:12) → Esther's accusation (7:6) → Haman hanged (7:10). **ELEVEN CHAPTERS of orchestration** → villain destroyed. **No miracle, no theophany, no named divine intervention—yet God's hand OBVIOUS.** **Lesson: God works invisibly but unstoppably.** Hidden providence = **most profound revelation** of God's sovereignty. **Esther's message: God reigns even when silent.** Satan plots (Haman), God orchestrates (sleepless nights, archived deeds, perfect timing). **Evil self-destructs; righteousness vindicated.** **Final typology: Haman's gallows → Christ's cross.** Haman hanged on instrument meant for innocent (Mordecai). Christ hanged on cross meant for guilty (us). **Substitutionary reversal**: Haman dies instead of Mordecai (justice). Christ dies instead of sinners (grace). **Esther's ironic reversals → Gospel's ultimate reversal**: Death → Life, Curse → Blessing, Condemnation → Justification.
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Justice*
