# Deuteronomy 15-17: Release, Festivals, and Leadership

## Part VI: Sabbath Year, Festivals, Leaders (Chapters 15-17)

### Chapter 15: Sabbath Year and Servant Release

#### 15:1-6 — Seventh Year Debt Release
**Lore:** "At the end of every seven years you shall grant a release. And this is the manner of the release: every creditor shall release what he has lent to his neighbor. He shall not exact it of his neighbor, his brother, because the LORD's release has been proclaimed" (15:1-2). **Sabbath year = debt cancellation!** Revolutionary economic justice. "Of a foreigner you may exact it, but whatever of yours is with your brother your hand shall release. But there will be no poor among you; for the LORD will bless you in the land that the LORD your God is giving you for an inheritance to possess it, if only you will strictly obey the voice of the LORD your God, being careful to do all this commandment that I am commanding you today" (15:3-5). Ideal: obedience → blessing → no poverty. "For the LORD your God will bless you, as he promised you, and you shall lend to many nations, but you shall not borrow, and you shall rule over many nations, but they shall not rule over you" (15:6). Economic dominance through God's blessing. Practically: Israel rarely fully observed this (Neh 5:1-13; Jer 34:8-22). Jesus: "The poor you always have with you" (Matt 26:11, quoting Deut 15:11). Jubilee (Lev 25) related but distinct. Principle: God's economy prioritizes people over profit.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Seventh year release*

#### 15:7-11 — Give Generously to Poor
**Lore:** "If among you, one of your brothers should become poor, in any of your towns within your land that the LORD your God is giving you, you shall not harden your heart or shut your hand against your poor brother, but you shall open your hand to him and lend him sufficient for his need, whatever it may be" (15:7-8). Open-handed generosity commanded. "Take care lest there be an unworthy thought in your heart and you say, 'The seventh year, the year of release is near,' and your eye look grudgingly on your poor brother, and you give him nothing, and he cry to the LORD against you, and you be guilty of sin" (15:9). Warning against refusing loans near sabbath year! Anticipates selfish calculation. "You shall give to him freely, and your heart shall not be grudging when you give to him, because for this the LORD your God will bless you in all your work and in all that you undertake. For there will never cease to be poor in the land. Therefore I command you, 'You shall open wide your hand to your brother, to the needy and to the poor, in your land'" (15:10-11). **Famous verse!** Poverty persists; generosity required perpetually. Paradox: 15:4 says "no poor" if obedient; 15:11 says "never cease to be poor." Resolution: ideal vs. reality. Sinful world = poverty exists. Response: constant compassion. Jesus references 15:11 (John 12:8). 2 Cor 8-9: Paul's collection for poor. James 2:15-16: faith without works dead.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Openhanded giving*

#### 15:12-18 — Hebrew Servant Release
**Lore:** "If your brother, a Hebrew man or a Hebrew woman, is sold to you, he shall serve you six years, and in the seventh year you shall let him go free from you. And when you let him go free from you, you shall not let him go empty-handed. You shall furnish him liberally out of your flock, out of your threshing floor, and out of your winepress. As the LORD your God has blessed you, you shall give to him" (15:12-14). Seventh year servant release + generous severance! Echoes Ex 21:2-6. "You shall remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt, and the LORD your God redeemed you; therefore I command you this today" (15:15). **Key motivation: remember your slavery!** Redemption memory → compassion for servants. "But if he says to you, 'I will not go out from you,' because he loves you and your household, since he is well-off with you, then you shall take an awl, and put it through his earlobe into the door, and he shall be your slave forever. And to your female slave you shall do the same" (15:16-17). Permanent servitude option. Love-based voluntary slavery! Ear pierced = sign of lifelong commitment. Psalm 40:6: "Sacrifice and offering you have not desired, but you have given me an open ear" (ESV footnote: "you have dug ears for me"). Typology: Christ = willing servant (Phil 2:7; Heb 10:5-7). "It shall not seem hard to you when you let him go free from you, for at half the cost of a hired worker he has served you six years. So the LORD your God will bless you in all that you do" (15:18). Economic benefit + divine blessing.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Free servants generously*

#### 15:19-23 — Firstborn Animals Consecrated
**Lore:** "All the firstborn males that are born of your herd and flock you shall dedicate to the LORD your God. You shall do no work with the firstborn of your herd, nor shear the firstborn of your flock. You shall eat it, you and your household, before the LORD your God year by year at the place that the LORD will choose" (15:19-20). Firstborn animals = sacred, for worship meal at sanctuary. "But if it has any blemish, if it is lame or blind or has any serious blemish whatever, you shall not sacrifice it to the LORD your God. You shall eat it within your towns. The unclean and the clean alike may eat it, as though it were a gazelle or a deer. Only you shall not eat its blood; you shall pour it out on the earth like water" (15:21-23). Blemished firstborn = secular food, not sacrificed. Maintains holiness standards. Malachi 1:8, 13-14: God rejects blemished offerings. Christ = unblemished Lamb (1 Pet 1:19).
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Consecrate firstborn*

### Chapter 16: Three Pilgrimage Festivals

#### 16:1-8 — Passover and Unleavened Bread
**Lore:** "Observe the month of Abib and keep the Passover to the LORD your God, for in the month of Abib the LORD your God brought you out of Egypt by night" (16:1). **Passover** (פֶּסַח, Pesach) = foundational festival. Abib = later called Nisan (March-April). "And you shall offer the Passover sacrifice to the LORD your God, from the flock or the herd, at the place that the LORD will choose, to make his name dwell there" (16:2). Centralized at sanctuary (unlike Ex 12 where each home). "You shall eat no leavened bread with it. Seven days you shall eat it with unleavened bread, the bread of affliction—for you came out of the land of Egypt in haste—that all the days of your life you may remember the day when you came out of the land of Egypt. No leaven shall be seen with you in all your territory for seven days, nor shall any of the flesh that you sacrifice on the evening of the first day remain all night until morning" (16:3-4). Unleavened bread (מַצָּה, matzah) = remembrance of hasty Exodus. Leaven = symbol of sin (1 Cor 5:6-8). "You may not offer the Passover sacrifice within any of your towns that the LORD your God is giving you, but at the place that the LORD your God will choose, to make his name dwell there, there you shall offer the Passover sacrifice, in the evening at sunset, at the time you came out of Egypt. And you shall cook it and eat it at the place that the LORD your God will choose. And in the morning you shall turn and go to your tents. For six days you shall eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day there shall be a solemn assembly to the LORD your God. You shall do no work on it" (16:5-8). Week-long celebration. Christ = our Passover (1 Cor 5:7). Last Supper = Passover meal (Luke 22:15). Crucifixion timing = Passover lamb slaughtered.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Remember the Exodus*

#### 16:9-12 — Feast of Weeks (Pentecost)
**Lore:** "You shall count seven weeks. Begin to count the seven weeks from the time the sickle is first put to the standing grain. Then you shall keep the Feast of Weeks to the LORD your God with the tribute of a freewill offering from your hand, which you shall give as the LORD your God blesses you" (16:9-10). **Feast of Weeks** (שָׁבֻעוֹת, Shavuot) = seven weeks (49 days) after Passover. Later called Pentecost (Greek: "50th day"). Harvest thanksgiving (Lev 23:15-21). "And you shall rejoice before the LORD your God, you and your son and your daughter, your male servant and your female servant, the Levite who is within your towns, the sojourner, the fatherless, and the widow who are among you, at the place that the LORD your God will choose, to make his name dwell there" (16:11). Inclusive celebration! All segments of society. "You shall remember that you were a slave in Egypt; and you shall be careful to observe these statutes" (16:12). Exodus memory motivates inclusion. NT fulfillment: Holy Spirit poured out at Pentecost (Acts 2). First fruits of Church (Rom 8:23).
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Harvest joy*

#### 16:13-17 — Feast of Booths (Tabernacles)
**Lore:** "You shall keep the Feast of Booths seven days, when you have gathered in the produce from your threshing floor and your winepress. You shall rejoice in your feast, you and your son and your daughter, your male servant and your female servant, the Levite, the sojourner, the fatherless, and the widow who are within your towns" (16:13-14). **Feast of Booths/Tabernacles** (סֻכּוֹת, Sukkot) = autumn harvest festival. Live in temporary shelters (Lev 23:33-43), remembering wilderness journey. "For seven days you shall keep the feast to the LORD your God at the place that the LORD will choose, because the LORD your God will bless you in all your produce and in all the work of your hands, so that you will be altogether joyful" (16:15). Most joyous festival! Celebrate God's provision. "Three times a year all your males shall appear before the LORD your God at the place that he will choose: at the Feast of Unleavened Bread, at the Feast of Weeks, and at the Feast of Booths. They shall not appear before the LORD empty-handed. Every man shall give as he is able, according to the blessing of the LORD your God that he has given you" (16:16-17). **Three pilgrimage festivals** = national assembly. Brings unity, worship, joy. Jesus attended these (John 7:2, 37—Tabernacles; John 10:22—Dedication; John 13:1—Passover). Kingdom fulfillment: nations celebrate Tabernacles (Zech 14:16).
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Pilgrimage festivals*

### Chapter 16-17: Justice and Leadership

#### 16:18-20 — Appoint Righteous Judges
**Lore:** "You shall appoint judges and officers in all your towns that the LORD your God is giving you, according to your tribes, and they shall judge the people with righteous judgment. You shall not pervert justice. You shall not show partiality, and you shall not accept a bribe, for a bribe blinds the eyes of the wise and subverts the cause of the righteous. Justice, justice, you shall pursue, that you may live and inherit the land that the LORD your God is giving you" (16:18-20). **"Justice, justice"** (צֶדֶק צֶדֶק, tzedek tzedek) = emphatic repetition! Pursuit of justice = condition for dwelling in land. Bribes condemned (Ex 23:8; Prov 17:23; Isa 1:23). Impartial judgment required (Lev 19:15; Deut 1:17). Prophets decry corrupt judges (Isa 1:21-23; Mic 3:9-11). Jesus: greatest Judge (John 5:22; Acts 17:31).
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Righteous justice*

#### 16:21-17:7 — No Asherah, Pure Worship, Stone Idolaters
**Lore:** "You shall not plant any tree as an Asherah beside the altar of the LORD your God that you shall make. And you shall not set up a pillar, which the LORD your God hates" (16:21-22). Asherah (אֲשֵׁרָה) = Canaanite goddess, wooden pole/tree symbol. Frequently erected by idolatrous Israelites (Judg 6:25-30; 1 Kgs 14:15, 23; 2 Kgs 23:4-15). "You shall not sacrifice to the LORD your God an ox or a sheep in which is a blemish, any defect whatever, for that is an abomination to the LORD your God" (17:1). Blemished offerings = insult to God. "If there is found among you, within any of your towns that the LORD your God is giving you, a man or woman who does what is evil in the sight of the LORD your God, in transgressing his covenant, and has gone and served other gods and worshiped them, or the sun or the moon or any of the host of heaven, which I have forbidden, and it is told you and you hear of it, then you shall inquire diligently, and if it is true and certain that such an abomination has been done in Israel, then you shall bring out to your gates that man or woman who has done this evil thing, and you shall stone that man or woman to death with stones. On the evidence of two witnesses or of three witnesses the one who is to die shall be put to death; a person shall not be put to death on the evidence of one witness. The hand of the witnesses shall be first against him to put him to death, and afterward the hand of all the people. So you shall purge the evil from your midst" (17:2-7). Idolatry = capital crime. Multiple witnesses required (Deut 19:15; Matt 18:16; 2 Cor 13:1; 1 Tim 5:19). Witnesses = executioners (John 8:7: "Let him who is without sin...cast the first stone"). Purge evil = maintain covenant purity.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Pure worship enforced*

#### 17:8-13 — Appeal to Central Court
**Lore:** "If any case arises requiring decision between one kind of homicide and another, one kind of legal right and another, or one kind of assault and another, any case within your towns that is too difficult for you, then you shall arise and go up to the place that the LORD your God will choose. And you shall come to the Levitical priests and to the judge who is in office in those days, and you shall consult them, and they shall declare to you the decision" (17:8-9). Supreme court at central sanctuary! Levitical priests + judge. Handles difficult cases. "Then you shall do according to what they declare to you from that place that the LORD will choose. And you shall be careful to do according to all that they direct you. According to the instructions that they give you, and according to the decision which they pronounce to you, you shall do. You shall not turn aside from the verdict that they declare to you, either to the right hand or to the left" (17:10-11). Binding authority. "The man who acts presumptuously by not obeying the priest who stands to minister there before the LORD your God, or the judge, that man shall die. So you shall purge the evil from Israel. And all the people shall hear and fear, and not act presumptuously again" (17:12-13). Contempt of court = death penalty! Ensures legal order. Later Sanhedrin traces origin to this.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Supreme court*

#### 17:14-20 — Regulations for Kings
**Lore:** "When you come to the land that the LORD your God is giving you, and you possess it and dwell in it and then say, 'I will set a king over me, like all the nations that are around me,' you may indeed set a king over you whom the LORD your God will choose. One from among your brothers you shall set as king over you. You may not put a foreigner over you, who is not your brother" (17:14-15). Permission for monarchy, but regulated! Must be God's choice (1 Sam 16:1—David). Israelite, not foreigner. "Only he must not acquire many horses for himself or cause the people to return to Egypt in order to acquire many horses, since the LORD has said to you, 'You shall never return that way again.' And he shall not acquire many wives for himself, lest his heart turn away, nor shall he acquire for himself excessive silver and gold" (17:16-17). **Three prohibitions:** (1) Many horses (military might; Egypt reliance). (2) Many wives (foreign alliances; idolatry). (3) Excessive wealth (pride; oppression). **Solomon violated ALL THREE!** (1 Kgs 10:26-29—horses from Egypt; 11:1-8—700 wives, turned to idols; 10:14-27—vast gold). "And when he sits on the throne of his kingdom, he shall write for himself in a book a copy of this law, approved by the Levitical priests. And it shall be with him, and he shall read in it all the days of his life, that he may learn to fear the LORD his God by keeping all the words of this law and these statutes, and doing them, that his heart may not be lifted up above his brothers, and that he may not turn aside from the commandment, either to the right hand or to the left, so that he may continue long in his kingdom, he and his children, in Israel" (17:18-20). **King must copy, carry, read Torah daily!** Subject to God's law. Prevents royal tyranny. Josiah exemplified this (2 Kgs 22-23). Jesus = perfect King (Rev 19:16), Word incarnate (John 1:1, 14).
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Humble kingship*
