# 2 Samuel 1-7: David's Triumphs Begin

## Overview
David's rise to kingship over all Israel. Triumphs: graceful lament, unification, Jerusalem conquered, Ark brought (holiness lesson), eternal Davidic Covenant received. Central themes: grace to enemies, God's holiness, eternal covenant pointing to Christ.

---

## Chapter 1: Song of the Bow

#### 1:1-8 — Amalekite's False Report
**Lore:** David in Ziklag. Amalekite arrives with news: Saul dead (contradicts 1 Sam 31:4). Brings Saul's crown. David tears clothes, grieves. "Tell me how the battle went" (1:4). Amalekite claims to have killed dying Saul. David's response: "Did you not fear to lift your hand to destroy the LORD's anointed?" (1:14). King's anointing = sacred.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Respect for anointed*

#### 1:9-16 — David Executes the Bearer
**Lore:** Amalekite begs no harsh judgment. "So David said to him, 'Your blood be on your head, for your own mouth has testified against you'" (1:16). Executes immediately. Consistent with 1 Sam 24, 26 principle—David spares Saul twice, but now executes one claiming to kill "the LORD's anointed." No triumphalism at enemy's death. Justice over celebration.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: No triumph in murder*

#### 1:17-27 — David's Lament: The Song of the Bow
**Lore:** "The Song of the Bow"—public lament taught to Judah. "How the mighty have fallen!" (1:19). Praises Saul and Jonathan with no mention of Saul's sins. Generous eulogy. "For Jonathan: 'your love to me was extraordinary, surpassing the love of women'" (1:26)—covenant loyalty (אַהֲבָה), deeper than marriage because grounded in God's purposes. Grace demonstrated—mourns enemy and friend equally. Type of Christ who wept over Jerusalem.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Graceful lament*

## Chapters 2-4: Civil War

#### 2:1-7 — David in Hebron
**Lore:** "David inquired of the LORD, 'Shall I go up into any of the cities of Judah?' And the LORD said to him, 'Yes.'" (2:1). Doesn't assume leadership—inquires of God. Goes to Hebron. Judahites anoint David king of Judah (third anointing—first: Samuel, second: Ziklag followers, third: official). "And the men of Judah came, and there they anointed David king over the house of Judah" (2:4).
*Verses: 7 | Theme: God's guidance*

#### 2:8-11 — Abner Makes Ish-bosheth King
**Lore:** "But Abner the son of Ner, commander of Saul's army, took Ish-bosheth the son of Saul and brought him over to Mahanaim, and made him king over Gilead and the Ashurites and Jezreel and Ephraim and Benjamin and all Israel" (2:8-9). Abner (powerful general) refuses to accept David. Makes Ish-bosheth ("man of shame") king over 10 tribes. "Ish-bosheth was forty years old when he became king over Israel, and he reigned two years" (2:10). Civil war begins—7.5 years of division.
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Divided kingdom*

#### 2:12-19 — Deadly Competition at Gibeon
**Lore:** "Abner the son of Ner and the servants of Ish-bosheth went out from Mahanaim to Gibeon. And Joab the son of Zeruiah and the servants of David went out and met them at the pool of Gibeon. And they sat down, the one on the one side of the pool and the other on the other side of the pool" (2:12-13). "Let the young men arise and compete before us" (2:14). "Twelve of Benjamin and of Ish-bosheth... and twelve of the servants of David" (2:15-16). "And they caught each other by the head and thrust his sword in the other's side, so they fell down together" (2:16). "Field of sword-edges"—all die. Symbolic deadlock.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Deadly competition*

#### 2:20-32 — Asahel's Pursuit and Death
**Lore:** "Asahel was as swift of foot as a wild gazelle" (2:18). "Asahel pursued Abner" (2:19). "Then Abner called to Asahel, 'Turn aside from following me. Why should I strike you to the ground?'" (2:22). Asahel refuses. "So Abner struck him in the stomach with the butt of his spear, so that the spear came out behind him, and he fell there and died on the spot" (2:23). Self-defense—creates blood feud with Joab (Asahel's brother). Battle ends. Casualties: 19 David's vs. 360 Saul's. David gaining strength.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Blood revenge begins*

## Chapter 3: Defection and Murder

#### 3:1-7 — David Grows Stronger
**Lore:** "And the war between the house of Saul and the house of David grew longer and longer. And David became stronger and stronger, while the house of Saul became weaker and weaker" (3:1). Power shifts to David. David's sons born in Hebron: Amnon, Daniel, Absalom, Adonijah, Shephatiah, Ithream (3:2-5). Large family = stability. Political children with different mothers = alliances.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Power shifts*

#### 3:8-16 — Abner Defects
**Lore:** "Abner had been making himself strong in the house of Saul" (3:6). Takes Saul's concubine (claiming throne?). Ish-bosheth accuses: "Why have you gone in to my father's concubine?" (3:7). Abner rages: "Am I a dog's head of Judah? This day I keep showing steadfast love to the house of Saul your father, to his brothers and his friends, and have not given you into the hand of David. And yet you charge me today with a fault concerning this woman!" (3:8-9). Defects. Demands Michal returned (David's wife, taken by Saul, given to another man). David agrees—reclaims legitimate wife.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Alliance shift*

#### 3:17-21 — Abner Persuades All Israel
**Lore:** "Abner conferred with the elders of Israel, saying, 'For some time you have been seeking David as king over you. Now then bring it about'" (3:17-18). Persuades power brokers. "Abner also spoke to Benjamin" (3:19). Builds consensus for David. "And Abner came to David at Hebron, and twenty men with him" (3:20). Brings delegation. Alliance sealed.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Consensus for David*

#### 3:22-28 — Joab Suspects Treachery
**Lore:** "Just then Joab and the servants of David came in from a raid and brought much spoil with them. But Abner was not with David at Hebron, for David had sent him away, and he had gone in peace" (3:22). Joab learns Abner visited. "Then Joab came to the king and said, 'What have you done? Behold, Abner came to you. Why is it that you have sent him away, so that he is gone?'" (3:24). Joab distrusts Abner's motives. "You know that Abner the son of Ner came to deceive you and to learn your comings and goings" (3:25). Accuses deception.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Joab's distrust*

#### 3:29-39 — Joab Murders Abner
**Lore:** "So Joab and Abishai his brother killed Abner, because he had killed their brother Asahel in the battle at Gibeon" (3:30). Blood revenge for Asahel. "Let it fall upon the head of Joab and upon all his father's house" (3:29)—David curses but doesn't execute (Joab too powerful). "And all the people wept over Abner" (3:32). Public mourning. David fasts, laments (3:35). Proves innocence. "So all the people and all Israel understood that day that it had not been the king's decision to put to death Abner the son of Ner" (3:37). Gentle David vs. severe sons of Zeruiah.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Murder and innocence*

---

## Chapter 4: Ish-bosheth's End

#### 4:1-12 — Assassination and Justice
**Lore:** "When Ish-bosheth heard that Abner had died at Hebron, his courage failed, and all Israel was dismayed" (4:1). Without Abner, Ish-bosheth loses support. "Two men who were captains of raiding bands" murder Ish-bosheth during nap, behead him, bring head to David for reward (4:5-7). David executes assassins: "Behold, as the LORD lives, who has redeemed my life out of every adversity, when one told me, 'Behold, Saul is dead,' thinking to bring good news, I seized him and killed him at Ziklag, instead of rewarding him for his news. How much more, when wicked men have killed a righteous man in his own house on his bed, shall I not now require his blood at your hand and destroy you from the earth?" (4:10-11). Won't gain throne through murder. Parallel to Amalekite (ch 1). Honorable burial for Ish-bosheth. God removes obstacles without David's participation.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Rejecting murder*

## Chapter 5: Jerusalem and Victory

#### 5:1-5 — All Israel United
**Lore:** "Then all the tribes of Israel came to David at Hebron and said, 'Behold, we are your bone and flesh... And the LORD said to you, "You shall be shepherd of my people Israel, and you shall be prince over Israel"'" (5:1-2). All tribes recognize David. "Shepherd" (רֹעֶה) of people. Covenant made. Third anointing over all Israel! "At that time he was thirty years old, and he reigned forty years" (5:4)—7.5 Hebron + 33 Jerusalem. "All the elders of Israel came to the king at Hebron; and King David made a covenant with them at Hebron before the LORD, and they anointed David king over Israel" (5:3).
*Verses: 5 | Theme: All Israel united*

#### 5:6-12 — City of David Conquered
**Lore:** "And the king and his men went to Jerusalem against the Jebusites, the inhabitants of the land" (5:6). Conquers Jerusalem from Jebusites (impregnable fortress). "Water shaft tunnel"—climbs through to enter. "Now in that day, David said, 'Whoever would smite the Jebusites, let him get up the water shaft, and smite the lame and the blind, who are hated by David's soul'" (5:8). Now "city of David." Fortifies (5:9). "KEY: David recognizes kingdom established 'for sake of his people Israel,' not self!" (5:12). "And Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David, and cedar trees, also carpenters and masons" (5:11). International recognition.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Capital established*

#### 5:13-16 — Royal Family
**Lore:** "And David took more concubines and wives from Jerusalem, after he came from Hebron, and more sons and daughters were born to David. And these are the names of those who were born to him in Jerusalem: Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, Solomon, Ibhar, Elishua, Nepheg, Japhia, Elishama, Eliada, and Eliphelet" (5:13-16). Eleven sons born in Jerusalem. Building dynasty security through multiple heirs. (Note: David's wives strategic—alliances, stability.)
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Dynasty building*

#### 5:17-25 — Philistines Defeated
**Lore:** "When the Philistines heard that David had been anointed king over Israel, all the Philistines went up to seek David. But David heard of it and went down to the stronghold" (5:17). David inquires of LORD before battle: "Shall I go up against the Philistines? Will you give them into my hand?" (5:19). Contrast Saul! "And the LORD said to David, 'Go up, for I will certainly give the Philistines into your hand'" (5:19). Victory at Baal-perazim ("lord of breaking through"). "Then the Philistines came up yet again and spread out in the Valley of Rephaim" (5:22). Second attack. David inquires again—different strategy: "Do not go up, but go around to their rear, and come upon them opposite the balsam trees. And when you hear the sound of marching in the tops of the balsam trees, then bestir yourself, for then the LORD has gone out before you to strike down the army of the Philistines" (5:23-24). Obeys, defeats them. "And the Philistines did not come again into the land of Israel" (5:25). Perfect obedience = total victory. Philistine threat neutralized.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Inquiring brings victory*

## Chapter 6: Ark Brought Home

#### 6:1-6 — First Attempt Fails
**Lore:** "David again gathered all the chosen men of Israel, thirty thousand. And David arose and went with all the people who were with him from Baale-judah to bring up from there the ark of God" (6:1-2). Ark = God's physical presence. "And they set the ark of God on a new cart... And Uzzah and Ahio, the sons of Abinadab, were driving the new cart" (6:3-4). **COPIES PHILISTINES**—violates Num 4:15. Levites only carry on shoulders. "And when they came to the threshing floor of Nacon, Uzzah put out his hand to the ark of God and took hold of it, for the oxen stumbled" (6:6). Stabilizes ark. Appears protective.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Disobedience begins*

#### 6:7-11 — Uzzah Dies, Ark Left Behind
**Lore:** "And the anger of the LORD was kindled against Uzzah, and God struck him down there because of his error, and he died there beside the ark of God" (6:7). **Uzzah dies instantly.** "And David was angry because the LORD had broken out against Uzzah" (6:8). David initially angry—doesn't understand holiness. "How can the ark of the LORD come to me?" (6:9). Afraid. "So David was unwilling to take the ark of the LORD into the city of David. But David took it aside to the house of Obed-edom the Gittite" (6:10). Leaves ark with Obed-edom 3 months. "And the LORD blessed Obed-edom and all his household" (6:11). Obedience blessed. David learns holiness lesson: "Good intentions don't excuse disobedience."
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Holy fear*

#### 6:12-17 — Successful Retrieval
**Lore:** "And it was told King David, 'The LORD has blessed the household of Obed-edom and all that belongs to him, because of the ark of God.' So David went and brought up the ark of God from the house of Obed-edom to the city of David with rejoicing" (6:12). Second attempt—Levites carry properly. "When those who bore the ark of the LORD had gone six steps, he sacrificed an ox and a fatling" (6:13). Careful obedience. "And David danced before the LORD with all his might" (6:14). Joyful uninhibited worship. "In an ephod of linen" (6:14). Priestly garment. "So they brought in the ark of the LORD and set it in its place inside the tent that David had pitched for it" (6:17). Ark enthroned in Jerusalem. Worship center moved from wandering to capital.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Joyful obedience*

#### 6:18-23 — Michal's Contempt
**Lore:** "And David blessed the people in the name of the LORD... And he distributed among all the people... a portion of meat and a cake of bread. Then all the people departed, each to his house" (6:18-19). David serves all Israel. "And when David returned to bless his household, Michal the daughter of Saul came out to meet David and said, 'How the king of Israel honored himself today, uncovering himself today before the eyes of his servants' maids, as one of the vulgar fellows shamelessly uncovers himself!'" (6:20). Michal despises from window. Saul's daughter can't understand humble worship. "And David said to Michal, 'It was before the LORD, who chose me above your father and above all his house, to appoint me as prince over Israel, the people of the LORD. Therefore I will celebrate before the LORD. I will make myself yet more contemptible than this, and I will be abased in your eyes'" (6:21-22). Willing to be "contemptible" if honors God. Worship for God, not human approval. "And Michal the daughter of Saul had no child to the day of her death" (6:23). Barren—Saul's line ends. No dynasty continuation.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Humble worship*

## Chapter 7: The Davidic Covenant

#### 7:1-9 — God Refuses Temple
**Lore:** "Now when the king sat in his house and the LORD had given him rest from all his enemies all around, the king said to Nathan the prophet, 'See now, I dwell in a house of cedar, but the ark of God dwells in a tent'" (7:1-2). David wants to build temple. Nathan approves (7:3). "But that same night the word of the LORD came to Nathan, 'Go and tell my servant David, "Thus says the LORD: Would you build me a house to dwell in?"'" (7:4-5). God says NO—David not builder. "Did I ever ask any of the judges of Israel, whom I commanded to be shepherd of my people Israel, saying, 'Why have you not built me a house of cedar?'" (7:7). Temple not required—relationship is. "Therefore tell my servant David thus: 'The LORD declares to you that the LORD will make you a house'" (7:11). Reversal: David wants to build God's house, **God builds David's house** (dynasty).
*Verses: 9 | Theme: God's promise*

#### 7:10-17 — Eternal Covenant
**Lore:** "'Moreover, the LORD declares to you that the LORD will make you a house. When your days are fulfilled and you lie down with your fathers, I will raise up your offspring after you, who shall come from your body, and I will establish his kingdom. He shall build a house for my name, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever. I will be to him a father, and he shall be to me a son. When he commits iniquity, I will discipline him with the rod of men, with the stripes of the sons of men, but my steadfast love will not depart from him, as I took it from Saul, whom I put away from before you. And your house and your kingdom shall be made sure forever before me. Your throne shall be established forever'" (7:11-16). **UNCONDITIONAL ETERNAL COVENANT**—points to Christ. "Your throne shall be established forever"—ultimate fulfillment in Jesus. Theological center of Samuel corpus.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Eternal kingdom*

#### 7:18-29 — David's Prayer of Acceptance
**Lore:** "Then King David went in and sat before the LORD and said, 'Who am I, O Lord GOD, and what is my house, that you have brought me thus far?'" (7:18). Overwhelmed. "And this was a small thing in your eyes, O Lord GOD, but you have spoken also of your servant's house for a great while to come... Therefore you are great, O LORD God. For there is none like you, and there is no God besides you" (7:19, 22). Repeated: "Your servant" (10x). Prayer of acceptance, not negotiation. "For your word's sake and according to your own heart, you have wrought all this greatness, to make your servant know it. Therefore you are great, O LORD God. For there is none like you" (7:21-22). Worship. "And now, O LORD God, you are God, and your words are true, and you have promised this good thing to your servant" (7:28). "Do as you have spoken" (7:25). David = model of humble submission to God's greater plan.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Humble worship*

**2 SAMUEL 1-7 COMPLETE!**

**Pericopes: 34** (7 chapters = 4.9/chapter average)

**Theme Summary:** David's Triumphs—graceful lament for Saul/Jonathan. Civil war unifies kingdom (God removes obstacles through assassination, not David's hand). Jerusalem conquered, capital established, royal family built. Ark brought with solemn lesson on holiness (Uzzah dies). Climax: Davidic Covenant—God promises eternal dynasty pointing to Christ. David responds with humble worship and prayer. Foundation laid for chapters 8-24.
