# Ecclesiastes Mnemonic Pericope Framework

## Overview

Complete pericope structure created for the book of Ecclesiastes following the EcMP (Ecclesiastes Mnemonic Pericope) "Lore" framework. This structure divides the 12 chapters into 43 memorable pericopes optimized for Scripture memorization.

## Eight EcMP Thematic Units

The 43 pericopes align with 8 major "Lore" units that structure Ecclesiastes:

1. **Unit 1: The Great Thesis** (Ch 1:1-11) - All is Hevel
2. **Unit 2: The Failed Quest** (Ch 1:12-2:26) - Wisdom, pleasure, and wealth fail
3. **Unit 3: Problem of Sovereignty** (Ch 3-4) - Time and oppression under the sun
4. **Unit 4: Problem of Possession** (Ch 5-6) - Riches and religion
5. **Unit 5: Limits of Wisdom** (Ch 7-8) - Search for justice
6. **Unit 6: The Great Equalizer** (Ch 9-10) - Death and carpe diem
7. **Unit 7: Final Counsel** (Ch 11-12:8) - Remember your Creator
8. **Unit 8: The Epilogue** (Ch 12:9-14) - The whole duty of man: Fear God

## Key Themes for Memorization

### The Hevel Thesis
- Hebrew word "hevel" (הֶבֶל) = vapor, breath, meaningless
- Central thesis: Life "under the sun" is hevel
- Repeated throughout the book as a refrain

### The "Carpe Diem" Passages
Three major passages encourage enjoying life as God's gift:
1. **First Carpe Diem** (2:24-26) - "The Gift of God to Enjoy"
2. **Second Carpe Diem** (5:18-20) - "Accept His Lot and Rejoice"
3. **Third Carpe Diem** (9:7-10) - "Eat Your Bread with Joy"

### The "Cannot Find Out" Refrain
God's work is beyond human comprehension:
- 3:11 - "He has made everything beautiful in its time"
- 7:14 - "God has made the one as well as the other"
- 8:17 - "Man cannot find out the work that is done under the sun"
- 11:5 - "You do not know the work of God"

### The Conclusion
**12:13-14** - "Fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole duty of man."

## Design Philosophy

The pericope divisions were crafted to:
- **Capture philosophical themes** - Each unit reflects Qoheleth's argument
- **Create memorable hooks** - Names like "All is Hevel" and "Two Are Better Than One"
- **Balance granularity** - Not too many pericopes (overwhelming) or too few (unhelpful)
- **Align with literary structure** - Follows scholarly division of Ecclesiastes
- **Support memorization** - Thematic coherence aids retention
