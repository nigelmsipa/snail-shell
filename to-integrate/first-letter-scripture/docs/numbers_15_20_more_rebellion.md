# Numbers 15-20: Laws, Rebellions, and Death

## Part VI: Laws and Korah's Revolt (Chapters 15-19)

### Chapter 15: Offerings and Sabbath-Breaker

#### 15:1-21 — Additional Offerings in the Land
**Lore:** "The LORD spoke to Moses, saying, 'Speak to the people of Israel and say to them, When you come into the land you are to inhabit, which I am giving you'" (15:1-2). Despite rebellion (ch 14), God still promises land! Regulations for grain, drink offerings accompanying sacrifices (15:3-12). Same law for native and sojourner (15:13-16). Firstfruits offering: wave cake from first ground meal (15:17-21). Grace amid judgment. God hasn't abandoned His promise. Future-oriented laws sustain hope. Israel WILL enter land—just not this generation.
*Verses: 21 | Theme: Promise remains*

#### 15:22-31 — Unintentional vs. Intentional Sin
**Lore:** Congregation's unintentional sin: bull for burnt offering, goat for sin offering (15:22-26). Individual's unintentional sin: female goat (15:27-29). "But the person who does anything with a high hand (בְּיָד רָמָה, beyad ramah, 'defiantly'), whether he is native or a sojourner, reviles the LORD, and that person shall be cut off from among his people. Because he has despised the word of the LORD and has broken his commandment, that person shall be utterly cut off; his iniquity shall be on him'" (15:30-31). High-handed sin = deliberate, defiant rebellion. No sacrifice for this—only judgment. Hebrews 10:26: "If we go on sinning deliberately...there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins." Intentional defiance cuts off from God.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Defiant sin condemned*

#### 15:32-41 — Sabbath-Breaker Stoned, Tassels Commanded
**Lore:** Man found gathering sticks on Sabbath (15:32-33). Brought to Moses; not clear what to do (15:34). "And the LORD said to Moses, 'The man shall be put to death; all the congregation shall stone him with stones outside the camp'" (15:35). Execute him (15:36). Example of high-handed sin—knowing violation of Sabbath (Ex 20:8-11). Harsh but teaches seriousness. **Tassels commanded** (15:37-41): "The LORD said to Moses, 'Speak to the people of Israel, and tell them to make tassels (צִיצִת, tzitzit) on the corners of their garments throughout their generations, and to put a cord of blue on the tassel of each corner. And it shall be a tassel for you to look at and remember all the commandments of the LORD, to do them, not to follow after your own heart and your own eyes, which you are inclined to whore after. So you shall remember and do all my commandments, and be holy to your God. I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt to be your God: I am the LORD your God'" (15:38-41). Visual reminder of God's law. Blue = royalty, heaven. Jesus wore tassels (Matt 9:20). Fringes prevent "spiritual adultery"—constantly call back to covenant loyalty.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Remember and obey*

### Chapter 16: Korah's Rebellion

#### 16:1-11 — Korah Challenges Priesthood
**Lore:** "Now Korah the son of Izhar, son of Kohath, son of Levi, and Dathan and Abiram the sons of Eliab, and On the son of Peleth, sons of Reuben, took men. And they rose up before Moses, with a number of the people of Israel, 250 chiefs of the congregation, chosen from the assembly, well-known men. They assembled themselves together against Moses and against Aaron and said to them, 'You have gone too far! For all in the congregation are holy, every one of them, and the LORD is among them. Why then do you exalt yourselves above the assembly of the LORD?'" (16:1-3). Korah = Levite, relative of Moses. Claims all Israel equally holy—why Moses/Aaron special? Democratic theology masking ambition. Moses falls on face (16:4). Tomorrow God will show who's chosen (16:5). "Is it too small a thing for you that the God of Israel has separated you from the congregation of Israel, to bring you near to himself, to do service in the tabernacle of the LORD and to stand before the congregation to minister to them, and that he has brought you near him, and all your brothers the sons of Levi with you? And would you seek the priesthood also?" (16:9-10). Korah already has honored role (Levite)—but wants priesthood. Discontent with God's assignments.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Ambition disguised as equality*

#### 16:12-22 — Dathan and Abiram Refuse, Moses Appeals to God
**Lore:** Moses summons Dathan and Abiram (Reubenites); they refuse: "Is it a small thing that you have brought us up out of a land flowing with milk and honey, to kill us in the wilderness, that you must also make yourself a prince over us? Moreover, you have not brought us into a land flowing with milk and honey, nor given us inheritance of fields and vineyards. Will you put out the eyes of these men? We will not come up" (16:12-14). They call Egypt "milk and honey"—total reversal! Accuse Moses of self-appointment. Moses angry, appeals to God: "Do not respect their offering. I have not taken one donkey from them, and I have not harmed one of them" (16:15). Moses's integrity defended. Tells Korah and 250: bring censers with incense tomorrow; God will choose (16:16-17). They gather at tent (16:18-19). "Then the glory of the LORD appeared to all the congregation" (16:19). God tells Moses/Aaron: "Separate yourselves from among this congregation, that I may consume them in a moment" (16:20-21). Moses/Aaron intercede: "O God...shall one man sin, and will you be angry with all the congregation?" (16:22). Plead for innocent.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Innocent protected*

#### 16:23-35 — Earth Swallows Rebels, Fire Consumes 250
**Lore:** God says: tell congregation, move away from Korah, Dathan, Abiram's tents (16:23-24). Moses goes to Dathan/Abiram with elders (16:25). Warns: "Depart...lest you be swept away with all their sins" (16:26). They stand defiantly at tent doors with families (16:27). Moses: "Hereby you shall know that the LORD has sent me to do all these works, and that it has not been of my own accord. If these men die as all men die, or if they are visited by the fate of all mankind, then the LORD has not sent me. But if the LORD creates something new, and the ground opens its mouth and swallows them up with all that belongs to them, and they go down alive into Sheol, then you shall know that these men have despised the LORD" (16:28-30). Test of authenticity. "And as soon as he had finished speaking all these words, the ground under them split apart. And the earth opened its mouth and swallowed them up, with their households and all the people who belonged to Korah and all their goods. So they and all that belonged to them went down alive into Sheol, and the earth closed over them, and they perished from the midst of the assembly" (16:31-33). Terrifying. All Israel flees (16:34). "And fire came out from the LORD and consumed the 250 men offering the incense" (16:35). Double judgment: earth swallows leaders, fire consumes followers. Jude 11 warns: "Woe to them! For...they perished in Korah's rebellion."
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Dramatic judgment*

#### 16:36-50 — Bronze Censers, Plague Stopped
**Lore:** God tells Eleazar (Aaron's son): take bronze censers from fire, scatter coals, hammer censers into plates for altar covering (16:36-38). "To be a reminder to the people of Israel, so that no outsider, who is not of the descendants of Aaron, should draw near to burn incense before the LORD, lest he become like Korah and his company" (16:40). Permanent memorial. Next day: "all the congregation of the people of Israel grumbled against Moses and against Aaron, saying, 'You have killed the people of the LORD'" (16:41). Unbelievable! They blame Moses for God's judgment. Cloud covers tent, glory appears (16:42). God: "Get away from the midst of this congregation, that I may consume them in a moment" (16:44-45). Moses/Aaron fall on faces (16:45). Plague begins killing people (16:46). Moses to Aaron: "Take your censer, and put fire on it from off the altar and lay incense on it and carry it quickly to the congregation and make atonement for them, for wrath has gone out from the LORD; the plague has begun" (16:46). Aaron runs into midst with censer, stands between dead and living, makes atonement, plague stops (16:47-48). Death toll: 14,700 (16:49). Aaron the intercessor—priestly role vindicated. Hebrews 7:25: Christ "always lives to make intercession."
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Priestly intercession*

### Chapter 17: Aaron's Rod Buds

#### 17:1-13 — Sign of God's Choice
**Lore:** "The LORD spoke to Moses, saying, 'Speak to the people of Israel, and get from them staffs, one for each fathers' house, from all their chiefs according to their fathers' houses, twelve staffs. Write each man's name on his staff, and write Aaron's name on the staff of Levi'" (17:1-3). Place staffs in tent overnight (17:4). "And the staff of the man whom I choose shall sprout. Thus I will make to cease from me the grumblings of the people of Israel, which they grumble against you" (17:5). Moses does this (17:6-7). "And on the next day Moses went into the tent of the testimony, and behold, the staff of Aaron for the house of Levi had sprouted and put forth buds and produced blossoms, and it bore ripe almonds" (17:8). Life from dead wood! Clear sign. Other staffs unchanged (17:9). "And the LORD said to Moses, 'Put back the staff of Aaron before the testimony, to be kept as a sign for the rebels, that you may make an end of their grumblings against me, lest they die'" (17:10). Permanent reminder. Hebrews 9:4: rod kept in ark. Almonds = first tree to blossom in spring—resurrection imagery. Aaron chosen by life, not death. Christ our High Priest—resurrected, living forever (Heb 7:24-25). People terrified: "Behold, we perish, we are undone, we are all undone. Everyone who comes near, who comes near to the tabernacle of the LORD, shall die. Are we all to perish?" (17:12-13). They finally grasp: approaching God requires His appointment.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Life from death*
