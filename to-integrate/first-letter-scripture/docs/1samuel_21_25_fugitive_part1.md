# 1 Samuel 21-25: David the Fugitive (Part 1)

## Overview
David's desperate flight—deception at Nob leads to priest massacre. Cave of Adullam—ragamuffin band gathers. Keilah saved but ungrateful. Ziph betrays. Spares Saul twice—"won't harm LORD's anointed." Abigail intercedes—prevents bloodguilt. God strikes fool, provides wise wife. Central theme: trusting God's timing, not forcing way to throne. Learning through wilderness.

---

## Chapter 21: Desperate Deception

#### 21:1-9 — Holy Bread at Nob
**Lore:** "Then David came to Nob, to Ahimelech the priest. And Ahimelech came to meet David trembling and said to him, 'Why are you alone, and no one with you?' And David said to Ahimelech the priest, 'The king has charged me with a matter and said to me, "Let no one know anything"'" (21:1-2). **DAVID LIES**—claims secret mission. Deception born of fear. "Now then, what do you have on hand? Give me five loaves of bread' And the priest answered David, 'I have no common bread on hand, but there is holy bread—if the young men have kept themselves from women'" (21:3-4). Bread of the Presence (showbread). "So the priest gave him the holy bread" (21:6). Emergency overrides ritual. Jesus cites this (Matt 12:3-4). "Now a certain man of the servants of Saul was there that day, detained before the LORD. His name was Doeg the Edomite" (21:7). **DOEG**—witness who will betray! "And the priest said, 'The sword of Goliath the Philistine, whom you struck down in the Valley of Elah, behold, it is here... And David said, 'There is none like that; give it to me'" (21:9). Goliath's sword—trophy becomes weapon. Low point for David.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Fear-driven lies*

#### 21:10-15 — Feigned Madness
**Lore:** "And David rose and fled that day from Saul and went to Achish the king of Gath" (21:10). Flees to enemy—desperate! "And the servants of Achish said to him, 'Is not this David the king of the land? Did they not sing to one another of him in dances, "Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his ten thousands"?'" (21:11). Recognized! "So he changed his behavior before them and pretended to be insane in their hands and made marks on the doors of the gate and let his spittle run down his beard" (21:13). Feigns madness—drools, scratches. Strategy works—dismissed as harmless (21:14-15). Deception and lack of trust. These actions have catastrophic consequences (priest massacre, ch 22). Psalm 34, 56 reference this episode—regret/repentance.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Desperate measures*

## Chapter 22: The Massacre

#### 22:1-5 — Cave of Adullam
**Lore:** "David departed from there and escaped to the cave of Adullam. And when his brothers and all his father's house heard it, they went down there to him. And everyone who was in distress, and everyone who was in debt, and everyone who was bitter in soul, gathered to him. And he became commander over them. And there were with him about four hundred men" (22:1-2). **CAVE OF ADULLAM**—first "army": distressed, indebted, discontented. Ragamuffin band. From royal court to outlaw. "And David went from there to Mizpeh of Moab. And he said to the king of Moab, 'Please let my father and my mother stay with you, till I know what God will do for me'" (22:3). Protects parents in Moab (Ruth connection).
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Outlaw leader*

#### 22:6-23 — Nob's Destruction
**Lore:** Saul's paranoid rant: "Then Saul heard that David was discovered... 'will the son of Jesse give every one of you fields and vineyards... that all of you have conspired against me?'" (22:6-8). "Then answered Doeg the Edomite... 'I saw the son of Jesse coming to Nob, to Ahimelech... and he inquired of the LORD for him and gave him provisions and gave him the sword of Goliath'" (22:9-10). Doeg reports—betrays priest. "Then the king sent to summon Ahimelech the priest... and all his father's house, the priests who were at Nob" (22:11). Accuses treason (22:12-13). "Then Ahimelech answered the king, 'And who among all your servants is so faithful as David... captain over your bodyguard... Is today the first time that I have inquired of God for him? No!'" (22:14-15). Logical defense. "And the king said, 'You shall surely die, Ahimelech, you and all your father's house.' And the king said to the guard... 'Turn and kill the priests of the LORD'... But the servants of the king would not put out their hand to strike the priests of the LORD" (22:16-17). Guards refuse! "Then the king said to Doeg, 'You turn and strike the priests.' And Doeg the Edomite turned and struck down the priests, and he killed on that day eighty-five persons who wore the linen ephod. And Nob, the city of the priests, he put to the sword; both man and woman, child and infant, ox, donkey and sheep" (22:18-19). **MASSACRE**—85 priests + entire city! Horrific. "But one of the sons of Ahimelech... named Abiathar, escaped and fled after David. And David said to Abiathar, 'I knew on that day, when Doeg the Edomite was there, that he would surely tell Saul. I have occasioned the death of all the persons of your father's house'" (22:20-22). David accepts responsibility. Guilt over deception at Nob. Nadir of Saul's reign—massacring God's priests = unforgivable.
*Verses: 18 | Theme: Paranoia breeds atrocity*

## Chapter 23: Betrayals

#### 23:1-13 — Keilah's Ingratitude
**Lore:** "Now they told David, 'Behold, the Philistines are fighting against Keilah and are robbing the threshing floors.' Therefore David inquired of the LORD, 'Shall I go and attack these Philistines?' And the LORD said to David, 'Go and attack the Philistines and save Keilah'" (23:1-2). Opportunity to serve. "And David and his men went to Keilah and fought with the Philistines and brought away their livestock and struck them with a great blow. So David saved the inhabitants of Keilah" (23:5). Rescues city! "Now it was told Saul that David had come to Keilah. And Saul said, 'God has given him into my hand, for he has shut himself in by entering a town that has gates and bars'" (23:7). Saul sees opportunity. "David knew that Saul was plotting harm against him. And he said to Abiathar the priest, 'Bring the ephod here.' Then David said, 'O LORD, the God of Israel... Will the men of Keilah surrender me into his hand? Will Saul come down?' And the LORD said, 'He will come down.' Then David said, 'Will the men of Keilah surrender me and my men into the hand of Saul?' And the LORD said, 'They will surrender you'" (23:9-12). Divine revelation—city he saved would betray him! "Ungrateful." "Then David and his men, who were about six hundred, arose and departed from Keilah... When Saul was told that David had escaped from Keilah, he gave up the expedition" (23:13). Flees in time—600 men now.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Ungrateful betrayal*

#### 23:14-29 — Ziph's Betrayal
**Lore:** "And David remained in the strongholds in the wilderness... And Saul sought him every day, but God did not give him into his hand" (23:14). Hunted daily—God protects. "And Jonathan, Saul's son, rose and went to David at Horesh, and strengthened his hand in God. And he said to him, 'Do not fear, for the hand of Saul my father shall not find you. You shall be king over Israel, and I shall be next to you. Saul my father also knows this'" (23:16-17). **JONATHAN'S VISIT**—encourages! Affirms kingship, offers to serve under him. "And the two of them made a covenant before the LORD" (23:18). Last meeting—Jonathan dies before David becomes king. "Then the Ziphites went up to Saul at Gibeah, saying, 'Is not David hiding among us in the strongholds at Horesh?'" (23:19). **ZIPHITES BETRAY**—own tribe! "And Saul said, 'May you be blessed by the LORD, for you have had compassion on me'" (23:21). Twisted gratitude. Saul closes in (23:24-26). "As Saul and his men were closing in on David and his men to capture them, a messenger came to Saul, saying, 'Hurry and come, for the Philistines have made a raid against the land.' So Saul returned from pursuing after David" (23:26-28). **LAST-MINUTE ESCAPE**—providence! God's invisible hand. "Therefore that place was called the Rock of Escape" (23:28). Fugitive life—serves Israel, yet betrayed.
*Verses: 16 | Theme: Divine deliverance*

## Chapter 24: First Sparing

#### 24:1-15 — Conscience in the Cave
**Lore:** "When Saul returned from following the Philistines, he was told, 'Behold, David is in the wilderness of En-gedi.' Then Saul took three thousand chosen men out of all Israel and went to seek David and his men in front of the Wildgoats' Rocks" (24:1-2). Pursuit resumes—3,000 vs. 600. "And he came to the sheepfolds by the way, where there was a cave, and Saul went in to relieve himself. Now David and his men were sitting in the innermost parts of the cave" (24:3). **INCREDIBLE PROVIDENCE**—Saul enters exact cave! "And the men of David said to him, 'Here is the day of which the LORD said to you, "Behold, I will give your enemy into your hand, and you shall do to him as it shall seem good to you."' Then David arose and stealthily cut off a corner of Saul's robe" (24:4). Men see it as God delivering enemy. David cuts robe corner. "And afterward David's heart struck him, because he had cut off a corner of Saul's robe. He said to his men, 'The LORD forbid that I should do this thing to my lord, the LORD's anointed, to put out my hand against him, seeing he is the LORD's anointed'" (24:5-6). **CONSCIENCE SMITTEN**—even cutting robe = wrong! "So David persuaded his men with these words and did not permit them to attack Saul" (24:7). "Afterward David also arose and went out of the cave, and called after Saul, 'My lord the king!' And when Saul looked behind him, David bowed with his face to the earth and paid homage" (24:8). Respect—bows! "See, my father, see the corner of your robe in my hand. For by the fact that I cut off the corner of your robe and did not kill you, you may know and see that there is no wrong or treason in my hands... May the LORD judge between me and you... but my hand shall not be against you" (24:11-12). **PROOF**—could have killed, chose not to. Appeals to divine judgment. "After a dead dog! After a flea!" (24:14). Self-deprecating.
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Sparing anointed*

#### 24:16-22 — Saul's Temporary Repentance
**Lore:** "As soon as David had finished speaking these words to Saul, Saul said, 'Is this your voice, my son David?' And Saul lifted up his voice and wept. He said to David, 'You are more righteous than I, for you have repaid me good, whereas I have repaid you evil... And now, behold, I know that you shall surely be king, and that the kingdom of Israel shall be established in your hand. Swear to me therefore by the LORD that you will not cut off my offspring after me, and that you will not destroy my name out of my father's house.' And David swore this to Saul" (24:16-22). Saul acknowledges David's righteousness, admits David will be king. Seeks protection for descendants. David swears (keeps—spares Mephibosheth, 2 Sam 9). But doesn't trust Saul—returns to stronghold. David's character: conscientiously serving God, won't stretch forth hand against LORD's anointed. Type of Christ who didn't retaliate. Key principle: respect for God's authority even when leaders fail.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Waiting on God*

## Chapter 25: Wisdom Prevents Sin

#### 25:1-13 — Nabal's Insult
**Lore:** "Now Samuel died. And all Israel assembled and mourned for him, and they buried him in his house at Ramah" (25:1). **SAMUEL DIES**—end of era. "And there was a man in Maon whose business was in Carmel. The man was very rich... Now the name of the man was Nabal, and the name of his wife Abigail. The woman was discerning and beautiful, but the man was harsh and badly behaved" (25:2-3). **NABAL** (נָבָל) = "fool." **ABIGAIL** (אֲבִיגַיִל) = "my father's joy." Opposite of husband. David sends polite request for provisions during shearing (25:4-8). "And Nabal answered David's servants, 'Who is David? Who is the son of Jesse? There are many servants these days who are breaking away from their masters'" (25:10-11). **INSULT**—treats David as runaway slave! "So David's young men turned away and came back and told him all this. And David said to his men, 'Every man strap on his sword!' And every man of them strapped on his sword. David also strapped on his sword. And about four hundred men went up after David" (25:12-13). David enraged—prepares for vengeance!
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Foolish insult*

#### 25:14-35 — Abigail's Intercession
**Lore:** Servant warns Abigail: "Behold, David sent messengers out of the wilderness to greet our master, and he railed at them. Yet the men were very good to us... They were a wall to us... Now therefore know this and consider what you should do, for harm is determined against our master... and he is such a worthless man that one cannot speak to him" (25:14-17). "Then Abigail made haste and took two hundred loaves and two skins of wine and five sheep already prepared and five seahs of parched grain and a hundred clusters of raisins and two hundred cakes of figs, and laid them on donkeys" (25:18). Quick action—generous provisions! "When Abigail saw David, she hurried and got down from the donkey and fell before David on her face and bowed to the ground. She fell at his feet and said, 'On me alone, my lord, be the guilt... Let not my lord regard this worthless fellow, Nabal, for as his name is, so is he. Nabal is his name, and folly is with him'" (25:23-25). Humble appeal. "Now then, my lord, as the LORD lives, and as your soul lives, because the LORD has restrained you from bloodguilt and from saving with your own hand... For the LORD will certainly make my lord a sure house, because my lord is fighting the battles of the LORD, and evil shall not be found in you... And when the LORD has done to my lord according to all the good that he has spoken concerning you and has appointed you prince over Israel, my lord shall have no cause of grief or pangs of conscience for having shed blood without cause'" (25:26-31). **KEY**—would regret unnecessary bloodshed when becomes king! Prophetic words. "And David said to Abigail, 'Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel, who sent you this day to meet me! Blessed be your discretion, and blessed be you, who have kept me this day from bloodguilt'" (25:32-33). David recognizes divine intervention!
*Verses: 22 | Theme: Wisdom intercedes*

#### 25:36-44 — God Strikes Fool
**Lore:** "And Abigail came to Nabal, and behold, he was holding a feast in his house, like the feast of a king. And Nabal's heart was merry within him, for he was very drunk" (25:36). Oblivious! "And in the morning, when the wine had gone out of Nabal, his wife told him these things, and his heart died within him, and he became as a stone. And about ten days later the LORD struck Nabal, and he died" (25:37-38). **GOD STRIKES NABAL**—heart attack. God will set matters right! "When David heard that Nabal was dead, he said, 'Blessed be the LORD who has avenged the insult I received at the hand of Nabal, and has kept back his servant from wrongdoing. The LORD has returned the evil of Nabal on his own head.' Then David sent and spoke to Abigail, to take her as his wife" (25:39). **DAVID MARRIES ABIGAIL**—wise woman! "Illustration of womanhood after the order of Christ." "David also took Ahinoam of Jezreel, and both of them became his wives. Saul had given Michal his daughter, David's wife, to Palti" (25:43-44). Polygamy begins—causes problems later. Teaches: Abigail = wisdom preventing sin, vengeance belongs to God, wise counsel saves from regret, God removes obstacles in His timing.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Divine vengeance*

**1 SAMUEL 21-25 COMPLETE!**

**Pericopes: 15** (5 chapters = 3/chapter)

**Theme Summary:** David's fugitive life—deception at Nob leads to priest massacre. Ragamuffin band at Adullam. Betrayed by those he rescues (Keilah) and own tribe (Ziph). Spares Saul in cave—won't harm LORD's anointed. Abigail intercedes—prevents bloodguilt, wisdom saves from sin. God strikes fool, provides wise wife. Pattern: David's mistakes, God's protection, waiting for God's timing rather than forcing way to throne.
