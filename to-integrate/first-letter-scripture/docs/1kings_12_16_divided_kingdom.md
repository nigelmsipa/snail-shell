# 1 Kings 12-16: The Divided Kingdom - The Great Schism

## Overview
The kingdom's rupture is immediate and devastating. Rehoboam's folly in rejecting the elders' counsel causes the northern tribes to rebel. Jeroboam, prophetically anointed (11:29-35), becomes king of the North (Israel, 10 tribes) while Rehoboam retains only the South (Judah + Benjamin, 2 tribes). This fracture defines the rest of biblical history. The framework traces: (1) **Kingdom Torn** (schism and false worship), (2) **Prophet's Test** (obedience lesson), (3) **First Generation's End** (consequences realized), (4) **Good King of Judah** (Asa's reform contrasts with northern chaos), (5) **Northern Spiral** (rapid political collapse, Ahab's evil). Key terms: מַלְכוּת (malkhuth = kingdom), שׁקֶר (sheker = falsehood), עֲבוֹדַת־אֱלִילִים (abodat-elilim = idolatry), תּוֹרָה (torah = law).

## Chapter 12: The Kingdom Torn Asunder

#### 12:1-15 — Rehoboam's Folly
**Lore:** "And Rehoboam went to Shechem, for all Israel had come to Shechem to make him king" (12:1). **Shechem** = ancient tribal center, neutral ground. All Israel gathers to confirm Rehoboam as successor (standard practice). "And when Jeroboam the son of Nebat heard of it (for he was still in Egypt, whither he had fled from the presence of King Solomon), Jeroboam returned from Egypt. And they sent and called him, and Jeroboam and all the assembly of Israel came and said to Rehoboam, 'Your father made our yoke heavy. Now therefore lighten the hard service of your father and his heavy yoke upon us, and we will serve you'" (12:2-4). **Legitimate grievance**: Solomon's **corvée labor** (forced work, 9:15) burdened northerners. Jeroboam returns (from Egypt where he fled 11:40) and becomes spokesperson. Request is reasonable—ease the burden. "And he said to them, 'Depart for three days, then come again to me.' And the people departed" (12:5). Rehoboam asks time to consider. "And King Rehoboam took counsel with the old men, who had stood before Solomon his father while he yet lived, saying, 'How do you advise me to answer this people?' And they said to him, 'If you will be a servant to this people today and serve them, and speak good words to them, then they will be your servants for ever'" (12:6-7). **Old men's wisdom** (זְקֵנִים, zekanim): servant-leadership. "Speak good words" = show compassion. Yet Rehoboam rejects them: "But he forsook the counsel of the old men which they had given him, and took counsel with the young men that had grown up with him and stood before him" (12:8). **Fatal decision**: youth over experience, arrogance over wisdom. "And he said to them, 'My little finger is thicker than my father's loins. And whereas my father did lade you with a heavy yoke, I will add to your yoke: my father chastised you with whips, but I will chastise you with scorpions'" (12:10-11). **Scorpions** (עַקְרַבִּים, akrabim) = whips with knots/spikes, causing worse pain. Arrogant threat! "So Jeroboam and all the people came to Rehoboam the third day, as the king had appointed. And the king answered the people roughly, and forsaking the counsel of the old men which they had given him, he spoke to them after the counsel of the young men, saying, 'My father made your yoke heavy, but I will add to your yoke; my father chastised you with whips, but I will chastise you with scorpions'" (12:12-14). **Public humiliation of rebels**—Rehoboam's foolish words confirmed. "Wherefore the king hearkened not unto the people; for the cause was from the LORD, that he might perform his saying, which the LORD spoke by Ahijah the Shilonite unto Jeroboam the son of Nebat" (12:15). **Ironic reversal**: Rehoboam's folly is **divinely orchestrated** to fulfill the prophecy (11:29-35). God uses human sin to accomplish His purpose.
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Foolish leadership causes division*

#### 12:16-24 — The Split Confirmed
**Lore:** "So when all Israel saw that the king hearkened not unto them, the people answered the king, saying, 'What portion have we in David? neither have we inheritance in the son of Jesse: to your tents, O Israel: now see to thine own house, David'" (12:16). **Rejection of dynasty**—not just political split, but **covenantal rejection**. "To your tents" = military assembly cry (Deut 20:8). "So Israel departed unto their tents... And the children of Israel that dwelt in the cities of Judah, Rehoboam reigned over them" (12:16-17). **Clean split**: Rehoboam keeps only Judah proper (and Benjamin). "Then King Rehoboam sent Adoram, who was taskmaster over the tribute... And all Israel stoned him with stones, that he died. Therefore King Rehoboam made speed to get him up to his chariot, to flee to Jerusalem" (12:18). **Adoram** (the forced labor officer) murdered by the rebels. Rehoboam flees—no military response possible. "So Israel rebelled against the house of David unto this day" (12:19). **"Unto this day"** = narrator's perspective (later after split). "And when Rehoboam was come to Jerusalem, he assembled all the house of Judah and the tribe of Benjamin, an hundred and fourscore thousand chosen men, which were warriors, to fight against the house of Israel, to bring the kingdom again to Rehoboam the son of Solomon" (12:21). **180,000 warriors** ready to wage civil war! Yet: "But the word of the LORD came unto Shemaiah the man of God, saying, 'Speak unto Rehoboam the son of Solomon, king of Judah, and unto all the house of Judah and Benjamin, and to the remnant of the people, saying, Thus saith the LORD, Ye shall not go up, nor fight against your brethren the children of Israel: return every man to his house; for this thing is from me'" (12:22-24). **Prophetic restraint**—God forbids civil war! "It is from me" = divinely ordained split. Rehoboam obeys: "And they hearkened unto the word of the LORD, and returned from going against Jeroboam" (12:24). Potential war averted. **Division is clean, final, accepted as God's will**.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Civil split as divine judgment*

#### 12:25-33 — Jeroboam's "Original Sin"
**Lore:** "And Jeroboam built Shechem in mount Ephraim, and dwelt therein; and went out from thence, and built Penuel" (12:25). Jeroboam fortifies twin cities as capitals. "And Jeroboam said in his heart, 'Now shall the kingdom return to the house of David: If this people go up to do sacrifice in the house of the LORD at Jerusalem, then shall the heart of this people turn again unto their lord, even unto Rehoboam king of Judah, and they will kill me, and go again to Rehoboam king of Judah'" (12:26-27). **Political anxiety**—fears religious pilgrimage = political unification. Smart calculation. "Whereupon the king took counsel, and made two calves of gold, and said unto them, 'It is too much for you to go up to Jerusalem: behold thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt'" (12:28). **GOLDEN CALVES**—"gods which brought thee up" echoes Exodus 32 (Aaron's calf). **False worship system** (צִדַּת עֲבוֹדָה, tsiddat avodah) invented to replace Temple worship. **This is Israel's fundamental sin** throughout its history. "And he set the one in Beth-el, and the other put he in Dan" (12:29). Two sanctuaries—North and South of kingdom—so worship distributed geographically. "And this thing became a sin: for the people went to worship before the one, even unto Dan" (12:30). **"This thing became a sin"** (וַתְּהִי־זֹאת־לַחַטָּאת) = **original sin of the Northern Kingdom**. Jeroboam's political calculation **becomes theological apostasy**. Every subsequent northern king "walked in the way of Jeroboam" (inherited sin, 15:34, 16:2, etc.). "Also he made houses of high places, and made priests of the lowest of the people, which were not of the sons of Levi" (12:31). **Unauthorized priesthood** (non-Levites)—violates Mosaic law. "And Jeroboam ordained a feast in the eighth month, on the fifteenth day of the month, like unto the feast that is in Judah... And he offered upon the altar" (12:32-33). **Unauthorized feast** (not Passover, Pentecost, or Tabernacles proper)—Jeroboam creates parallel worship calendar. Everything about the northern worship is **counterfeit/false** (שׁקֶר, sheker).
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Political calculation becomes religious apostasy*

## Chapter 13: The Prophet's Test

#### 13:1-10 — The Man of God's Fearless Rebuke
**Lore:** "And, behold, there came a man of God out of Judah by the word of the LORD unto Beth-el... And he cried against the altar in the word of the LORD... And he said, 'O altar, altar, thus saith the LORD; Behold, a child shall be born unto the house of David, Phineas by name; and upon thee shall he offer the priests of the high places that burn incense upon thee; and men's bones shall be burned upon thee'" (13:1-2). **Anonymous "Man of God"** from Judah appears at Beth-el altar. **Prophecy**: A future king (Josiah, 2 Kings 23:15-16) will desecrate this altar by burning bones on it—143 years later! Extraordinary specific prophecy. **Sign**: "And he gave a sign the same day, saying, 'This is the sign which the LORD hath spoken; Behold, the altar shall be rent, and the ashes that are upon it shall be poured out'" (13:3). "And it came to pass, when king Jeroboam heard the saying of the man of God... that he put forth his hand from the altar, saying, 'Lay hold on him!' And his hand, which he put forth against him, dried up, so that he could not pull it in again to him" (13:4). **Jeroboam's hand withers instantly**—God's power demonstrated. "And the altar also was rent, and the ashes poured out from the altar, according to the sign which the man of God had given by the word of the LORD" (13:5). **Sign fulfilled immediately**. Fear grips Jeroboam: "And the king said unto the man of God, 'Intreat now the face of the LORD thy God, and pray for me, that my hand may be restored me.' And the man of God besought the LORD, and the king's hand was restored him again, and became as it was before" (13:6). Jeroboam asks for intercessory prayer. Man of God prays, hand healed. "And the king said unto the man of God, 'Come home with me, and refresh thyself, and I will give thee a reward'" (13:7). Jeroboam invites him to rest and eat. Yet: "And the man of God said unto the king, 'If thou wilt give me half thine house, I will not go with thee, neither will I eat bread nor drink water in this place: For so was it charged me by the word of the LORD, saying, Eat no bread, nor drink water, nor turn again by the same way that thou camest'" (13:8-9). **Divine command explicitly stated**: Don't eat, don't drink, don't return same way. Man of God obeys! **Fearless**: rebuked king, rejected hospitality, followed strict command. "So he went another way, and returned not by the way that he came to Beth-el" (13:10). Obedience complete. **Lesson setup**: Man of God = model of obedience to God's direct command.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Fearless obedience*

#### 13:11-25 — The Old Prophet's Deception
**Lore:** "Now there dwelt an old prophet in Beth-el... And his sons came and told him all the works that the man of God had done that day in Beth-el... And their father said unto them, 'What way went he?' For his sons had seen what way the man of God went, which came from Judah" (13:11-12). Old prophet hears report. "And he said unto his sons, 'Saddle me the ass.' So they saddled him the ass... And he went after the man of God, and found him sitting under an oak'" (13:13-14). Old prophet pursues. "And he said unto him, 'Art thou the man of God that camest from Judah?' And he said, 'I am'" (13:14). Confirmed. "Then he said unto him, 'Come home with me, and eat bread.' And he said, 'I may not return with thee, nor go in with thee: neither will I eat bread nor drink water with thee in this place: For it was said to me by the word of the LORD, Thou shalt eat no bread nor drink water there, nor turn again to go by the way that thou camest'" (13:16-17). Man of God reiterates the divine command. **Crucially, he resists once.** Yet: "He said unto him, 'I am a prophet also as thou art; and an angel spake unto me by the word of the LORD, saying, Bring him back with thee into thine house, that he may eat bread and drink water.' But he lied unto him" (13:18). **Old prophet LIES**—claims angel spoke! Makes false claim to prophetic authority. "So he went back with him, and did eat bread in his house, and drank water" (13:19). Man of God **allows himself to be over-persuaded**. **Failed test**: didn't maintain obedience when pressured by false prophetic claim. "And it came to pass, as they sat at the table, that the word of the LORD came unto the prophet that brought him back... And he cried unto the man of God that came from Judah, saying, 'Thus saith the LORD, Forasmuch as thou hast disobeyed the mouth of the LORD, and hast not kept the commandment which the LORD thy God commanded thee, but camest back, and hast eaten bread and drunk water in the place, of which the LORD did say to thee, Eat no bread and drink no water; thy carcase shall not come unto the sepulchre of thy fathers'" (13:20-22). **True prophet (old prophet) now delivers judgment** to false-obedient Man of God. "And it came to pass, after he had eaten bread, and after he had drunk, that he saddled for him the ass, to wit, for the prophet whom he had brought back. And when he was gone, a lion met him by the way, and slew him" (13:23-24). **Lion kills him**—divine judgment. "And he laid his carcase in the way, and the ass stood by it, the lion also stood by the carcase" (13:24). Body left on road with lion standing guard. **Grotesque sign** of God's judgment.
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Obedience cannot be compromised*

#### 13:26-34 — The Lesson Applied
**Lore:** "And when the prophet that brought him back heard thereof, he said, 'It is the man of God, who was disobedient unto the word of the LORD... Therefore the LORD hath delivered him unto the lion, which hath torn him, and slain him, according to the word of the LORD which he spake unto him'" (13:26). Old prophet recognizes: **judgment is for disobedience**. "And he spake to his sons, saying, 'Saddle me the ass.' And they saddled him. And he went and found his carcase cast in the way, and the ass and the lion standing by the carcase: the lion had not eaten the carcase, nor torn the ass. And the prophet took up the carcase of the man of God, and laid it upon the ass, and brought it back" (13:27-29). Old prophet retrieves body, buries it in his own sepulcher. "And he laid his carcase in his own grave; and they mourned over him, saying, 'Alas, my brother!'" (13:30). Mourning, yet the message stands. "And after he had buried him, he spake to his sons, saying, 'When I am dead, then bury me in the sepulchre wherein the man of God is buried; lay my bones beside his bones'" (13:31). Old prophet wants to be buried with Man of God—spiritual kinship despite the confrontation. **Theological lesson**: "For the saying which he cried by the word of the LORD against the altar in Beth-el, and against all the houses of the high places which are in the cities of Samaria, shall surely come to pass" (13:32). The prophecy stands—Josiah will come. Yet: "After this thing Jeroboam returned not from his evil way, but again made of the lowest of the people priests of the high places: whosoever would, he consecrated him, and he became one of the priests of the high places. And this thing became sin unto the house of Jeroboam, even to cut it off, and to destroy it from off the face of the earth" (13:33-34). **Jeroboam continued in apostasy**—God's word didn't move him. His "sin" (חַטָּאת) = idolatry, unauthorized priests, will result in **complete destruction of his house** (fulfilled 15:29). **Core truth**: When the Lord gives a man a command (צִוָּה, tzivah), He Himself must countermand it—no false prophet can override God's direct command.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Lesson taught and applied*

## Chapter 14: The First Generation's End

#### 14:1-20 — Jeroboam's House Judged
**Lore:** "At that time Abijah the son of Jeroboam fell sick... And Jeroboam said to his wife, 'Up, I pray thee, and disguise thyself, that thou be not known to be the wife of Jeroboam; and get thee to Shiloh: behold, there is Ahijah the prophet, which told me that I should be king over this people'" (14:1-2). Jeroboam's son Abijah (אֲבִיָּה) falls ill. Jeroboam remembers Ahijah (the prophet who anointed him in 11:29-35). He asks his wife to disguise herself and consult Ahijah. "And Ahijah could not see; for his eyes were set by reason of his age... But the LORD said unto Ahijah, 'Behold, the wife of Jeroboam cometh to ask a thing of thee... Thus and thus shalt thou say unto her: for it shall be, when she cometh in, that she shall feign herself to be another woman'" (14:4). God tells Ahijah (who is blind!) the truth. "And it was so, when Ahijah heard the sound of her feet... that he said, 'Come in, thou wife of Jeroboam... I am sent to thee with heavy tidings'" (14:5-6). Ahijah **sees spiritually** though physically blind. "For thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Forasmuch as I exalted thee from among the people, and made thee prince over my people Israel, and rent the kingdom away from the house of David, and gave it thee; and yet thou hast not been as my servant David, who kept my commandments, and who followed me with all his heart, to do that only which is right in mine eyes; but hast done evil above all that were before thee: for thou hast gone and made thee other gods... and hast cast me behind thy back" (14:7-9). **Severe indictment**: Despite being raised up and given the kingdom, Jeroboam **did evil above all before him** and **cast God behind his back**. "Therefore, behold, I will bring evil upon the house of Jeroboam, and will cut off from Jeroboam him that micturates against the wall, both him that is shut up and him that is left in Israel... And he that dieth of Jeroboam in the city shall the dogs eat; and him that dieth in the field shall the fowls of the air eat: for the LORD hath spoken it" (14:10-11). **Graphic judgment**: none will escape, bodies unburied (shameful). "But concerning thee, even thy son Abijah... he shall come to the grave; for in him there is found some good thing toward the LORD God of Israel in the house of Jeroboam" (14:12-13). **Mercy**: only Abijah has "good thing" (טוֹב, tov) toward God—he alone will die peacefully, be properly buried. "Moreover the LORD shall raise him up a king over Israel, who shall cut off the house of Jeroboam that day: but what? even now. For the LORD shall smite Israel, as a reed is shaken in the water, and he shall root up Israel out of this good land which he gave to their fathers, and shall scatter them beyond the river, because they have made their groves, provoking the LORD to anger" (14:14-15). **Immediate prophecy**: Another king will rise and **completely destroy Jeroboam's house** (fulfilled 15:29). Yet more: **Israel itself will be scattered beyond river** (Euphrates)—exile! This is prophecy of Assyrian captivity (~722 BC). "And he shall give Israel up because of the sins of Jeroboam, who did sin, and who made Israel to sin" (14:16). Jeroboam's sin contaminates entire nation. "And Jeroboam's wife arose, and departed, and came to Tirzah: and when she came to the threshold of the house, the child died. And they buried him; and all Israel mourned for him, according to the word of the LORD, which he spake by his servant Ahijah the prophet" (14:17-18). Abijah dies, receives proper burial (mercy toward the good).
*Verses: 20 | Theme: Judgment on apostasy*

#### 14:21-31 — Rehoboam's Reign and Decline
**Lore:** "And Rehoboam the son of Solomon reigned in Judah... And he was forty and one years old when he began to reign, and he reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem... And his mother's name was Naamah an Ammonitess... And Judah did evil in the sight of the LORD, and they provoked him to jealousy with their sins which they had committed, above all that their fathers had done" (14:21-22). **Rehoboam's failure**: "Judah did evil" — unlike David who walked faithfully (11:4), Rehoboam led the people to apostasy. "For they also built them high places, and images, and groves, on every high hill, and under every green tree" (14:23). **Unauthorized worship** — high places (במות, bamot), pagan images, sacred poles (Asherah groves). "And there were also sodomites in the land: and they did according to all the abominations of the nations which the LORD cast out before the children of Israel" (14:24). **Sexual idolatry** — sodomites in temples. Despicable sins. "And it came to pass in the fifth year of king Rehoboam, that Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem... And he took the fenced cities which pertained to Judah, and came to Jerusalem" (14:25-26). **Shishak** (Egyptian Pharaoh Shoshenq I) invaded Judah. Rehoboam had built 15 fortified cities (11:5-12 LXX), now lost. "And he took away the treasures of the house of the LORD, and the treasures of the king's house; he took away also the shields of gold which Solomon had made" (14:26). **Temple treasures plundered** — the golden shields from 10:16 taken. "And king Rehoboam made in their stead brasen shields, and committed them to the hands of the chief of the guard, which kept the door of the king's house" (14:27). Rehoboam replaced them with brass shields—foreshadowing: gold (eternal) → brass (judgment). "And when he went into the house of the LORD, the guard bare them, and brought them back into the guard chamber" (14:28). Ceremonial humiliation. "And the rest of the acts of Rehoboam... are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah? And there was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam continually. And Rehoboam slept with his fathers, and was buried in the city of David" (14:29-31). Rehoboam dies after 17 years—continuous warfare. **First generation of divided kingdom ends**: Both founders (Jeroboam and Rehoboam) judged, both dead, both had sons. The schism's consequences manifest.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Rehoboam's failure and invasion*

## Chapter 15: The Good King of Judah and the Northern Chaos

#### 15:1-8 — Abijam's Evil
**Lore:** "Now in the eighteenth year of king Jeroboam the son of Nebat began Abijam to reign over Judah... Three years reigned he in Jerusalem: and his mother's name was Maachah... And he walked in all the sins of his father Asa had done before him: and his heart was not perfect with the LORD his God, as the heart of David his father" (15:1-3). **Abijam** (אֲבִיָּם) = "father is sea" or variant "Abijah" in chronicles. Reigned only 3 years. "His heart was not perfect" — contrast to David. "Nevertheless for David's sake did the LORD his God give him a lamp in Jerusalem, to set up his son after him, and to establish Jerusalem: Because David did that which was right in the eyes of the LORD, and turned not aside from any thing that he commanded him all the days of his life, save only in the matter of Uriah the Hittite" (15:4-5). **Covenantal grace**: David's **one major sin** (Uriah, 2 Samuel 11) is noted, yet **David fundamentally kept God's commandments**. Grace toward David shields his dynasty. "And there was war between Abijam and Jeroboam... And Abijam slept with his fathers" (15:7-8). Brief reign, constant warfare, death at young age.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Wickedness and covenantal protection*

#### 15:9-24 — Asa's Reform: The Good King
**Lore:** "And in the twentieth year of Jeroboam king of Israel began Asa to reign over Judah... And he reigned forty and one years in Jerusalem... And his mother's name was Maachah... And Asa did that which was right in the eyes of the LORD, as did David his father" (15:9-11). **ASA** (אָסָא) — "healer" — **first good king after split**! Reigned 41 years (longest so far). "Asa did right as David his father" — echoes David's faithful pattern. "For he took away the altars of the strange gods, and the high places, and brake down the images, and cut down the groves" (15:12). **Reform**: removed idolatrous altars, high places, images, Asherah poles. "And also he removed his mother Maachah from being queen, because she had made an idol in a grove; and Asa destroyed her idol, and burnt it by the brook Kidron" (15:13). **Asa removed his own mother** (מַלְכָּה, malkah) from office because of idolatry! Demonstrates **principle over family**. "Though the high places were not removed, nevertheless Asa's heart was perfect with the LORD all his days" (15:14). **Caveat**: high places remained (worship sites outside Temple), yet **Asa's heart toward God was fully devoted**. "And he brought into the house of the LORD the things which his father had dedicated, and the things which himself had dedicated, silver, and gold, and vessels" (15:15). Dedicated treasure to Temple. "And there was no more war until the thirty and fifth year of the reign of Asa" (15:16). **35 years of peace** under Asa's faithful reign! Contrast to northern constant warfare. Later: "And Baasha king of Israel went up against Judah, and built Ramah, to prevent any from going out or coming in to Asa king of Judah" (15:17). Even late in life, threats arise. Asa appeals to Ben-hadad of Syria with treasure to attack Baasha (15:18-19). Pragmatic diplomacy. "And Asa took all the silver and the gold that were left in the treasures of the house of the LORD... and sent them to Ben-hadad... And Ben-hadad hearkened unto king Asa" (15:15,19). Syrian attack forces Baasha to cease threat. "And Asa built with the stones and with the timber of Ramah a fort, even Geba of Benjamin, and Mizpah" (15:22). Asa uses Baasha's materials to fortify his own cities. **Successful reign**: reform, peace, strength. "Asa's heart was perfect with the LORD all his days" — model of faithfulness.
*Verses: 16 | Theme: Faithful reformation and peace*

#### 15:25-34 — Northern Kings: Nadab and Baasha
**Lore:** "And Nadab the son of Jeroboam began to reign over Israel... And he reigned over Israel two years... And he did evil in the sight of the LORD, and walked in the way of his father, and in his sin wherewith he made Israel to sin... And Baasha the son of Ahijah, of the house of Issachar, conspired against him; and Baasha smote him at Gibbethon, which belonged to the Philistines... And Baasha reigned in his stead" (15:25-28). **Nadab** — son of Jeroboam, ruled 2 years. **Did evil, walked in Jeroboam's way** (the pattern). **Baasha** murdered him and seized throne. "And it came to pass, when he reigned, that he smote all the house of Jeroboam: he left not to Jeroboam any that breathed, until he had destroyed him, according to the saying of the LORD... by his servant Ahijah the Shilonite" (15:29). **Fulfillment of prophecy** (14:10-15)! Baasha **completely destroyed Jeroboam's house** — not one survivor. God's word executed. Yet Baasha is no better: "Nevertheless after him Baasha the son of Ahijah did evil in the sight of the LORD, and walked in the way of Jeroboam, and in his sin wherewith he made Israel to sin" (15:34). **Baasha also walked in Jeroboam's way** — the idolatrous pattern inherited by every northern king. **Key theological point**: Northern kingdom is **locked in cycle of apostasy** — each king perpetuates Jeroboam's original sin.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Northern chaos and southern stability*

## Chapter 16: The Northern Spiral into Anarchy

#### 16:1-7 — Baasha's Dynasty Judged
**Lore:** "Then the word of the LORD came to Jehu the son of Hanani against Baasha, saying, 'Forasmuch as I exalted thee out of the dust, and made thee prince over my people Israel; and thou hast walked in the way of Jeroboam, and hast made my people Israel to sin, to provoke me to anger with their sins; Behold, I will take away the posterity of Baasha, and the posterity of his house; and will make thy house like the house of Jeroboam the son of Nebat'" (16:2-3). **Jehu** (יְהוּא, different person than later king Jehu, 2 Kings 9) prophesies against Baasha. Despite overthrowing Jeroboam's house, **Baasha himself walked in Jeroboam's sin** — so his house will be destroyed like Jeroboam's. "Him that dieth of Baasha in the city shall the dogs eat; and him that dieth of his in the fields shall the fowls of the air eat" (16:4). Same judgment as Jeroboam (14:11). "Now the rest of the acts of Baasha... are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel? So Baasha slept with his fathers, and was buried in Tirzah" (16:5-6). Baasha dies, yet his son reigns briefly.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Judgment cycles*

#### 16:8-14 — Elah and Zimri: Seven Days of Chaos
**Lore:** "And Elah his son reigned in his stead... And his servant Zimri, captain of half his chariots, conspired against him. And Elah was in Tirzah, drinking himself drunk in the house of Arza steward of his house in Tirzah... And Zimri went in and smote him, and killed him... And Zimri reigned in his stead" (16:8-10). **Elah** — Baasha's son, ruled 2 years, **drunk when assassinated**! **Zimri** (צִמְרִי) — chariot captain, kills him. "And it came to pass, when he began to reign... that he smote all the house of Baasha... neither left him one that pisseth against a wall... according to the saying of the LORD... by his servant Jehu" (16:11). **Prophecy fulfilled**: Baasha's house destroyed (16:3). Yet immediately: "But Zimri reigned seven days in Tirzah... And the people that were encamped heard say, 'Zimri hath conspired, and also hath slain the king: wherefore all Israel made Omri, the captain of the host, king over Israel that day... And Omri went up from Gibbethon, and all Israel with him, and they besieged Tirzah" (16:15-17). **Zimri reigns only 7 days**! Omri (another military commander) leads a counter-coup. "And it came to pass, when Zimri saw that the city was taken, that he went into the palace of the king's house, and burnt the king's house over him with fire, and died" (16:18). **Zimri burns himself alive** — dramatic suicide. "So Zimri died for his sins which he sinned in doing evil in the sight of the LORD, and walking in the way of Jeroboam" (16:19). Zimri also "walked in Jeroboam's way." **Death acknowledges:** sin → judgment.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Anarchy and self-destruction*

#### 16:21-34 — Omri's Dynasty and Ahab's Evil
**Lore:** "Then were the people of Israel divided into two parts... But the people that followed Omri prevailed against the people that followed Tibni... So Tibni died, and Omri reigned" (16:21-22). **Civil war** between Omri and Tibni supporters. Omri prevails. "And Omri wrought evil in the sight of the LORD, and did worse than all that were before him... For he walked in all the way of Jeroboam... and in his sins wherewith he made Israel to sin, to provoke the LORD God of Israel to anger with their vanities" (16:25-26). **Omri also walked in Jeroboam's way** — the unbroken cycle. Yet Omri was strategically successful — he "bought the hill Samaria of Shemer for two talents of silver, and built on the hill, and called the name of the city which he built, after the name of Shemer, owner of the hill, Samaria" (16:24). **Samaria** becomes northern capital. "And Omri slept with his fathers, and was buried in Samaria: and Ahab his son reigned in his stead" (16:28). Omri dies; his son **Ahab** takes throne. "And Ahab the son of Omri did evil in the sight of the LORD above all that were before him... And it came to pass... that Ahab took to wife Jezebel the daughter of Ethbaal king of the Sidonians... And Ahab did more to provoke the LORD God of Israel to anger than all the kings of Israel that were before him" (16:30-33). **AHAB** (אַחְאָב) = "father's brother"? No, means "father of a brother." **More evil than all before!** Key details: (1) He "took to wife Jezebel" — marriage alliance with Sidonians (like Solomon's Egyptian princess). (2) **Jezebel** (אִיזֶבֶל) — becomes a byword for evil. She's **Sidonian** (Phoenician), worships Baal. "Jezebel his wife stirred him up" (21:25) — she has **power over the king** that God doesn't. "And Ahab made a grove; and Ahab did more to provoke the LORD God of Israel to anger than all the kings of Israel that were before him" (16:33). Ahab **adds Asherah worship** to the idolatry. "And in his days did Hiel the Bethelite build Jericho... and he set up the gates thereof in Abiram his youngest son" (16:34). Parenthetical: **Jericho rebuilt**, violating Joshua's curse (Joshua 6:26). Hiel loses sons (sacrifice of firstborn and youngest to foundation/gates — pagan practice). Anarchy reaches peak: assassinations, brief reigns, fragmented control, then Ahab emerges as **worst king yet**. **Northern Spiral Complete**: started with Jeroboam's false worship, devolved into anarchy, now ruled by Ahab and Jezebel — a **kingdom given over to Baal**.
*Verses: 14 | Theme: Anarchy and the rise of the greatest evil*

**Theme Summary:** Rehoboam's foolish rejection of elders' counsel causes kingdom split. Jeroboam invents false worship system (golden calves, false priests, false feasts) as political measure to prevent pilgrimage to Jerusalem. Man of God tests obedience through Jeroboam's false prophet's deception — **lesson: only God's direct commands override prior commands**. First generation ends with judgment: Jeroboam's house destroyed, Israel under curse. Contrasts Judah's stable succession (though weak kings) with North's anarchy: Nadab → Baasha (assassination) → Baasha's house destroyed → Elah (assassination) → Zimri (7 days, suicide) → Omri (civil war) → Ahab (married to Jezebel). **Key pattern**: every northern king "walks in the way of Jeroboam" — locked in apostasy cycle. Judah has Asa — a good king who reforms, removes idols, his mother for idolatry, achieves 35 years of peace. Yet North spirals: from false worship to anarchy to Ahab/Jezebel (Baal worship, child sacrifice hints). **Theological pivot**: institutional apostasy leads to political chaos. God's word stands (fulfilled prophecies), yet mercy possible through repentance (Asa model). The kingdom divided is the kingdom compromised, leading to slow rot in North, struggle in South.
