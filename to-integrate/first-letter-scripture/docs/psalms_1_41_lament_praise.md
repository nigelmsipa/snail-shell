# Psalms 1-41: Lament & Praise (Book 1)

## Overview
Book 1 (Psalms 1-41) forms the foundation of the Psalter, dominated by David's psalms (37 of 41) and individual laments. This collection establishes the "Great Controversy" at the personal level—the righteous suffer under adversaries yet maintain trust in Yahweh. Psalm 1 (Wisdom) and Psalm 2 (Messianic) form a gateway, framing the entire human struggle. Book 1 concludes with the doxology: "Blessed be the LORD God of Israel from everlasting to everlasting. Amen and Amen" (41:13).

---

## Psalm 1: The Two Ways

#### 1:1-6 — Blessed Man Meditates on God's Law
**Lore:** Opening psalm—the blessed person (אַשְׁרֵי, ashrei—"Oh the happiness\!") is defined by what they avoid (counsel of wicked, path of sinners, seat of scoffers) and what they do (delight in YHWH's law, meditate day and night). **MEDITATE** (הָגָה, hagah) = mutter, ponder constantly like a cow chewing cud. Psalm 1 functions as prologue to the entire Psalter, establishing dichotomy between righteous and wicked. The righteous are "like a tree planted by streams of water" (עֵץ שָׁתוּל, etz shatul—fruitful, prosperous, echoes Jer 17:7-8). The wicked are "like chaff driven by wind" (מֹץ, motz—worthless, blown away at threshing). Final verse: "For the LORD knows the way of the righteous, but the way of the wicked will perish" (יֹאבֵדוּ, yovedu—utterly destroyed). Two ways theology (Deut 30:19, Matt 7:13-14). **Gateway Psalm**—sets Psalter's theme.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Blessing through meditation on God's law*

## Psalm 2: The Anointed King

#### 2:1-12 — The King and His Anointed
**Lore:** The Messiah psalm. Kings of earth rebel against YHWH and His anointed (מְשִׁיחוֹ, meshicho—anointed one, MESSIAH). Quoted Acts 4:25-26 (early church identifies with Jesus' trial). YHWH laughs at rebellion (2:4) and appoints His Son as king over Zion. **SONSHIP DECREE**: "You are my Son; today I have begotten you" (2:7, quoted Heb 1:5, 5:5—Christ's divine Sonship). "Ask of me, and I will make nations your inheritance" (2:8—global dominion, fulfilled in Great Commission). "Break them with rod of iron" (2:9, quoted Rev 2:27, 12:5, 19:15—Christ's authority). **KISS THE SON** (נַשְּׁקוּ־בַר, nashqu-bar) = show submission, pay homage. Aramaic "bar" (son) emphasizes universal call. "Blessed are all who take refuge in him" (2:12—ends where Ps 1 begins, "blessed"). Psalms 1-2 form gateway: way of righteous (Ps 1) leads to submission to Messiah (Ps 2). **Royal/Messianic Psalm**—foundational to messianic hope, quoted 9x in NT.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Divine appointment of the Messiah*

## Psalm 3: Morning Deliverance

#### 3:1-8 — Trust in God Amid Enemies
**Lore:** "A Psalm of David, when he fled from Absalom his son" (superscription links to 2 Sam 15-18). David's greatest personal crisis—son's rebellion. "LORD, how many are my foes\! Many are rising against me" (3:1). Numerical escalation creates urgency. "Many are saying of my soul, 'There is no salvation for him in God.' Selah" (3:2). **SELAH** (סֶלָה)—pause/musical interlude (71x in Psalms). Enemies' taunt: God abandoned you. "But you, O LORD, are a shield about me, my glory, and the lifter of my head" (3:3). **SHIELD** (מָגֵן, magen)—divine protection. God restores honor. "I cried aloud to the LORD, and he answered me from his holy hill" (3:4—prayer answered from Zion). "I lay down and slept; I woke again, for the LORD sustained me" (3:5). **Night rest despite danger**—confidence, type of resurrection ("I woke"). "I will not be afraid of many thousands of people who have set themselves against me all around" (3:6). Faith increases as numbers escalate. "Salvation belongs to the LORD; your blessing be on your people\!" (3:8). Personal deliverance becomes communal hope. **Morning psalm**—sunrise vindication after night crisis.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Trust protects us in affliction*

## Psalm 4: Evening Rest

#### 4:1-8 — Peace in God's Presence
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: with stringed instruments. A Psalm of David" (superscription). **STRINGED INSTRUMENTS** (נְגִינוֹת, neginot). Pairs with Ps 3 (morning/evening liturgy). "Answer me when I call, O God of my righteousness\! You have given me relief when I was in distress" (4:1). **GOD OF MY RIGHTEOUSNESS** (אֱלֹהֵי צִדְקִי, elohei tzidqi)—God who vindicates. "You have given me relief" (הִרְחַבְתָּ, hirchavta) = made room, opened space (opposite of distress = narrow place). "Stand in awe, and do not sin; ponder in your own hearts on your beds, and be silent. Selah" (4:4, quoted Eph 4:26 "Be angry and do not sin"). **Night meditation**—examine conscience, be still before God. "You have put more joy in my heart than they have when their grain and wine abound" (4:7). **Joy in God exceeds material abundance**. "In peace I will both lie down and sleep; for you alone, O LORD, make me dwell in safety" (4:8). **Evening rest**—perfect peace (שָׁלוֹם, shalom), total security in God alone. Contrast to Ps 3 (awaking)—full day/night cycle of trust.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Joy and peace in God's presence*

## Psalm 5: Morning Prayer for Justice

#### 5:1-12 — Morning Petition for Vindication
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: for the flutes. A Psalm of David" (superscription). **FLUTES** (נְחִילוֹת, nechilot—wind instruments, uncertain). Another **morning psalm**. "Give ear to my words, O LORD; consider my groaning. Give attention to the sound of my cry, my King and my God, for to you do I pray" (5:1-2). Three-fold appeal: words, groaning (הֲגִיגִי, hagigi = meditation/murmuring), cry. **MY KING**—royal language. "O LORD, in the morning you hear my voice; in the morning I prepare a sacrifice for you and watch" (5:3). **PREPARE** (עָרַךְ, arakh) = arrange, set in order—priestly language (Lev 1:7). **Prayer as morning sacrifice**. "For you are not a God who delights in wickedness; evil may not dwell with you" (5:4). God's holiness incompatible with sin. "Lead me, O LORD, in your righteousness because of my enemies; make your way straight before me" (5:8). **LEAD** (נָחָה, nachah) = guide. "For there is no truth in their mouth; their inmost self is destruction; their throat is an open grave; they flatter with their tongue" (5:9, quoted Rom 3:13—universal indictment of sin). **OPEN GRAVE** (קֶבֶר פָּתוּחַ, qever patuach)—death-spewing speech. "But let all who take refuge in you rejoice; let them ever sing for joy, and spread your protection over them, that those who love your name may exult in you. For you bless the righteous, O LORD; you cover him with favor as with a shield" (5:11-12). **SHIELD** again (cf. 3:3)—God encircles His own with favor like defensive covering.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Morning prayer for divine justice*

## Psalm 6: Plea for Healing

#### 6:1-10 — Cry from Deep Distress
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: with stringed instruments; according to The Sheminith. A Psalm of David" (superscription). **SHEMINITH** (שְׁמִינִית) = "the eighth" (octave? lower register?). First of **Seven Penitential Psalms** (6, 32, 38, 51, 102, 130, 143). "O LORD, rebuke me not in your anger, nor discipline me in your wrath" (6:1). Acknowledges sin, pleads for mercy over wrath. **REBUKE** (יָכַח, yakach) = correct, reprove. "Be gracious to me, O LORD, for I am languishing; heal me, O LORD, for my bones are troubled" (6:2). **LANGUISHING** (אֻמְלַל, umlal) = feeble, withering. Physical illness from spiritual distress. "My soul also is greatly troubled. But you, O LORD—how long?" (6:3). **HOW LONG?** (עַד־מָתַי, ad-matai)—classic lament cry (appears 14x in Psalms, expressing anguish of waiting). "For in death there is no remembrance of you; in Sheol who will give you praise?" (6:5). Pre-resurrection theology—Sheol (שְׁאוֹל, grave) is silence. Better to live and praise\! "I am weary with my moaning; every night I flood my bed with tears; I drench my couch with my weeping" (6:6). **Night weeping**—extreme grief, physical toll. Hyperbole expresses depth of pain. **SUDDEN SHIFT to confidence**: "Depart from me, all you workers of evil, for the LORD has heard the sound of my weeping. The LORD has heard my plea; the LORD accepts my prayer" (6:8-9). Three-fold LORD (Yahweh)—covenant name emphasizes faithfulness. God heard\! Lament transforms to confidence. "Let all my enemies be ashamed and greatly troubled; let them turn back and be put to shame in a moment" (6:10). Tables turned—enemies experience shame psalmist felt.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: From weeping to confidence in God's hearing*

## Psalm 7: Divine Judge Vindicates

#### 7:1-9 — Plea for Justice and Oath of Innocence
**Lore:** "A Shiggaion of David, which he sang to the LORD concerning the words of Cush, a Benjaminite" (superscription). **SHIGGAION** (שִׁגָּיוֹן) = wild, passionate song (uncertain meaning). Cush otherwise unknown. "O LORD my God, in you do I take refuge; save me from all my pursuers and deliver me" (7:1). Appeal to covenant God for protection. "Lest like a lion they tear my soul apart, rending it in pieces, with none to deliver" (7:2). **Lion imagery**—enemy as predator (used of Saul, 1 Sam 26:20). "O LORD my God, if I have done this, if there is wrong in my hands, if I have repaid my friend with evil or plundered my enemy without cause, let the enemy pursue my soul and overtake it, and let him trample my life to the ground and lay my glory in the dust. Selah" (7:3-5). **OATH OF INNOCENCE**—David protests false accusations with self-curse if guilty. Conditional: "IF I have done this..." Legal form of vindication. "Arise, O LORD, in your anger; lift yourself up against the fury of my enemies; awake for me; you have appointed a judgment" (7:6). **IMPERATIVE PRAYER**—calls God to act as judge. "Let the assembly of the peoples be gathered about you; over it return on high. The LORD judges the peoples; judge me, O LORD, according to my righteousness and according to the integrity that is in me" (7:7-8). David appeals for **public vindication** before nations. Confident in innocence.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Appeal to divine justice*

#### 7:10-17 — God's Righteous Judgment
**Lore:** "My shield is with God, who saves the upright in heart. God is a righteous judge, and a God who feels indignation every day" (7:10-11). **God's daily wrath** against wickedness—not passive observer. "If a man does not repent, God will whet his sword; he has bent and readied his bow; he has prepared for him his deadly weapons, making his arrows fiery shafts" (7:12-13). **Divine warrior imagery**—God armed and ready. Conditional: repentance averts judgment. "Behold, the wicked man conceives evil and is pregnant with mischief and gives birth to lies. He makes a pit, digging it out, and falls into the hole that he has made. His mischief returns upon his own head, and on his own skull his violence descends" (7:14-16). **RETRIBUTIVE JUSTICE**—wicked caught in own trap (Prov 26:27, Ps 9:15). Pregnancy imagery: evil conceived → born as lies. Self-destruction inevitable. "I will give to the LORD the thanks due to his righteousness, and I will sing praise to the name of the LORD, the Most High" (7:17). **Vow of praise**. **EL ELYON** (עֶלְיוֹן, Elyon) = Most High, emphasizing God's sovereignty over all.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Retributive justice against the wicked*

## Psalm 8: Human Dignity and Dominion

#### 8:1-9 — Glory of God and Majesty of Man
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: according to The Gittith. A Psalm of David" (superscription). **GITTITH** (גִּתִּית) = from Gath? wine-press tune? (uncertain). "O LORD, our Lord, how majestic is your name in all the earth\! You have set your glory above the heavens" (8:1). **Inclusio**—v.1 and v.9 identical, framing psalm. "Out of the mouth of babes and infants, you have established strength because of your foes, to still the enemy and the avenger" (8:2, quoted Matt 21:16—children's praise silences opponents at Jesus' triumphal entry). "When I look at your heavens, the work of your fingers, the moon and the stars, which you have set in place, what is man that you are mindful of him, and the son of man that you care for him?" (8:3-4). **ASTRONOMY PROMPTS THEOLOGY**—vastness of creation, smallness of humanity. Yet: "You have made him a little lower than the heavenly beings and crowned him with glory and honor" (8:5, quoted Heb 2:6-9, applied to Christ). **LITTLE LOWER** (מְעַט מֵאֱלֹהִים, me'at me'elohim) = "little less than God/angels" (elohim can mean both). "You have given him dominion over the works of your hands; you have put all things under his feet" (8:6). **Edenic mandate** restored (Gen 1:26-28). Psalm celebrates humanity's God-given dignity and role in creation.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: God's glory and human dignity in creation*

## Psalm 9: Judgment on the Nations

#### 9:1-12 — Praise for God's Righteous Judgment
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: according to Muth-labben. A Psalm of David" (superscription). **MUTH-LABBEN** (מוּת לַבֵּן) = "death of the son" or tune name (uncertain). Psalms 9 and 10 form **acrostic pair** (single alphabet poem in Hebrew, though imperfect). "I will give thanks to the LORD with my whole heart; I will recount all of your wonderful deeds" (9:1). **Whole-hearted praise**. "When my enemies turn back, they stumble and perish before your presence" (9:3). God's presence = enemies' defeat. "For you have maintained my just cause; you have sat on the throne, giving righteous judgment. You have rebuked the nations; you have made the wicked perish; you have blotted out their name forever and ever" (9:4-5). **Divine judge** on throne. **NAME BLOTTED OUT**—ultimate judgment, total erasure. "The LORD has made himself known; he has executed judgment; the wicked are snared in the work of their own hands. Higgaion. Selah" (9:16). **HIGGAION** (הִגָּיוֹן) = meditation/musical note (only here in Psalms). **Retribution theme**—wicked trapped by own deeds (cf. 7:15-16, Prov 26:27).
*Verses: 12 | Theme: God's righteous judgment on nations*

#### 9:13-20 — Plea for Mercy and Final Judgment
**Lore:** "Be gracious to me, O LORD\! See my affliction from those who hate me, O you who lift me up from the gates of death, that I may recount all your praises, that in the gates of the daughter of Zion I may rejoice in your salvation" (9:13-14). Appeal to God's past deliverance. "The nations have sunk in the pit that they made; in the net that they hid, their own foot has been caught" (9:15). **Self-destruction** of wicked (cf. 7:15-16). "The wicked shall return to Sheol, all the nations that forget God" (9:17). **SHEOL** (שְׁאוֹל) = grave/realm of dead. **Forgetting God** = practical atheism, leads to death. "Arise, O LORD\! Let not man prevail; let the nations be judged before you\! Put them in fear, O LORD\! Let the nations know that they are but men\! Selah" (9:19-20). **IMPERATIVE PRAYER**—nations must recognize mortality, God's sovereignty. They are merely "men" (אֱנוֹשׁ, enosh—frail, mortal humanity).
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Nations judged for forgetting God*

## Psalm 10: Why Do the Wicked Prosper?

#### 10:1-11 — The Wicked's Arrogance and Oppression
**Lore:** Acrostic pair with Ps 9 (single alphabet poem in Hebrew). No superscription—likely continuation. "Why, O LORD, do you stand far away? Why do you hide yourself in times of trouble?" (10:1). **Complaint of God's absence**—core lament question. "In arrogance the wicked hotly pursue the poor; let them be caught in the schemes that they have devised" (10:2). **PRIDE** (גַּאֲוָה, ga'avah) = root sin. "For the wicked boasts of the desires of his soul, and the one greedy for gain curses and renounces the LORD" (10:3). Perverted values—celebrates greed, rejects God. "In the pride of his face the wicked does not seek him; all his thoughts are, 'There is no God'" (10:4). **Practical atheism**—God absent from thoughts (cf. 14:1, "The fool says in his heart, 'There is no God'"). "He says in his heart, 'God has forgotten, he has hidden his face, he will never see it'" (10:11). Wicked's theology: God doesn't care, doesn't intervene.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: The wicked deny God's justice*

#### 10:12-18 — God Defends the Oppressed
**Lore:** "Arise, O LORD; O God, lift up your hand; forget not the afflicted" (10:12). **IMPERATIVE**—break Your silence\! "Why does the wicked renounce God and say in his heart, 'You will not call to account'?" (10:13). Wicked assume no accountability. "But you do see, for you note mischief and vexation, that you may take it into your hands; to you the helpless commits himself; you have been the helper of the fatherless" (10:14). **GOD SEES**—nothing hidden. Poor **commit** (עָזַב, azav = leave, entrust) themselves to God. "Break the arm of the wicked and evildoer; call his wickedness to account till you find none" (10:15). Prayer for wicked's **power to be broken** (arm = strength/military might). "The LORD is king forever and ever; the nations perish from his land. O LORD, you hear the desire of the afflicted; you will strengthen their heart; you will incline your ear" (10:16-17). **Yahweh reigns eternally**—nations fade, God endures. Humble's prayers heard, hearts prepared.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: God hears and defends the oppressed*

## Psalm 11: Refuge in the Righteous LORD

#### 11:1-7 — The LORD Tests the Righteous
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster. Of David" (superscription). "In the LORD I take refuge; how can you say to my soul, 'Flee like a bird to your mountain, for behold, the wicked bend the bow; they have fitted their arrow to the string to shoot in the dark at the upright in heart; if the foundations are destroyed, what can the righteous do?'" (11:1-3). Counselors urge flight—**flee to mountain** like bird. Wicked ready arrows in darkness. **Rhetorical question**: if foundations crumble, what hope? David's answer: "The LORD is in his holy temple; the LORD's throne is in heaven; his eyes see, his eyelids test the children of man. The LORD tests the righteous, but his soul hates the wicked and the one who loves violence" (11:4-5). **God's throne secure in heaven**—earthly chaos doesn't dethrone Him. God **tests** (בָּחַן, bachan) righteous, **hates** (שָׂנֵא, sane) wicked—strong anthropomorphic language. "On the wicked he will rain coals of fire and sulfur; a scorching wind shall be the portion of their cup" (11:6, echoes Sodom/Gomorrah judgment, Gen 19:24). "For the LORD is righteous; he loves righteous deeds; the upright shall behold his face" (11:7). **Behold God's face**—ultimate blessing (cf. Num 6:25-26, Matt 5:8).
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Refuge in the LORD who sees and judges*

## Psalm 12: When the Godly Cease

#### 12:1-8 — Save, O LORD, for the Godly Are Gone
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: according to The Sheminith. A Psalm of David" (superscription). **SHEMINITH** = eighth (cf. Ps 6). "Save, O LORD, for the godly one is gone; for the faithful have vanished from among the children of man" (12:1). **Lament of societal decay**—godly remnant disappearing. "Everyone utters lies to his neighbor; with flattering lips and a double heart they speak" (12:2). **DOUBLE HEART** (לֵב וָלֵב, lev valev) = divided loyalty, duplicity. "May the LORD cut off all flattering lips, the tongue that makes great boasts, those who say, 'With our tongue we will prevail, our lips are with us; who is master over us?'" (12:3-4). Arrogant speech claims autonomy—"who is lord over us?" "Because the poor are plundered, because the needy groan, I will now arise," says the LORD; "I will place him in the safety for which he longs" (12:5). **God's direct response**—divine speech rare in Psalms. God arises to defend oppressed. "The words of the LORD are pure words, like silver refined in a furnace on the ground, purified seven times" (12:6). **God's words pure**—contrast to human lies (v.2). Seven-fold purification = complete perfection. "You, O LORD, will keep them; you will guard us from this generation forever" (12:7). God preserves faithful remnant.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: God's pure word amid human deceit*

## Psalm 13: How Long, O LORD?

#### 13:1-6 — From Lament to Trust
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster. A Psalm of David" (superscription). **Classic lament structure**: complaint → petition → trust. "How long, O LORD? Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me? How long must I take counsel in my soul and have sorrow in my heart all the day? How long shall my enemy be exalted over me?" (13:1-2). **FOUR-FOLD "HOW LONG?"**—intensifying anguish (עַד־אָנָה, ad-anah). God's silence = hiding face (cf. 10:1, 22:1). "Consider and answer me, O LORD my God; light up my eyes, lest I sleep the sleep of death, lest my enemy say, 'I have prevailed over him,' lest my foes rejoice because I am shaken" (13:3-4). **Sleep of death** = permanent death. Prayer: light eyes (= grant life/hope). "But I have trusted in your steadfast love; my heart shall rejoice in your salvation. I will sing to the LORD, because he has dealt bountifully with me" (13:5-6). **SUDDEN SHIFT** from lament to confidence. **STEADFAST LOVE** (חֶסֶד, chesed) = covenant loyalty. Trust declared, praise anticipated though deliverance not yet seen. **Faith precedes sight**.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: From despair to trust in God's steadfast love*

## Psalm 14: The Fool Denies God

#### 14:1-7 — Universal Corruption and Hope of Restoration
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster. Of David" (superscription). Duplicate of Ps 53 (with minor variations). "The fool says in his heart, 'There is no God.' They are corrupt, they do abominable deeds; there is none who does good" (14:1). **THE FOOL** (נָבָל, naval) = morally deficient, not intellectually stupid. Practical atheism—lives as if God doesn't exist (cf. 10:4). "The LORD looks down from heaven on the children of man, to see if there are any who understand, who seek after God. They have all turned aside; together they have become corrupt; there is none who does good, not even one" (14:2-3, quoted Rom 3:10-12—Paul's universal indictment of sin). **God's survey finds universal corruption**. "Have they no knowledge, all the evildoers who eat up my people as they eat bread and do not call upon the LORD?" (14:4). Wicked consume God's people like food, don't pray. "There they are in great terror, for God is with the generation of the righteous. You would shame the plans of the poor, but the LORD is his refuge" (14:5-6). Terror seizes wicked when they realize God is with righteous. "Oh, that salvation for Israel would come out of Zion\! When the LORD restores the fortunes of his people, let Jacob rejoice, let Israel be glad" (14:7). **Eschatological hope**—salvation from Zion, restoration of Israel. Anticipates messianic deliverance.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Universal corruption and hope in God's salvation*

## Psalm 15: Who May Dwell with God?

#### 15:1-5 — Requirements for God's Presence
**Lore:** "A Psalm of David" (superscription). **Entrance liturgy**—questions and answers for temple worship (cf. Ps 24:3-6). "O LORD, who shall sojourn in your tent? Who shall dwell on your holy hill?" (15:1). **Sojourn** (גּוּר, gur) = dwell as guest. **Holy hill** = Zion/temple mount. Question: Who qualifies for God's presence? "He who walks blamelessly and does what is right and speaks truth in his heart" (15:2). **Three-fold qualification**: blameless walk, righteous deeds, honest speech. "who does not slander with his tongue and does no evil to his neighbor, nor takes up a reproach against his friend" (15:3). **Negative qualities** (slander, harm, reproach) excluded. "in whose eyes a vile person is despised, but who honors those who fear the LORD; who swears to his own hurt and does not change" (15:4). **Discernment** (despises evil, honors godly). **Integrity**—keeps oath even when costly. "who does not put out his money at interest and does not take a bribe against the innocent. He who does these things shall never be moved" (15:5). **Financial ethics**—no usury to poor (Exod 22:25), no bribes (Exod 23:8, Deut 16:19). **Conclusion**: "shall never be moved" (לֹא־יִמּוֹט, lo-yimmot)—secure forever. **High ethical standard** for access to God—anticipates Christ as perfect fulfillment, granting access through His righteousness.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Ethical requirements for dwelling with God*

## Psalm 16: You Will Not Abandon My Soul

#### 16:1-11 — Joy in God's Presence
**Lore:** "A Miktam of David" (superscription). **MIKTAM** (מִכְתָּם) = golden psalm? engraved? (uncertain, appears in Pss 16, 56-60). "Preserve me, O God, for in you I take refuge. I say to the LORD, 'You are my Lord; I have no good apart from you'" (16:1-2). Total dependence on God. "As for the saints in the land, they are the excellent ones, in whom is all my delight" (16:3). Fellowship with God's people. "The sorrows of those who run after another god shall multiply; their drink offerings of blood I will not pour out or take their names on my lips" (16:4). **Idolatry rejected**. "The LORD is my chosen portion and my cup; you hold my lot. The lines have fallen for me in pleasant places; indeed, I have a beautiful inheritance" (16:5-6). **PORTION** (חֵלֶק, cheleq) = inheritance/lot—Levites' language (Num 18:20, "I am your portion"). "I bless the LORD who gives me counsel; in the night also my heart instructs me. I have set the LORD always before me; because he is at my right hand, I shall not be shaken" (16:7-8, quoted Acts 2:25—Peter's Pentecost sermon). "Therefore my heart is glad, and my whole being rejoices; my flesh also dwells secure. For you will not abandon my soul to Sheol, or let your holy one see corruption" (16:9-10, quoted Acts 2:27, 13:35—**resurrection prophecy**, applied to Christ by early church). **HOLY ONE** (חָסִיד, chasid) = faithful/godly one. God will not let Him decay in grave. "You make known to me the path of life; in your presence there is fullness of joy; at your right hand are pleasures forevermore" (16:11). **Ultimate joy in God's presence**—anticipates eternal life. **Messianic/resurrection psalm**.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Joy and resurrection hope in God*

## Psalm 17: Vindicate Me, O LORD

#### 17:1-9 — Prayer for Vindication
**Lore:** "A Prayer of David" (superscription). "Hear a just cause, O LORD; attend to my cry\! Give ear to my prayer from lips free of deceit\! From your presence let my vindication come\! Let your eyes behold the right\!" (17:1-2). **Appeal for vindication**—confident in innocence. "You have tried my heart, you have visited me by night, you have tested me, and you will find nothing; I have purposed that my mouth will not transgress" (17:3). **TESTED** (צָרַף, tzaraf) = refined like metal. Night vigil = God's examination. "With regard to the works of man, by the word of your lips I have avoided the ways of the violent. My steps have held fast to your paths; my feet have not slipped" (17:4-5). Obedience to God's word kept from violence. "I call upon you, for you will answer me, O God; incline your ear to me; hear my words. Wondrously show your steadfast love, O Savior of those who seek refuge from their adversaries at your right hand" (17:6-7). **WONDROUSLY SHOW** (הַפְלֵה, hafleh) = display marvelously. **STEADFAST LOVE** (חֶסֶד, chesed). "Keep me as the apple of your eye; hide me in the shadow of your wings" (17:8). **APPLE OF EYE** (אִישׁוֹן בַּת־עַיִן, ishon bat-ayin) = pupil, most protected part. **Shadow of wings** = cherubim imagery, God's protective presence.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Plea for divine vindication*

#### 17:10-15 — Deliverance from the Wicked
**Lore:** "They close their hearts to pity; with their mouths they speak arrogantly. They have now surrounded our steps; they set their eyes to cast us to the ground" (17:10-11). Enemies encircle, speak pride. "He is like a lion eager to tear, as a young lion lurking in ambush" (17:12). Lion imagery—predator waiting to pounce. "Arise, O LORD\! Confront him, subdue him\! Deliver my soul from the wicked by your sword, from men by your hand, O LORD, from men of the world whose portion is in this life" (17:13-14). **IMPERATIVE PRAYER**. Wicked's portion = temporal only. "As for me, I shall behold your face in righteousness; when I awake, I shall be satisfied with your likeness" (17:15). **Beholding God's face**—ultimate reward. **When I awake** = resurrection hope? morning vindication? **Satisfied with likeness** = conformed to God's image (cf. 1 John 3:2).
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Deliverance and beholding God's face*

## Psalm 18: The LORD is My Rock (Part 1)

#### 18:1-6 — David's Testimony of Deliverance
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster. A Psalm of David, the servant of the LORD, who addressed the words of this song to the LORD on the day when the LORD delivered him from the hand of all his enemies, and from the hand of Saul" (superscription). **Longest superscription**—links to 2 Sam 22 (duplicate with variations). **Royal thanksgiving psalm**. "I love you, O LORD, my strength. The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer, my God, my rock, in whom I take refuge, my shield, and the horn of my salvation, my stronghold" (18:1-2). **SEVEN METAPHORS for God**: strength, rock, fortress, deliverer, shield, horn (power), stronghold. "I call upon the LORD, who is worthy to be praised, and I am saved from my enemies" (18:3). "The cords of death encompassed me; the torrents of destruction assailed me; the cords of Sheol entangled me; the snares of death confronted me" (18:4-5). **Four-fold death imagery**—near-death experience. "In my distress I called upon the LORD; to my God I cried for help. From his temple he heard my voice, and my cry to him reached his ears" (18:6). Prayer from temple heard—God responds from heavenly dwelling.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: God as rock and deliverer*

#### 18:7-15 — Theophany: God Descends in Power
**Lore:** "Then the earth reeled and rocked; the foundations also of the mountains trembled and quaked, because he was angry" (18:7). **THEOPHANY**—God's appearance in nature (cf. Exod 19 Sinai, 1 Kings 19 Elijah). "Smoke went up from his nostrils, and devouring fire from his mouth; glowing coals flamed forth from him. He bowed the heavens and came down; thick darkness was under his feet" (18:8-9). **Anthropomorphic imagery**—God as divine warrior. "He rode on a cherub and flew; he came swiftly on the wings of the wind. He made darkness his covering, his canopy around him, thick clouds dark with water. Out of the brightness before him hailstones and coals of fire broke through his clouds" (18:10-12). God rides cherubim (cf. Ezek 1, 10). "The LORD also thundered in the heavens, and the Most High uttered his voice, hailstones and coals of fire. And he sent out his arrows and scattered them; he flashed forth lightnings and routed them. Then the channels of the sea were seen, and the foundations of the world were laid bare at your rebuke, O LORD, at the blast of the breath of your nostrils" (18:13-15). **COSMIC WARFARE**—God's voice thunders, arrows = lightning. Creation itself responds to God's rebuke (cf. creation at Red Sea, Exod 14-15).
*Verses: 9 | Theme: God's powerful theophanic deliverance*

#### 18:16-24 — God Draws David Out
**Lore:** "He sent from on high, he took me; he drew me out of many waters. He rescued me from my strong enemy and from those who hated me, for they were too mighty for me" (18:16-17). **Drew me out** (מָשָׁה, mashah)—same verb as Moses (Exod 2:10), salvation from waters. "They confronted me in the day of my calamity, but the LORD was my support. He brought me out into a broad place; he rescued me, because he delighted in me" (18:18-19). **BROAD PLACE** (מֶרְחָב, merchav) = spacious, freedom (opposite of narrow distress). God's delight = election/love. "The LORD dealt with me according to my righteousness; according to the cleanness of my hands he rewarded me. For I have kept the ways of the LORD, and have not wickedly departed from my God. For all his rules were before me, and his statutes I did not put away from me. I was blameless before him, and I kept myself from my guilt. So the LORD has rewarded me according to my righteousness, according to the cleanness of my hands in his sight" (18:20-24). **David's righteousness**—not sinless perfection but covenant faithfulness. Confident in integrity (cf. Pss 7, 15, 17).
*Verses: 9 | Theme: God rewards the righteous*

#### 18:25-36 — God's Ways with Humanity
**Lore:** "With the merciful you show yourself merciful; with the blameless man you show yourself blameless; with the purified you show yourself pure; and with the crooked you make yourself seem tortuous" (18:25-26). **Retribution principle**—God mirrors human response. "For you save a humble people, but the haughty eyes you bring down" (18:27). God exalts humble, humbles proud. "For it is you who light my lamp; the LORD my God lightens my darkness. For by you I can run against a troop, and by my God I can leap over a wall" (18:28-29). God empowers for battle—military metaphors. "This God—his way is perfect; the word of the LORD proves true; he is a shield for all those who take refuge in him" (18:30). God's way = perfect (תָּמִים, tamim), word = tested/refined. "For who is God, but the LORD? And who is a rock, except our God?—the God who equipped me with strength and made my way blameless" (18:31-32). **Rhetorical questions**—only Yahweh is God, only He is rock. "He made my feet like the feet of a deer and set me secure on the heights. He trains my hands for war, so that my arms can bend a bow of bronze. You have given me the shield of your salvation, and your right hand supported me, and your gentleness made me great" (18:33-36). **Deer feet** = surefootedness in battle (cf. Hab 3:19). God trains warrior. **Gentleness** (עֲנָוָה, anavah) = humility—God's humble care makes David great.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: God's perfect ways and empowerment*

#### 18:37-45 — Victory Over Enemies
**Lore:** "I pursued my enemies and overtook them, and did not turn back till they were consumed. I thrust them through, so that they were not able to rise; they fell under my feet" (18:37-38). **Military conquest**. "For you equipped me with strength for the battle; you made those who rise against me sink under me. You made my enemies turn their backs to me, and those who hated me I destroyed" (18:39-40). God grants victory. "They cried for help, but there was none to save; they cried to the LORD, but he did not answer them. I beat them fine as dust before the wind; I cast them out like the mire of the streets" (18:41-42). Enemies' prayers unanswered—divine judgment. "You delivered me from strife with the people; you made me the head of the nations; people whom I had not known served me. As soon as they heard of me they obeyed me; foreigners came cringing to me. Foreigners lost heart and came trembling out of their fortresses" (18:43-45). **International dominion**—David's kingdom expands. Nations submit. Anticipates messianic reign (Ps 2, 72).
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Military victory and international dominion*

#### 18:46-50 — Doxology: Praise to the Living God
**Lore:** "The LORD lives, and blessed be my rock, and exalted be the God of my salvation—the God who gave me vengeance and subdued peoples under me, who delivered me from my enemies; yes, you exalted me above those who rose against me; you rescued me from the man of violence" (18:46-48). **Doxology**—"The LORD lives\!" (חַי־יְהוָה, chai-Yahweh). God as living rock, giver of salvation. "For this I will praise you, O LORD, among the nations, and sing to your name" (18:49, quoted Rom 15:9—Paul applies to Gentile mission). David's praise extends to nations. "Great salvation he brings to his king, and shows steadfast love to his anointed, to David and his offspring forever" (18:50). **ANOINTED** (מָשִׁיחַ, mashiach) = messiah. God's covenant love (חֶסֶד, chesed) extends to David's line—anticipates Christ, son of David. **Royal psalm** with messianic overtones.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Praise for God's salvation and covenant love*

## Psalm 19: The Heavens Declare God's Glory

#### 19:1-6 — General Revelation: Creation's Testimony
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster. A Psalm of David" (superscription). **Two-part structure**: general revelation (vv.1-6), special revelation (vv.7-14). "The heavens declare the glory of God, and the sky above proclaims his handiwork. Day to day pours out speech, and night to night reveals knowledge" (19:1-2). **NATURAL THEOLOGY**—creation reveals Creator (Rom 1:20). "There is no speech, nor are there words, whose voice is not heard. Their voice goes out through all the earth, and their words to the end of the world" (19:3-4, quoted Rom 10:18—Paul applies to gospel proclamation). Silent yet universal testimony. "In them he has set a tent for the sun, which comes out like a bridegroom leaving his chamber, and, like a strong man, runs its course with joy. Its rising is from the end of the heavens, and its circuit to the end of them, and there is nothing hidden from its heat" (19:4-6). **Sun as bridegroom/athlete**—poetic personification. Universal reach (nothing hidden). Ancient cosmology yet profound theology.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Creation proclaims God's glory*

#### 19:7-14 — Special Revelation: The Law's Perfection
**Lore:** "The law of the LORD is perfect, reviving the soul; the testimony of the LORD is sure, making wise the simple" (19:7). **SIX-FOLD DESCRIPTION of Scripture**: law (תּוֹרָה, torah), testimony (עֵדוּת, edut), precepts (פִּקּוּדִים, piqudim), commandment (מִצְוָה, mitzvah), fear (יִרְאָה, yir'ah), rules (מִשְׁפָּטִים, mishpatim). Each paired with attribute (perfect, sure, right, pure, clean, true) and benefit (revives soul, makes wise, rejoices heart, enlightens eyes, endures, righteous). "The precepts of the LORD are right, rejoicing the heart; the commandment of the LORD is pure, enlightening the eyes; the fear of the LORD is clean, enduring forever; the rules of the LORD are true, and righteous altogether" (19:8-9). "More to be desired are they than gold, even much fine gold; sweeter also than honey and drippings of the honeycomb" (19:10). Word more valuable than gold, sweeter than honey (cf. Ps 119:72, 103). "Moreover, by them is your servant warned; in keeping them there is great reward. Who can discern his errors? Declare me innocent from hidden faults. Keep back your servant also from presumptuous sins; let them not have dominion over me\! Then I shall be blameless, and innocent of great transgression" (19:11-13). **Two types of sin**: hidden/unintentional vs. presumptuous/willful (cf. Num 15:27-31). Prayer for cleansing from both. "Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable in your sight, O LORD, my rock and my redeemer" (19:14). **Perfect conclusion**—prayer for acceptable speech and thought. **MY REDEEMER** (גֹּאֲלִי, go'ali)—kinsman-redeemer language (cf. Job 19:25, Ruth, Isaiah 59:20).
*Verses: 8 | Theme: God's perfect law revives the soul*

## Psalm 20: Prayer for the King

#### 20:1-9 — May the LORD Answer You
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster. A Psalm of David" (superscription). **Royal liturgy**—prayer for king before battle. "May the LORD answer you in the day of trouble\! May the name of the God of Jacob protect you\! May he send you help from the sanctuary and give you support from Zion\!" (20:1-2). Communal prayer for king. Sanctuary = source of help. "May he remember all your offerings and regard with favor your burnt sacrifices\! Selah" (20:3). Offerings recalled. "May he grant you your heart's desire and fulfill all your plans\! May we shout for joy over your salvation, and in the name of our God set up our banners\! May the LORD fulfill all your petitions\!" (20:4-5). Five-fold "may he" (jussives)—wishes for king's success. "Now I know that the LORD saves his anointed; he will answer him from his holy heaven with the saving might of his right hand" (20:6). **Confidence declared**—God saves His **anointed** (מָשִׁיחַ, mashiach = messiah). "Some trust in chariots and some in horses, but we trust in the name of the LORD our God" (20:7). **FAMOUS ANTITHESIS**—military power vs. trust in God. "They collapse and fall, but we rise and stand upright" (20:8). Result: enemies fall, we stand. "O LORD, save the king\! May he answer us when we call" (20:9). Final petition for king and people.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Trust in God's name, not military might*

## Psalm 21: Thanksgiving for Victory

#### 21:1-7 — The King Rejoices in God's Strength
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster. A Psalm of David" (superscription). **Pair with Ps 20**—prayer before battle (20), thanksgiving after victory (21). "O LORD, in your strength the king rejoices, and in your salvation how greatly he exults\! You have given him his heart's desire and have not withheld the request of his lips. Selah" (21:1-2). King's joy in God's deliverance. Petitions of Ps 20 answered. "For you meet him with rich blessings; you set a crown of fine gold upon his head. He asked life of you; you gave it to him, length of days forever and ever" (21:3-4). **Crown of gold**—royal imagery. **Length of days forever**—Davidic covenant promise (2 Sam 7, Ps 89). "His glory is great through your salvation; splendor and majesty you bestow on him. For you make him most blessed forever; you make him glad with the joy of your presence" (21:5-6). King's glory derives from God. **Presence** = source of joy. "For the king trusts in the LORD, and through the steadfast love of the Most High he shall not be moved" (21:7). Trust + steadfast love (חֶסֶד, chesed) = security.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: King's joy in God's deliverance*

#### 21:8-13 — Victory Over Enemies
**Lore:** "Your hand will find out all your enemies; your right hand will find out those who hate you. You will make them as a blazing oven when you appear. The LORD will swallow them up in his wrath, and fire will consume them" (21:8-9). **Divine judgment**—God's wrath as consuming fire. "You will destroy their descendants from the earth, and their offspring from among the children of man. Though they plan evil against you, though they devise mischief, they will not succeed" (21:10-11). Enemies' plots fail. "For you will put them to flight; you will aim at their faces with your bows" (21:12). God's arrows aimed at enemies. "Be exalted, O LORD, in your strength\! We will sing and praise your power" (21:13). **Doxology**—concludes with praise to God's power. **Royal/Messianic psalm**—anticipates Christ's ultimate victory over enemies.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: God's victory over the king's enemies*

## Psalm 22: My God, Why Have You Forsaken Me?

#### 22:1-5 — The Cry of Abandonment
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: according to The Doe of the Dawn. A Psalm of David" (superscription). **DOE OF THE DAWN** (אַיֶּלֶת הַשַּׁחַר, ayyelet hashachar) = tune name (uncertain). **MOST FAMOUS MESSIANIC PSALM**—Jesus quotes v.1 on cross (Matt 27:46, Mark 15:34). "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me? Why are you so far from saving me, from the words of my groaning?" (22:1). **Cry of dereliction**—most anguished prayer in Scripture. Yet still "MY God" (אֵלִי, eli)—maintains relationship even in abandonment. "O my God, I cry by day, but you do not answer, and by night, but I find no rest" (22:2). Unanswered prayer, sleepless nights. "Yet you are holy, enthroned on the praises of Israel. In you our fathers trusted; they trusted, and you delivered them. To you they cried and were rescued; in you they trusted and were not put to shame" (22:3-5). **Appeal to history**—ancestors trusted and were saved. Why not me?
*Verses: 5 | Theme: The cry of the forsaken*

#### 22:6-11 — The Reproach and Trust from Birth
**Lore:** "But I am a worm and not a man, scorned by mankind and despised by the people" (22:6). **WORM** (תּוֹלַעַת, tola'at) = crimson worm used for dye, crushed to produce color—image of utter humiliation. "All who see me mock me; they make mouths at me; they wag their heads; 'He trusts in the LORD; let him deliver him; let him rescue him, for he delights in him\!'" (22:7-8, quoted Matt 27:43—mockers at crucifixion use these exact words). Enemies mock sufferer's faith. "Yet you are he who took me from the womb; you made me trust you at my mother's breasts. On you was I cast from my birth, and from my mother's womb you have been my God. Be not far from me, for trouble is near, and there is none to help" (22:9-11). **Trust from birth**—God sustained from beginning. Now, in greatest need, God seems absent.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Mocked yet trusting from birth*

#### 22:12-21 — Surrounded by Enemies, Death Imminent
**Lore:** "Many bulls encompass me; strong bulls of Bashan surround me; they open wide their mouths at me, like a ravening and roaring lion" (22:12-13). **Animal imagery**—bulls, lions = powerful enemies. **Bashan** = region known for cattle. "I am poured out like water, and all my bones are out of joint; my heart is like wax; it is melted within my breast; my strength is dried up like a potsherd, and my tongue sticks to my jaws; you lay me in the dust of death" (22:14-15). **Physical suffering**—bones disjointed, heart melted, strength gone, tongue parched. Describes crucifixion symptoms without naming it (psalm predates crucifixion as Roman practice). "For dogs encompass me; a company of evildoers encircles me; they have pierced my hands and feet—I can count all my bones—they stare and gloat over me; they divide my garments among them, and for my clothing they cast lots" (22:16-18). **PIERCED HANDS AND FEET** (כָּאֲרִי, ka'ari = "like a lion" MT, or כָּרוּ, karu = "pierced" LXX/DSS)—textual debate but Christian interpretation as crucifixion prophecy (John 19:24, 37, 20:25). **Dividing garments** exactly fulfilled at cross (Matt 27:35, John 19:23-24). "But you, O LORD, do not be far off\! O you my help, come quickly to my aid\! Deliver my soul from the sword, my precious life from the power of the dog\! Save me from the mouth of the lion\! You have rescued me from the horns of the wild oxen\!" (22:19-21). **Threefold petition**: deliver, save, rescue. Prayer for deliverance from death.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Extreme suffering and plea for deliverance*

#### 22:22-31 — From Lament to Praise: Testimony to the Nations
**Lore:** **SUDDEN SHIFT** from suffering to praise (v.22+). "I will tell of your name to my brothers; in the midst of the congregation I will praise you" (22:22, quoted Heb 2:12—applied to Christ declaring God's name to church). "You who fear the LORD, praise him\! All you offspring of Jacob, glorify him, and stand in awe of him, all you offspring of Israel\!" (22:23). Call to worship. "For he has not despised or abhorred the affliction of the afflicted, and he has not hidden his face from him, but has heard, when he cried to him" (22:24). **God heard**—reverses v.1-2 (why forsaken?). Vindication comes. "From you comes my praise in the great congregation; my vows I will perform before those who fear him. The afflicted shall eat and be satisfied; those who seek him shall praise the LORD\! May your hearts live forever\!" (22:25-26). **Great congregation**—public testimony. Feast imagery (Messianic banquet, Isa 25:6). "All the ends of the earth shall remember and turn to the LORD, and all the families of the nations shall worship before you" (22:27). **UNIVERSAL SALVATION**—from personal suffering to global worship. Anticipates Great Commission. "For kingship belongs to the LORD, and he rules over the nations" (22:28). God's sovereignty established. "All the prosperous of the earth eat and worship; before him shall bow all who go down to the dust, even the one who could not keep himself alive. Posterity shall serve him; it shall be told of the Lord to the coming generation; they shall come and proclaim his righteousness to a people yet unborn, that he has done it" (22:29-31). **Eschatological vision**—all humanity (living, dying, unborn) will worship. **"It is finished"** (עָשָׂה, asah = "he has done it")—final word echoes John 19:30. **Psalm's structure**: Lament (vv.1-21) → Praise (vv.22-31). Crucifixion → Resurrection. Death → Life. **Most quoted psalm in NT passion narrative**.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: From suffering to universal praise*

## Psalm 23: The LORD is My Shepherd

#### 23:1-6 — The Good Shepherd's Care
**Lore:** "A Psalm of David" (superscription). **MOST BELOVED PSALM** in Christian tradition. "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want" (23:1). **SHEPHERD** (רֹעֶה, ro'eh)—David's background (1 Sam 16:11), God's role (Gen 48:15, 49:24, Ezek 34), Jesus' identity (John 10:11, "I am the good shepherd"). "I shall not want" (לֹא אֶחְסָר, lo echsar) = lack nothing. "He makes me lie down in green pastures. He leads me beside still waters. He restores my soul. He leads me in paths of righteousness for his name's sake" (23:2-3). **Pastoral imagery**: green pastures (מִרְעֵה דֶשֶׁא, mir'eh deshe), still waters (מֵי מְנֻחוֹת, mei menuchot = waters of rest), restored soul (נַפְשִׁי יְשׁוֹבֵב, nafshi yeshobev = refreshes life), paths of righteousness (מַעְגְּלֵי־צֶדֶק, ma'gelei-tzedeq). "For his name's sake" = for His reputation/glory. "Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I fear no evil, for you are with me; your rod and your staff, they comfort me" (23:4). **VALLEY OF SHADOW OF DEATH** (גֵּיא צַלְמָוֶת, gei tzalmavet) = deep darkness, death's shadow. **Rod and staff** = shepherd's tools (protection and guidance). Divine presence = courage. "You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies; you anoint my head with oil; my cup overflows" (23:5). **Shift from shepherd to host**—God provides feast amid danger. **Anointing** = honor, joy. **Overflowing cup** (כּוֹסִי רְוָיָה, kosi revayah) = abundance. "Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life, and I shall dwell in the house of the LORD forever" (23:6). **GOODNESS AND MERCY** (טוֹב וָחֶסֶד, tov vachesed) = divine attributes personified as companions. **Forever** (לְאֹרֶךְ יָמִים, le'orekh yamim) = length of days, eternal dwelling. **Structure**: Shepherd (vv.1-4) → Host (v.5) → Resident (v.6). **Christ the Good Shepherd** (John 10), **Bread of Life** (John 6), **Eternal dwelling** (John 14:2-3). **Psalm of total trust**.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: The LORD as shepherd, host, and eternal home*

## Psalm 24: The King of Glory

#### 24:1-6 — Who May Ascend the Hill of the LORD?
**Lore:** "A Psalm of David" (superscription). **Entrance liturgy** for temple worship (cf. Ps 15). "The earth is the LORD's and the fullness thereof, the world and those who dwell therein, for he has founded it upon the seas and established it upon the rivers" (24:1-2, quoted 1 Cor 10:26). **Creation theology**—God owns earth as Creator. "Who shall ascend the hill of the LORD? And who shall stand in his holy place? He who has clean hands and a pure heart, who does not lift up his soul to what is false and does not swear deceitfully" (24:3-4). **Entrance requirements**: clean hands (ethical conduct), pure heart (inner motive), truthfulness. "He will receive blessing from the LORD and righteousness from the God of his salvation. Such is the generation of those who seek him, who seek the face of the God of Jacob. Selah" (24:5-6). **Blessing** for the qualified. **Seek God's face**—desire for divine presence.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Requirements for entering God's presence*

#### 24:7-10 — Lift Up Your Heads, O Gates
**Lore:** "Lift up your heads, O gates\! And be lifted up, O ancient doors, that the King of glory may come in" (24:7). **Liturgical dialogue**—processional hymn as ark enters temple (2 Sam 6, 1 Chr 15-16). Gates personified. "Who is this King of glory? The LORD, strong and mighty, the LORD, mighty in battle\!" (24:8). **KING OF GLORY** (מֶלֶךְ הַכָּבוֹד, melekh hakavod)—divine warrior. "Lift up your heads, O gates\! And lift them up, O ancient doors, that the King of glory may come in. Who is this King of glory? The LORD of hosts, he is the King of glory\! Selah" (24:9-10). **Repetition** for emphasis. **LORD OF HOSTS** (יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת, Yahweh Tzeva'ot) = God of angel armies. **Christological interpretation**: Christ's triumphal entry (Palm Sunday), ascension (Eph 4:8), second coming. **Psalm structure**: Earth belongs to God (vv.1-2) → Who may enter? (vv.3-6) → King of Glory enters\! (vv.7-10).
*Verses: 4 | Theme: The King of glory enters his temple*

## Psalm 25: Teach Me Your Paths

#### 25:1-7 — Prayer for Guidance and Forgiveness
**Lore:** "Of David" (superscription). **Acrostic psalm** (each verse begins with successive letter of Hebrew alphabet, with irregularities). "To you, O LORD, I lift up my soul. O my God, in you I trust; let me not be put to shame; let not my enemies exult over me" (25:1-2). **Soul lifted** = prayer of dependence. "Make me to know your ways, O LORD; teach me your paths. Lead me in your truth and teach me, for you are the God of my salvation; for you I wait all the day long" (25:4-5). **Four-fold petition**: make known, teach, lead, teach—emphasis on divine instruction. **WAIT** (קִוִּיתִי, qivviti) = hope expectantly. "Remember your mercy, O LORD, and your steadfast love, for they have been from of old. Remember not the sins of my youth or my transgressions; according to your steadfast love remember me, for the sake of your goodness, O LORD\!" (25:6-7). Appeal to God's **steadfast love** (חֶסֶד, chesed) and **goodness** (טוּב, tuv). Confession of youthful sins.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Prayer for divine guidance*

#### 25:8-15 — The LORD Instructs Sinners
**Lore:** "Good and upright is the LORD; therefore he instructs sinners in the way. He leads the humble in what is right, and teaches the humble his way. All the paths of the LORD are steadfast love and faithfulness, for those who keep his covenant and his testimonies" (25:8-10). **God teaches sinners**—grace to the undeserving. **Humble** (עֲנָוִים, anavim) = those who receive instruction. "For your name's sake, O LORD, pardon my guilt, for it is great. Who is the man who fears the LORD? Him will he instruct in the way that he should choose. His soul shall abide in well-being, and his offspring shall inherit the land. The friendship of the LORD is for those who fear him, and he makes known to them his covenant" (25:11-14). **Pardon** despite great guilt. **FRIENDSHIP** (סוֹד, sod) = intimate counsel, secret fellowship. Covenant revealed to those who fear God. "My eyes are ever toward the LORD, for he will pluck my feet out of the net" (25:15). Eyes fixed on God—He delivers from traps.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: God instructs the humble*

#### 25:16-22 — Relieve My Troubles
**Lore:** "Turn to me and be gracious to me, for I am lonely and afflicted. The troubles of my heart are enlarged; bring me out of my distresses. Consider my affliction and my trouble, and forgive all my sins" (25:16-18). **Loneliness and affliction**—emotional/spiritual distress. "Consider how many are my foes, and with what violent hatred they hate me. Oh, guard my soul, and deliver me\! Let me not be put to shame, for I take refuge in you. May integrity and uprightness preserve me, for I wait for you" (25:19-21). Enemies multiply, prayer for protection. **Integrity and uprightness** = ethical shields. "Redeem Israel, O God, out of all his troubles" (25:22). **Corporate ending**—personal lament becomes national prayer. **REDEEM** (פְּדֵה, pedeh)—kinsman-redeemer language.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Deliverance from troubles and enemies*

## Psalm 26: Vindicate Me, O LORD

#### 26:1-12 — Declaration of Integrity
**Lore:** "Of David" (superscription). **Psalm of innocence** (cf. Pss 7, 15, 17). "Vindicate me, O LORD, for I have walked in my integrity, and I have trusted in the LORD without wavering. Prove me, O LORD, and try me; test my heart and my mind" (26:1-2). Appeal for vindication based on integrity. **PROVE... TRY... TEST** (בְּחָנֵנִי... נַסֵּנִי... צָרְפָה, bechaneni... nasseni... tzorfah) = refine like metal. Invites divine examination. "For your steadfast love is before my eyes, and I walk in your faithfulness" (26:3). **Steadfast love** (חֶסֶד, chesed) motivates obedience. "I do not sit with men of falsehood, nor do I consort with hypocrites. I hate the assembly of evildoers, and I will not sit with the wicked" (26:4-5). **Negative confession**—avoids wicked company. "I wash my hands in innocence and go around your altar, O LORD, proclaiming thanksgiving aloud, and telling all your wondrous deeds. O LORD, I love the habitation of your house and the place where your glory dwells" (26:6-8). **Liturgical participation**—worships at altar, loves God's house. "Do not sweep my soul away with sinners, nor my life with bloodthirsty men, in whose hands are evil devices, and whose right hands are full of bribes" (26:9-10). Prayer to be separated from wicked's fate. "But as for me, I shall walk in my integrity; redeem me, and be gracious to me. My foot stands on level ground; in the great assembly I will bless the LORD" (26:11-12). **Walk in integrity** (בְּתֻמִּי, betummi) = blameless way. **Level ground** = secure standing. Public praise in congregation.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Integrity before God and separation from evil*

## Psalm 27: The LORD is My Light

#### 27:1-6 — Confidence in God's Protection
**Lore:** "Of David" (superscription). **Two-part structure**: confidence (vv.1-6), lament (vv.7-14). "The LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? The LORD is the stronghold of my life; of whom shall I be afraid?" (27:1). **FAMOUS OPENING**—light metaphor (cf. John 1:4-5, 8:12). **Rhetorical questions** express confidence. "When evildoers assail me to eat up my flesh, my adversaries and foes, it is they who stumble and fall" (27:2). **Eat up my flesh**—predatory enemies (cf. 23:5, "table before enemies"). "Though an army encamp against me, my heart shall not fear; though war arise against me, yet I will be confident" (27:3). **Military threat** doesn't shake confidence. "One thing have I asked of the LORD, that will I seek after: that I may dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, to gaze upon the beauty of the LORD and to inquire in his temple" (27:4). **ONE THING**—singular focus on God's presence. **BEAUTY** (נֹעַם, no'am) = pleasantness, delight. Temple = place of seeking God. "For he will hide me in his shelter in the day of trouble; he will conceal me under the cover of his tent; he will lift me high upon a rock. And now my head shall be lifted up above my enemies all around me, and I will offer in his tent sacrifices with shouts of joy; I will sing and make melody to the LORD" (27:5-6). God's protection = shelter, tent, high rock. Worship response = sacrifices, shouts, singing.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: God as light, salvation, and refuge*

#### 27:7-14 — Do Not Hide Your Face
**Lore:** "Hear, O LORD, when I cry aloud; be gracious to me and answer me\! You have said, 'Seek my face.' My heart says to you, 'Your face, LORD, do I seek.' Hide not your face from me. Turn not your servant away in anger, O you who have been my help. Cast me not off; forsake me not, O God of my salvation\!" (27:7-9). **Shift to lament**—petition for God's presence. **Seek God's face**—divine invitation and human response. "For my father and my mother have forsaken me, but the LORD will take me in" (27:10). Even parental abandonment doesn't sever God's care. "Teach me your way, O LORD, and lead me on a level path because of my enemies. Give me not up to the will of my adversaries; for false witnesses have risen against me, and they breathe out violence" (27:11-12). Prayer for guidance amid false accusations. "I believe that I shall look upon the goodness of the LORD in the land of the living. Wait for the LORD; be strong, and let your heart take courage; wait for the LORD\!" (27:13-14). **FAITH DECLARATION**—confidence in seeing God's goodness. **WAIT** (קַוֵּה, qavveh) repeated—patient expectation. Self-exhortation to courage.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Seeking God's face with patient hope*

## Psalm 28: My Rock, Do Not Be Deaf

#### 28:1-5 — Cry for Help Against the Wicked
**Lore:** "Of David" (superscription). "To you, O LORD, I call; my rock, be not deaf to me, lest, if you be silent to me, I become like those who go down to the pit" (28:1). **ROCK** (צוּר, tzur)—God as solid foundation. Silence = death. "Hear the voice of my pleas for mercy, when I cry to you for help, when I lift up my hands toward your most holy sanctuary" (28:2). **Lifted hands** = prayer posture. **Most holy sanctuary** (דְּבִיר, devir) = Holy of Holies. "Do not drag me off with the wicked, with the workers of evil, who speak peace with their neighbors while evil is in their hearts" (28:3). Prayer not to share wicked's fate. **Hypocrisy** exposed—speak peace, harbor evil. "Give to them according to their work and according to the evil of their deeds; give to them according to the work of their hands; render them their due reward. Because they do not regard the works of the LORD or the work of his hands, he will tear them down and build them up no more" (28:4-5). **IMPRECATORY PRAYER**—retributive justice. Wicked ignore God's works, face judgment.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Plea to not share the wicked's fate*

#### 28:6-9 — Blessed Be the LORD
**Lore:** "Blessed be the LORD\! For he has heard the voice of my pleas for mercy. The LORD is my strength and my shield; in him my heart trusts, and I am helped; my heart exults, and with my song I give thanks to him" (28:6-7). **SUDDEN SHIFT** from lament to praise—God heard\! **Strength and shield**—protection and power. Heart trusts → helped → exults → sings. "The LORD is the strength of his people; he is the saving refuge of his anointed" (28:8). **Corporate application**—God saves His **anointed** (מָשִׁיחַ, mashiach = messiah/king). "Oh, save your people and bless your heritage\! Be their shepherd and carry them forever" (28:9). **Shepherd imagery** (cf. Ps 23)—God carries people like lambs (Isa 40:11). Personal lament becomes national intercession.
*Verses: 4 | Theme: From lament to praise for hearing*

## Psalm 29: The Voice of the LORD

#### 29:1-2 — Ascribe Glory to the LORD
**Lore:** "A Psalm of David" (superscription). **Theophanic hymn**—God's glory in storm. "Ascribe to the LORD, O heavenly beings, ascribe to the LORD glory and strength. Ascribe to the LORD the glory due his name; worship the LORD in the splendor of holiness" (29:1-2). Call to **heavenly beings** (בְּנֵי אֵלִים, benei elim = "sons of gods/mighty ones," divine council, cf. Ps 82, Job 1-2) to worship. **Splendor of holiness** (הַדְרַת־קֹדֶשׁ, hadrat-qodesh) = holy array/beauty.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Heavenly worship of God's glory*

#### 29:3-9 — The Voice of the LORD Over the Waters
**Lore:** "The voice of the LORD is over the waters; the God of glory thunders, the LORD, over many waters" (29:3). **VOICE OF THE LORD** (קוֹל יְהוָה, qol Yahweh) repeated **7 times** (vv.3,4,4,5,7,8,9)—perfect number. Voice = thunder in storm. "The voice of the LORD is powerful; the voice of the LORD is full of majesty. The voice of the LORD breaks the cedars; the LORD breaks the cedars of Lebanon" (29:4-5). Power and majesty displayed. **Cedars of Lebanon**—tallest, strongest trees, yet broken by God's voice. "He makes Lebanon to skip like a calf, and Sirion like a young wild ox" (29:6). **Lebanon and Sirion** (Mount Hermon) = mountains dance like calves—poetic hyperbole of God's power. "The voice of the LORD flashes forth flames of fire. The voice of the LORD shakes the wilderness; the LORD shakes the wilderness of Kadesh. The voice of the LORD makes the deer give birth and strips the forests bare, and in his temple all cry, 'Glory\!'" (29:7-9). Storm effects: lightning, earthquake, premature birth, deforestation. **Temple response**: "Glory\!" (כָּבוֹד, kavod). Creation's chaos becomes temple's worship.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: God's powerful voice in the storm*

#### 29:10-11 — The LORD Sits Enthroned
**Lore:** "The LORD sits enthroned over the flood; the LORD sits enthroned as king forever. May the LORD give strength to his people\! May the LORD bless his people with peace\!" (29:10-11). **Enthroned over flood** (מַבּוּל, mabbul)—same word as Noah's flood (Gen 6-9). God reigns over chaos. **PEACE** (שָׁלוֹם, shalom)—final word contrasts storm's violence. God's power brings peace to people. **Psalm structure**: Heavenly worship (vv.1-2) → God's powerful voice (vv.3-9) → God's reign and blessing (vv.10-11).
*Verses: 2 | Theme: God reigns and blesses with peace*

## Psalm 30: You Have Turned My Mourning Into Dancing

#### 30:1-5 — Thanksgiving for Healing
**Lore:** "A Psalm of David. A song at the dedication of the temple" (superscription). **Dedication of temple**—likely Solomon's temple (1 Kings 8) or rededication (Hanukkah tradition uses this psalm, though written before Maccabees). "I will extol you, O LORD, for you have drawn me up and have not let my foes rejoice over me. O LORD my God, I cried to you for help, and you have healed me. O LORD, you have brought up my soul from Sheol; you restored me to life from among those who go down to the pit" (30:1-3). **Thanksgiving for healing**—near-death experience. **Drawn up** (דִּלִּיתָנִי, dillitani) = like water from well. "Sing praises to the LORD, O you his saints, and give thanks to his holy name. For his anger is but for a moment, and his favor is for a lifetime. Weeping may tarry for the night, but joy comes with the morning" (30:4-5). **FAMOUS VERSE**—anger momentary, favor eternal. **Night weeping → morning joy** (cf. Pss 3-5, morning/evening cycle). God's favor outweighs His anger.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Thanksgiving for healing*

#### 30:6-12 — From Prosperity to Calamity to Restoration
**Lore:** "As for me, I said in my prosperity, 'I shall never be moved.' By your favor, O LORD, you made my mountain stand strong; you hid your face; I was dismayed" (30:6-7). **Prosperity breeds presumption**—"never moved" (cf. Prov 16:18, "Pride before fall"). God's hidden face = discipline. "To you, O LORD, I cry, and to the Lord I plead for mercy: 'What profit is there in my death, if I go down to the pit? Will the dust praise you? Will it tell of your faithfulness? Hear, O LORD, and be merciful to me\! O LORD, be my helper\!'" (30:8-10). Prayer during crisis—appeals to God's interests (dead don't praise). "You have turned for me my mourning into dancing; you have loosed my sackcloth and clothed me with gladness, that my glory may sing your praise and not be silent. O LORD my God, I will give thanks to you forever\!" (30:11-12). **TRANSFORMATION**—mourning → dancing, sackcloth → gladness. **Purpose**: eternal thanksgiving. **Psalm structure**: Crisis → Prayer → Healing → Praise. Personal testimony becomes liturgical praise.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: From mourning to dancing*

## Psalm 38: O LORD, Rebuke Me Not

#### 38:1-12 — Suffering Under Sin's Weight
**Lore:** "A Psalm of David, for the memorial offering" (superscription). Third Penitential Psalm. "O LORD, rebuke me not in your anger, nor discipline me in your wrath\! For your arrows have sunk into me, and your hand has come down on me" (38:1-2). Divine arrows = discipline. "For my iniquities have gone over my head; like a heavy burden, they are too heavy for me" (38:4). Sin as crushing weight. "I am utterly bowed down and prostrate; all the day I go about mourning" (38:6). Physical toll. "My friends and companions stand aloof from my plague, and my nearest kin stand far off" (38:11). Social isolation.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Sin's crushing burden*

#### 38:13-22 — I Wait for You, O LORD
**Lore:** "But I am like a deaf man; I do not hear, like a mute man who does not open his mouth" (38:13). Silent before accusers. "But for you, O LORD, do I wait; it is you, O LORD my God, who will answer" (38:15). Trust in God's answer. "Do not forsake me, O LORD\! O my God, be not far from me\! Make haste to help me, O Lord, my salvation\!" (38:21-22). Final plea for nearness and help.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Waiting for God's answer*

## Psalm 39: The Brevity of Life

#### 39:1-6 — Man is a Mere Breath
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: to Jeduthun. A Psalm of David" (superscription). **JEDUTHUN** = Levitical singer (1 Chr 16:41-42). "I said, 'I will guard my ways, that I may not sin with my tongue; I will guard my mouth with a muzzle, so long as the wicked are in my presence'" (39:1). Vow of silence. "Behold, you have made my days a few handbreadths, and my lifetime is as nothing before you. Surely all mankind stands as a mere breath\! Selah. Surely a man goes about as a shadow\! Surely for nothing they are in turmoil; man heaps up wealth and does not know who will gather\!" (39:5-6). **Life's brevity**—breath, shadow, vanity (cf. Ecclesiastes). Wealth meaningless.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Life's brevity*

#### 39:7-13 — My Hope is in You
**Lore:** "And now, O Lord, for what do I wait? My hope is in you. Deliver me from all my transgressions. Do not make me the scorn of the fool\!" (39:7-8). Hope in God alone. "Hear my prayer, O LORD, and give ear to my cry; hold not your peace at my tears\! For I am a sojourner with you, a guest, like all my fathers. Look away from me, that I may smile again, before I depart and am no more\!" (39:12-13). **Sojourner**—temporary resident (cf. Gen 47:9, 1 Chr 29:15). Prayer for respite before death.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Hope in God amid life's brevity*

## Psalm 40: I Waited Patiently for the LORD

#### 40:1-5 — He Put a New Song in My Mouth
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster. A Psalm of David" (superscription). "I waited patiently for the LORD; he inclined to me and heard my cry. He drew me up from the pit of destruction, out of the miry bog, and set my feet upon a rock, making my steps secure. He put a new song in my mouth, a song of praise to our God. Many will see and fear, and put their trust in the LORD" (40:1-3). **Deliverance testimony**—pit → rock. **NEW SONG** (cf. 33:3). "Blessed is the man who makes the LORD his trust, who does not turn to the proud, to those who go astray after a lie\!" (40:4). Trust in LORD brings blessing.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Patient waiting rewarded*

#### 40:6-10 — Sacrifice and Obedience
**Lore:** "In sacrifice and offering you have not delighted, but you have given me an open ear. Burnt offering and sin offering you have not required. Then I said, 'Behold, I have come; in the scroll of the book it is written of me: I delight to do your will, O my God; your law is within my heart'" (40:6-8, quoted Heb 10:5-7—Christ's incarnation and obedience). **Obedience over sacrifice** (1 Sam 15:22). **Messianic application**—Christ comes to do God's will. "I have told the glad news of deliverance in the great congregation; behold, I have not restrained my lips, as you know, O LORD" (40:9). Public testimony.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Obedience surpasses sacrifice*

#### 40:11-17 — Evils Have Encompassed Me
**Lore:** "For evils have encompassed me beyond number; my iniquities have overtaken me, and I cannot see; they are more than the hairs of my head; my heart fails me" (40:12). **Sudden shift** to lament—sins overwhelm. "Be pleased, O LORD, to deliver me\! O LORD, make haste to help me\!" (40:13). Urgent plea. "But I am poor and needy; may the Lord think of me. You are my help and my deliverer; do not delay, O my God\!" (40:17). **Poor and needy** (עָנִי וְאֶבְיוֹן, ani ve'evyon)—humble dependence. Psalm duplicated as Ps 70.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: From praise to urgent plea*

## Psalm 41: Blessed is the One Who Considers the Poor

#### 41:1-3 — Blessing for the Compassionate
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster. A Psalm of David" (superscription). **Final psalm of Book 1**. "Blessed is the one who considers the poor\! In the day of trouble the LORD delivers him; the LORD protects him and keeps him alive; he is called blessed in the land; you do not give him up to the will of his enemies. The LORD sustains him on his sickbed; in his illness you restore him to full health" (41:1-3). **Beatitude**—blessing for those who care for poor. Divine protection promised.
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Blessing for compassion*

#### 41:4-10 — Even My Close Friend Has Lifted His Heel
**Lore:** "As for me, I said, 'O LORD, be gracious to me; heal me, for I have sinned against you\!'" (41:4). Confession of sin. "My enemies say of me in malice, 'When will he die, and his name perish?' And when one comes to see me, he utters empty words, while his heart gathers iniquity; when he goes out, he tells it abroad. All who hate me whisper together about me; they imagine the worst for me" (41:5-7). Enemies wish death, spread rumors. "Even my close friend in whom I trusted, who ate my bread, has lifted his heel against me" (41:9, quoted John 13:18—Jesus applies to Judas' betrayal). **Lifted heel** = betrayal. Close friend's treachery.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Betrayal by close friend*

#### 41:11-13 — Doxology Closing Book 1
**Lore:** "By this I know that you delight in me: my enemy will not shout in triumph over me. But you have upheld me because of my integrity, and set me in your presence forever" (41:11-12). God's delight shown in vindication. Presence forever. "Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel, from everlasting to everlasting\! Amen and Amen" (41:13). **DOXOLOGY**—concludes Book 1 of Psalter. **From everlasting to everlasting** (מֵעוֹלָם וְעַד־עוֹלָם, me'olam ve'ad-olam) = eternal praise. **Double Amen**—emphatic affirmation.
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Eternal blessing to the LORD*
