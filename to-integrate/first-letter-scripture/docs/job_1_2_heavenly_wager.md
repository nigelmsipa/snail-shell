# Job 1-2: The Heavenly Wager

## Overview
The prose prologue establishes the cosmic conflict that frames the entire book. Job, a righteous man from Uz, becomes the subject of a heavenly legal challenge. Satan (הַשָּׂטָן, "the adversary/accuser") accuses Job of serving God only for blessings, not integrity. God permits two rounds of testing. Job loses wealth, children, and health, yet maintains his integrity: "Though He slay me, yet will I trust Him." Central themes: Cosmic conflict invisible to sufferers, Satan as accuser before God's throne, integrity tested through suffering, God's sovereignty over trials, faithful response to inexplicable loss.

## Chapter 1: The First Test

#### 1:1-5 — Blameless in Uz: The Righteous Man
**Lore:** "There was a man in the land of Uz, whose name was Job; and that man was perfect and upright, and one that feared God, and eschewed evil" (1:1). **Job** (אִיּוֹב, Iyyov) = uncertain etymology, possibly "persecuted" or "returning/repentant." **Land of Uz** (אֶרֶץ עוּץ, Eretz Utz) = likely Edom/northern Arabia (Lam 4:21, Jer 25:20). Four-fold description: 1) **Perfect** (תָּם, tam) = blameless, complete integrity (not sinless, but wholehearted devotion); 2) **Upright** (יָשָׁר, yashar) = straight, morally right; 3) **Feared God** (יְרֵא אֱלֹהִים, yere Elohim) = reverential awe, worship; 4) **Eschewed evil** (וְסָר מֵרָע, vesar mera) = actively turned from wickedness. God Himself uses identical language (1:8, 2:3), validating assessment. "And there were born unto him seven sons and three daughters. His substance also was seven thousand sheep, and three thousand camels, and five hundred yoke of oxen, and five hundred she asses, and a very great household; so that this man was the greatest of all the men of the east" (1:2-3). **Numerical symbolism**—seven sons (completeness, perfection), massive livestock (7,000 sheep, 3,000 camels, 500 oxen, 500 donkeys). "Greatest of all men of the east" (גָּדוֹל מִכָּל־בְּנֵי־קֶדֶם, gadol mikol-benei-qedem) = peak wealth, wisdom, honor. Patriarchal era (pre-Mosaic, no temple/priesthood). "And his sons went and feasted in their houses, every one his day; and sent and called for their three sisters to eat and to drink with them" (1:4). Family harmony—sons rotate hosting, include sisters (unusual honor for women). "And it was so, when the days of their feasting were gone about, that Job sent and sanctified them, and rose up early in the morning, and offered burnt offerings according to the number of them all: for Job said, It may be that my sons have sinned, and cursed God in their hearts. Thus did Job continually" (1:5). **Priestly intercession**—Job acts as family priest (patriarchal pattern: Abraham, Gen 22; Noah, Gen 8:20). "Sanctified them" (וַיְקַדְּשֵׁם, vayqaddeshem) = ritual purification. "Burnt offerings" (עֹלוֹת, olot) = whole animal consumed, atonement for potential sin. "Cursed God in their hearts" (בֵּרְכוּ אֱלֹהִים בִּלְבָבָם, berakhu Elohim bilvavam) = euphemism for blasphemy ("blessed" = "cursed" by scribal reverence). "Continually" (תָּמִיד, tamid) = habitual, regular practice. Job's righteousness = **proactive spiritual leadership**, not reactive damage control. **SETUP COMPLETE**: Establishes Job's unimpeachable righteousness BEFORE suffering begins. Refutes friends' later claim that calamity proves hidden sin. **Theological foundation**: If the righteous suffer, simplistic retribution theology ("sin = suffering, blessing = righteousness") collapses. Book explores mystery of divine providence beyond human formulas.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Righteousness*

#### 1:6-12 — The Heavenly Court: Satan's First Challenge
**Lore:** "Now there was a day when the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan came also among them" (1:6). **COSMIC SCENE**—shifts from earth to heaven. "Sons of God" (בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים, benei ha'Elohim) = angelic beings (cf. Job 38:7, Gen 6:2). "Present themselves" (לְהִתְיַצֵּב, lehityatzev) = stand before, report (royal court imagery). **Satan** (הַשָּׂטָן, HaSatan) = "the adversary/accuser" (definite article = title, not yet proper name). Not yet developed as "devil" of NT (that's later Jewish/Christian theology), but functions as **divine prosecutor**, testing covenant loyalty (cf. Zech 3:1-2, 1 Chr 21:1). "And the LORD said unto Satan, Whence comest thou? Then Satan answered the LORD, and said, From going to and fro in the earth, and from walking up and down in it" (1:7). God initiates dialogue. Satan's activity: "going to and fro" (מִשּׁוּט, misshut) = roaming, patrolling (1 Pet 5:8, "walketh about, seeking whom he may devour"). "And the LORD said unto Satan, Hast thou considered my servant Job, that there is none like him in the earth, a perfect and an upright man, one that feareth God, and escheweth evil?" (1:8). **GOD HIGHLIGHTS JOB**—not Satan's initiative! "Considered" (שַׂמְתָּ לִבְּךָ, samta libkha) = set your heart/mind on. God repeats four-fold description from 1:1. "None like him in earth"—unique righteousness. Why does God "point out" Job to Satan? Mystery, but establishes that test comes FROM God's sovereign will, not Satan's independent attack. "Then Satan answered the LORD, and said, Doth Job fear God for nought? Hast not thou made an hedge about him, and about his house, and about all that he hath on every side? thou hast blessed the work of his hands, and his substance is increased in the land. But put forth thine hand now, and touch all that he hath, and he will curse thee to thy face" (1:9-11). **THE ACCUSATION**—"Doth Job fear God for nought?" (הַחִנָּם, hachinam) = gratuitously, without cause/reward. Satan's thesis: Job's piety is **mercenary**, not genuine. Remove blessings ("hedge of protection"), Job will "curse thee to thy face" (יְבָרֶכְךָ אֶל־פָּנֶיךָ, yevarekha el-panekha) = blaspheme directly (euphemism again). Implies: 1) God "buys" loyalty with blessings; 2) True disinterested righteousness doesn't exist. If Satan correct, God's entire moral order is bribery, not covenant love. "And the LORD said unto Satan, Behold, all that he hath is in thy power; only upon himself put not forth thine hand. So Satan went forth from the presence of the LORD" (1:12). **GOD PERMITS TEST**—"in thy power" (בְּיָדֶךָ, beyadekha) = in your hand/control. Boundary: don't touch Job's person (yet). **Theological crisis**: Why does God "allow" Satan to destroy innocent man's life? Book never explains, but establishes: 1) Satan requires God's permission (limited power); 2) Suffering ≠ punishment here; 3) Cosmic conflict invisible to Job. **Pattern**: Suffering can serve purposes beyond human comprehension. Faith = trusting God's character when circumstances seem contradictory.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Accusation*

#### 1:13-22 — The Fourfold Calamity: "The LORD Gave"
**Lore:** "And there was a day when his sons and his daughters were eating and drinking wine in their eldest brother's house" (1:13). **Catastrophe begins**—family feast (1:4 pattern). "And there came a messenger unto Job, and said, The oxen were plowing, and the asses feeding beside them: And the Sabeans fell upon them, and took them away; yea, they have slain the servants with the edge of the sword; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee" (1:14-15). **First disaster**—Sabeans (שְׁבָא, Sheva, Arabian raiders) steal oxen/donkeys, kill servants. "While he was yet speaking, there came also another, and said, The fire of God is fallen from heaven, and hath burned up the sheep, and the servants, and consumed them; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee" (1:16). **Second disaster**—"fire of God" (אֵשׁ אֱלֹהִים, esh Elohim) = lightning (divine agency perceived by messenger, ironic given Satan's role). Sheep and shepherds consumed. "While he was yet speaking, there came also another, and said, The Chaldeans made out three bands, and fell upon the camels, and have carried them away, yea, and slain the servants with the edge of the sword; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee" (1:17). **Third disaster**—Chaldeans (כַּשְׂדִּים, Kasdim, Babylonian nomads) raid camels, kill servants. Pattern: messengers arrive in rapid succession, each sole survivor. Suspense builds. "While he was yet speaking, there came also another, and said, Thy sons and thy daughters were eating and drinking wine in their eldest brother's house: And, behold, there came a great wind from the wilderness, and smote the four corners of the house, and it fell upon the young men, and they are dead; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee" (1:18-19). **Fourth disaster—CLIMAX**—all ten children killed. "Great wind" (רוּחַ גְּדוֹלָה, ruach gedolah) from wilderness (tornado/sandstorm). House collapses. Ultimate loss: wealth replaceable, children irreplaceable. "Then Job arose, and rent his mantle, and shaved his head, and fell down upon the ground, and worshipped" (1:20). **Job's response**—ritual mourning: "rent mantle" (tore robe), "shaved head" (sign of grief). BUT: "worshipped" (וַיִּשְׁתָּחוּ, vayishtachu) = bowed in worship. "And said, Naked came I out of my mother's womb, and naked shall I return thither: the LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD" (1:21). **ICONIC VERSE**—profound theology: 1) "Naked came... naked return" (Job 1:21, cf. Eccl 5:15, 1 Tim 6:7)—own nothing ultimately; 2) "LORD gave, LORD taken away"—recognizes divine sovereignty (doesn't know Satan's role); 3) "Blessed be name of LORD" (יְהִי שֵׁם יְהוָה מְבֹרָךְ, yehi shem YHWH mevorakh) = praises God despite loss. **NOT** resignation, but worship. "In all this Job sinned not, nor charged God foolishly" (1:22). **VERDICT**—"sinned not" (לֹא־חָטָא, lo-chata), "nor charged God foolishly" (וְלֹא־נָתַן תִּפְלָה לֵאלֹהִים, velo-natan tiflah lEilohim) = didn't attribute unseemliness/wrong to God. **Satan's accusation FAILS**—Job's integrity proven genuine, not mercenary. Worship persists without "hedge."
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Integrity*

## Chapter 2: The Second Test

#### 2:1-6 — The Second Challenge: Touching Bone and Flesh
**Lore:** "Again there was a day when the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan came also among them to present himself before the LORD" (2:1). **Second heavenly scene**—same court, same prosecutor. "And the LORD said unto Satan, From whence comest thou? And Satan answered the LORD, and said, From going to and fro in the earth, and from walking up and down in it" (2:2). Identical exchange (1:7). "And the LORD said unto Satan, Hast thou considered my servant Job, that there is none like him in the earth, a perfect and an upright man, one that feareth God, and escheweth evil? and still he holdeth fast his integrity, although thou movedst me against him, to destroy him without cause" (2:3). **GOD VINDICATES JOB**—repeats four-fold description + adds: "still holdeth fast his integrity" (וְעֹדֶנּוּ מַחֲזִיק בְּתֻמָּתוֹ, veodenu machaziq betummato) = maintains blamelessness. **Key phrase**: "although thou movedst me against him, to destroy him without cause" (חִנָּם, chinnam) = gratuitously, same word Satan used (1:9). God acknowledges Job's suffering is "without cause"—refutes retribution theology preemptively. But raises mystery: why does God say Satan "moved" Him? Divine authorship/permission of suffering without explanation. "And Satan answered the LORD, and said, Skin for skin, yea, all that a man hath will he give for his life. But put forth thine hand now, and touch his bone and his flesh, and he will curse thee to thy face" (2:4-5). **Escalated accusation**—"Skin for skin" (עוֹר בְּעַד עוֹר, or be'ad or) = proverb meaning "self-preservation above all." Satan claims: Job sacrificed wealth/family to save himself. Touch his BODY ("bone and flesh"), he'll curse. Implies Job's worship (1:21) was selfish survival instinct. "And the LORD said unto Satan, Behold, he is in thine hand; but save his life" (2:6). **Second permission granted**—"in thine hand" (בְּיָדֶךָ, beyadekha), boundary: "save his life" (אַךְ אֶת־נַפְשׁוֹ שְׁמֹר, akh et-nafsho shemor) = preserve his soul/life. Can afflict body, can't kill. Why? Test must be survivable to prove integrity; also, dead Job can't choose worship/curse.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Escalation*

#### 2:7-10 — The Affliction: From Sole to Crown
**Lore:** "So went Satan forth from the presence of the LORD, and smote Job with sore boils from the sole of his foot unto his crown" (2:7). **Physical affliction**—"sore boils" (שְׁחִין רָע, shechin ra) = painful skin disease, possibly leprosy, elephantiasis, or pemphigus (blistering disorder). "From sole... unto crown" = total body coverage. Medical horror + social isolation (unclean). "And he took him a potsherd to scrape himself withal; and he sat down among the ashes" (2:8). "Potsherd" (חֶרֶשׂ, cheres) = broken pottery shard (scrapes pus/scabs). "Sat among ashes" (בְּתוֹךְ־הָאֵפֶר, betokh-ha'efer) = ash heap/garbage dump outside town (leper's location, ultimate degradation). Contrast: greatest man of East (1:3) → ash heap outcast. "Then said his wife unto him, Dost thou still retain thine integrity? curse God, and die" (2:9). **Wife's counsel**—"still retain integrity" (עֹדְךָ מַחֲזִיק בְּתֻמָּתֶךָ, odekha machaziq betummatekha) = echoes God's commendation (2:3), but she sees it as foolish stubbornness. "Curse God, and die" (בָּרֵךְ אֱלֹהִים וָמֻת, barekh Elohim vamut) = blaspheme and die (either by divine judgment or suicide by provoking God). She becomes Satan's unwitting mouthpiece—exact outcome Satan predicted (1:11, 2:5, "curse to thy face"). Later Jewish tradition calls her "Satan's tool" but text doesn't condemn her harshly—she's grieving mother who lost 10 children, watching husband rot alive. "But he said unto her, Thou speakest as one of the foolish women speaketh. What? shall we receive good at the hand of God, and shall we not receive evil? In all this did not Job sin with his lips" (2:10). **Job's response**—rebukes wife gently: "foolish women" (נְבָלוֹת, nevalot) = morally senseless, not intellectually stupid. **Key theology**: "Shall we receive good... and not... evil?" (הַגַּם אֶת־הַטּוֹב נְקַבֵּל מֵאֵת הָאֱלֹהִים וְאֶת־הָרָע לֹא נְקַבֵּל, hagam et-hatov neqabbel me'et ha'Elohim ve'et-hara lo neqabbel). Acknowledges God's sovereignty over both blessing and calamity (cf. Isa 45:7, "I form light and create darkness, I make peace and create evil"). **NOT** fatalism, but submission to divine prerogative. "Did not Job sin with his lips" (לֹא־חָטָא אִיּוֹב בִּשְׂפָתָיו, lo-chata Iyyov bisefatav) = maintained verbal integrity. **Satan's second accusation FAILS**—Job doesn't curse God even under physical torment. Integrity genuine, proven through maximum suffering. YET: "with his lips" foreshadows coming struggle—Job will speak, and his words (Ch 3ff) reveal inner torment even as he doesn't formally "curse God."
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Submission*

#### 2:11-13 — The Arrival of the Comforters: Silent Grief
**Lore:** "Now when Job's three friends heard of all this evil that was come upon him, they came every one from his own place; Eliphaz the Temanite, and Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite: for they had made an appointment together to come to mourn with him and to comfort him" (2:11). **Three friends introduced**—1) **Eliphaz the Temanite** (אֱלִיפַז הַתֵּימָנִי, Eliphaz haTeimani) = from Teman, Edom (Gen 36:11, Edomite wisdom tradition, Jer 49:7). Name = "God is fine gold." 2) **Bildad the Shuhite** (בִּלְדַּד הַשּׁוּחִי, Bildad haShuhi) = from Shuah (Gen 25:2, Abraham's descendant via Keturah). Name = "son of contention." 3) **Zophar the Naamathite** (צוֹפַר הַנַּעֲמָתִי, Tzofar haNa'amati) = from Naamah (unknown location, possibly Arabia). Name = "chirping bird." All sages from "east" (wisdom tradition, 1 Kings 4:30). Came "to mourn... and comfort" (לָנוּד לוֹ וּלְנַחֲמוֹ, lanud lo ulnachamo) = express sympathy and console. **Good intention**—not villains initially, but "miserable comforters" (16:2) due to false theology. "And when they lifted up their eyes afar off, and knew him not, they lifted up their voice, and wept; and they rent every one his mantle, and sprinkled dust upon their heads toward heaven" (2:12). **Recognition scene**—"knew him not" (לֹא הִכִּירֻהוּ, lo hikiruhu) = unrecognizable (disease disfigured him). Ritual mourning: tore robes, sprinkled dust/ashes on heads (grief gestures, Josh 7:6, Lam 2:10). "So they sat down with him upon the ground seven days and seven nights, and none spake a word unto him: for they saw that his grief was very great" (2:13). **Seven days of silence**—traditional mourning period (Gen 50:10, 1 Sam 31:13). "None spake" (וְאֵין־דֹּבֵר אֵלָיו דָּבָר, ve'ein dover elav davar) = total silence. Why? "Grief was very great" (כִּי־גָדוֹל הַכְּאֵב מְאֹד, ki-gadol hake'ev me'od) = pain beyond words. **THIS is true comfort**—silent presence, shared grief (cf. Rom 12:15, "weep with those who weep"). Later, when they SPEAK, they become "miserable comforters." **Lesson**: Ministry of presence > ministry of explanation. Silence often more pastoral than words. **Transition**: Silence ends when JOB speaks (Ch 3), breaking forth in anguished lament. Friends' silence was gift; their speeches (Ch 4-31) are curse. Prologue (prose) complete. Poetic dialogue (Ch 3-41) begins.
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Presence*
