# Job 15-21: The Second Cycle — Accusations Intensify, Hope Emerges

## Overview
The second dialogue cycle escalates the conflict. The friends abandon pastoral gentleness for harsh accusation; Job abandons hope in earthly vindication but grasps at cosmic hope. **Eliphaz** moves from mystical experience to direct accusation of Job's wickedness. **Job** declares God his enemy yet proclaims his heavenly Witness and Redeemer. **Bildad** paints vivid pictures of the wicked's doom. **Zophar** doubles down on retribution theology. Job responds with devastating empirical evidence: the wicked prosper. Central themes: Escalation of conflict, Job's isolation intensifies, proto-Christology emerges (Witness in heaven 16:19, Redeemer lives 19:25), tension between present suffering and future vindication, destruction of simplistic retribution theology through observation.

## Chapter 15: Eliphaz's Second Speech — The Wicked in Torment

#### 15:1-6 — Eliphaz Accuses: Your Own Mouth Condemns You
**Lore:** "Then answered Eliphaz the Temanite, and said, Should a wise man utter vain knowledge, and fill his belly with the east wind?" (15:1-2). **Second cycle begins**—friends abandon gentleness. "East wind" (קָדִים, qadim) = scorching sirocco from desert, destructive. Metaphor: Job's words hot air, damaging, empty. "For thy mouth uttereth thine iniquity, and thou choosest the tongue of the crafty" (15:5). **Direct accusation shift**—Cycle 1: "Perhaps you sinned unknowingly." Cycle 2: "You ARE wicked." "Thine own mouth condemneth thee, and not I: yea, thine own lips testify against thee" (15:6). Eliphaz weaponizes Job's lament (Ch 3) as proof of guilt. Twisted logic: suffering proves sin, complaint proves wickedness. **Theological danger**: Confusing lament with rebellion. Psalms of lament (22, 88) show complaint ≠ sin. Eliphaz lacks pastoral discernment—cannot distinguish grief from guilt. Cross-reference: Jesus' honest Gethsemane prayer (Matt 26:38-39) = godly lament. NT: "Cast all your anxiety on him because he cares for you" (1 Pet 5:7).
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Accusation*

#### 15:7-16 — Are You the First Man Born? Wisdom Belongs to the Aged
**Lore:** "Art thou the first man that was born? or wast thou made before the hills?" (15:7). **Sarcastic challenge**—"Are you primordial Adam? Were you at creation?" (cf. Prov 8:22-31, Wisdom personified present at creation). Eliphaz mocks Job's claims to wisdom. "Hast thou heard the secret of God? and dost thou restrain wisdom to thyself?" (15:8). "Secret of God" (סוֹד אֱלוֹהַּ, sod Eloah) = divine council (cf. Job 1:6-12, Ps 82:1, Jer 23:18). Ironic: Eliphaz doesn't know heavenly wager either. "What knowest thou, that we know not? what understandest thou, which is not in us? With us are both the grayheaded and very aged men, much elder than thy father" (15:9-10). **Appeal to tradition**—Eliphaz's authority = age/tradition, not revelation. Problem: traditions can be wrong (cf. Job 32:9, Elihu challenges age = wisdom). "What is man, that he should be clean? and he that is born of a woman, that he should be righteous?" (15:14). **Anthropological pessimism**—echoes Eliphaz's 4:17. True: humans sinful (Rom 3:23). False inference: all suffering = specific sin. Behold, he putteth no trust in his saints; yea, the heavens are not clean in his sight. How much more abominable and filthy is man, which drinketh iniquity like water?" (15:15-16). Exaggerated rhetoric—"drinks iniquity like water" (implies compulsive sinfulness). Missing: grace, redemption, God's pleasure in His children (Zeph 3:17). NT contrast: "saints" trusted because of Christ's righteousness (2 Cor 5:21).
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Tradition*

#### 15:17-35 — The Wicked Man Travails in Pain All His Days
**Lore:** "I will shew thee, hear me; and that which I have seen I will declare; Which wise men have told from their fathers, and have not hid it" (15:17-18). **Appeal to inherited wisdom**—Eliphaz doubles down on tradition. "The wicked man travaileth with pain all his days, and the number of years is hidden to the oppressor. A dreadful sound is in his ears: in prosperity the destroyer shall come upon him" (15:20-21). **Retribution theology redux**—wicked = constant torment, even in prosperity (paranoia). Psychological torment precedes physical. "Trouble and anguish shall make him afraid; they shall prevail against him, as a king ready to the battle" (15:24). Vivid imagery: wicked besieged like city under attack. "For he stretcheth out his hand against God, and strengtheneth himself against the Almighty. He runneth upon him, even on his neck, upon the thick bosses of his bucklers" (15:25-26). **Military metaphor**—wicked charge God like warrior attacking superior force. Eliphaz's implicit accusation: Job doing this through complaint. "Because he covereth his face with his fatness, and maketh collops of fat on his flanks" (15:27). Obesity = greed, prosperity leading to arrogance. "He shall not be rich, neither shall his substance continue, neither shall he prolong the perfection thereof upon the earth" (15:29). Ironically describes Job's PAST state (was rich, lost all). Eliphaz's speech boomerangs—condemns Job using Job's own story without realizing it. "He shall not depart out of darkness; the flame shall dry up his branches, and by the breath of his mouth shall he go away. Let not him that is deceived trust in vanity: for vanity shall be his recompence" (15:30-31). "Breath of his mouth" (רוּחַ פִּיו, ruach piv) = God's destroying word. Cf. 2 Thess 2:8, Christ destroys lawless one with breath. "He shall shake off his unripe grape as the vine, and shall cast off his flower as the olive. For the congregation of hypocrites shall be desolate, and fire shall consume the tabernacles of bribery. They conceive mischief, and bring forth vanity, and their belly prepareth deceit" (15:33-35). **Botanical + pregnancy metaphors**—wicked barren/aborted. "Conceive mischief, bring forth vanity" echoes Ps 7:14, Isa 59:4. **Eliphaz's error**: Assumes Job's suffering MUST fit this pattern. **Job's challenge (Ch 21)**: Wicked DON'T always suffer. Retribution theology too simplistic.
*Verses: 19 | Theme: Retribution*

## Chapter 16-17: Job's Response — Miserable Comforters, Heavenly Witness

#### 16:1-5 — Miserable Comforters: I Could Speak as You Do
**Lore:** "Then Job answered and said, I have heard many such things: miserable comforters are ye all" (16:1-2). **Famous indictment**—"miserable comforters" (מְנַחֲמֵי עָמָל, menachamei amal) = "comforters of trouble" (oxymoron). They increase pain instead of relieving it. Pastoral failure. "Shall vain words have an end? or what emboldeneth thee that thou answerest?" (16:3). Job turns Eliphaz's "east wind" accusation (15:2) back on him. "I also could speak as ye do: if your soul were in my soul's stead, I could heap up words against you, and shake mine head at you" (16:4). **Empathy test**—"I could condemn you too IF I lacked compassion." Job demonstrates moral superiority: restraint vs. friends' harshness. "But I would strengthen you with my mouth, and the moving of my lips should asswage your grief" (16:5). **Contrast**: Job would comfort, not condemn. Model for pastoral care: bear burdens (Gal 6:2), weep with those who weep (Rom 12:15), don't add to suffering. Friends violate Prov 25:20: "As he that taketh away a garment in cold weather... so is he that singeth songs to an heavy heart." **Lesson**: theology must be pastorally applied, not wielded as weapon.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Compassion*

#### 16:6-14 — God Has Made Me His Target: I Am Torn and Shattered
**Lore:** "Though I speak, my grief is not asswaged: and though I forbear, what am I eased?" (16:6). **Inescapable anguish**—speaking doesn't help, silence doesn't help. Total entrapment. "But now he hath made me weary: thou hast made desolate all my company" (16:7). Direct address to God (shifts from 3rd to 2nd person). "Thou hast made desolate" = God responsible for isolation. "And thou hast filled me with wrinkles, which is a witness against me: and my leanness rising up in me beareth witness to my face" (16:8). Physical deterioration interpreted as divine testimony of guilt. Body becomes evidence in cosmic trial. "He teareth me in his wrath, who hateth me: he gnasheth upon me with his teeth; mine enemy sharpeneth his eyes upon me" (16:9). **God as enemy**—"teareth" (יִטְרֹף, yitrov, like wild animal), "gnasheth teeth" (like Ps 37:12), "sharpeneth eyes" (hostile glare). Job experiences God as predator. Theologically shocking but emotionally honest. "They have gaped upon me with their mouth; they have smitten me upon the cheek reproachfully; they have gathered themselves together against me" (16:10). Shifts to human enemies (friends + mockers). Cf. Christ's Passion: struck on cheek (Matt 26:67), mocked by crowd. Job = type of Christ in suffering. "God hath delivered me to the ungodly, and turned me over into the hands of the wicked. I was at ease, but he hath broken me asunder: he hath also taken me by my neck, and shaken me to pieces, and set me up for his mark. His archers compass me round about, he cleaveth my reins asunder, and doth not spare; he poureth out my gall upon the ground" (16:11-13). **Violent imagery cascade**—broken, shaken, target for arrows, kidneys split, gall (bile) poured out. "Archers compass me round" (warriors surrounding). Job = battlefield, God = attacker. "He breaketh me with breach upon breach, he runneth upon me like a giant" (16:14). Military siege metaphor—repeated assaults. "Runneth upon me like a giant" (גִּבּוֹר, gibbor) = mighty warrior. God depicted as overwhelming military force. **Theological tension**: How can God be both loving Father and attacking giant? Job holds both truths in tension (won't curse God, but won't lie about experience). Preparation for Incarnation: God enters suffering, not just observes it.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Assault*

#### 16:15-17 — I Have Sewed Sackcloth and Defiled My Horn in the Dust
**Lore:** "I have sewed sackcloth upon my skin, and defiled my horn in the dust" (16:15). **Mourning posture**—"sewed sackcloth" (permanent, not temporary). "Horn" (קֶרֶן, qeren) = symbol of strength/dignity (Ps 89:17, 92:10). "Defiled in dust" = strength humiliated. "My face is foul with weeping, and on my eyelids is the shadow of death; Not for any injustice in mine hands: also my prayer is pure" (16:16-17). **Protestation of innocence**—"shadow of death" (צַלְמָוֶת, tzalmavet, deep darkness). "No injustice in hands" echoes Ps 24:4 (clean hands). "Prayer is pure" (זַכָּה, zakkah) = morally clean. Job maintains integrity despite appearance. Friends judge by sight (suffering = guilt), Job judges by conscience. NT: God judges heart (1 Sam 16:7). **Key theological point**: Suffering doesn't prove sin. Prosperity gospel refuted—righteous CAN suffer (cf. Heb 11:36-38, martyrs).
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Innocence*

#### 16:18-22 — O Earth, Cover Not My Blood: My Witness Is in Heaven
**Lore:** "O earth, cover not thou my blood, and let my cry have no place" (16:18). **Blood cries from ground**—allusion to Gen 4:10 (Abel's blood cries out). Job invokes murdered innocent paradigm. Anticipates martyrdom theology (Rev 6:10, souls under altar crying "How long?"). "Also now, behold, my witness is in heaven, and my record is on high" (16:19). **PROTO-CHRISTOLOGY**—"witness in heaven" (עֵדִי בַשָּׁמַיִם, edi bashamayim). Who is this witness? Options: (1) God Himself as witness against Himself (paradox Job lives in); (2) Heavenly advocate/mediator (foreshadows Christ, 1 Tim 2:5). Job gropes toward mediator concept (9:33, needed umpire; now heavenly witness). "My friends scorn me: but mine eye poureth out tears unto God. O that one might plead for a man with God, as a man pleadeth for his neighbour!" (16:20-21). **Mediator longing intensifies**—"one might plead" (יוֹכִיחַ, yokhiach) = legal advocate. "For a man with God" = representing human before divine court. Christ fulfills: fully God, fully man, mediates between (Heb 7:25, "always lives to intercede"). "When a few years are come, then I shall go the way whence I shall not return" (16:22). Mortality clock ticking—needs vindication before death. OT Sheol = no return (cf. 7:9). Urgency: justice delayed = justice denied if death intervenes. NT answer: resurrection ensures justice even beyond grave (Rom 8:34, Christ risen intercedes).
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Witness*

#### 17:1-9 — My Days Are Extinct, the Graves Are Ready for Me
**Lore:** "My breath is corrupt, my days are extinct, the graves are ready for me" (17:1). **Death imminent**—"breath corrupt" (רוּחִי חֻבָּלָה, ruchi chubalah, spirit destroyed). "Graves ready" (plural, קְבָרִים, qevarim) = grave awaits eagerly. "Are there not mockers with me? and doth not mine eye continue in their provocation?" (17:2). Friends = mockers. "Eye continue in provocation" = constantly witnessing their hostility. "Lay down now, put me in a surety with thee; who is he that will strike hands with me?" (17:3). **Legal appeal**—"put me in surety" (עָרְבֵנִי, arveni) = provide legal guarantee/pledge. "Strike hands" = covenant gesture. Job asks God to guarantee justice. Cf. Heb 7:22, Jesus = "surety of a better covenant." "For thou hast hid their heart from understanding: therefore shalt thou not exalt them" (17:4). Friends' hardness of heart from God (judicial hardening, cf. Rom 1:24, God "gave them over"). Won't be exalted = vindication denied to them. "He that speaketh flattery to his friends, even the eyes of his children shall fail" (17:5). Proverb: betraying friends brings family curse. "He hath made me also a byword of the people; and aforetime I was as a tabret" (17:6). "Byword" (מָשָׁל, mashal) = proverbial example of cursed man. "Tabret" (תֹּפֶת, tofet) = object of spitting/contempt. Public humiliation. "Mine eye also is dim by reason of sorrow, and all my members are as a shadow" (17:7). Physical decline from grief—"dim eye", "members as shadow" (wasting away). "Upright men shall be astonied at this, and the innocent shall stir up himself against the hypocrite" (17:8). Future vindication hope—"upright astonished" at Job's suffering, "innocent stirred up" against friends (called hypocrites). Job prophesies his vindication will expose friends' error. "The righteous also shall hold on his way, and he that hath clean hands shall be stronger and stronger" (17:9). **Perseverance promise**—despite suffering, righteous continue. "Clean hands stronger" (faith refined through trial, cf. 23:10, "when he hath tried me, I shall come forth as gold"). NT: Heb 10:36, "need endurance"; James 1:3-4, testing produces perseverance.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Vindication*

#### 17:10-16 — Where Then Is My Hope? Who Shall See It?
**Lore:** "But as for you all, do ye return, and come now: for I cannot find one wise man among you" (17:10). **Dismissal of friends**—"do ye return" = leave, your wisdom useless. "Not one wise man" = total rejection of their counsel. "My days are past, my purposes are broken off, even the thoughts of my heart" (17:11). Dreams/plans shattered. "They change the night into day: the light is short because of darkness" (17:12). Friends' claims (light/hope near) false—darkness remains. "If I wait, the grave is mine house: I have made my bed in the darkness. I have said to corruption, Thou art my father: to the worm, Thou art my mother, and my sister" (17:13-14). **Death intimacy**—grave = house, corruption/worms = family. Morbid but realistic. Reversal of normal family: instead of children, has worms. "And where is now my hope? as for my hope, who shall see it? They shall go down to the bars of the pit, when our rest together is in the dust" (17:15-16). **Despair peak**—hope itself will die with him in Sheol. "Bars of pit" (בַּדֵּי־שְׁאוֹל, baddei-sheol) = gates of underworld locked. "Rest together in dust" = hope and Job buried together. **Theological nadir**—yet Job hasn't cursed God. Honest despair within faith. Prefigures Christ's "Why have you forsaken me?" (Ps 22:1, Matt 27:46)—anguish without abandonment. Job's hope WILL be vindicated (Ch 42), but in darkness doesn't see it. Faith = "assurance of things hoped for, evidence of things not seen" (Heb 11:1).
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Despair*

## Chapter 18: Bildad's Second Speech — The Fate of the Wicked

#### 18:1-4 — How Long Till You Stop Talking? We Are Not Beasts
**Lore:** "Then answered Bildad the Shuhite, and said, How long will it be ere ye make an end of words? mark, and afterwards we will speak" (18:1-2). **Impatience**—"How long?" Bildad frustrated. "Make an end of words" = stop talking already. "Mark" (consider our wisdom). "Wherefore are we counted as beasts, and reputed vile in your sight?" (18:3). Offended by Job's dismissal (17:10). "Counted as beasts" (בְּהֵמָה, behemah) = stupid animals. Job's rhetoric wounded their pride. "He teareth himself in his anger: shall the earth be forsaken for thee? and shall the rock be removed out of his place?" (18:4). Rhetorical question: Should natural order change for you? Implies Job arrogant to expect special treatment. Ironic: Job hasn't asked for special treatment, asked for justice. Bildad strawmans Job's position.
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Impatience*

#### 18:5-21 — The Light of the Wicked Shall Be Put Out
**Lore:** "Yea, the light of the wicked shall be put out, and the spark of his fire shall not shine" (18:5). **Light vs. darkness theme**—wicked's lamp extinguished (cf. Prov 13:9, 24:20). Bildad's thesis: Job suffering = lamp out = proves wickedness. "The light shall be dark in his tabernacle, and his candle shall be put out with him. The steps of his strength shall be straitened, and his own counsel shall cast him down" (18:6-7). Domestic and personal ruin. "His own counsel" = self-destruction. "For he is cast into a net by his own feet, and he walketh upon a snare. The gin shall take him by the heel, and the robber shall prevail against him. The snare is laid for him in the ground, and a trap for him in the way" (18:8-10). **Hunting metaphors cascade**—net, snare, gin, trap. Six different Hebrew trap words. Wicked can't escape, ensnared everywhere. Bildad implies Job trapped by own sin. "Terrors shall make him afraid on every side, and shall drive him to his feet. His strength shall be hungerbitten, and destruction shall be ready at his side. It shall devour the strength of his skin: even the firstborn of death shall devour his strength" (18:11-13). "Terrors on every side" (מְבַלְּהִים, mebalehim) = panic. "Firstborn of death" (בְּכוֹר מָוֶת, bekhor mavet) = disease/plague personified as Death's eldest son. Vivid mythology. "His confidence shall be rooted out of his tabernacle, and it shall bring him to the king of terrors. It shall dwell in his tabernacle, because it is none of his: brimstone shall be scattered upon his habitation" (18:14-15). "King of terrors" (מֶלֶךְ בַּלָּהוֹת, melekh ballahot) = Death personified as monarch. "Brimstone" (גָּפְרִית, gafrit) = Sodom/Gomorrah judgment (Gen 19:24). "His roots shall be dried up beneath, and above shall his branch be cut off. His remembrance shall perish from the earth, and he shall have no name in the street. He shall be driven from light into darkness, and chased out of the world" (18:16-18). Botanical + social death—roots dead, branches cut, name forgotten. Ultimate curse: no legacy. "He shall neither have son nor nephew among his people, nor any remaining in his dwellings. They that come after him shall be astonied at his day, as they that went before were affrighted" (18:19-20). **No descendants**—greatest OT curse. "Astonied" = horrified at his fate. "Surely such are the dwellings of the wicked, and this is the place of him that knoweth not God" (18:21). **Conclusion**: This MUST be Job (circular reasoning). **Bildad's error**: Vivid description of wicked's fate ≠ proof Job wicked. **Job's counter (Ch 21)**: Wicked DON'T always suffer this way. Bildad theorizes, Job observes reality.
*Verses: 17 | Theme: Darkness*

## Chapter 19: Job's Response — I Know That My Redeemer Lives

#### 19:1-6 — How Long Will You Torment Me and Break Me With Words?
**Lore:** "Then Job answered and said, How long will ye vex my soul, and break me in pieces with words?" (19:1-2). Echoes Bildad's "How long?" (18:2) but with opposite meaning. Friends' words weapons. "Break in pieces" (תְּדַכְּאוּנַנִי, tedakkunani) = crush/pulverize. "These ten times have ye reproached me: ye are not ashamed that ye make yourselves strange to me" (19:3). "Ten times" = complete/many (idiom, cf. Gen 31:7). "Make yourselves strange" (תַּכְלִימוּנִי, takhlimuni) = harden/estrange. Friends distant. "And be it indeed that I have erred, mine error remaineth with myself" (19:4). Conditional: "Even IF I sinned, it's my business, not yours." Proper boundaries. "If indeed ye will magnify yourselves against me, and plead against me my reproach: Know now that God hath overthrown me, and hath compassed me with his net" (19:5-6). **Surprising admission**—"God overthrew me." Not friends = enemy, God = enemy. God's net (vs. wicked's net in 18:8-10). Job trapped by divine hunter, not own sin. Bildad's hunting metaphors turned against friends' theology.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Torment*

#### 19:7-12 — Behold, I Cry 'Violence!' But I Am Not Heard
**Lore:** "Behold, I cry out of wrong, but I am not heard: I cry aloud, but there is no judgment" (19:7). "Cry out of wrong" (חָמָס, chamas, violence/injustice). Legal cry for help (Hab 1:2). No answer. Cf. Ps 22:2, "I cry... but thou hearest not." Christ's experience. "He hath fenced up my way that I cannot pass, and he hath set darkness in my paths. He hath stripped me of my glory, and taken the crown from my head" (19:8-9). God blocks path, removes honor. "Crown" (עֲטֶרֶת, ateret) = dignity/status. "He hath destroyed me on every side, and I am gone: and mine hope hath he removed like a tree. He hath also kindled his wrath against me, and he counteth me unto him as one of his enemies" (19:10-11). Total destruction. "Hope removed like tree" uprooted. **God counts Job as enemy**—theological crisis. Covenant relationship felt severed. "His troops come together, and raise up their way against me, and encamp round about my tabernacle" (19:12). Military siege imagery—God's army surrounds Job. Spiritual warfare experienced as God attacking, not Satan (ironic: Satan = attacker via God's permission, Job perceives God as source).
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Injustice*

#### 19:13-22 — He Has Put My Family Far From Me; All My Close Friends Detest Me
**Lore:** "He hath put my brethren far from me, and mine acquaintance are verily estranged from me" (19:13). **Social isolation**—family estranged. God blamed (He put them far). "My kinsfolk have failed, and my familiar friends have forgotten me. They that dwell in mine house, and my maids, count me for a stranger: I am an alien in their sight" (19:14-15). Servants treat him as foreigner. Total status reversal. "I called my servant, and he gave me no answer; I intreated him with my mouth. My breath is strange to my wife, and I intreated for the children's sake of mine own body" (19:16-17). **Domestic humiliation**—servant ignores master, wife repulsed by breath (disease-caused), children mock (children of concubines? or street children?). "Yea, young children despised me; I arose, and they spake against me. All my inward friends abhorred me: and they whom I loved are turned against me" (19:18-19). "Young children despised" = lowest social status (children show contempt). "Inward friends" (אַנְשֵׁי סוֹדִי, anshei sodi, men of my council) = closest advisors. Psalm 41:9 echo: "mine own familiar friend... lifted up his heel against me" (cited re: Judas, John 13:18). Job = type of betrayed Christ. "My bone cleaveth to my skin and to my flesh, and I am escaped with the skin of my teeth" (19:20). **Physical emaciation**—"bone cleaveth to skin" = skeletal. "Skin of my teeth" = proverbial (barely survived, narrow escape). "Have pity upon me, have pity upon me, O ye my friends; for the hand of God hath touched me" (19:21). Repeated plea: "have pity." "Hand of God touched me" (not Satan, Satan invisible to Job). Touch = strike (euphemism). "Why do ye persecute me as God, and are not satisfied with my flesh?" (19:22). Friends persecute like God persecutes (both attacking). "Not satisfied with flesh" = vultures consuming corpse. Friends tearing at him verbally.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Isolation*

#### 19:23-27 — I Know That My Redeemer Lives!
**Lore:** "Oh that my words were now written! oh that they were printed in a book! That they were graven with an iron pen and lead in the rock for ever!" (19:23-24). **Longing for permanence**—wants testimony preserved forever. "Graven with iron pen and lead in rock" = ancient inscription method. Ironically, Job's words ARE preserved in Scripture (fulfilled wish). **"For I know that my redeemer liveth, and that he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth: And though after my skin worms destroy this body, yet in my flesh shall I see God: Whom I shall see for myself, and mine eyes shall behold, and not another; though my reins be consumed within me"** (19:25-27). **MOST IMPORTANT OT CHRISTOLOGICAL PROPHECY**—"my redeemer" (גֹּאֲלִי, go'ali) = kinsman-redeemer (Hebrew goel). Legal role: family member who avenges blood (Num 35:19), redeems land (Lev 25:25), marries widow (Ruth 3:9-13, Boaz = goel). **Christ = ultimate Goel**—kinsman (became human, Heb 2:14), redeemer (bought us back, 1 Pet 1:18-19), avenger (executes justice). "Liveth" (חָי, chai) = alive now and forever (Christ's eternal life, Rev 1:18). "Stand at latter day upon earth" (עַל־עָפָר, al-afar, upon dust) = resurrection day, Christ returns to earth. "After my skin worms destroy this body" = AFTER death and decay. "Yet in my flesh shall I see God" (וּמִבְּשָׂרִי אֶחֱזֶה אֱלוֹהַּ, umivsari echezeh Eloah) = **BODILY RESURRECTION**. Not just spiritual immortality, physical resurrection. NT: 1 Cor 15:52, bodies raised imperishable. Job glimpses resurrection truth 2000+ years before Christ. "Whom I shall see for myself" = personal, direct vision. "Mine eyes shall behold" = literal sight. "Not another" = not through mediator, face-to-face (1 Cor 13:12, "then face to face"). "My reins consumed" (kidneys = seat of emotion) = even inner being consumed, yet resurrection. **Theological breakthrough**—Job transcends OT Sheol theology, grasps future hope beyond grave. Validates present suffering with future vindication. Faith anchor when present unbearable. Cross-reference: Martha's confession (John 11:24, "I know he will rise again in resurrection at last day") echoes Job. Christ = resurrection and life (John 11:25).
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Redeemer*

#### 19:28-29 — Why Do We Persecute Him? The Root of the Matter Is in Him
**Lore:** "But ye should say, Why persecute we him, seeing the root of the matter is found in me?" (19:28). After resurrection hope, returns to address friends. "Root of matter in me" = Job has substance/truth. Friends should examine themselves. "Be ye afraid of the sword: for wrath bringeth the punishments of the sword, that ye may know there is a judgment" (19:29). **Warning to friends**—divine judgment coming for false accusers. "Sword" (חֶרֶב, cherev) = God's wrath. "That ye may know there is judgment" (יֵשׁ־דִּין, yesh-din) = there IS justice (friends claim justice automatic; Job warns justice will catch THEM). Prophetic: Ch 42:7-8, God angry with friends, requires Job's intercession for forgiveness. Job vindicated, friends corrected. **Lesson**: Misrepresenting God = serious sin. Speaking FOR God requires accuracy (James 3:1, teachers judged strictly).
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Judgment*

## Chapter 20: Zophar's Second Speech — The Wicked's Triumph Is Short

#### 20:1-11 — The Triumph of the Wicked Is Short
**Lore:** "Then answered Zophar the Naamathite, and said, Therefore do my thoughts cause me to answer, and for this I make haste" (20:1-2). Zophar offended, impatient. "For this I make haste" = eager to refute Job. "For I have heard the check of my reproach, and the spirit of my understanding causeth me to answer" (20:3). "Check of my reproach" = feels insulted. "Spirit of understanding" = claims wisdom, but will demonstrate folly. "Knowest thou not this of old, since man was placed upon earth, That the triumphing of the wicked is short, and the joy of the hypocrite but for a moment?" (20:4-5). **Zophar's thesis**—wicked prosper temporarily only. True IF "wicked" and "short" defined properly. False if assumes all suffering = wickedness, or all wicked suffer immediately. "Though his excellency mount up to the heavens, and his head reach unto the clouds; Yet he shall perish for ever like his own dung: they which have seen him shall say, Where is he?" (20:6-7). Vivid imagery—"perish like dung" (גָּלָל, galal). Exalted → humiliated. "He shall fly away as a dream, and shall not be found: yea, he shall be chased away as a vision of the night. The eye also which saw him shall see him no more; neither shall his place any more behold him" (20:8-9). Ephemeral existence. "His children shall seek to please the poor, and his hands shall restore their goods. His bones are full of the sin of his youth, which shall lie down with him in the dust" (20:10-11). Generational consequences—children pay restitution. "Bones full of sin" = inner corruption manifests physically. Zophar assumes Job's suffering = this pattern. **Error**: Job's children already dead (not seeking to please poor), Job didn't hoard wealth (was generous, Ch 29).
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Brevity*

#### 20:12-23 — He Swallows Riches But Vomits Them; God Casts Them Out
**Lore:** "Though wickedness be sweet in his mouth, though he hide it under his tongue; Though he spare it, and forsake it not; but keep it still within his mouth: Yet his meat in his bowels is turned, it is the gall of asps within him" (20:12-14). **Food metaphor**—sin sweet initially ("hide under tongue" like savoring), becomes poison ("gall of asps," snake venom). "He hath swallowed down riches, and he shall vomit them up again: God shall cast them out of his belly. He shall suck the poison of asps: the viper's tongue shall slay him" (20:15-16). Riches swallowed but vomited—can't keep ill-gotten gain. Poison motif continues. "He shall not see the rivers, the floods, the brooks of honey and butter. That which he laboured for shall he restore, and shall not swallow it down: according to his substance shall the restitution be, and he shall not rejoice therein" (20:17-18). Denied enjoyment of prosperity. "Rivers of honey and butter" = abundance (Promised Land imagery, Exod 3:8). Wicked excluded. "Because he hath oppressed and hath forsaken the poor; because he hath violently taken away an house which he builded not; Surely he shall not feel quietness in his belly, he shall not save of that which he desired" (20:19-20). Specific sins: oppression, abandoning poor, property theft. Inner restlessness. "There shall none of his meat be left; therefore shall no man look for his goods. In the fulness of his sufficiency he shall be in straits: every hand of the wicked shall come upon him" (20:21-22). Peak prosperity → sudden collapse. "When he is about to fill his belly, God shall cast the fury of his wrath upon him, and shall rain it upon him while he is eating" (20:23). Judgment during feast—prosperity interrupted by wrath. **Zophar's implication**: Job feasting when calamity struck (true, Job 1:13-19, but irrelevant to guilt/innocence). Correlation ≠ causation.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Poison*

#### 20:24-29 — The Heavens Shall Reveal His Iniquity
**Lore:** "He shall flee from the iron weapon, and the bow of steel shall strike him through. It is drawn, and cometh out of the body; yea, the glittering sword cometh out of his gall: terrors are upon him" (20:24-25). **Violent death imagery**—arrow through body. "Glittering sword from gall" (bitter death). "All darkness shall be hid in his secret places: a fire not blown shall consume him; it shall go ill with him that is left in his tabernacle. The heaven shall reveal his iniquity; and the earth shall rise up against him" (20:26-27). Cosmic witness—heaven reveals sin, earth rises against (creation itself opposes wicked). "Fire not blown" = divine fire, spontaneous (cf. Lev 10:2, Nadab/Abihu; 2 Kgs 1:10, Elijah calls fire). "The increase of his house shall depart, and his goods shall flow away in the day of his wrath. This is the portion of a wicked man from God, and the heritage appointed unto him by God" (20:28-29). **Conclusion**—"portion" (חֵלֶק, cheleq) and "heritage" (נַחֲלָה, nachalah) ironic: instead of inheritance, gets destruction. "From God" = divine decree. **Zophar's error**: Assumes Job's losses = this divine judgment. **Missing**: Possibility of testing (as actual case), Satan's role, mystery of suffering. **Job's response (Ch 21)**: Empirically false—wicked DO prosper long-term.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Wrath*

## Chapter 21: Job's Response — The Wicked Do Prosper

#### 21:1-6 — Hear My Speech; Let This Be Your Consolation
**Lore:** "But Job answered and said, Hear diligently my speech, and let this be your consolations" (21:1-2). Sarcastic—"this consolation" = proving you wrong. "Suffer me that I may speak; and after that I have spoken, mock on" (21:3). Permission to speak, acknowledges they'll mock anyway. "As for me, is my complaint to man? and if it were so, why should not my spirit be troubled? Mark me, and be astonished, and lay your hand upon your mouth" (21:4-5). "Complaint to man?" = No, complaint to God (higher court). "Be astonished, lay hand on mouth" = be shocked into silence (cf. 40:4). "Even when I remember I am afraid, and trembling taketh hold on my flesh" (21:6). What he's about to say terrifies him (challenges divine justice as friends present it).
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Challenge*

#### 21:7-16 — Why Do the Wicked Live, Grow Old, and Prosper?
**Lore:** "Wherefore do the wicked live, become old, yea, are mighty in power?" (21:7). **Central question**—demolishes friends' retribution theology. Observable fact: wicked DO prosper. "Their seed is established in their sight with them, and their offspring before their eyes. Their houses are safe from fear, neither is the rod of God upon them" (21:8-9). Wicked have children (vs. 18:19, Bildad claimed no offspring), safe houses (vs. 18:15, Bildad claimed brimstone), no divine rod (vs. friends' claim God strikes wicked). "Their bull gendereth, and faileth not; their cow calveth, and casteth not her calf. They send forth their little ones like a flock, and their children dance. They take the timbrel and harp, and rejoice at the sound of the organ" (21:10-12). Pastoral + domestic prosperity—fertile livestock, many healthy children, music/celebration. Contradicts Zophar's "no enjoyment" (20:17-18). "They spend their days in wealth, and in a moment go down to the grave. Therefore they say unto God, Depart from us; for we desire not the knowledge of thy ways" (21:13-14). **Shocking claim**—wicked TELL God to leave, don't want His ways, YET prosper lifelong and die quickly (not lingering suffering). "What is the Almighty, that we should serve him? and what profit should we have, if we pray unto him?" (21:15). Wicked's utilitarian theology—serve God only if profitable. Yet they prosper WITHOUT serving! Contradicts retribution theology's core claim. "Lo, their good is not in their hand: the counsel of the wicked is far from me" (21:16). Job distances self—not endorsing wickedness, just observing reality. **Theological honesty**—Job won't lie about what he sees to preserve friends' neat system.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Prosperity*

#### 21:17-21 — How Often Is the Wicked's Candle Put Out?
**Lore:** "How oft is the candle of the wicked put out? and how oft cometh their destruction upon them? God distributeth sorrows in his anger" (21:17). Rhetorical question: "How often?" Answer: NOT often. Bildad claimed wicked's lamp extinguished (18:5-6). Job: RARELY happens. "They are as stubble before the wind, and as chaff that the storm carrieth away" (21:18). Friends' claim. Job sarcastically quotes their position. "God layeth up his iniquity for his children: he rewardeth him, and he shall know it" (21:19). Friends' claim: generational punishment (Exod 20:5). Job's counter (next verse): "Let his own eyes see his destruction, and let him drink of the wrath of the Almighty. For what pleasure hath he in his house after him, when the number of his months is cut off in the midst?" (21:20-21). Job's counter-theology—punishment SHOULD be personal, not transferred to innocent children. Wicked dead doesn't care about children's fate. Moral: punishment must affect the guilty themselves to be just. **Anticipates Ezekiel 18**—"soul who sins shall die," not punish children for father's sin. More just theology than friends'.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Justice*

#### 21:22-26 — Shall Any Teach God Knowledge? One Dies in Strength, Another in Bitterness
**Lore:** "Shall any teach God knowledge? seeing he judgeth those that are high" (21:22). Rhetorical—who teaches God? Yet friends claim to know God's ways perfectly. Arrogance. "One dieth in his full strength, being wholly at ease and quiet. His breasts are full of milk, and his bones are moistened with marrow. And another dieth in the bitterness of his soul, and never eateth with pleasure" (21:23-25). **Empirical observation**—death's randomness. Prosperous die prosperous, bitter die bitter. No correlation between virtue and death circumstances. "They shall lie down alike in the dust, and the worms shall cover them" (21:26). **Equality in death**—rich and poor, righteous and wicked, all decay alike. Ecclesiastes theme (Eccl 2:14-16, 3:19-20). Death as equalizer doesn't prove moral order in life. **Challenge to retribution theology**—if God judges, why don't outcomes match morality?
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Equality*

#### 21:27-34 — I Know Your Thoughts; Your Answers Remain Falsehood
**Lore:** "Behold, I know your thoughts, and the devices which ye wrongfully imagine against me. For ye say, Where is the house of the prince? and where are the dwelling places of the wicked?" (21:27-28). Job reads their minds—they think his lost house proves wickedness. "Have ye not asked them that go by the way? and do ye not know their tokens, That the wicked is reserved to the day of destruction? they shall be brought forth to the day of wrath" (21:29-30). **Appeal to travelers/empirical witnesses**—anyone observing world knows wicked often escape present judgment, reserved for FUTURE "day of wrath." Job articulates delayed judgment theology (more biblical than immediate retribution). Rom 2:5, "storing up wrath for day of wrath." "Who shall declare his way to his face? and who shall repay him what he hath done? Yet shall he be brought to the grave, and shall remain in the tomb. The clods of the valley shall be sweet unto him, and every man shall draw after him, as there are innumerable before him" (21:31-33). Wicked dies unchallenged, honored funeral, peaceful grave ("clods sweet"), large funeral procession ("every man draw after him"). Ultimate irony: wicked honored in death. "How then comfort ye me in vain, seeing in your answers there remaineth falsehood?" (21:34). **Final indictment**—"comfort in vain," "answers remain falsehood" (כָּזָב, kazav, lie). Friends not just wrong, but dishonest (claim certainty where reality ambiguous). **Job's contribution**: Honesty about suffering's mystery > false certainty. Prepares for God's answer (Ch 38ff)—mystery embraced, not explained away. NT: Rom 11:33, "How unsearchable his judgments!" Mystery ≠ meaninglessness.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Falsehood*

---

**JMP 4 Summary**: Second cycle intensifies conflict—friends harsher, Job more isolated but more profound. Eliphaz accuses directly, Bildad paints vivid wicked's doom, Zophar doubles down on retribution. Job responds with miserable comforters indictment, heavenly Witness hope, **Redeemer lives** breakthrough (proto-resurrection), empirical demolition of retribution theology (wicked DO prosper). Theological center: Mystery of suffering can't be solved by simplistic formulas. Hope anchored not in present justice but future vindication through Redeemer. Christ fulfills: Witness (1 Tim 2:5), Redeemer (1 Pet 1:18-19), Resurrected (1 Cor 15:20). Job's suffering foreshadows Christ's (isolation, false accusation, physical torment, cry "Why?"), and Job's hope foreshadows Christian hope (bodily resurrection, face-to-face vision of God).
