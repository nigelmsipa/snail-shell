# Nehemiah 1-2: Burden and Mission

## Overview
Nehemiah, cupbearer to Artaxerxes, hears devastating news about Jerusalem's broken walls and burned gates. His response: weeping, fasting, and profound prayer of corporate confession. God stirs the king's heart to grant Nehemiah letters of safe passage, timber, and authority to rebuild. Secret night inspection reveals the true extent of devastation. Opposition begins immediately (Sanballat, Tobiah). Central themes: Prayer as prerequisite for action, divine providence moving pagan hearts, wise assessment before public announcement, burden transformed into vision.

---

## Chapter 1: The Weeping Cupbearer

#### 1:1-11 — Burden Turned to Prayer: The Cupbearer's Intercession
**Lore:** "The words of Nehemiah the son of Hacaliah. And it came to pass in the month Chisleu, in the twentieth year, as I was in Shushan the palace, that Hanani, one of my brethren, came, he and certain men of Judah; and I asked them concerning the Jews that had escaped, which were left of the captivity, and concerning Jerusalem" (1:1-2). **Nehemiah** (נְחֶמְיָה, Nechemyah) = "Yahweh comforts"—name foreshadows his role. Twentieth year of Artaxerxes (445 BC), ninety-one years after Cyrus's decree (Ezra 1). Nehemiah serves as **cupbearer** (מַשְׁקֶה, mashqeh)—trusted advisor who tastes wine (poison protection), intimate access to king. Asks about "the Jews... and Jerusalem"—burden already present. "They said unto me, The remnant that are left of the captivity there in the province are in great affliction and reproach: the wall of Jerusalem also is broken down, and the gates thereof are burned with fire" (1:3). **BURDEN RECEIVED**—"affliction" (רָעָה, ra'ah) = disaster, calamity; "reproach" (חֶרְפָּה, cherpah) = disgrace, shame. Broken walls = military vulnerability + spiritual humiliation. Ezra's temple stands (rebuilt 516 BC), but city defenseless. "And it came to pass, when I heard these words, that I sat down and wept, and mourned certain days, and fasted, and prayed before the God of heaven" (1:4). **VISCERAL RESPONSE**—parallel to Ezra's grief (Ezra 9:3-4). "Sat down" = stunned silence. "Mourned certain days" (plural)—extended grieving, not momentary emotion. Fasting + prayer = spiritual preparation for mission. "And said, I beseech thee, O LORD God of heaven, the great and terrible God, that keepeth covenant and mercy for them that love him and observe his commandments" (1:5). **Covenant appeal**—"great and terrible" (הַגָּדוֹל וְהַנּוֹרָא, hagadol vehanora) = awesome, inspiring fear (Deut 7:21). "Keepeth covenant" (שֹׁמֵר הַבְּרִית, shomer habrit)—God is faithful even when Israel isn't. "Let thine ear now be attentive, and thine eyes open, that thou mayest hear the prayer of thy servant, which I pray before thee now, day and night, for the children of Israel thy servants, and confess the sins of the children of Israel, which we have sinned against thee: both I and my father's house have sinned" (1:6). **CORPORATE CONFESSION**—"we have sinned," "I... have sinned" (though Nehemiah personally guiltless of temple destruction). Priestly intercession = identify with people's guilt (cf. Daniel 9:5, Ezra 9:6). Prays "day and night"—persistent intercession (cf. Luke 18:1-8, parable of persistent widow). "We have dealt very corruptly against thee, and have not kept the commandments, nor the statutes, nor the judgments, which thou commandedst thy servant Moses" (1:7). Acknowledges comprehensive failure—commandments (mitzvot), statutes (chuqqim), judgments (mishpatim). "Remember, I beseech thee, the word that thou commandedst thy servant Moses, saying, If ye transgress, I will scatter you abroad among the nations: But if ye turn unto me, and keep my commandments, and do them; though there were of you cast out unto the uttermost part of the heaven, yet will I gather them from thence, and will bring them unto the place that I have chosen to set my name there" (1:8-9). **COVENANT PROMISES**—quotes Deut 30:1-5 (promise of return after exile). "Though there were of you cast out unto the uttermost part of the heaven"—Nehemiah in Persia = fulfillment of scattering. Now claims promise of gathering. "Now these are thy servants and thy people, whom thou hast redeemed by thy great power, and by thy strong hand. O Lord, I beseech thee, let now thine ear be attentive to the prayer of thy servant, and to the prayer of thy servants, who desire to fear thy name: and prosper, I pray thee, thy servant this day, and grant him mercy in the sight of this man. For I was the king's cupbearer" (1:10-11). **PETITION FOR FAVOR**—"this man" = Artaxerxes. Doesn't ask directly for mission yet, prays for favor. Final note: "I was the king's cupbearer"—strategic position, providential placement. God positioned Nehemiah (like Joseph in Egypt, Esther in Persia) for such a time. **LEADERSHIP THEOLOGY**: Burden → Prayer → Tears → Fasting → Confession → Petition. Restoration begins with broken heart, not building plan.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Intercession*

## Chapter 2: The King's Hand

#### 2:1-10 — Providence and Permission: The King's Favor
**Lore:** "And it came to pass in the month Nisan, in the twentieth year of Artaxerxes the king, that wine was before him: and I took up the wine, and gave it unto the king. Now I had not been beforetime sad in his presence" (2:1). Four months pass from Chislev (1:1) to Nisan (March/April 445 BC)—Nehemiah continues praying without acting prematurely. "I had not been sad... in presence"—palace protocol forbids showing negative emotion before Persian king (could be seen as disloyalty or bad omen). To appear sad = death risk (cf. Esther 4:2, no mourning clothes in king's gate). "Wherefore the king said unto me, Why is thy countenance sad, seeing thou art not sick? this is nothing else but sorrow of heart. Then I was very sore afraid" (2:2). **King notices**—"very sore afraid" (וָאִירָא הַרְבֵּה מְאֹד, va'ira harbeh me'od) = exceedingly terrified. Nehemiah's burden now visible despite attempts to hide. Providential "discovery"—God orchestrates timing. "And said unto the king, Let the king live for ever: why should not my countenance be sad, when the city, the place of my fathers' sepulchres, lieth waste, and the gates thereof are consumed with fire?" (2:3). **Courageous honesty**—appeals to Persian respect for ancestral burial sites. Doesn't say "Jerusalem" initially (avoid triggering political suspicion—Ezra 4:12-16 accused Jews of rebellion). "Then the king said unto me, For what dost thou make request? So I prayed to the God of heaven" (2:4). **FLASH PRAYER**—king asks what Nehemiah wants. Before answering, **"I prayed to the God of heaven"**—silent, instant prayer (contrast with Chapter 1's extended prayer). Pattern: long prayers in private, quick prayers in crisis. Both necessary. Nehemiah's entire leadership: alternating between action and prayer. "And I said unto the king, If it please the king, and if thy servant have found favour in thy sight, that thou wouldest send me unto Judah, unto the city of my fathers' sepulchres, that I may build it" (2:5). **Direct request**—now says "Judah," still avoids "Jerusalem" by name. "City of my fathers' sepulchres" = respectful framing. "Build it" (וְאֶבְנֶנָּה, ve'evnenah)—Nehemiah will personally lead work, not remote management. "And the king said unto me, (the queen also sitting by him,) For how long shall thy journey be? and when wilt thou return? So it pleased the king to send me; and I set him a time" (2:6). **Permission granted**—parenthetical note about queen suggests she influenced king's favor (cf. Esther). King asks practical questions: duration, return date. "I set him a time"—Nehemiah plans temporary governorship (serves 12 years, Neh 5:14, then returns to Persia, Neh 13:6). "Moreover I said unto the king, If it please the king, let letters be given me to the governors beyond the river, that they may convey me over till I come into Judah; And a letter unto Asaph the keeper of the king's forest, that he may give me timber to make beams for the gates of the palace which appertained to the house, and for the wall of the city, and for the house that I shall enter into. And the king granted me, according to the good hand of my God upon me" (2:7-8). **Detailed requests**—Nehemiah prepared. Three needs: 1) Letters of safe passage through provinces (official travel documents); 2) Timber from royal forest (cedars for gates, beams); 3) Materials for personal residence. "Good hand of my God upon me" (כְּיַד־אֱלֹהַי הַטּוֹבָה עָלָי, keyad-Elohai hatovah alai)—**DIVINE FAVOR** recurring phrase (Ezra 7:6, 9, 28; 8:18). God moves king's heart to grant excessive generosity. **PROVIDENCE THEOLOGY**: Same God who stirred Cyrus (Ezra 1:1) now stirs Artaxerxes. "The king's heart is in the hand of the LORD, as the rivers of water: he turneth it whithersoever he will" (Prov 21:1). "Then I came to the governors beyond the river, and gave them the king's letters. Now the king had sent captains of the army and horsemen with me" (2:9). Military escort provided (contrast Ezra 8:22, who refused escort to demonstrate faith). No contradiction—Nehemiah's mission = political (rebuilding fortified city), Ezra's = spiritual (teaching Law). Different contexts, different decisions. Both honor God. "But when Sanballat the Horonite, and Tobiah the servant, the Ammonite, heard of it, it grieved them exceedingly that there was come a man to seek the welfare of the children of Israel" (2:10). **OPPOSITION INTRODUCED**—first mention of Nehemiah's archenemies. **Sanballat** (סַנְבַלַּט, Sanballat) = governor of Samaria (rival region). "Horonite" = from Beth-horon or Horonaim (Moabite city). **Tobiah** (טוֹבִיָּה, Toviyah) = "Yahweh is good" (ironic—has Hebrew name but opposes God's work). "The Ammonite"—descendant of Lot (Gen 19:38), excluded from assembly (Deut 23:3). "The servant" (הָעֶבֶד, ha'eved) = Persian official or derogatory term (slave). "Grieved them exceedingly" (וַיֵּרַע לָהֶם רָעָה גְדֹלָה, vayera lahem ra'ah gedolah) = deeply displeased, evil seemed great to them. Why? Political power threatened (Samaritan influence over Judah declining). **SPIRITUAL OPPOSITION**—foreshadows spiritual warfare (Eph 6:12). Satan opposes God's building projects. Pattern: Whenever God moves, enemy mobilizes.

#### 2:11-20 — Secret Assessment by Night: Wisdom Before Action
**Lore:** "So I came to Jerusalem, and was there three days" (2:11). **Three days**—same pattern as Ezra (Ezra 8:32). Rest, acclimatization, prayer, observation. Doesn't rush. "And I arose in the night, I and some few men with me; neither told I any man what my God had put in my heart to do at Jerusalem: neither was there any beast with me, save the beast that I rode upon" (2:12). **SECRET NIGHT INSPECTION**—key leadership decision. "What my God had put in my heart" (אֲשֶׁר אֱלֹהַי נֹתֵן אֶל־לִבִּי, asher Elohai noten el-libi)—divine vision internalized. Doesn't announce plan publicly before assessing reality. Why secrecy? 1) Avoid premature opposition; 2) Verify scope before committing; 3) Prevent discouragement if situation worse than reported; 4) Wisdom = gather data before declaring strategy. "Few men"—trusted advisors only. "Save the beast that I rode upon"—single mount, minimal noise. Stealth mission. "And I went out by night by the gate of the valley, even before the dragon well, and to the dung port, and viewed the walls of Jerusalem, which were broken down, and the gates thereof were consumed with fire" (2:13). **Detailed inspection**—"gate of the valley" (southwest), "dragon well" (unknown location, possibly En-rogel), "dung port" (refuse gate, south). "Viewed" (שֹׂבֵר, sover) = examined carefully, assessed damage. Walls "broken down" (פְּרוּצִים, perutzim) = breached, gaps everywhere. Gates "consumed" (אֻכְּלוּ, ukhlu) = devoured by fire (from Babylonian destruction 586 BC, 141 years prior—still in ruins!). "Then I went on to the gate of the fountain, and to the king's pool: but there was no place for the beast that was under me to pass" (2:14). So much rubble, even animal can't navigate. Ruins worse than expected. "Then went I up in the night by the brook, and viewed the wall, and turned back, and entered by the gate of the valley, and so returned" (2:15). Completes circuit—full assessment of perimeter. "Brook" = Kidron Valley (east side). Night inspection prevents enemies seeing Nehemiah's reaction to devastation. "And the rulers knew not whither I went, or what I did; neither had I as yet told it to the Jews, nor to the priests, nor to the nobles, nor to the rulers, nor to the rest that did the work" (2:16). **Total secrecy maintained**—even Jewish leaders unaware. "As yet"—implies he will tell them, but timing matters. Premature announcement = premature opposition. "Then said I unto them, Ye see the distress that we are in, how Jerusalem lieth waste, and the gates thereof are burned with fire: come, and let us build up the wall of Jerusalem, that we be no more a reproach" (2:17). **PUBLIC ANNOUNCEMENT**—now Nehemiah speaks. Note: "Ye see"—makes them acknowledge reality themselves (not Nehemiah's opinion). "The distress that WE are in"—identifies with people's shame. "Reproach" (חֶרְפָּה, cherpah)—same word from his prayer (1:3). Broken walls = public disgrace. Invitation: "Come, let us build"—inclusive, corporate project. Goal: remove reproach. "Then I told them of the hand of my God which was good upon me; as also the king's words that he had spoken unto me. And they said, Let us rise up and build. So they strengthened their hands for this good work" (2:18). **Testimony of God's favor**—recounts answered prayer, king's provision. Evidence of divine backing = crucial for mobilizing people. "Let us rise up and build" (נָקוּם וּבָנִינוּ, naqum uvaninu)—immediate, unified response. "Strengthened their hands" (וַיְחַזְּקוּ יְדֵיהֶם, vayechazzequ yedehem)—courageous resolve (same phrase in Ezra 6:22). "Good work" (מְלָאכָה הַטּוֹבָה, melakhah hatovah)—not just construction, but God's work. "But when Sanballat the Horonite, and Tobiah the servant, the Ammonite, and Geshem the Arabian, heard it, they laughed us to scorn, and despised us, and said, What is this thing that ye do? will ye rebel against the king?" (2:19). **Opposition escalates**—now adds **Geshem the Arabian** (גֶּשֶׁם הָעַרְבִי, Geshem ha'Aravi), likely ruler of Arabian territories south of Judah. Three-front opposition (north, east, south)—geographically surrounded. "Laughed us to scorn" (וַיַּלְעִגוּ, vayal'igu) = mocked, ridiculed. "Despised us" (וַיִּבְזוּ, vayivzu) = treated with contempt. Accusation: "Will ye rebel?"—same charge from Ezra 4:12-16 (previously halted rebuilding). False accusation, but politically dangerous. "Then answered I them, and said unto them, The God of heaven, he will prosper us; therefore we his servants will arise and build: but ye have no portion, nor right, nor memorial, in Jerusalem" (2:20). **Nehemiah's response**—short, decisive, theological. "God of heaven will prosper us"—faith declaration (cf. 1:5, 2:4). "We his servants"—identity as covenant people. "Will arise and build"—restates commitment despite mockery. Boundary: "Ye have NO portion, nor right, nor memorial, in Jerusalem." Three terms: 1) **Portion** (חֵלֶק, cheleq) = share, inheritance; 2) **Right** (צְדָקָה, tzedaqah) = legal claim; 3) **Memorial** (זִכָּרוֹן, zikaron) = remembered stake, historical connection. Enemies are outsiders—no covenant standing. **LEADERSHIP LESSON**: 1) Pray long before acting (Ch 1); 2) Wait for divine timing (4 months); 3) Flash-pray in moment of opportunity (2:4); 4) Assess reality secretly before public announcement (night inspection); 5) Cast vision with evidence of God's favor (king's letters); 6) Respond to mockers with faith, not defensiveness. Nehemiah = model of prayerful, wise, bold leadership.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Wisdom*
