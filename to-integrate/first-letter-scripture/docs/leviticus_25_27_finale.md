# Leviticus 25-27: Jubilee, Covenant Consequences, and Vows

### Chapters 25: Sabbath Year and Jubilee

#### 25:1-7 — Covenant with Creation: Sabbath Year
**Lore:** "The LORD spoke to Moses on Mount Sinai, saying, 'Speak to the people of Israel and say to them, When you come into the land that I give you, the land shall keep a Sabbath to the LORD'" (25:1-2). Even the land observes Sabbath! "For six years you shall sow your field, and for six years you shall prune your vineyard and gather in its fruits, but in the seventh year there shall be a Sabbath of solemn rest for the land, a Sabbath to the LORD. You shall not sow your field or prune your vineyard" (25:3-4). Every seventh year, the land lies fallow—agricultural rest. "What grows of itself in your harvest you shall not reap, and the grapes of your undressed vine you shall not gather; it shall be a year of solemn rest for the land. The Sabbath of the land shall provide food for you, for yourself and for your male and female slaves and for your hired worker and the sojourner who lives with you, and for your cattle and for the wild animals that are in your land: all its yield shall be for food" (25:5-7). Voluntary growth may be eaten by all—humans and animals—but no organized harvesting or storing. This tests faith: Will you trust God to provide in the sixth year enough for three years (sixth, seventh Sabbath year, eighth until new harvest)? Exodus 23:10-11 mentions the Sabbath year; Leviticus expands it. Deuteronomy 15:1-11 adds debt cancellation every seventh year. The Sabbath year declares God as the ultimate owner of land and people. It prevents soil exhaustion, protects the poor (who can glean during Sabbath year), and breaks economic cycles. Disobedience brings exile—the land will finally get its Sabbaths (Leviticus 26:34-35, fulfilled in Babylonian captivity, 2 Chronicles 36:21).
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Land Sabbath*

#### 25:8-17 — The Year of Jubilee
**Lore:** "You shall count seven weeks of years, seven times seven years, so that the time of the seven weeks of years shall give you forty-nine years. Then you shall sound the loud trumpet on the tenth day of the seventh month. On the Day of Atonement you shall sound the trumpet throughout all your land. And you shall consecrate the fiftieth year, and proclaim liberty (דְּרוֹר, deror, "release, freedom") throughout the land to all its inhabitants. It shall be a jubilee (יוֹבֵל, yovel, "ram's horn, jubilee") for you, when each of you shall return to his property and each of you shall return to his clan'" (25:8-10). The Jubilee—most radical economic law in Scripture. After seven cycles of seven years (49 years), the 50th year is Jubilee. Proclaimed on Yom Kippur with trumpet blasts. Three mandates: (1) Land returns to original families (25:10, 13). (2) Slaves/servants are freed (25:10, 40-41). (3) Debts are canceled (implied). Isaiah 61:1-2 echoes Jubilee language: "The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me...to proclaim liberty to the captives...to proclaim the year of the LORD's favor." Jesus reads this in the Nazareth synagogue (Luke 4:18-19), declaring, "Today this scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing" (Luke 4:21). Jesus inaugurates the ultimate Jubilee—spiritual liberation. "In this year of jubilee each of you shall return to his property" (25:13). Land sales are actually leases until Jubilee. "You shall not wrong one another, but you shall fear your God, for I am the LORD your God" (25:17). Economic fairness rooted in reverence for God. The Jubilee prevents permanent poverty and ensures land stays within tribal inheritance—fulfilling God's land distribution (Joshua 13-21). Did Israel ever observe Jubilee? No clear biblical evidence. The principle remains: God alone owns; we are stewards. Typology: Christ's ministry is Jubilee—freedom from sin's slavery, restoration of inheritance (Ephesians 1:14), release from debt (Colossians 2:14).
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Jubilee liberation*

#### 25:18-34 — Redemption and Release: Land and Vows
**Lore:** "Therefore you shall do my statutes and keep my rules and perform them, and then you will dwell in the land securely. The land will yield its fruit, and you will eat your fill and dwell in it securely" (25:18-19). Obedience = security. "And if you say, 'What shall we eat in the seventh year, if we may not sow or gather in our crop?' I will command my blessing on you in the sixth year, so that it will produce a crop sufficient for three years" (25:20-21). God promises supernatural provision—the sixth year's harvest sustains through Sabbath year and eighth year planting/harvest. **Land redemption** (25:23-28): "The land shall not be sold in perpetuity, for the land is mine. For you are strangers and sojourners with me" (25:23). Ultimate ownership: God's. Humans are tenants. If someone sells land due to poverty, a near kinsman may redeem it (25:25). If no redeemer, and the seller prospers, he may buy it back, calculating years until Jubilee (25:26-27). If unable to redeem, the land returns at Jubilee (25:28). **Houses** (25:29-31): A house in a walled city may be redeemed within one year of sale; after that, it belongs permanently to the buyer (25:29-30). But village houses (unwalled) are treated like fields—redeemable anytime, returning at Jubilee (25:31). **Levitical cities** (25:32-34): Levites may redeem their houses in Levitical cities at any time; at Jubilee, they return (25:32-33). Pasturelands of Levitical cities may never be sold—perpetual possession (25:34). The laws ensure land stays within families and tribes, preventing concentration of wealth. The kinsman-redeemer (גֹּאֵל, goel) foreshadows Christ, our Redeemer who buys us back (Ruth 3:9, 4:1-10; Job 19:25; Isaiah 59:20). Peter: "You were ransomed...with the precious blood of Christ" (1 Peter 1:18-19).
*Verses: 17 | Theme: Redemption laws*

#### 25:35-55 — Mercy to the Marginalized: Poor and Servants
**Lore:** "If your brother becomes poor and cannot maintain himself with you, you shall support him as though he were a stranger and a sojourner, and he shall live with you" (25:35). Care for the poor is mandatory. "Take no interest (נֶשֶׁךְ, neshekh, "usury, bite") from him or profit, but fear your God, that your brother may live beside you. You shall not lend him your money at interest, nor give him your food for profit" (25:36-37). No charging interest to fellow Israelites—economics motivated by compassion, not profit. "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt to give you the land of Canaan, and to be your God" (25:38). Redemption memory grounds ethics. **Israelite servitude** (25:39-43): If a fellow Israelite sells himself due to poverty, don't treat him as a slave but as a hired worker. He serves until Jubilee, then goes free with his children (25:40-41). "For they are my servants, whom I brought out of the land of Egypt; they shall not be sold as slaves. You shall not rule over him ruthlessly but shall fear your God" (25:42-43). Israelites belong to God; they can't be enslaved by humans. **Foreign slaves** (25:44-46): May buy slaves from surrounding nations; they are permanent property, inherited (25:45-46). "But over your brothers the people of Israel you shall not rule, one over another ruthlessly" (25:46). Distinction: foreigners may be enslaved; Israelites may not. Reflects ancient Near Eastern norms but with limits. **Redemption from foreign owners** (25:47-55): If an Israelite sells himself to a foreigner living in Israel, he may be redeemed by a kinsman or himself if he prospers (25:48-49). Calculate years to Jubilee; redemption price prorated (25:50-52). If not redeemed, he goes free at Jubilee (25:54). "For it is to me that the people of Israel are servants. They are my servants whom I brought out of the land of Egypt: I am the LORD your God" (25:55). Israelites serve God alone, not humans. This extraordinary covenant with creation and community declares God the ultimate owner of land and people. It anticipates messianic themes of liberty (Isaiah 61) and warns that defying God's provision courts famine (Deuteronomy 28 blessings/curses). The trumpet blasts on Day of Atonement signal Jubilee (25:9). The chapter closes with "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of Egypt" (25:38, 25:54), linking mercy and obedience to redemption identity. Thus, the land laws and Jubilee frame mercy and obedience as core to covenant holiness.
*Verses: 21 | Theme: Compassion for the poor*

### Chapter 26: Blessings and Curses

#### 26:1-13 — Love's Ultimatum: Blessings for Obedience
**Lore:** "You shall not make idols for yourselves or erect an image or pillar, and you shall not set up a figured stone in your land to bow down to it, for I am the LORD your God. You shall keep my Sabbaths and reverence my sanctuary: I am the LORD" (26:1-2). Frame: no idolatry, keep Sabbaths, revere sanctuary. **Blessings** (26:3-13): "If you walk in my statutes and observe my commandments and do them, then I will give you your rains in their season, and the land shall yield its increase, and the trees of the field shall yield their fruit" (26:3-4). Agricultural abundance. "Your threshing shall last to the time of the grape harvest, and the grape harvest shall last to the time for sowing. And you shall eat your bread to the full and dwell in your land securely" (26:5). Continuous harvest—supernatural blessing. "I will give peace (שָׁלוֹם, shalom) in the land, and you shall lie down, and none shall make you afraid. And I will remove harmful beasts from the land, and the sword shall not go through your land" (26:6). Peace, safety, victory over enemies (26:7-8). "I will turn to you and make you fruitful and multiply you and will confirm my covenant with you" (26:9). Abrahamic promises fulfilled. "You shall eat old store long kept, and you shall clear out the old to make way for the new" (26:10). So much food you discard old stock! "I will make my dwelling (מִשְׁכָּן, mishkan, "tabernacle") among you, and my soul shall not abhor you. And I will walk among you and will be your God, and you shall be my people" (26:11-12). The ultimate blessing: God's presence. This echoes Exodus 29:45-46. "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, that you should not be their slaves. And I have broken the bars of your yoke and made you walk erect" (26:13). Redemption reaffirmed. Walking erect = dignity restored. This poem is classic "treaty form"—conditional promise. If obedient → blessing. The blessings focus on provision, peace, and presence.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Blessings of obedience*

#### 26:14-39 — Warnings for Disobedience
**Lore:** "But if you will not listen to me and will not do all these commandments, if you spurn my statutes, and if your soul abhors my rules, so that you will not do all my commandments, but break my covenant, then I will do this to you..." (26:14-16). Five cycles of escalating judgment—each more severe. **First cycle** (26:14-17): Terror, wasting disease, fever, enemies prevail. "You shall sow your seed in vain, for your enemies shall eat it" (26:16). You'll flee when no one pursues (26:17). **Second cycle** (26:18-20): "If in spite of this you will not listen to me, then I will discipline you again sevenfold for your sins" (26:18). Sevenfold increase. Sky like iron, earth like bronze—drought (26:19). Strength spent in vain; land and trees yield nothing (26:20). **Third cycle** (26:21-22): "If you walk contrary to me and will not listen to me, I will continue striking you, sevenfold for your sins. And I will let loose the wild beasts against you, which shall bereave you of your children and destroy your livestock" (26:21-22). Wild animals attack. Roads desolate. **Fourth cycle** (26:23-26): "If by this discipline you are not turned to me but walk contrary to me, then I also will walk contrary to you, and I myself will strike you sevenfold for your sins" (26:23-24). God Himself fights against them. Pestilence, siege, famine—"bread by weight" rationed (26:26). **Fifth cycle** (26:27-39): "If in spite of all this you will not listen to me, but walk contrary to me, then I will walk contrary to you in fury, and I myself will discipline you sevenfold for your sins. You shall eat the flesh of your sons, and you shall eat the flesh of your daughters" (26:27-29). Cannibalism during siege (fulfilled in 2 Kings 6:28-29, Lamentations 4:10). Destruction of high places, corpses piled on idol ruins (26:30). Cities waste, sanctuaries desolate (26:31). The land itself devastated so enemies are appalled (26:32). "And I will scatter you among the nations, and I will unsheathe the sword after you, and your land shall be a desolation, and your cities shall be a waste" (26:33). Exile. "Then the land shall enjoy its Sabbaths as long as it lies desolate, while you are in your enemies' land; then the land shall rest, and enjoy its Sabbaths" (26:34). The land finally gets its rest—forcibly. Fulfilled in Babylonian exile (2 Chronicles 36:21). Survivors live in terror: "the sound of a driven leaf shall put them to flight" (26:36). They stumble over one another fleeing imaginary enemies (26:37). "And you shall perish among the nations, and the land of your enemies shall eat you up" (26:38). Assimilation and death in exile. "And those of you who are left shall rot away in your enemies' lands because of their iniquity, and also because of the iniquities of their fathers they shall rot away like them" (26:39). Generational consequences.
*Verses: 26 | Theme: Escalating judgment*

#### 26:40-46 — Hope: Repentance and Restoration
**Lore:** "But if they confess their iniquity and the iniquity of their fathers in their treachery that they committed against me, and also in walking contrary to me, so that I walked contrary to them and brought them into the land of their enemies—if then their uncircumcised heart is humbled and they make amends for their iniquity, then I will remember my covenant with Jacob, and I will remember my covenant with Isaac and my covenant with Abraham, and I will remember the land" (26:40-42). After dire judgment, there is a promise of repentance and restoration! God will remember the covenant. "But for their sake I will remember the covenant with their forefathers, whom I brought out of the land of Egypt in the sight of the nations, that I might be their God: I am the LORD" (26:45). Grace triumphs. Even in exile, God doesn't abandon them. "These are the statutes and rules and laws that the LORD made between himself and the people of Israel through Moses on Mount Sinai" (26:46). Conclusion of the Holiness Code. Leviticus 26 ends as it began with "I am the LORD your God" (26:12, 13, 45), bookending the covenant. SDA theology highlights that after judgment, there is a promise (26:40-42) of restoration; the chapter underlines God's holiness and justice tempered by mercy. Deuteronomy 28-30 parallels this chapter. The exilic and post-exilic prophets (Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Daniel, Ezra, Nehemiah) reflect on Leviticus 26's blessings/curses as explaining Israel's history. Ultimately, Jesus fulfills the blessings for those in Him and bears the curses on the cross (Galatians 3:13-14).
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Repentance brings restoration*

### Chapter 27: Redemption of Vows

#### 27:1-25 — Holy Vows: Dedicating People and Property
**Lore:** "The LORD spoke to Moses, saying, 'Speak to the people of Israel and say to them, If anyone makes a special vow (נֶדֶר, neder) to the LORD involving the valuation of persons, then the valuation of a male from twenty years old up to sixty years old shall be fifty shekels of silver'" (27:1-3). The final chapter governs vows—dedicating people, animals, houses, or fields to God. **Valuation of persons** (27:2-8): A sliding scale based on age and gender: Males 20-60: 50 shekels. Females 20-60: 30 shekels. Males 5-20: 20 shekels. Females 5-20: 10 shekels. Males 1 month-5 years: 5 shekels. Females 1 month-5 years: 3 shekels. Males 60+: 15 shekels. Females 60+: 10 shekels. If too poor to pay, the priest assesses based on ability (27:8). These valuations aren't about intrinsic worth but economic/labor capacity. The vow dedicates a person to sanctuary service; the price redeems them. **Animals** (27:9-13): If a clean animal vowed, it's holy—cannot be substituted (27:9-10). If unclean animal, priest assesses value; add 20% to redeem it (27:11-13). **Houses** (27:14-15): Dedicate a house, priest assesses value. Add 20% to redeem (27:15). **Fields** (27:16-25): If someone dedicates inherited land, valuation based on seed required (homer of barley seed = 50 shekels). If dedicated at Jubilee start, full value; if mid-cycle, prorated to next Jubilee (27:17-18). Add 20% to redeem; otherwise, it becomes sanctuary property permanently (27:19-20). If not redeemed and sold, at Jubilee it goes to priests as "most holy to the LORD" (27:21). If someone dedicates purchased land (not ancestral), valuation to Jubilee, then returns to original owner (27:22-24). All valuations in sanctuary shekels (20 gerahs per shekel, 27:25).
*Verses: 19 | Theme: Redeeming vows*

#### 27:26-34 — Devoted Things and Conclusion
**Lore:** "But a firstborn of animals, which as a firstborn belongs to the LORD, no man may dedicate; whether ox or sheep, it is the LORD's" (27:26). Firstborn already belong to God (Exodus 13:2); can't be vowed. Unclean animal firstborn may be redeemed at valuation plus 20%, or sold (27:27). **Devoted things** (27:28-29): "But no devoted thing (חֵרֶם, cherem, "devoted to destruction") that a man devotes to the LORD, of anything that he has, whether man or beast, or of his inherited field, shall be sold or redeemed; every devoted thing is most holy to the LORD. No one devoted, who is to be devoted for destruction from mankind, shall be ransomed; he shall surely be put to death" (27:28-29). Cherem = irrevocably consecrated to God, often for destruction (e.g., Jericho, Joshua 6:17; Achan's violation, Joshua 7). Once devoted, no redemption possible. **Tithes** (27:30-33): "Every tithe (מַעֲשֵׂר, ma'aser, "tenth") of the land, whether of the seed of the land or of the fruit of the trees, is the LORD's; it is holy to the LORD" (27:30). To redeem a tithe (keep it), add 20% (27:31). Every tenth animal that passes under the shepherd's rod is holy—no substitution allowed (27:32-33). If substituted, both become holy. **Closing** (27:34): "These are the commandments that the LORD commanded Moses for the people of Israel on Mount Sinai." Final statement. The book closes by reinforcing that vow-declarations must honor God's holiness and that unauthorized impurity in holy things is not tolerated. Leviticus 27 is a liturgical epilogue underscoring total devotion as fitting for God's people. Notice the symmetry: the first words of Leviticus are "the LORD called" (1:1), and the book ends with commandments given "on Mount Sinai" (27:34), highlighting covenant authority. Everything—life, possessions, produce—ultimately belongs to God. The vow system allows people to dedicate things to God, with redemption possible by adding 20%. This teaches generosity, accountability, and God's ownership. Typology: We are "bought with a price" (1 Corinthians 6:20, 7:23). Christ is our redemption (Ephesians 1:7). We vow ourselves to God through Christ, who paid the ultimate price.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: All belongs to God*

**LEVITICUS COMPLETE.**

**Summary:** 27 chapters detailing holiness in worship and life. The book moves from offerings (1-7) to priesthood (8-10) to purity (11-15) to Yom Kippur (16) to the Holiness Code (17-26) to vows (27). Central theme: "Be holy, for I am holy" (19:2, 20:7, 26). Key concepts: atonement through blood, distinction between holy/common and clean/unclean, love for God and neighbor, social justice, Jubilee, covenant blessings/curses. Typology: The entire sacrificial system prefigures Christ's perfect sacrifice. The priesthood points to Christ's high priestly ministry. The purity laws teach humanity's need for cleansing. Yom Kippur foreshadows final judgment and heavenly sanctuary cleansing. The Holiness Code reveals God's character and ethical demands, fulfilled in Jesus' teaching.
