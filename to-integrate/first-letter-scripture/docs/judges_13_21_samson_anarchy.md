# Judges 13-21: Samson and Total Anarchy

## Part VI: Samson the Nazarite (Chapters 13-16)

### Chapter 13: Samson's Birth

#### 13:1-25 — Miraculous Annunciation
**Lore:** "And the people of Israel again did what was evil in the sight of the LORD, so the LORD gave them into the hand of the Philistines for forty years" (13:1). Longest oppression yet. "There was a certain man of Zorah, of the tribe of the Danites, whose name was Manoah. And his wife was barren and had no children" (13:2). Barrenness motif—like Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel, Hannah. "And the angel of the LORD appeared to the woman and said to her, 'Behold, you are barren and have not borne children, but you shall conceive and bear a son. Therefore be careful and drink no wine or strong drink, and eat nothing unclean, for behold, you shall conceive and bear a son. No razor shall come upon his head, for the child shall be a Nazarite to God from the womb, and he shall begin to save Israel from the hand of the Philistines'" (13:3-5). **Nazarite vow** (Numbers 6): no wine, no unclean food, no haircut. "Begin to save"—incomplete deliverance foreshadowed. Woman tells husband, who prays for angel's return (13:6-8). Angel appears again with instructions (13:9-14). Manoah prepares offering, doesn't recognize it's angel (13:15-18). "So Manoah took the young goat with the grain offering, and offered it on the rock to the LORD, to the one who works wonders, and Manoah and his wife were watching. And when the flame went up toward heaven from the altar, the angel of the LORD went up in the flame of the altar. Now Manoah and his wife were watching, and they fell on their faces to the ground" (13:19-20). Theophany revealed. "Then Manoah said to his wife, 'We shall surely die, for we have seen God.' But his wife said to him, 'If the LORD had meant to kill us, he would not have accepted a burnt offering and a grain offering at our hands, or shown us all these things, or now announced to us such things as these'" (13:22-23). Woman's faith. "And the woman bore a son and called his name Samson. And the young man grew, and the LORD blessed him. And the Spirit of the LORD began to stir him in Mahaneh-dan, between Zorah and Eshtaol" (13:24-25). Spirit begins working.
*Verses: 25 | Theme: Nazarite birth*

### Chapters 14-15: Samson's Early Exploits

#### 14:1-20 — Timnite Wedding Riddle
**Lore:** "Samson went down to Timnah, and at Timnah he saw one of the daughters of the Philistines. Then he came up and told his father and mother, 'I saw one of the daughters of the Philistines at Timnah. Now get her for me as my wife'" (14:1-2). **Bad start**—marries outside Israel (Deut 7:3). "Then his father and his mother said to him, 'Is there not a woman among the daughters of your relatives, or among all our people, that you must go to take a wife from the uncircumcised Philistines?'" (14:3). Parents object. "His father and mother did not know that it was from the LORD, for he was seeking an opportunity against the Philistines" (14:4). Divine purpose behind bad choice. "Then Samson went down with his father and mother to Timnah, and they came to the vineyards of Timnah. And behold, a young lion came toward him roaring. Then the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him, and although he had nothing in his hand, he tore the lion in pieces as one tears a young goat" (14:5-6). **First feat**—supernatural strength. Later finds honey in lion carcass, eats it—breaks Nazarite vow (touching dead animal, 14:8-9). At wedding feast, proposes riddle: "Out of the eater came something to eat. Out of the strong came something sweet" (14:14). Wager: 30 linen garments and 30 changes of clothes. Wife pressures him, reveals answer to Philistines (14:15-17). "And the men of the city said to him on the seventh day before the sun went down, 'What is sweeter than honey? What is stronger than a lion?' And he said to them, 'If you had not plowed with my heifer, you would not have found out my riddle'" (14:18). Betrayed. "And the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him, and he went down to Ashkelon and struck down thirty men of the town and took their spoil and gave the garments to those who had told the riddle. In hot anger he went back to his father's house. And Samson's wife was given to his companion, who had been his best man" (14:19-20). Violence and abandonment.
*Verses: 20 | Theme: Intermarriage folly*

#### 15:1-20 — Vengeance and Slaughter
**Lore:** Samson returns with gift, father-in-law gave wife to another (15:1-2). "And Samson said to them, 'This time I shall be innocent in regard to the Philistines, when I do them harm.' So Samson went and caught 300 foxes and took torches. And he turned them tail to tail and put a torch between each pair of tails. And when he had set fire to the torches, he let the foxes go into the standing grain of the Philistines and set fire to the shocks and the standing grain, as well as the olive orchards" (15:3-5). **Foxes with torches**—destroys harvest. Philistines burn wife and father in retaliation (15:6). "And Samson said to them, 'If this is what you do, I swear I will be avenged on you, and after that I will quit.' And he struck them hip and thigh with a great blow, and he went down and stayed in the cleft of the rock of Etam" (15:7-8). Escalating violence. Philistines invade Judah, men of Judah bind Samson, hand him over (15:9-13). "When he came to Lehi, the Philistines came shouting to meet him. Then the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him, and the ropes that were on his arms became as flax that has caught fire, and his bonds melted off his hands. And he found a fresh jawbone of a donkey, and put out his hand and took it, and with it he struck 1,000 men" (15:14-15). **Famous feat**—1,000 killed with donkey jawbone! "And Samson said, 'With the jawbone of a donkey, heaps upon heaps, with the jawbone of a donkey have I struck down a thousand men'" (15:16). Victory song. Becomes very thirsty, God provides water from rock (15:18-19). "And he judged Israel in the days of the Philistines twenty years" (15:20). Long judgeship but never defeats Philistines completely.
*Verses: 20 | Theme: Supernatural strength*

### Chapter 16: Delilah and Death

#### 16:1-3 — Gaza Gate Incident
**Lore:** "Samson went to Gaza, and there he saw a prostitute, and he went in to her" (16:1). Sexual sin pattern continues. "The Gazites were told, 'Samson has come here.' And they surrounded the place and set an ambush for him all night at the gate of the city. They kept quiet all night, saying, 'Let us wait till the light of the morning; then we will kill him.' But Samson lay till midnight, and at midnight he arose and took hold of the doors of the gate of the city and the two posts, and pulled them up, bar and all, and put them on his shoulders and carried them to the top of the hill that is in front of Hebron" (16:2-3). Escapes by carrying city gates 38 miles uphill! Supernatural feat but compromised character.
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Strength with sin*

#### 16:4-22 — Delilah's Betrayal
**Lore:** "After this he loved a woman in the Valley of Sorek, whose name was Delilah. And the lords of the Philistines came up to her and said to her, 'Seduce him, and see where his great strength lies, and by what means we may overpower him, that we may bind him to humble him. And we will each give you 1,100 pieces of silver'" (16:4-5). Delilah = hired spy. Huge bribe. "So Delilah said to Samson, 'Please tell me where your great strength lies, and how you might be bound, that one could subdue you'" (16:6). Three false answers: fresh bowstrings (16:7-9), new ropes (16:10-12), weaving hair into loom (16:13-14). Each time she tests, he breaks free. "And she said to him, 'How can you say, "I love you," when your heart is not with me? You have mocked me these three times, and you have not told me where your great strength lies.' And when she pressed him hard with her words day after day, and urged him, his soul was vexed to death" (16:15-16). Persistent nagging. "And he told her all his heart, and said to her, 'A razor has never come upon my head, for I have been a Nazirite to God from my mother's womb. If my head is shaved, then my strength will leave me, and I shall become weak and be like any other man'" (16:17). **Reveals secret**—fatal mistake! "When Delilah saw that he had told her all his heart, she sent and called the lords of the Philistines, saying, 'Come up again, for he has told me all his heart.' Then the lords of the Philistines came up to her and brought the money in their hands" (16:18). Betrayal finalized. "She made him sleep on her knees. And she called a man and had him shave off the seven locks of his head. Then she began to torment him, and his strength left him. And she said, 'The Philistines are upon you, Samson!' And he awoke from his sleep and said, 'I will go out as at other times and shake myself free.' But he did not know that the LORD had left him" (16:19-20). **Tragic moment**—God's presence departed! "And the Philistines seized him and gouged out his eyes and brought him down to Gaza and bound him with bronze shackles. And he ground at the mill in the prison" (16:21). Blinded, enslaved, grinding grain like animal. Ultimate humiliation. "But the hair of his head began to grow again after it had been shaved" (16:22). Hope hint.
*Verses: 19 | Theme: Compromise brings captivity*

#### 16:23-31 — Final Victory in Death
**Lore:** "Now the lords of the Philistines gathered to offer a great sacrifice to Dagon their god and to rejoice, and they said, 'Our god has given Samson our enemy into our hand'" (16:23). Temple celebration. "And when the people saw him, they praised their god. For they said, 'Our god has given our enemy into our hand, the ravager of our country, who has killed many of us'" (16:24). Mock Samson. "And when their hearts were merry, they said, 'Call Samson, that he may entertain us.' So they called Samson out of the prison, and he entertained them. They made him stand between the pillars" (16:25). Spectacle. "And Samson said to the young man who held him by the hand, 'Let me feel the pillars on which the house rests, that I may lean against them'" (16:26). Positions himself. "Now the house was full of men and women. All the lords of the Philistines were there, and on the roof there were about 3,000 men and women, who looked on while Samson entertained" (16:27). Packed temple—3,000+ total. "Then Samson called to the LORD and said, 'O Lord GOD, please remember me and please strengthen me only this once, O God, that I may be avenged on the Philistines for my two eyes'" (16:28). **Final prayer**—personal vengeance motive, yet God answers. "And Samson grasped the two middle pillars on which the house rested, and he leaned his weight against them, his right hand on the one and his left hand on the other. And Samson said, 'Let me die with the Philistines.' Then he bowed with all his strength, and the house fell upon the lords and upon all the people who were in it. So the dead whom he killed at his death were more than those whom he had killed during his life" (16:29-30). **Climactic destruction**—kills more in death than life! "Then his brothers and all his family came down and took him and brought him up and buried him between Zorah and Eshtaol in the tomb of Manoah his father. He had judged Israel twenty years" (16:31). Buried with honor despite tragic life. Typology: Samson = flawed deliverer, yet Hebrews 11:32 includes him among faith heroes. Points to need for perfect Deliverer (Christ).
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Victory through death*

## Part VII: Total Anarchy (Chapters 17-21)

### Chapters 17-18: Religious Anarchy

#### 17:1-13 — Micah's Private Shrine
**Lore:** "There was a man of the hill country of Ephraim, whose name was Micah. And he said to his mother, 'The 1,100 pieces of silver that were taken from you, about which you uttered a curse, and also spoke it in my ears, behold, the silver is with me; I took it.' And his mother said, 'Blessed be my son by the LORD'" (17:1-2). Theft and curse—dysfunctional family. "And he restored the 1,100 pieces of silver to his mother. And his mother said, 'I dedicate the silver to the LORD from my hand for my son, to make a carved image and a metal image. Now therefore I will restore it to you'" (17:3). **Bizarre syncretism**—dedicates to LORD but makes idol! "And when he restored the money to his mother, his mother took 200 pieces of silver and gave it to the silversmith, who made it into a carved image and a metal image. And it was in the house of Micah. And the man Micah had a shrine, and he made an ephod and household gods, and ordained one of his sons, who became his priest" (17:4-5). DIY religion—private shrine, homemade ephod, son as priest. "In those days there was no king in Israel. Everyone did what was right in his own eyes" (17:6). **Key refrain**—moral/spiritual relativism. "Now there was a young man of Bethlehem in Judah, of the family of Judah, who was a Levite, and he sojourned there. And the man departed from the town of Bethlehem in Judah to sojourn where he could find a place. And he came to the hill country of Ephraim to the house of Micah, as he journeyed" (17:7-8). Wandering Levite—should be teaching law, instead becomes hired priest! "And Micah said to him, 'Where do you come from?' And he said to him, 'I am a Levite of Bethlehem in Judah, and I am going to sojourn where I may find a place.' And Micah said to him, 'Stay with me, and be to me a father and a priest, and I will give you ten pieces of silver a year and a suit of clothes and your living.' And the Levite went in" (17:9-10). Employment agreement. "And the Levite was content to dwell with the man, and the young man became to him like one of his sons. And Micah ordained the Levite, and the young man became his priest, and was in the house of Micah. Then Micah said, 'Now I know that the LORD will prosper me, because I have a Levite as priest'" (17:11-13). False confidence—thinks Levite priest legitimizes idolatry!
*Verses: 13 | Theme: DIY religion*

#### 18:1-31 — Dan Steals Micah's Idols
**Lore:** "In those days the tribe of the Danites was seeking for itself an inheritance to dwell in, for until then no inheritance among the tribes of Israel had fallen to them" (18:1). Dan failed to conquer original territory (1:34), now migrates north. Five spies discover Micah's shrine, recognize Levite (18:2-6). Scout Laish in north—peaceful, unsuspecting (18:7-10). "Then 600 men of the tribe of the Danites, armed with weapons of war, set out from Zorah and Eshtaol" (18:11). Migration. "And they passed on and came to the house of Micah...And the five men who had gone to scout out the land went up and entered and took the carved image, the ephod, the household gods, and the metal image, while the priest stood by the entrance of the gate with the 600 men armed with weapons of war" (18:17-18). **Steal idols!** "And when these went into Micah's house and took the carved image, the ephod, the household gods, and the metal image, the priest said to them, 'What are you doing?' And they said to him, 'Keep quiet; put your hand on your mouth and come with us and be to us a father and a priest. Is it better for you to be priest to the house of one man, or to be priest to a tribe and clan in Israel?'" (18:19-20). Levite's spiritual opportunism—goes for bigger paycheck! "And the priest's heart was glad. He took the ephod and the household gods and the carved image and went along with the people" (18:20). Micah pursues, Danites threaten him, he gives up (18:22-26). "And they captured Laish, and struck it with the edge of the sword and burned the city with fire...And they rebuilt the city and lived in it. And they named the city Dan, after the name of Dan their father" (18:27-29). Conquer peaceful city. "And the people of Dan set up the carved image for themselves, and Jonathan the son of Gershom, son of Moses, was priest to the tribe of the Danites until the day of the captivity of the land" (18:30). **Shocking**—Moses's grandson becomes idolatrous priest! Northern tribe establishes false worship center rivaling Jerusalem. "And they set up Micah's carved image that he made, as long as the house of God was at Shiloh" (18:31). Idol worship continues until exile.
*Verses: 31 | Theme: Religious corruption*

### Chapters 19-21: Moral and Civil Anarchy

#### 19:1-30 — The Levite's Concubine
**Lore:** "In those days, when there was no king in Israel, a certain Levite was sojourning in the remote parts of the hill country of Ephraim, who took to himself a concubine from Bethlehem in Judah. And his concubine was unfaithful to him, and she went away from him to her father's house at Bethlehem in Judah, and was there some four months" (19:1-2). Troubled relationship. Levite retrieves her, they stay with father-in-law several days (19:3-9). Leave late, stop at Gibeah (Benjamite city) for night (19:10-15). Old man takes them in, warns against staying in square (19:16-21). "As they were making their hearts merry, behold, the men of the city, worthless fellows, surrounded the house, beating on the door. And they said to the old man, the master of the house, 'Bring out the man who came into your house, that we may know him'" (19:22). **Exact parallel to Sodom** (Genesis 19)! Israel = new Sodom. "And the man, the master of the house, went out to them and said to them, 'No, my brothers, do not act so wickedly; since this man has come into my house, do not do this vile thing. Behold, here are my virgin daughter and his concubine. Let me bring them out now. Violate them and do with them what seems good to you, but against this man do not do this outrageous thing'" (19:23-24). Offers women—shocking. "But the men would not listen to him. So the man seized his concubine and made her go out to them. And they knew her and abused her all night until the morning. And as the dawn began to break, they let her go" (19:25). **Horrific gang rape**—all night abuse. "And as morning appeared, the woman came and fell down at the door of the man's house where her master was, until it was light" (19:26). "And her master rose up in the morning, and when he opened the doors of the house and went out to go on his way, behold, there was his concubine lying at the door of the house, with her hands on the threshold. He said to her, 'Get up, let us be going.' But there was no answer" (19:27-28). Dead or dying. "Then he put her on the donkey, and the man rose up and went away to his home. And when he entered his house, he took a knife, and taking hold of his concubine he divided her, limb by limb, into twelve pieces, and sent her throughout all the territory of Israel. And all who saw it said, 'Such a thing has never happened or been seen from the day that the people of Israel came up out of the land of Egypt until this day; consider it, take counsel, and speak'" (19:29-30). **Dismembers body**—sends pieces to 12 tribes as call to action. Darkest story in Bible—Israel worse than Canaanites.
*Verses: 30 | Theme: Moral depravity*

#### 20:1-48 — Civil War Against Benjamin
**Lore:** "Then all the people of Israel came out, from Dan to Beersheba, including the land of Gilead, and the congregation assembled as one man to the LORD at Mizpah" (20:1). National assembly. Levite tells story (20:2-7). "So all the men of Israel gathered against the city, united as one man" (20:11). Demand justice from Benjamin: "Now therefore give up the men, the worthless fellows in Gibeah, that we may put them to death and purge evil from Israel" (20:13). "But the Benjaminites would not listen to the voice of their brothers, the people of Israel" (20:13). Tribal loyalty over justice! "And the Benjaminites came together out of the cities to Gibeah to go out to battle against the people of Israel. And the Benjaminites mustered out of their cities on that day 26,000 men who drew the sword, besides the inhabitants of Gibeah, who mustered 700 chosen men" (20:14-15). 26,700 total. "Among all these were 700 chosen men who were left-handed; every one could sling a stone at a hair and not miss" (20:16). Elite slingers. "And the men of Israel, apart from Benjamin, mustered 400,000 men who drew the sword; all these were men of war" (20:17). Massive disparity: 400,000 vs. 26,700. First day: Israel loses 22,000 (20:19-21). Second day: Israel loses 18,000 (20:22-25). Total: 40,000 casualties! Israel fasts, offers sacrifices, inquires of LORD (20:26-28). Third day: ambush strategy like Ai, Benjamin defeated (20:29-48). "And all who fell that day of Benjamin were 25,000 men who drew the sword, all of them men of valor" (20:46). Almost entire tribe wiped out! "But 600 men turned and fled toward the wilderness to the rock of Rimmon and remained at the rock of Rimmon four months" (20:47). 600 survive. "And the men of Israel turned back against the people of Benjamin and struck them with the edge of the sword, the city, men and beasts and all that they found. And they set on fire all the towns that they found" (20:48). Total destruction.
*Verses: 48 | Theme: Civil war catastrophe*

#### 21:1-25 — Preserving Benjamin
**Lore:** "Now the men of Israel had sworn at Mizpah, 'No one of us shall give his daughter in marriage to Benjamin'" (21:1). **Rash oath**—like Jephthah! "And the people of Israel had compassion for Benjamin their brother and said, 'One tribe is cut off from Israel this day'" (21:6). Regret. "They said, 'What one is there of the tribes of Israel that did not come up to the LORD to Mizpah?' And behold, no one had come to the camp from Jabesh-gilead to the assembly" (21:8). Discover Jabesh-gilead absent. "So the congregation sent 12,000 of their bravest men there and commanded them, 'Go and strike the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead with the edge of the sword; also the women and the little ones. Every male and every woman that has lain with a male you shall devote to destruction'" (21:10-11). **More slaughter**—kill all except virgins! "And they found among the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead 400 young virgins who had not known a man by lying with him, and they brought them to the camp at Shiloh" (21:12). 400 virgins captured. "Then the whole congregation sent word to the people of Benjamin who were at the rock of Rimmon and proclaimed peace to them. And Benjamin returned at that time. And they gave them the women whom they had saved alive of the women of Jabesh-gilead, but they were not enough for them" (21:13-14). Still 200 short. "And the people had compassion on Benjamin because the LORD had made a breach in the tribes of Israel" (21:15). Clever "solution": "Behold, there is the yearly feast of the LORD at Shiloh...And they commanded the people of Benjamin, saying, 'Go and lie in ambush in the vineyards and watch. If the daughters of Shiloh come out to dance in the dances, then come out of the vineyards and snatch each man his wife from the daughters of Shiloh, and go to the land of Benjamin'" (21:19-21). **Kidnap brides** at festival! Technically doesn't violate oath. "And the people of Benjamin did so and took their wives, according to their number, from the dancers whom they carried off. Then they went and returned to their inheritance and rebuilt the towns and lived in them" (21:23). "In those days there was no king in Israel. Everyone did what was right in his own eyes" (21:25). **Final refrain**—total moral collapse. Book ends in chaos, crying out for righteous king (foreshadows 1 Samuel).
*Verses: 25 | Theme: Everyone does what's right in own eyes*

**Book Summary:** Downward spiral from incomplete conquest → cyclical apostasy → progressively worse judges → total anarchy. Othniel (ideal) → Ehud (left-handed) → Deborah (woman) → Gideon (compromised) → Abimelech (tyrant) → Jephthah (rash vow) → Samson (most compromised). Epilogue shows religious corruption (Micah, Dan) and moral collapse (Gibeah, civil war). Refrain: "No king...everyone did what was right in own eyes" (17:6; 21:25). Sets stage for monarchy—need for righteous king ultimately fulfilled in David and perfectly in Christ.
