# Nehemiah 13: The Zealous Reformer

## Overview
After wall dedication's joyful climax, Nehemiah returns to Persia (13:6). Upon his return to Jerusalem, he discovers shocking reversals: Tobiah the Ammonite living in temple chamber, Levites abandoned (no tithes), Sabbath profaned by commerce, and intermarriage resumed. Nehemiah's response: righteous anger and radical action—throws Tobiah's furniture out personally, rebukes nobles, physically confronts men who married foreign women. Central themes: Restoration not permanent, covenant vigilance required, zealous leadership necessary to confront compromise, holiness demands costly separation. This chapter = theological climax (not anticlimactic appendix)—shows that human resolve (Ch 10 covenant) insufficient without continual enforcement.

## Chapter 13: The Cost of Vigilance

#### 13:1-9 — Tobiah in the Temple: Casting Out the Enemy
**Lore:** "On that day they read in the book of Moses in the audience of the people; and therein was found written, that the Ammonite and the Moabite should not come into the congregation of God for ever; Because they met not the children of Israel with bread and with water, but hired Balaam against them, that he should curse them: howbeit our God turned the curse into a blessing" (13:1-2). **Law reading reveals exclusion**—Deuteronomy 23:3-6 read publicly. "On that day" (בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא, bayyom hahu) = unspecified time, likely during Nehemiah's absence (13:6 reveals he returned to Persia). Ammonites/Moabites excluded "forever" (עַד־עוֹלָם, ad-olam) because: 1) Didn't provide hospitality during wilderness wandering; 2) Hired Balaam to curse Israel (Num 22-24). "Turned curse into blessing" = Balaam's oracles became blessings (Num 23:20). "Now when they had heard the law, that they separated from Israel all the mixed multitude" (13:3). "Mixed multitude" (כָּל־עֵרֶב, kol-erev) = foreigners, specifically Ammonites/Moabites. Enforced separation (contrast to modern inclusivity—covenant community has boundaries). "And before this, Eliashib the priest, having the oversight of the chamber of the house of our God, was allied unto Tobiah" (13:4). **CRISIS REVEALED**—"before this" = earlier, during Nehemiah's absence. **Eliashib the high priest** (cf. 3:1, led wall building) now "allied unto Tobiah" (קָרוֹב לְטוֹבִיָּה, qarov leToviyah) = related/connected (possibly through intermarriage, cf. 6:18). Tobiah = Ammonite enemy who opposed wall (2:10, 19; 4:3, 7; 6:12-14, 17-19). How did enemy infiltrate temple? Through high priest's compromise! "And he had prepared for him a great chamber, where aforetime they laid the meat offerings, the frankincense, and the vessels, and the tithes of the corn, the new wine, and the oil, which was commanded to be given to the Levites, and the singers, and the porters; and the offerings of the priests" (13:5). **Tobiah living in temple storeroom**—"great chamber" (לִשְׁכָּה גְדוֹלָה, lishkah gedolah) = large room. Originally for: tithes, offerings, vessels, frankincense. Eliashib EVICTED temple supplies to house Tobiah. Abomination: 1) Ammonite forbidden in assembly (Deut 23:3); 2) Living in sacred space (temple proper restricted to priests, Num 18:7); 3) Displacing provisions for Levites (causing them to abandon posts, 13:10). "But in all this time was not I at Jerusalem: for in the two and thirtieth year of Artaxerxes king of Babylon came I unto the king, and after certain days obtained I leave of the king" (13:6). **Nehemiah's absence**—32nd year of Artaxerxes (433 BC, 12 years after initial mission, cf. 5:14). Returned to Persia (as promised, 2:6). "After certain days" = unspecified time, possibly years. While away, reforms collapsed. "And I came to Jerusalem, and understood of the evil that Eliashib did for Tobiah, in preparing him a chamber in the courts of the house of God" (13:7). Nehemiah returns, discovers "evil" (רָעָה, ra'ah). "Courts of the house of God" = temple precincts (sacred space). "And it grieved me sore: therefore I cast forth all the household stuff of Tobiah out of the chamber" (13:8). **NEHEMIAH'S RESPONSE**—"grieved me sore" (וַיֵּרַע לִי מְאֹד, vayera li me'od) = exceedingly displeased, burned with anger. Action: **"I cast forth" (וָאַשְׁלִיכָה, va'ashlikhah) = threw out, hurled**. Personally evicted Tobiah's furniture. Didn't form committee, didn't dialogue—direct, immediate, physical action. Parallel: Jesus cleansing temple (John 2:15, overturned tables). Some sins demand zealous, confrontational response. "Then I commanded, and they cleansed the chambers: and thither brought I again the vessels of the house of God, with the meat offering and the frankincense" (13:9). **Purification**—chambers ritually cleansed (contaminated by Ammonite presence). Vessels restored to proper function. Order reestablished. **LEADERSHIP LESSON**: Compromise tolerated becomes compromise celebrated. High priest's alliance with enemy led to enemy occupying God's house. Requires courageous leader willing to physically confront evil (even when perpetrator is religious authority). Eliashib not deposed (no record), but Nehemiah overrides his authority. Civil governor trumps corrupt priest when holiness at stake.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Confrontation*

#### 13:10-14 — Forsaken Levites: Restoring Tithes and Worship
**Lore:** "And I perceived that the portions of the Levites had not been given them: for the Levites and the singers, that did the work, were fled every one to his field" (13:10). **Second crisis**—"portions" (מְנָיוֹת, menayot) = tithes/offerings. Not given = Levites unpaid, abandoned temple service, returned to farming ("fled to his field"). Caused by Tobiah occupying storeroom (13:5)—tithes had nowhere to be stored/distributed. Worship collapsed. Same problem in Malachi's era (Mal 3:8-10, "Will a man rob God? Yet ye have robbed me... in tithes and offerings"). "Then contended I with the rulers, and said, Why is the house of God forsaken? And I gathered them together, and set them in their place" (13:11). **Nehemiah confronts nobles**—"contended" (וָאָרִיבָה, va'arivah) = disputed, rebuked sharply. "Why is house of God forsaken?" (נֶעֱזָב, ne'ezav)—echoes 10:39, covenant vow "we will not forsake house of our God." Broken promise within 12 years. Gathered Levites, reinstated them ("set them in their place" = restored to posts). "Then brought all Judah the tithe of the corn and the new wine and the oil unto the treasuries" (13:12). **Tithes resumed**—Nehemiah's leadership shames people into obedience. "And I made treasurers over the treasuries, Shelemiah the priest, and Zadok the scribe, and of the Levites, Pedaiah: and next to them was Hanan the son of Zaccur, the son of Mattaniah: for they were counted faithful, and their office was to distribute unto their brethren" (13:13). **Administrative reform**—appoints four treasurers (priest, scribe, Levite, assistant). Key qualification: "counted faithful" (נֶאֱמָנִים נֶחְשָׁבוּ, ne'emanim nechshavu) = proven trustworthy (unlike Eliashib who gave storeroom to Tobiah). Ensures proper distribution. "Remember me, O my God, concerning this, and wipe not out my good deeds that I have done for the house of my God, and for the offices thereof" (13:14). **Nehemiah's prayer #1**—"Remember me" (זָכְרָה־לִּי, zokhrah-li). Not boasting but covenant appeal (cf. 5:19, same phrase). "Wipe not out my good deeds" (אַל־תֶּמַח חֲסָדַי, al-temach chasaday) = don't erase my loyal acts. "Chasad" (חֶסֶד) = covenant loyalty, steadfast love. Nehemiah performed "chasad" toward God by restoring worship. Asks God to remember (heavenly record, Matt 6:4, "Father sees in secret will reward"). **Pattern: Crisis → Nehemiah's anger → Confrontation → Rebuke → Practical solution → Prayer for remembrance.** Repeats 4x in chapter (Tobiah, tithes, Sabbath, intermarriage).
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Restoration*

#### 13:15-22 — Sabbath Profaned: Shutting the Gates
**Lore:** "In those days saw I in Judah some treading wine presses on the sabbath, and bringing in sheaves, and lading asses; as also wine, grapes, and figs, and all manner of burdens, which they brought into Jerusalem on the sabbath day: and I testified against them in the day wherein they sold victuals" (13:15). **Third crisis—Sabbath violation**—"in those days" (בַּיָּמִים הָהֵם, bayamim hahem) = during Nehemiah's reforms. Jews working on Sabbath: treading winepresses, harvesting ("bringing sheaves"), transporting goods ("lading asses"), selling food ("victuals"). Direct violation of Ex 20:8-11, Deut 5:12-15. Covenant vow broken (10:31, "would not buy... on sabbath"). "I testified against them" (וָאָעִיד, va'a'id) = warned solemnly, bore witness. "There dwelt men of Tyre also therein, which brought fish, and all manner of ware, and sold on the sabbath unto the children of Judah, and in Jerusalem" (13:16). **Foreign merchants**—Tyrians (Phoenicians) selling in Jerusalem on Sabbath. Not subject to Jewish Law BUT Jews buying = sin. Can't control pagans, but CAN control Jewish participation. "Then I contended with the nobles of Judah, and said unto them, What evil thing is this that ye do, and profane the sabbath day?" (13:17). **Confronts nobles**—"contended" (וָאָרִיבָה, same as 13:11). "What evil thing" (מָה־הַדָּבָר הָרָע הַזֶּה, mah-hadavar hara hazzeh)—rhetorical question showing gravity. "Profane the sabbath" (מְחַלְּלִים, mechallelim) = desecrate, treat as common (opposite of holy). "Did not your fathers thus, and did not our God bring all this evil upon us, and upon this city? yet ye bring more wrath upon Israel by profaning the sabbath" (13:18). **Historical warning**—fathers' sin (including Sabbath violation, Jer 17:21-27) led to exile. Jeremiah prophesied: keep Sabbath or Jerusalem destroyed. City burned 586 BC (proving Jeremiah right). "Yet ye bring MORE wrath" (וְאַתֶּם מוֹסִיפִים חָרוֹן, ve'attem mosifim charon) = adding fierce anger. Learned nothing from history. "And it came to pass, that when the gates of Jerusalem began to be dark before the sabbath, I commanded that the gates should be shut, and charged that they should not be opened till after the sabbath: and some of my servants set I at the gates, that there should no burden be brought in on the sabbath day" (13:19). **Practical enforcement**—as sun sets Friday evening (Sabbath begins), Nehemiah orders gates closed. "Began to be dark" (צָלֲלוּ, tzalelu) = shadowed, dusk. Guards posted (Nehemiah's personal servants = loyal) to prevent merchants entering. Physical barrier prevents sin. Modern application: eliminate temptation (internet filters, accountability, environmental controls). "So the merchants and sellers of all kind of ware lodged without Jerusalem once or twice" (13:20). **Merchants camp outside walls**—hoping Jews will come out to buy. Loophole seeking. "Then I testified against them, and said unto them, Why lodge ye about the wall? if ye do so again, I will lay hands on you. From that time forth came they no more on the sabbath" (13:21). **Threat of force**—"I will lay hands on you" (יָד אֶשְׁלַח בָּכֶם, yad eshlach bakhem) = use violence. Not actual violence, but credible threat. Merchants stop coming. "And I commanded the Levites that they should cleanse themselves, and that they should come and keep the gates, to sanctify the sabbath day. Remember me, O my God, concerning this also, and spare me according to the greatness of thy mercy" (13:22). **Levites assigned gate duty**—Sabbath enforcement = spiritual task (not just civil). "Cleanse themselves" (מִטַּהֲרִים, mittaharim) = ritual purification. "Sanctify the sabbath" (לְקַדֵּשׁ, leqaddesh) = keep holy. **Nehemiah's prayer #2**—"Remember... spare me according to greatness of thy mercy" (חַסְדֶּךָ, chasdekha). Asks for divine favor based on covenant loyalty. **LEADERSHIP PRINCIPLE**: Words insufficient—must create systems preventing sin (closed gates, guards). Environment shapes behavior. Also: external merchants less problem than internal compromise (Jews choosing to buy).

#### 13:23-31 — Intermarriage Crisis: Pulling Out Hair and Curses
**Lore:** "In those days also saw I Jews that had married wives of Ashdod, of Ammon, and of Moab: And their children spake half in the speech of Ashdod, and could not speak in the Jews' language, but according to the language of each people" (13:23-24). **Fourth crisis—Intermarriage AGAIN**—same sin as Ezra 9-10. Married women from: 1) **Ashdod** (Philistine city); 2) **Ammon** (excluded, Deut 23:3); 3) **Moab** (excluded, Deut 23:3). Worse: children can't speak Hebrew! "Half in speech of Ashdod" (אַשְׁדּוֹדִית, Ashdodit, Philistine language). Cultural/linguistic assimilation = losing covenant identity. Can't read Torah if can't speak Hebrew. Next generation won't know God's Word. "And I contended with them, and cursed them, and smote certain of them, and plucked off their hair, and made them swear by God, saying, Ye shall not give your daughters unto their sons, nor take their daughters unto your sons, or for yourselves" (13:25). **MOST VIOLENT RESPONSE**—five actions: 1) **Contended** (וָאָרִיב, va'ariv) = rebuked; 2) **Cursed them** (וָאֲקַלְלֵם, va'aqallem) = pronounced covenant curses (Deut 27-28); 3) **Smote** (וָאַכֶּה, va'akkeh) = struck, hit (not necessarily beating, possibly symbolic striking); 4) **Plucked off their hair** (וָאֶמְרְטָה, va'emretah) = pulled out hair (extreme humiliation, cf. Ezra pulled his OWN hair, 9:3; Nehemiah pulls THEIRS). Physical confrontation. 5) **Made them swear** (וָאַשְׁבִּיעֵם, va'ashbi'em) = forced oath. Content: "Ye shall not give daughters... nor take daughters"—no intermarriage (repeats Deut 7:3, covenant vow 10:30). Controversial actions by modern standards—is violence justified? Context: 1) Covenant breach threatens entire community (Messianic line at risk); 2) Symbolic act (like prophets' sign-acts, Ezek 4-5); 3) Public shame to deter others; 4) Zealous for God's holiness (Phinehas parallel, Num 25:6-13, "zealous for my sake"). Not personal vendetta but covenantal enforcement. "Did not Solomon king of Israel sin by these things? yet among many nations was there no king like him, who was beloved of his God, and God made him king over all Israel: nevertheless even him did outlandish women cause to sin" (13:26). **Solomon's cautionary tale**—greatest, wisest king (1 Kings 3:12) yet foreign wives led to idolatry (1 Kings 11:1-8). "Outlandish women" (נָשִׁים נָכְרִיּוֹת, nashim nokhriyot) = foreign women. If Solomon fell, no one immune. Intermarriage = spiritual cancer. "Shall we then hearken unto you to do all this great evil, to transgress against our God in marrying strange wives?" (13:27). Rhetorical question—"Shall we listen to YOU (who justify intermarriage)?" No! "Great evil" (רָעָה הַגְּדוֹלָה, ra'ah haggedolah) = massive wickedness. "Transgress against our God" (לִמְעֹל בֵּאלֹהֵינוּ, lim'ol be'Eloheinu) = covenant treachery (ma'al, same term from Ezra 9:2, 10:2). "And one of the sons of Joiada, the son of Eliashib the high priest, was son in law to Sanballat the Horonite: therefore I chased him from me" (13:28). **High priest's grandson married Sanballat's daughter**—ultimate compromise! Eliashib's grandson (Joiada's son) intermarried with archenemy Sanballat (2:10, 19; 4:1, 7; 6:1-14). Some scholars identify this priest as **Manasseh** (Josephus, Antiquities 11.7.2), who later founded rival temple on Mount Gerizim (Samaritan schism). "I chased him" (וָאַבְרִיחֵהוּ, va'avrichehu) = drove away, expelled. Banishment. Priestly intermarriage with enemy = disqualification. "Remember them, O my God, because they have defiled the priesthood, and the covenant of the priesthood and of the Levites" (13:29). **Nehemiah's prayer #3**—asks God to remember THEIR sin (opposite of 13:14, 22, 31 where he asks God to remember his righteousness). "Defiled the priesthood" (גֹאֲלֵי הַכְּהֻנָּה, go'alei hakehunnah) = polluted, corrupted priestly office. "Covenant of the priesthood" (בְּרִית הַכְּהֻנָּה, berit hakehunnah) = Phinehas's covenant (Num 25:10-13, perpetual priesthood promised to his line). Intermarriage threatens this. "Thus cleansed I them from all strangers, and appointed the wards of the priests and the Levites, every one in his business; And for the wood offering, at times appointed, and for the firstfruits. Remember me, O my God, for good" (13:30-31). **SUMMARY**—"cleansed from all strangers" (וְטִהַרְתִּים מִכָּל־נֵכָר, vetihartim mikol-nekhar) = purified from foreign influence. Reestablished priestly/Levitical duties (repeats 10:34, wood offering covenant vow). **Nehemiah's prayer #4—FINAL WORDS**: "Remember me, O my God, for good" (זָכְרָה־לִּי אֱלֹהַי לְטוֹבָה, zokhrah-li Elohai letovah). Book ends with prayer (began with prayer, 1:4-11). No resolution reported—book ends abruptly. Why? Emphasizes ongoing nature of spiritual warfare. Restoration never "done"—requires constant vigilance. Nehemiah's final act = prayer. Leaves results to God. **CHAPTER 13 = THEOLOGICAL CLIMAX**: Proves covenant vows (Ch 10) insufficient without enforcement. Human resolve weak—needs strong leadership willing to confront sin. Four crises (Tobiah, tithes, Sabbath, intermarriage) = four tests of covenantal vigilance. Nehemiah passes all—throws out enemy, restores worship, closes gates, expels compromisers. Harsh? Yes. Necessary? Biblical answer: YES. Holiness requires radical separation from evil. "Come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord" (2 Cor 6:17). Modern church's failure: tolerance of sin disguised as "grace." Nehemiah = model of zealous, uncompromising leadership. Loved God more than popularity.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Vigilance*
