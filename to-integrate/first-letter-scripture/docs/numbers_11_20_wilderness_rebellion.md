# Numbers 11-20: Wilderness Rebellion

## Part V: The Rebellion Begins (Chapters 11-14)

### Chapter 11: Complaints and Consequences

#### 11:1-3 — Taberah: Fire Judgment
**Lore:** "And the people complained in the hearing of the LORD about their misfortunes, and when the LORD heard it, his anger was kindled, and the fire of the LORD burned among them and consumed some outlying parts of the camp" (11:1). Three days into the journey (10:33), complaints begin. God's fire strikes camp outskirts. People cry to Moses, he prays, fire stops (11:2). Place named Taberah (תַּבְעֵרָה, "burning," 11:3). Warning shot. Exodus generation's pattern: deliverance → grumbling → judgment. Foreshadows greater rebellions. 1 Corinthians 10:10: "Do not grumble, as some of them did and were destroyed."
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Grumbling judged*

#### 11:4-15 — Craving for Meat, Moses Overwhelmed
**Lore:** "Now the rabble that was among them had a strong craving. And the people of Israel also wept again and said, 'Oh that we had meat to eat! We remember the fish we ate in Egypt that cost nothing, the cucumbers, the melons, the leeks, the onions, and the garlic. But now our strength is dried up, and there is nothing at all but this manna to look at'" (11:4-6). Selective memory! Forget slavery, remember food. Manna = God's provision, but they despise it (11:6). "Moses heard the people weeping throughout their clans, everyone at the door of his tent. And the anger of the LORD blazed hotly" (11:10). Moses breaks: "Why have you dealt ill with your servant? And why have I not found favor in your sight, that you lay the burden of all this people on me?...I am not able to carry all this people alone; the burden is too heavy for me. If you will treat me like this, kill me at once" (11:11-15). Brutal honesty. Even great leaders hit limits. God responds with help (70 elders) and judgment (quail plague).
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Despising provision*

#### 11:16-23 — Seventy Elders Receive the Spirit
**Lore:** "Then the LORD said to Moses, 'Gather for me seventy men of the elders of Israel...And I will take some of the Spirit that is on you and put it on them, and they shall bear the burden of the people with you, so that you may not bear it yourself alone'" (11:16-17). God shares leadership burden. Also promises meat: "You shall eat...not one day, or two days, or five days, or ten days, or twenty days, but a whole month, until it comes out at your nostrils and becomes loathsome to you" (11:19-20). Judgment disguised as answer. Moses doubts: "600,000 men + families...shall flocks and herds be slaughtered for them?" (11:21-22). God: "Is the LORD's hand shortened? Now you shall see whether my word will come true for you or not" (11:23). God's power unlimited.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Shared leadership*

#### 11:24-30 — Spirit Falls, Two Prophesy in Camp
**Lore:** Moses gathers 70 elders at tent. "Then the LORD came down in the cloud and spoke to him, and took some of the Spirit that was on him and put it on the seventy elders. And as soon as the Spirit rested on them, they prophesied. But they did not continue doing it" (11:25). One-time event. But two men (Eldad and Medad) remained in camp, Spirit fell on them too, they prophesied (11:26). Joshua objects: "Moses, my lord, stop them!" (11:28). Moses: "Are you jealous for my sake? Would that all the LORD's people were prophets, that the LORD would put his Spirit on them!" (11:29). Humble response. Spirit not limited to sanctuary. Joel 2:28-29 promises this for all. Acts 2 = Pentecost fulfillment.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Spirit for all*

#### 11:31-35 — Quail and Plague at Kibroth-hattaavah
**Lore:** "Then a wind from the LORD sprang up, and it brought quail from the sea and let them fall beside the camp...The people rose all that day and all night and all the next day, and gathered the quail...And while the meat was yet between their teeth, before it was consumed, the anger of the LORD was kindled against the people, and the LORD struck down the people with a very great plague" (11:31-33). God gives request, adds judgment. Gluttony punished. "Therefore the name of that place was called Kibroth-hattaavah (קִבְרוֹת הַתַּאֲוָה, 'graves of craving'), because there they buried the people who had the craving" (11:34). Psalm 78:29-31 recalls this. Warning: be careful what you crave. Craving ≠ contentment. Philippians 4:11: "I have learned...to be content."
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Greed punished*

### Chapter 12: Miriam's Rebellion

#### 12:1-9 — Miriam and Aaron Challenge Moses
**Lore:** "Miriam and Aaron spoke against Moses because of the Cushite woman whom he had married, for he had married a Cushite woman" (12:1). Cushite = Ethiopian/Midianite (Zipporah? new wife?). Real issue: authority. "And they said, 'Has the LORD indeed spoken only through Moses? Has he not spoken through us also?'" (12:2). Jealousy. "And the LORD heard it" (12:2). Uh-oh. "Now the man Moses was very meek, more than all people who were on the face of the earth" (12:3). Narrator defends Moses. God summons all three to tent (12:4-5). Defends Moses: "If there is a prophet among you, I the LORD make myself known to him in a vision; I speak with him in a dream. Not so with my servant Moses. He is faithful in all my house. With him I speak mouth to mouth, clearly, and not in riddles, and he beholds the form of the LORD. Why then were you not afraid to speak against my servant Moses?" (12:6-8). Moses = unique status. God's anger burns against them (12:9).
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Attacking God's anointed*

#### 12:10-16 — Miriam Struck with Leprosy
**Lore:** "When the cloud removed from over the tent, behold, Miriam was leprous, like snow" (12:10). Immediate judgment. Aaron confesses: "Oh, my lord, do not punish us because we have done foolishly and have sinned" (12:11). Moses intercedes: "O God, please heal her—please" (12:13). Brief, desperate prayer. God: "If her father had but spit in her face, should she not be shamed seven days? Let her be shut outside the camp seven days, and after that she may be brought in again" (12:14). Seven-day quarantine (Lev 13-14). Miriam isolated, people wait (12:15). Then march to Paran (12:16). Why Miriam punished, not Aaron? Possibly she instigated. Or as prophetess (Ex 15:20), held to higher standard. Lesson: rebellion against God's chosen leaders = rebellion against God. Even family not exempt. Hebrews 13:17: "Obey your leaders."
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Pride humbled*

### Chapter 13: Spying Out Canaan

#### 13:1-16 — Twelve Spies Sent
**Lore:** "The LORD spoke to Moses, saying, 'Send men to spy out the land of Canaan, which I am giving to the people of Israel. From each tribe of their fathers you shall send a man, every one a chief among them'" (13:1-2). Twelve spies, one per tribe (13:3-15). From Judah: Caleb son of Jephunneh (13:6). From Ephraim: Hoshea (Moses renames him Joshua, יְהוֹשֻׁעַ, "Yahweh saves," 13:16). Mission: scout land, people, cities, soil, produce (13:17-20). "Be of good courage" (13:20). They go 40 days (13:25). Deuteronomy 1:22 clarifies people requested this—not God's original plan. Lack of faith disguised as wisdom.
*Verses: 16 | Theme: Faith or fear?*

#### 13:17-25 — Scouting the Land
**Lore:** Moses commands: "Go up into the Negeb and go up into the hill country, and see what the land is, and whether the people who dwell in it are strong or weak, whether they are few or many, and whether the land that they dwell in is good or bad, and whether the cities that they dwell in are camps or strongholds, and whether the land is rich or poor, and whether there is wood in it or not. Be of good courage and bring some of the fruit of the land" (13:17-20). Time of first ripe grapes (13:20). They scout from Zin to Rehob, Hebron (where Anak's descendants live, 13:21-22). Cut cluster of grapes so large two men carry it on pole, plus pomegranates and figs (13:23). Valley of Eshcol (אֶשְׁכּוֹל, "cluster," 13:24). Return after 40 days (13:25). Evidence: land flows with milk and honey!
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Abundant land*

#### 13:26-33 — Fearful Report vs. Faithful Report
**Lore:** Spies return to Kadesh, show fruit (13:26-27). Report: "We came to the land...It flows with milk and honey, and this is its fruit. However, the people who dwell in the land are strong, and the cities are fortified and very large. And besides, we saw the descendants of Anak there" (13:27-28). Giants! Fear grows. "Caleb quieted the people before Moses and said, 'Let us go up at once and occupy it, for we are well able to overcome it'" (13:30). Faith! But ten spies counter: "We are not able to go up against the people, for they are stronger than we are...The land, through which we have gone to scout it, is a land that devours its inhabitants, and all the people that we saw in it are of great height. And there we saw the Nephilim (the sons of Anak, who come from the Nephilim), and we seemed to ourselves like grasshoppers, and so we seemed to them" (13:31-33). Bad report spreads. Nephilim = giants (Gen 6:4). Exaggerated fear. They forgot God who defeated Egypt. Faith sees God; fear sees obstacles.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Grasshoppers or conquerors?*

### Chapter 14: The Great Rebellion

#### 14:1-10 — Israel Weeps, Wants to Return to Egypt
**Lore:** "Then all the congregation raised a loud cry, and the people wept that night. And all the people of Israel grumbled against Moses and Aaron. The whole congregation said to them, 'Would that we had died in the land of Egypt! Or would that we had died in this wilderness! Why is the LORD bringing us into this land, to fall by the sword? Our wives and our little ones will become a prey. Would it not be better for us to go back to Egypt?'" (14:1-3). Full revolt. "And they said to one another, 'Let us choose a leader and go back to Egypt'" (14:4). Attempted coup! Moses and Aaron fall on faces before assembly (14:5). Joshua and Caleb tear clothes: "The land, which we passed through to spy it out, is an exceedingly good land. If the LORD delights in us, he will bring us into this land...Only do not rebel against the LORD. And do not fear the people of the land, for they are bread for us. Their protection is removed from them, and the LORD is with us; do not fear them" (14:7-9). But congregation threatens to stone them (14:10). Then "the glory of the LORD appeared at the tent of meeting to all the people of Israel" (14:10). God intervenes.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Total rebellion*

#### 14:11-25 — God Threatens Destruction, Moses Intercedes
**Lore:** "And the LORD said to Moses, 'How long will this people despise me? And how long will they not believe in me, in spite of all the signs that I have done among them? I will strike them with the pestilence and disinherit them, and I will make of you a nation greater and mightier than they'" (14:11-12). God offers to start over with Moses (echoes Ex 32:10). Moses intercedes: "Then the Egyptians will hear of it...and they will tell the inhabitants of this land...Now if you kill this people as one man, then the nations...will say, 'It is because the LORD was not able to bring this people into the land that he swore to give to them that he has killed them in the wilderness'" (14:13-16). Moses appeals to God's reputation! Then quotes God's own words: "The LORD is slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love, forgiving iniquity and transgression" (14:18; Ex 34:6-7). "Please pardon the iniquity of this people, according to the greatness of your steadfast love" (14:19). Bold intercession. God: "I have pardoned, according to your word" (14:20). But judgment still comes.
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Intercession prevails*

#### 14:26-38 — Forty Years of Wandering Decreed
**Lore:** "How long shall this wicked congregation grumble against me?...Say to them, 'As I live, declares the LORD, what you have said in my hearing I will do to you: your dead bodies shall fall in this wilderness, and of all your number, listed in the census from twenty years old and upward, who have grumbled against me, not one shall come into the land where I swore that I would make you dwell, except Caleb the son of Jephunneh and Joshua the son of Nun'" (14:26-30). All adults 20+ will die in wilderness. Only Caleb and Joshua enter. "And your children, who you said would become a prey, I will bring in, and they shall know the land that you have rejected. But as for you, your dead bodies shall fall in this wilderness. And your children shall be shepherds in the wilderness forty years and shall suffer for your faithlessness, until the last of your dead bodies lies in the wilderness. According to the number of the days that you spied out the land, forty days, a year for each day, you shall bear your iniquity forty years, and you shall know my displeasure" (14:31-34). 40 days spying → 40 years wandering. Entire generation lost. Ten faithless spies die by plague immediately (14:36-38). Hebrews 3:16-19 reflects on this: unbelief prevents entering rest.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Unbelief's consequence*

#### 14:39-45 — Presumptuous Attack Fails
**Lore:** When Moses tells Israel the decree, "the people mourned greatly" (14:39). Next morning: "Here we are. We will go up to the place that the LORD has promised, for we have sinned" (14:40). Too late! Moses warns: "Why now are you transgressing the command of the LORD, when that will not succeed? Do not go up, for the LORD is not among you...the Amalekites and the Canaanites are there before you, and you shall fall by the sword" (14:41-43). They go anyway. "But they presumed to go up to the heights of the hill country, although neither the ark of the covenant of the LORD nor Moses departed out of the camp. Then the Amalekites and the Canaanites who lived in that hill country came down and defeated them and pursued them, even to Hormah" (14:44-45). Disobedience in both directions: refusing to go when God says go, going when God says stop. No ark, no Moses = no God = defeat. Presumption as sinful as unbelief.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Presumption punished*
