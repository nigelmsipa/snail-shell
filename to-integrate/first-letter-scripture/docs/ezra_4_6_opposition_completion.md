# Ezra 4-6: Opposition and Completion

## Overview
The adversaries test the remnant's faithfulness through syncretistic offers and imperial opposition. Work stalls for 16 years due to discouragement (human failure). Prophets Haggai and Zechariah stir the people. Persian kings unknowingly serve God's purposes: Darius searches archives, finds Cyrus's decree, funds completion. Temple dedicated with joy. Central themes: Satanic devices, failed faith, prophetic encouragement, divine sovereignty over empires, worship restored.

---

## Chapter 4: Opposition Begins

#### 4:1-5 — Adversaries Offer "Help"
**Lore:** "Now when the adversaries of Judah and Benjamin heard that the returned exiles were building a temple to the LORD, the God of Israel, they approached Zerubbabel and the heads of fathers' houses and said to them, 'Let us build with you, for we worship your God as you do, and we have been sacrificing to him ever since the days of Esarhaddon king of Assyria who brought us here'" (4:1-2). **SATANIC DEVICE**—syncretistic offer. "Adversaries" (צָרִים, tsarim) = enemies, not allies. Who? Samaritans—mixed population from Assyrian resettlement (2 Kings 17:24-41). Assyria deported northern Israel (722 BC), replaced with pagan colonists who adopted corrupted Yahweh worship mixed with idolatry. "We worship your God as you do"—FALSE. They worship Yahweh + Baal + Asherah (2 Kings 17:33, "feared the LORD and also served their own gods"). This offer = **"device of Satan to beguile God's people into union and fellowship with His enemies"** (commentary). Syncretism destroys covenant purity—cannot mix holy and profane (Lev 10:10). "But Zerubbabel, Jeshua, and the rest of the heads of fathers' houses in Israel said to them, 'You have nothing to do with us in building a house to our God; but we alone will build to the LORD, the God of Israel, as King Cyrus the king of Persia has commanded us'" (4:3). **Firm refusal**—not xenophobia, but theological necessity. Separation = obedience. "Then the people of the land discouraged the people of Judah and made them afraid to build and bribed counselors against them to frustrate their purpose, all the days of Cyrus king of Persia, even until the reign of Darius king of Persia" (4:4-5). Rejection breeds hostility. Opposition escalates to intimidation + bribery. **TEST BEGINS**: Will remnant persevere despite pressure?
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Adversaries*

#### 4:6-16 — Letters to Ahasuerus and Artaxerxes
**Lore:** "And in the reign of Ahasuerus, in the beginning of his reign, they wrote an accusation against the inhabitants of Judah and Jerusalem" (4:6). Chronological jump—Ahasuerus (likely Xerxes I, 486-465 BC) and Artaxerxes (465-424 BC) reign AFTER temple completed (515 BC). Verses 6-23 = parenthetical flashback showing **pattern of opposition** across reigns. Probably refers to later wall-rebuilding (Neh 1-2), inserted here to illustrate ongoing adversarial tactics. "And in the days of Artaxerxes, Bishlam and Mithredath and Tabeel and the rest of their associates wrote to Artaxerxes king of Persia. The letter was written in Aramaic and translated" (4:7). **Aramaic section begins (4:8-6:18)**—language of Persian diplomacy. "Rehum the commander and Shimshai the scribe wrote a letter against Jerusalem to Artaxerxes the king" (4:8). Formal accusation: "The Jews who came up from you to us have gone to Jerusalem. They are rebuilding that rebellious and wicked city... finishing the walls and repairing the foundations. Now be it known to the king that if this city is rebuilt and the walls finished, they will not pay tribute, custom, or toll, and the royal revenue will be impaired" (4:12-13). **Political threat**: Jerusalem = historic rebel (true—see 2 Kings 24-25). Appeal to king's self-interest: lost tax revenue. "Moreover, we have sent and informed the king, in order that search may be made in the book of the records of your fathers. You will find in the book of the records and learn that this city is a rebellious city, hurtful to kings and provinces, and that sedition was stirred up in it from of old" (4:14-15). Accusation based on partial truth—Jerusalem did rebel, but omits: 1) Babylon destroyed it (no longer threat); 2) Cyrus authorized rebuilding.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Accusation*

#### 4:17-24 — Work Stopped by Royal Decree
**Lore:** "The king sent an answer: 'To Rehum the commander and Shimshai the scribe and the rest of their associates who live in Samaria and in the rest of the province Beyond the River, greeting. And now the letter that you sent to us has been plainly read before me. And I made a decree, and search has been made, and it has been found that this city from of old has risen against kings, and that rebellion and sedition have been made in it'" (4:17-19). Artaxerxes investigates, finds (selective) evidence. "Therefore make a decree that these men be made to cease, and that this city be not rebuilt, until a decree is made by me" (4:21). Orders halt. "Then, when the copy of King Artaxerxes' letter was read before Rehum and Shimshai the scribe and their associates, they went in haste to the Jews at Jerusalem and by force and power made them cease" (4:23). Adversaries enforce with violence. "Then the work on the house of God that is in Jerusalem stopped, and it ceased until the second year of the reign of Darius king of Persia" (4:24). **16-YEAR STALL** (536-520 BC). Why so long? Verse 24 returns to temple chronology (vv6-23 were parenthetical). Real reason for stall: NOT just external opposition, but **internal discouragement**. People "became discouraged and failed the test of their faith" (commentary). Instead of appealing to God, they "sought relief from human government"—faithless response. Prioritized building their own "paneled houses" over God's house (Hag 1:4). **Lesson: External opposition succeeds when internal faith fails.**
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Stalled-work*

## Chapter 5: Work Resumes

#### 5:1-5 — Prophets Stir the Remnant
**Lore:** "Now the prophets, Haggai and Zechariah the son of Iddo, prophesied to the Jews who were in Judah and Jerusalem, in the name of the God of Israel who was over them" (5:1). **Prophetic intervention**—God sends messengers to rebuke and encourage. Haggai's message (520 BC): "Is it a time for you yourselves to dwell in your paneled houses, while this house lies in ruins?" (Hag 1:4). Zechariah's message: "Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit, says the LORD" (Zech 4:6). Prophets expose misplaced priorities and call for faith-driven action. "Then Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel and Jeshua the son of Jozadak arose and began to rebuild the house of God that is in Jerusalem, and the prophets of God were with them, supporting them" (5:2). Leaders respond obediently—work resumes despite Artaxerxes' earlier order (different context: 4:21 was about walls; 5:2 returns to temple). "At the same time Tattenai the governor of the province Beyond the River and Shethar-bozenai and their associates came to them and spoke to them thus: 'Who gave you a decree to build this house and to finish this structure?'" (5:3). New opposition—different governor, different king (Darius now reigning). Challenge authority. "They also asked them this: 'What are the names of the men who are building this building?'" (5:4). Intimidation tactic—demand names for potential punishment. "But the eye of their God was on the elders of the Jews, and they did not stop them until the report should reach Darius and then an answer be returned by letter concerning it" (5:5). **KEY PHRASE**: "the eye of their God was on" them—divine protection. Work continues despite inquiry. Faith renewed.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Prophets-stir*

#### 5:6-17 — Tattenai's Report to Darius
**Lore:** "This is a copy of the letter that Tattenai the governor of the province Beyond the River and Shethar-bozenai and his associates, the governors who were in the province Beyond the River, sent to Darius the king" (5:6). Tattenai writes report (unlike Rehum's hostile letter, this one = neutral inquiry). "They sent him a report, in which was written as follows: 'To Darius the king, all peace. Be it known to the king that we went to the province of Judah, to the house of the great God. It is being built with huge stones, and timber is laid in the walls. This work goes on diligently and prospers in their hands'" (5:7-8). Acknowledges progress—"great God," not "their God" (respect?). "Then we asked those elders and spoke to them thus: 'Who gave you a decree to build this house and to finish this structure?' We also asked them their names, for your information, that we might write down the names of their leaders" (5:9-10). Formal inquiry. Elders' response: "We are the servants of the God of heaven and earth, and we are rebuilding the house that was built many years ago, which a great king of Israel built and finished. But because our fathers had angered the God of heaven, he gave them into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, the Chaldean, who destroyed this house and carried away the people to Babylonia" (5:11-12). **Honest confession**—acknowledge exile = divine judgment for sin, not Babylonian superiority. Theology > politics. "However, in the first year of Cyrus king of Babylon, Cyrus the king made a decree that this house of God should be rebuilt" (5:13). Cite legal precedent—Cyrus authorized this. Request: "Therefore, if it seems good to the king, let search be made in the royal archives there in Babylon, to see whether a decree was issued by Cyrus the king for the rebuilding of this house of God in Jerusalem. And let the king send us his pleasure in this matter" (5:17). Appeal to bureaucracy—find the original decree. **Providential setup**: Tattenai's inquiry becomes God's instrument to secure even greater support.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Report-sent*

## Chapter 6: Temple Completed

#### 6:1-12 — Darius's Decree: Full Support
**Lore:** "Then Darius the king made a decree, and search was made in Babylonia, in the house of the archives where the documents were stored" (6:1). King orders search. "And in Ecbatana, the citadel that is in the province of Media, a scroll was found on which this was written: 'A record. In the first year of Cyrus the king, Cyrus the king issued a decree: Concerning the house of God at Jerusalem, let the house be rebuilt, the place where sacrifices were offered, and let its foundations be retained. Its height shall be sixty cubits and its breadth sixty cubits, with three layers of great stones and one layer of timber. Let the cost be paid from the royal treasury'" (6:2-4). **ORIGINAL DECREE FOUND**—divine providence! Cyrus's authorization validated. Includes dimensions + funding from royal treasury. "Moreover, I make a decree regarding what you shall do for these elders of the Jews for the rebuilding of this house of God. The cost is to be paid to these men in full and without delay from the royal revenue, the tribute of the province from Beyond the River" (6:8). Darius not only confirms Cyrus's decree but **exceeds it**—orders immediate, full funding from provincial taxes! "And whatever is needed—bulls, rams, or sheep for burnt offerings to the God of heaven, wheat, salt, wine, or oil, as the priests at Jerusalem require—let that be given to them day by day without fail, that they may offer pleasing sacrifices to the God of heaven and pray for the life of the king and his sons" (6:9-10). Supplies sacrifices + requests Jewish prayers for royal family. Pagan king supports God's worship! "Also I make a decree that if anyone alters this edict, a beam shall be pulled out of his house, and he shall be impaled on it, and his house shall be made a dunghill" (6:11). Severe penalty for interference. "May the God who has caused his name to dwell there overthrow any king or people who shall put out a hand to alter this, or to destroy this house of God that is in Jerusalem. I, Darius, make a decree; let it be done with all diligence" (6:12). Darius invokes God's name—acknowledges Jerusalem = God's chosen place (cf. Deut 12:5). **DIVINE SOVEREIGNTY DEMONSTRATED**: God "turned the heart of the king" (6:22). Tattenai's opposition → becomes conduit for miraculous provision. Lesson: **God uses even adversarial inquiries to advance His purposes.**
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Kings-heart-turned*

#### 6:13-15 — Temple Finished
**Lore:** "Then, according to the word sent by Darius the king, Tattenai, the governor of the province Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai, and their associates did with all diligence what Darius the king had ordered" (6:13). Obedience immediate—those who questioned now fund and facilitate. "And the elders of the Jews built and prospered through the prophesying of Haggai the prophet and Zechariah the son of Iddo. They finished their building by decree of the God of Israel and by decree of Cyrus and Darius and Artaxerxes king of Persia" (6:14). **Triple decree**: God + Cyrus + Darius (+ Artaxerxes, who later supports Ezra, ch 7). Human decrees execute divine decree. "And this house was finished on the third day of the month of Adar, in the sixth year of the reign of Darius the king" (6:15). March 12, 515 BC—exactly 70 years after Solomon's temple destroyed (586 BC)—another prophetic precision (Jer 25:11). **From decree (538) to completion (515) = 23 years.** Why so long? Human failure (16-year stall, 4:24), not God's delay. Once faith renewed (5:2), completion swift (4 years). Lesson: **God's timing precise; human obedience variable.**
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Completion*

#### 6:16-18 — Dedication with Joy
**Lore:** "And the people of Israel, the priests and the Levites, and the rest of the returned exiles, celebrated the dedication of this house of God with joy" (6:16). **Dedication ceremony** (חֲנֻכַּת, chanukat)—cf. Solomon's dedication (1 Kings 8). "They offered at the dedication of this house of God 100 bulls, 200 rams, 400 lambs, and as a sin offering for all Israel 12 male goats, according to the number of the tribes of Israel" (6:17). Sacrifices = costly worship. **12 goats for 12 tribes**—even though only Judah/Benjamin/Levi primarily returned, remnant represents ALL Israel (cf. James 1:1, "twelve tribes in the dispersion"). Theological statement: Restoration for entire nation, not just Judah. "And they set the priests in their divisions and the Levites in their courses, for the service of God at Jerusalem, as it is written in the Book of Moses" (6:18). Mosaic order restored. **Continuity with Law**—not innovation, but restoration of ancient pattern. Worship = covenantal obedience.
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Dedication*

#### 6:19-22 — Passover Celebrated
**Lore:** "On the fourteenth day of the first month, the returned exiles kept the Passover" (6:19). First Passover in new temple—one month after dedication. Passover = foundational redemption narrative (Ex 12). "For the priests and the Levites had purified themselves together; all of them were clean. So they slaughtered the Passover lamb for all the returned exiles, for their fellow priests, and for themselves" (6:20). Ritual purity prerequisite (Num 9:6-13). Corporate purification = unity. "It was eaten by the people of Israel who had returned from exile, and also by everyone who had joined them and separated himself from the uncleanness of the peoples of the land to worship the LORD, the God of Israel" (6:21). Open to "everyone who had joined them and separated"—proselytes welcome IF they renounce idolatry (cf. 4:3, where Samaritans REFUSED separation). Purity = separation from idolatrous practices, not ethnic exclusion. "And they kept the Feast of Unleavened Bread seven days with joy, for the LORD had made them joyful and had turned the heart of the king of Assyria to them, so that he aided them in the work of the house of God, the God of Israel" (6:22). "King of Assyria" = anachronism for Persia (Assyrian Empire long gone), or title holdover from when Persia controlled former Assyrian territory. **KEY PHRASE**: "the LORD... had turned the heart of the king"—same language as 1:1 (God stirred Cyrus). Divine sovereignty bookends chapters 1-6. **LESSON**: From decree (ch 1) to dedication (ch 6), God orchestrates world empires to accomplish restoration. Human opposition delays, but cannot derail divine plan. Worship restored = covenant renewed.
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Passover-joy*
