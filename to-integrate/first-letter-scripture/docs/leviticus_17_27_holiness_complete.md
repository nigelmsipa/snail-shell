# Leviticus 17-27: The Holiness Code - Complete

## Part II: Living in Holiness (Leviticus 17-27)

### Chapter 17: The Sacredness of Blood

#### 17:1-16 — One Altar: Blood and Life
**Lore:** "And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying, 'Speak to Aaron and his sons and to all the people of Israel and say to them, This is what the LORD has commanded'" (17:1-2). Chapter 17 transitions from ritual purity to ethical holiness—from cult to community. **Centralized worship** (17:3-9): Any Israelite who slaughters an ox, lamb, or goat inside or outside the camp without bringing it to the tent entrance as an offering to the LORD "shall be guilty of bloodshed. He has shed blood, and that man shall be cut off from among his people" (17:4). All animal slaughter must go through the tabernacle. Why? "So that the people of Israel may bring their sacrifices that they sacrifice in the open field, that they may bring them to the LORD, to the priest at the entrance of the tent of meeting, and sacrifice them as sacrifices of peace offerings to the LORD" (17:5). Purpose: prevent offering to "goat demons" (שְׂעִירִם, se'irim, possibly pagan deities) they formerly worshiped (17:7). Worship centralized at God's altar prevents syncretism. Anyone offering burnt or sacrifice elsewhere is cut off (17:8-9). **The sacredness of blood** (17:10-16): "If any one of the house of Israel or of the strangers who sojourn among them eats any blood, I will set my face against that person who eats blood and will cut him off from among his people. For the life (נֶפֶשׁ, nephesh, "soul, life") of the flesh is in the blood, and I have given it for you on the altar to make atonement for your souls, for it is the blood that makes atonement by the life" (17:11). Central theological statement: blood = life. God designates blood for atonement; therefore, consuming it is forbidden. The life-force belongs to God alone. Even strangers (resident aliens) are bound by this law (17:10, 12). Hunters who kill animals for food must pour out the blood and cover it with earth (17:13). "For the life of every creature is its blood: its blood is its life" (17:14). Eating blood brings being cut off. Eating animals found dead (which still contain blood) makes one unclean until evening (17:15-16). Peter Lee explains this reminds Israel that life is sacred (17:14) and must not be consumed as food. All sacrifices use blood for atonement. The daily diet de-emphasizes meat (Israel's diet is mostly vegetarian according to the laws). This unit closes the sanctuary-centered material, pivoting Leviticus's concern from cult to community life. Typology: Hebrews 9:22, "Without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins." Christ's blood is the ultimate atonement. John 6:53-56, Jesus shocks hearers: "Unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood, you have no life in you." He redefines "consuming blood"—not literal but spiritual participation in His atoning death. The Lord's Supper: "This cup is the new covenant in my blood" (Luke 22:20). Believers spiritually "drink" Christ's blood, appropriating His atonement.
*Verses: 16 | Theme: Life is in the blood*

### Chapter 18: Sexual Purity

#### 18:1-30 — Sacred Boundaries: Forbidden Unions
**Lore:** "And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying, 'Speak to the people of Israel and say to them, I am the LORD your God. You shall not do as they do in the land of Egypt, where you lived, and you shall not do as they do in the land of Canaan, to which I am bringing you. You shall not walk in their statutes. You shall follow my rules and keep my statutes and walk in them. I am the LORD your God'" (18:1-4). Frame: Don't imitate Egypt or Canaan. Israel's sexual ethics must be distinct. "You shall therefore keep my statutes and my rules; if a person does them, he shall live by them: I am the LORD" (18:5). Obedience brings life; Paul quotes this in Romans 10:5 and Galatians 3:12. **Forbidden sexual relationships** (18:6-23): The phrase "uncover nakedness" (גָּלָה עֶרְוָה, galah ervah) is a euphemism for sexual intimacy. Prohibited unions: (1) Close relatives (18:6-18): Mother, stepmother, sister, half-sister, granddaughter, aunt, daughter-in-law, sister-in-law, stepdaughter, woman and her daughter or granddaughter. Incest is strictly forbidden—it violates family bonds and human dignity. (2) Menstruating woman (18:19): Sexual relations during menstruation defile. (3) Neighbor's wife (18:20): Adultery defiles. (4) Child sacrifice to Molech (18:21): "You shall not give any of your children to offer them to Molech, and so profane the name of your God." Human sacrifice is abomination. (5) Homosexual acts (18:22): "You shall not lie with a male as with a woman; it is an abomination (תּוֹעֵבָה, to'evah)." (6) Bestiality (18:23): "And you shall not lie with any animal and so make yourself unclean with it, neither shall any woman give herself to an animal to lie with it: it is perversion (תֶּבֶל, tevel, "confusion, violation of natural order")." **Warnings** (18:24-30): "Do not make yourselves unclean by any of these things, for by all these the nations I am driving out before you have become unclean, and the land became unclean, so that I punished its iniquity, and the land vomited out its inhabitants" (18:24-25). Sexual immorality defiles the land itself. The land is pictured as "vomiting out" those who commit such acts (18:28). "Therefore keep my charge never to practice any of these abominable customs that were practiced before you, and never to make yourselves unclean by them: I am the LORD your God" (18:30). The refrain "I am the LORD" appears throughout, reminding Israel that disobedience to these commandments—which defile body and land—incurs divine punishment. Peter Lee notes Leviticus 18's moral laws "anticipate the history of Israel and their habitual failure"; they underscore that sin defiles Israel's holy calling unless atoned. Typology: 1 Corinthians 6:18-20, "Flee from sexual immorality. Every other sin a person commits is outside the body, but the sexually immoral person sins against his own body. Or do you not know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you...? So glorify God in your body." Sexual purity is temple maintenance. Romans 1:24-27 echoes Leviticus 18's prohibitions, showing God's wrath against sexual perversion. Matthew 5:27-28, Jesus expands adultery to include lust—heart-level holiness.
*Verses: 30 | Theme: Sexual holiness*

### Chapter 19: The Heart of the Holiness Code

#### 19:1-37 — Holiness Code: Love, Justice, and Prohibitions
**Lore:** "And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying, 'Speak to all the congregation of the people of Israel and say to them, You shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy (קְדֹשִׁים תִּהְיוּ כִּי קָדוֹשׁ אֲנִי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם, kedoshim tihyu ki kadosh ani YHWH Eloheikhem)'" (19:1-2). This is THE verse—the core of Leviticus, the apex of the Holiness Code. Be holy because God is holy. What follows is a mixtape of moral commands, cultic rules, and social ethics—all facets of holiness. **Structure:** Christian commentators note a fivefold structure: (1) **Duties to parents and Sabbath** (19:3): "Every one of you shall revere his mother and his father, and you shall keep my Sabbaths: I am the LORD your God." Echoes the Decalogue's fifth and fourth commandments. (2) **Idolatry and offerings** (19:4-8): No idols. Peace offerings must be eaten within two days; third-day leftovers are profane (19:6-8). (3) **Social justice and neighbor love** (19:9-18): Gleaning laws—leave corners of fields and fallen grapes for the poor and sojourner (19:9-10). Echoed in Ruth 2. "You shall not steal; you shall not deal falsely; you shall not lie to one another" (19:11). "You shall not swear by my name falsely, and so profane the name of your God" (19:12). "You shall not oppress your neighbor or rob him. The wages of a hired worker shall not remain with you all night until the morning" (19:13). Economic justice. "You shall not curse the deaf or put a stumbling block before the blind, but you shall fear your God: I am the LORD" (19:14). Protect the vulnerable. "You shall do no injustice in court. You shall not be partial to the poor or defer to the great, but in righteousness shall you judge your neighbor" (19:15). Impartial justice. "You shall not go around as a slanderer among your people, and you shall not stand up against the life of your neighbor: I am the LORD" (19:16). "You shall not hate your brother in your heart, but you shall reason frankly with your neighbor, lest you incur sin because of him. You shall not take vengeance or bear a grudge against the sons of your own people, but you shall love your neighbor as yourself (וְאָהַבְתָּ לְרֵעֲךָ כָּמוֹךָ, ve'ahavta lere'akha kamokha): I am the LORD" (19:17-18). The climax! "Love your neighbor as yourself"—cited by Jesus as the second greatest commandment (Matthew 22:39, Mark 12:31, Luke 10:27). Paul: "The whole law is fulfilled in one word: 'You shall love your neighbor as yourself'" (Galatians 5:14). James calls it the "royal law" (James 2:8). (4) **Various laws** (19:19-25): Don't crossbreed livestock, sow fields with mixed seed, or wear mixed fabrics (19:19)—maintaining boundaries reflects God's order. Sexual relations with a slave woman betrothed to another: guilt offering required (19:20-22). Fruit trees: first three years forbidden, fourth year holy to the LORD, fifth year edible (19:23-25). (5) **Prohibition of pagan practices** (19:26-31): No eating blood, no divination, no fortune-telling (19:26). Don't cut hair at temples' edges or mar beard edges (pagan mourning practices, 19:27). No cuts on body or tattoos for the dead (19:28). Don't profane your daughter by making her a prostitute (19:29). Keep Sabbaths, revere the sanctuary (19:30). "Do not turn to mediums or necromancers; do not seek them out, and so make yourselves unclean by them: I am the LORD your God" (19:31). (6) **Social justice continued** (19:32-37): "You shall stand up before the gray head and honor the face of an old man, and you shall fear your God: I am the LORD" (19:32). Respect elders. "When a stranger sojourns with you in your land, you shall not do him wrong. You shall treat the stranger who sojourns with you as the native among you, and you shall love him as yourself, for you were strangers in the land of Egypt: I am the LORD your God" (19:33-34). Love the foreigner—revolutionary! "You shall do no wrong in judgment, in measures of length or weight or quantity" (19:35). Honest business. "You shall keep all my statutes and all my rules, and do them: I am the LORD" (19:37). Each ordinance punctuated by "I am the LORD." The refrain appears 16 times in chapter 19. In memory, the stunning climax is "you shall love your neighbor as yourself" (19:18) set amidst these laws. Adventists highlight that Jesus repeatedly cited Leviticus 19 in His teaching; thus this code bridges ritual and life. Peter Lee: "Leviticus 19 as 'the Lord's holiness in a nutshell.'" Holiness encompasses worship, ethics, economics, justice, compassion.
*Verses: 37 | Theme: Be holy as God is holy*

### Chapter 20: Penalties for Defilement

#### 20:1-27 — Consequences of Defilement: Purge or Perish
**Lore:** "The LORD spoke to Moses, saying, 'Say to the people of Israel, Any one of the people of Israel or of the strangers who sojourn in Israel who gives any of his children to Molech shall surely be put to death. The people of the land shall stone him with stones'" (20:1-2). Chapter 20 enumerates punishments for many sins listed in 18-19. It serves as a practical appendix: what happens if the people aren't holy. **Death penalties** (20:2-21): Seven crimes carry capital punishment: (1) Child sacrifice to Molech (20:2-5). Death by stoning. If the community ignores it, God Himself will cut off the offender and family. (2) Consulting mediums/necromancers (20:6). Cut off by God. (3) Cursing parents (20:9). Death penalty. Echoes Exodus 21:17. (4) Adultery (20:10). Both participants put to death. (5) Incest (20:11-12, 14). Death. A man who marries a woman and her mother: all three burned with fire (20:14). (6) Homosexual acts (20:13). "If a man lies with a male as with a woman, both of them have committed an abomination; they shall surely be put to death; their blood is upon them." (7) Bestiality (20:15-16). Death for human and animal. **Lesser penalties** (20:17-21): Sexual relations with sister, menstruating woman, aunt: cut off or childlessness. **Call to holiness** (20:7-8, 22-27): "Consecrate yourselves, therefore, and be holy, for I am the LORD your God. Keep my statutes and do them; I am the LORD who sanctifies you" (20:7-8). The refrain "you shall be holy" continues (e.g., 20:7, 26). "You shall therefore keep all my statutes and all my rules and do them, that the land where I am bringing you to live may not vomit you out" (20:22). Defilement leads to exile—land itself rejects the unholy. "And you shall be holy to me, for I the LORD am holy and have separated you from the peoples, that you should be mine" (20:26). Israel's distinctiveness = holiness. "A man or a woman who is a medium or a necromancer shall surely be put to death. They shall be stoned with stones; their blood shall be upon them" (20:27). At the same time, God's holiness is in view: Leviticus 20:8 promises forgiveness if one repents. The chapter closes with "I am the LORD who brought you up from Egypt" (20:24), reminding Israel that their destiny in the land depends on maintaining sanctity typified by the sanctuary rituals. Notably, seven crimes carry the death penalty—underscoring that such sins defile community and land. Peter Lee notes the severity reinforces the holiness theme: community must purge evil to remain in God's presence.
*Verses: 27 | Theme: Penalties enforce holiness*

### Chapters 21-22: Priestly Holiness

#### 21:1-24 — Ordained to Be Clean: Priestly Regulations
**Lore:** "And the LORD said to Moses, 'Speak to the priests, the sons of Aaron, and say to them: No one shall make himself unclean for the dead among his people'" (21:1). Chapter 21 sets stricter purity laws on priests—they serve a higher standard. **General priests** (21:1-9): May not touch corpses except for close relatives (mother, father, son, daughter, brother, unmarried sister, 21:2-3). No pagan mourning rituals: no baldness, beard shaving, body cuts (21:5). "They shall be holy to their God and not profane the name of their God" (21:6). They offer the LORD's food offerings. Marriage restrictions: priests may not marry prostitutes, divorced women, or defiled women; only virgins or priestly widows (21:7-8). If a priest's daughter profanes herself by prostitution, she defiles her father; she shall be burned (21:9). **High priest** (21:10-15): Even stricter. May not let hair hang loose or tear clothes (signs of mourning). May not go near any dead body, even father or mother (21:11). "He shall not go out of the sanctuary, lest he profane the sanctuary of his God, for the consecration of the anointing oil of his God is upon him: I am the LORD" (21:12). Marriage: must marry a virgin of his own people, not a widow, divorced woman, prostitute, or defiled woman (21:13-14). "He shall not profane his offspring among his people, for I am the LORD who sanctifies him" (21:15). **Physical defects disqualify** (21:16-24): No descendant of Aaron with a blemish may approach to offer the LORD's food offerings: blind, lame, mutilated face, limb too long, injured foot/hand, hunchback, dwarf, defect in sight, itching disease, scabs, crushed testicles (21:18-20). He may eat the holy food but may not come near the veil or altar "because he has a blemish, that he may not profane my sanctuaries" (21:23). The restriction isn't about personal sin but maintaining the sanctuary's unblemished standard—everything offered to God must be perfect. These chapters emphasize that God's servants must reflect His holiness in person and service. Liturgically, they isolate the sacred. Verse 22:16 warns a priest who profanes holy gifts will be "cut off"—a theme echoing Nadab/Abihu. Notice again "I am the LORD" tags each section, tying ethics to divine identity. Typology: Hebrews 7:26, Christ is the perfect High Priest: "holy, innocent, unstained, separated from sinners, and exalted above the heavens." He has no defect, no blemish. He fulfills the priestly ideal.
*Verses: 24 | Theme: Higher standard for priests*

#### 22:1-33 — Sacred Offerings and Acceptable Sacrifices
**Lore:** "And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying, 'Tell Aaron and his sons to keep away from the holy things of the people of Israel, which they dedicate to me, so that they do not profane my holy name: I am the LORD'" (22:1-2). Priests with uncleanness (discharge, leprosy, touched corpse, etc.) must not eat holy food until clean (22:3-7). Eating while unclean = being cut off (22:3). Priests' families may eat holy food, but not hired workers or visitors (22:10-11). Priest's daughter who marries outside the priesthood may not eat offerings; if widowed or divorced without children, she may return and eat (22:12-13). Anyone who eats holy food accidentally must repay plus 20% (22:14). "They shall not profane the holy things of the people of Israel, which they contribute to the LORD, and so cause them to bear iniquity and guilt, by eating their holy things: for I am the LORD who sanctifies them" (22:15-16). **Acceptable sacrifices** (22:17-25): Animals offered must be without blemish—male for burnt offerings, either gender for freewill offerings (22:18-19). Anything with defect is not acceptable: blind, injured, maimed, ulcer, scab, discharge (22:22-24). Even animals from foreigners must be unblemished (22:25). **Additional regulations** (22:26-30): Newborn animals must remain with mother 7 days; from 8th day acceptable (22:27). Don't slaughter cow/ewe and offspring on same day (22:28). Thanksgiving offerings must be eaten same day (22:30). "So you shall keep my commandments and do them: I am the LORD. And you shall not profane my holy name, that I may be sanctified among the people of Israel. I am the LORD who sanctifies you, who brought you out of the land of Egypt to be your God: I am the LORD" (22:31-33). Fourfold "I am the LORD" closes the section. Holiness requires perfection—unblemished animals, pure priests, reverent handling. Christ is the unblemished Lamb.
*Verses: 33 | Theme: Only the unblemished approach God*

### Chapter 23: The Sacred Calendar

#### 23:1-44 — Divine Rhythms: Sabbaths and Feasts
**Lore:** "The LORD spoke to Moses, saying, 'Speak to the people of Israel and say to them, These are the appointed feasts (מוֹעֲדִים, mo'adim, "appointed times") of the LORD that you shall proclaim as holy convocations (מִקְרָא קֹדֶשׁ, miqra qodesh, "sacred assemblies"); they are my appointed feasts'" (23:1-2). God's calendar structures Israel's year around holy rhythms. **Weekly Sabbath** (23:3): "Six days shall work be done, but on the seventh day is a Sabbath of solemn rest, a holy convocation. You shall do no work. It is a Sabbath to the LORD in all your dwellings." Foundation of the calendar. **Spring feasts** (23:4-22): (1) **Passover** (23:5): Fourteenth day of first month (Nisan) at twilight. (2) **Unleavened Bread** (23:6-8): Fifteenth day, 7 days, first and last days are holy convocations. No leaven. (3) **Firstfruits** (23:9-14): Day after Sabbath during Unleavened Bread, bring sheaf of first grain, wave before the LORD. Burnt and grain offerings. No eating new grain until this offering. (4) **Weeks/Pentecost** (23:15-22): Count 50 days from Firstfruits. Two loaves of leavened bread (unique—leaven allowed because it's not burned on altar), burnt offerings, sin offering, peace offerings. Gleaning laws reiterated (23:22). **Fall feasts** (23:23-44): (5) **Trumpets/Rosh Hashanah** (23:23-25): First day of seventh month (Tishri), holy convocation, trumpet blast, no work. (6) **Day of Atonement/Yom Kippur** (23:26-32): Tenth day of seventh month. "Afflict yourselves" (fast). No work. "For on this day shall atonement be made for you to cleanse you. You shall be clean before the LORD from all your sins" (23:28). Most solemn day. Anyone who doesn't afflict themselves or who works is cut off (23:29-30). "Sabbath of solemn rest" (23:32). (7) **Tabernacles/Booths/Sukkot** (23:33-43): Fifteenth day of seventh month, 7 days. First and eighth days are holy convocations. Live in booths (temporary shelters) to remember the wilderness (23:42-43). Take choice fruit, palm fronds, leafy branches, willows; rejoice before the LORD (23:40). "You shall dwell in booths for seven days. All native Israelites shall dwell in booths, that your generations may know that I made the people of Israel dwell in booths when I brought them out of the land of Egypt: I am the LORD your God" (23:42-43). Theologically, these festivals reenact Israel's history (Egypt, wilderness, harvest, atonement, dwelling in God's presence) and foreshadow Christ. **Passover** = Christ our Passover sacrificed (1 Corinthians 5:7). **Unleavened Bread** = purge the leaven of sin (1 Corinthians 5:8). **Firstfruits** = Christ's resurrection, "the firstfruits of those who have slept" (1 Corinthians 15:20). **Pentecost** = outpouring of the Holy Spirit (Acts 2). **Trumpets** = final trumpet, resurrection/rapture (1 Thessalonians 4:16, 1 Corinthians 15:52). **Day of Atonement** = judgment and final cleansing (Hebrews 9:7-14, SDA sanctuary theology). **Tabernacles** = Millennium or eternal dwelling with God (Zechariah 14:16, Revelation 21:3). In structure, the chapter is a patterned tour from spring to fall. For memory: Sabbath (23:2-3), Passover (23:4-8), Harvest (23:9-22), Seventh Moon (23:23-25), Atonement (23:26-32), Booths (23:33-43). The chapter frames Israel's communal life around God's presence and timing—a holy "rhythm" for the nation.
*Verses: 44 | Theme: Holy festivals*

### Chapter 24: Sanctuary Light and Justice

#### 24:1-9 — Light of the Sanctuary: Lampstand and Bread
**Lore:** "The LORD spoke to Moses, saying, 'Command the people of Israel to bring you pure oil from beaten olives for the lamp, that a light may be kept burning regularly (תָּמִיד, tamid, "continually")'" (24:1-2). Aaron shall arrange the lamps on the lampstand before the LORD from evening to morning regularly (24:3-4). Pure lampstand, perpetual light—symbolizing God's eternal presence. **Showbread** (24:5-9): "You shall take fine flour and bake twelve loaves of it...And you shall set them in two piles, six in a pile, on the table of pure gold before the LORD. And you shall put pure frankincense on each pile, that it may go with the bread as a memorial portion as a food offering to the LORD. Every Sabbath day Aaron shall arrange it before the LORD regularly; it is from the people of Israel as a covenant forever" (24:5-8). Twelve loaves (one per tribe) set before God, replaced every Sabbath. Old loaves eaten by priests in a holy place (24:9). "It is for him and his descendants as his portion forever from the LORD's food offerings, a perpetual due" (24:9). These images recall creation (lights in 24:3) and covenant (bread as gratitude). The lamp's continual light symbolizes God's eternal presence; the bread, fresh each Sabbath, symbolizes God's ongoing provision. Jesus later calls Himself the "bread of life" (John 6:35), echoing this holy bread. Matthew 12:3-4, Jesus defends His disciples by citing David eating the showbread (1 Samuel 21:1-6)—showing mercy trumps ritual. This pericope stands as a quiet prelude to the crisis that follows—even holy objects must be tended exactly.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Light and bread before God*

#### 24:10-23 — Justice Among the People: Blasphemy and Retribution
**Lore:** "Now an Israelite woman's son, whose father was an Egyptian, went out among the people of Israel. And the Israelite woman's son and a man of Israel fought in the camp, and the Israelite woman's son blasphemed the Name (הַשֵּׁם, ha-Shem, "the Name" = YHWH), and cursed" (24:10-11). Tragic case: a half-Israelite blasphemes God's name in anger. They bring him to Moses. "And the LORD said to Moses, 'Bring out of the camp the one who cursed, and let all who heard him lay their hands on his head, and let all the congregation stone him'" (24:13-14). Public execution for blasphemy. "And speak to the people of Israel, saying, Whoever curses his God shall bear his sin. Whoever blasphemes the name of the LORD shall surely be put to death. All the congregation shall stone him. The sojourner as well as the native, when he blasphemes the Name, shall be put to death" (24:15-16). Equal law for native and foreigner. Then the famous lex talionis ("law of retaliation"): "Whoever takes a human life shall surely be put to death. Whoever takes an animal's life shall make it good, life for life. If anyone injures his neighbor, as he has done it shall be done to him, fracture for fracture, eye for eye, tooth for tooth; whatever injury he has given a person shall be given to him" (24:17-20). Proportional justice—not personal vengeance but court-administered equity. Jesus reinterprets this in Matthew 5:38-39: "You have heard that it was said, 'An eye for an eye and a tooth for a tooth.' But I say to you, Do not resist the one who is evil. But if anyone slaps you on the right cheek, turn to him the other also." Jesus moves from legal justice to personal mercy. "You shall have the same rule for the sojourner and for the native, for I am the LORD your God" (24:22). The blasphemer is stoned (24:23). This pericope teaches that treating God's name or image irreverently brings death (24:16), just as violating others' bodies earns the same measure of penalty. The placement is thematic: the cult rules are punctuated by a real-life case reinforcing God's holiness and communal responsibility. Holiness undergirds justice: God's reputation must be protected by impartial law.
*Verses: 14 | Theme: Blasphemy and justice*
