# Job 22-28: The Third Cycle — Arguments Collapse, Wisdom Sought

## Overview
The third dialogue cycle disintegrates. **Eliphaz** abandons theology for false accusations of specific sins. **Job** responds with cry for God to appear, then surprisingly describes wicked's judgment (friends' words in his mouth?). **Bildad** offers brief, weak speech (friends running out of steam). **Zophar silent** (third cycle incomplete—dialogue breakdown). Job delivers long monologue (Ch 26-27) asserting innocence. **Chapter 28: Wisdom Poem**—chiastic center of book, distinct from surrounding dialogue. Neither Job nor friends claim to possess Wisdom; only God knows where it dwells. Poem functions as pause, divine editorial comment: human wisdom (friends' theology, Job's protests) inadequate. Central themes: Breakdown of human argumentation, false accusations emerge when theology fails, proto-apologetics (Job defends innocence), Wisdom transcendent/inaccessible to mortals, fear of Lord = human wisdom's limit.

## Chapter 22: Eliphaz's Third Speech — False Accusations

#### 22:1-11 — Can a Man Be Profitable to God? You Must Have Sinned
**Lore:** "Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered and said, Can a man be profitable unto God, as he that is wise may be profitable unto himself?" (22:1-2). **Eliphaz's premise**—God doesn't NEED humans (true), therefore doesn't punish arbitrarily (false inference). "Is it any pleasure to the Almighty, that thou art righteous? or is it gain to him, that thou makest thy ways perfect?" (22:3). True: God self-sufficient. False: Therefore suffering must be deserved. "Will he reprove thee for fear of thee? will he enter with thee into judgment? Is not thy wickedness great? and thine iniquities infinite?" (22:4-5). **Logical leap**—since God doesn't need you, suffering = proof of "infinite iniquities." Circular reasoning. **FALSE ACCUSATIONS BEGIN**: "For thou hast taken a pledge from thy brother for nought, and stripped the naked of their clothing. Thou hast not given water to the weary to drink, and thou hast withholden bread from the hungry" (22:6-7). **Fabricated sins**—Eliphaz inventing specific transgressions (no evidence). Violates Exod 22:26-27 (returning pledges), Isa 58:7 (feed hungry, clothe naked). Job's actual character opposite (29:12-17, defended poor). **Diagnostic**: When theology fails to explain reality, humans invent facts to fit theology. "But as for the mighty man, he had the earth; and the honourable man dwelt in it. Thou hast sent widows away empty, and the arms of the fatherless have been broken" (22:8-9). More false accusations—oppressing widows/orphans (Exod 22:22-24 prohibits). Job actually rescued these (29:12-13). "Therefore snares are round about thee, and sudden fear troubleth thee; Or darkness, that thou canst not see; and abundance of waters cover thee" (22:10-11). Conclusion: snares/darkness/floods = punishment for fabricated sins. **Eliphaz's failure**: Abandoned evidence-based reasoning, resorted to slander. Violates 9th commandment (false witness). **Warning**: Defend bad theology with lies = compounding error.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Slander*

#### 22:12-20 — Is Not God High? Yet You Say, 'What Does God Know?'
**Lore:** "Is not God in the height of heaven? and behold the height of the stars, how high they are! And thou sayest, How doth God know? can he judge through the dark cloud?" (22:12-13). **Strawman argument**—Eliphaz falsely claims Job denies God's omniscience. Job never said this. "Thick clouds are a covering to him, that he seeth not; and he walketh in the circuit of heaven" (22:14). Quote of imagined Job position—God can't see through clouds (absurd, Job never claimed). "Hast thou marked the old way which wicked men have trodden? Which were cut down out of time, whose foundation was overflown with a flood" (22:15-16). **Flood generation reference**—Gen 6-8. Wicked destroyed. "Which said unto God, Depart from us: and what can the Almighty do for them?" (22:17). Quotes wicked's words (actually Job quoted similar in 21:14-15, but Job reporting, not endorsing). Eliphaz conflates Job with wicked. "Yet he filled their houses with good things: but the counsel of the wicked is far from me" (22:18). Ironic: Eliphaz quotes Job's own words (21:16) back at him, as if Job said opposite. Confusion or dishonesty? "The righteous see it, and are glad: and the innocent laugh them to scorn. Whereas our substance is not cut down, but the remnant of them the fire consumeth" (22:19-20). Righteous rejoice at wicked's destruction. Implied: I (Eliphaz) rejoice at YOUR destruction. Cruel pastoral failure.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Ignorance*

#### 22:21-30 — Acquaint Yourself With God and Be at Peace
**Lore:** "Acquaint now thyself with him, and be at peace: thereby good shall come unto thee" (22:21). **Call to repentance**—"acquaint" (סָכַן, sakhan, be of use/benefit). "Receive, I pray thee, the law from his mouth, and lay up his words in thine heart. If thou return to the Almighty, thou shalt be built up, thou shalt put away iniquity far from thy tabernacles" (22:22-23). Standard repentance formula—receive Torah, return, remove iniquity. Problem: assumes Job departed (false). "Then shalt thou lay up gold as dust, and the gold of Ophir as the stones of the brooks. Yea, the Almighty shall be thy defence, and thou shalt have plenty of silver" (22:24-25). **Prosperity gospel**—repent = wealth restored. "Gold as dust" (ironic reversal: gold common as dirt). Ophir = legendary gold source (1 Kgs 9:28). "For then shalt thou have thy delight in the Almighty, and shalt lift up thy face unto God. Thou shalt make thy prayer unto him, and he shall hear thee, and thou shalt pay thy vows" (22:26-27). Promises: delight, answered prayer, fulfilled vows. Conditional on repentance for sins Job didn't commit. "Thou shalt also decree a thing, and it shall be established unto thee: and the light shall shine upon thy ways. When men are cast down, then thou shalt say, There is lifting up; and he shall save the humble person" (22:28-29). Name-it-claim-it theology—"decree a thing, established." "He shall deliver the island of the innocent: and it is delivered by the pureness of thine hands" (22:30). **Final irony**—"innocent delivered by pure hands." Job IS innocent with pure hands (16:17), yet suffering. Eliphaz's own theology refutes his accusations. **Eliphaz's tragedy**: Started with mystical experience (Ch 4), devolved to slander (Ch 22). **Lesson**: Wrong theology + pride = cruelty. Better to confess ignorance (Agur, Prov 30:2-4) than invent lies.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Repentance*

## Chapter 23-24: Job's Response — Oh, That I Knew Where to Find Him!

#### 23:1-7 — Oh, That I Knew Where I Might Find Him!
**Lore:** "Then Job answered and said, Even to day is my complaint bitter: my stroke is heavier than my groaning" (23:1-2). "Complaint bitter" (מְרִי, meri, rebellion?). "Stroke heavier than groaning" = suffering exceeds capacity to express. **"Oh that I knew where I might find him! that I might come even to his seat! I would order my cause before him, and fill my mouth with arguments"** (23:3-4). **Longing for divine encounter**—"where I might find Him" = God hidden (Isa 45:15, "Deus absconditus"). "Come to his seat" (תְּכוּנָה, tekhunah, established place) = throne/court. "Order my cause" (עָרַךְ, arakh) = legal term, arrange case systematically. "Fill mouth with arguments" (תּוֹכָחוֹת, tokhachot) = reasoned rebuttals. Job confident in his case IF God would appear. "I would know the words which he would answer me, and understand what he would say unto me. Will he plead against me with his great power? No; but he would put strength in me" (23:5-6). **Surprising confidence**—expects God would empower him, not crush him. Believes God ultimately just, wants fair hearing. "There the righteous might dispute with him; so should I be delivered for ever from my judge" (23:7). "Righteous dispute with Him" = covenant lawsuit (רִיב, riv). Confident in acquittal IF trial held. **Theology**: Access to God = solution. Problem: God inaccessible. Anticipates Christ as Mediator (1 Tim 2:5), way to Father (John 14:6).
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Longing*

#### 23:8-12 — I Go Forward, But He Is Not There; Backward, But I Cannot Perceive Him
**Lore:** "Behold, I go forward, but he is not there; and backward, but I cannot perceive him: On the left hand, where he doth work, but I cannot behold him: he hideth himself on the right hand, that I cannot see him" (23:8-9). **Four-directional search**—forward (east?), backward (west?), left (north), right (south). Comprehensive spatial search = futile. God omnipresent yet hidden (Ps 139:7-12, nowhere to flee FROM God, but here can't flee TO God). "But he knoweth the way that I take: when he hath tried me, I shall come forth as gold" (23:10). **FAMOUS VERSE**—"tried me, come forth as gold." Refining metaphor (Ps 66:10, Zech 13:9, Mal 3:3, 1 Pet 1:7). Suffering = refining fire, not punishing fire. Job = ore being purified. Emerges more valuable. Faith statement: present suffering has purpose (even if unknown). "My foot hath held his steps, his way have I kept, and not declined. Neither have I gone back from the commandment of his lips; I have esteemed the words of his mouth more than my necessary food" (23:11-12). **Integrity assertion**—"held his steps" (followed closely), "kept his way," "not declined," valued God's words above food (cf. Matt 4:4, Deut 8:3, "man lives by... word of God"). Earnest obedience, yet suffering. Refutes Eliphaz's slander. **Lesson**: Obedience doesn't guarantee ease. Tested faith more valuable than untested (1 Pet 1:6-7).
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Refining*

#### 23:13-17 — He Is in One Mind; Who Can Turn Him? He Performs What Is Appointed
**Lore:** "But he is in one mind, and who can turn him? and what his soul desireth, even that he doeth. For he performeth the thing that is appointed for me: and many such things are with him" (23:13-14). **Divine sovereignty**—"in one mind" (בְּאֶחָד, be'echad, in one/alone), unchangeable. "Who can turn him?" (rhetorical, none can). "What his soul desires, he does" = absolute freedom. "Performs what is appointed" (חֻקִּי, chuqi, my decree/portion). Determinism? Or God's purposes accomplished regardless of human resistance? "Many such things with him" = many mysterious purposes. "Therefore am I troubled at his presence: when I consider, I am afraid of him. For God maketh my heart soft, and the Almighty troubleth me: Because I was not cut off before the darkness, neither hath he covered the darkness from my face" (23:15-17). **Terror before sovereign God**—"troubled at his presence," "afraid," "heart soft" (רָכַךְ, rakhakh, timid/faint). Yet Job still wants encounter (paradox: fear + desire). "Not cut off before darkness" = wishes he'd died before suffering. "Not covered darkness from face" = forced to endure. **Tension**: Sovereign God + hidden God + suffering saint = mystery. Job doesn't resolve, holds in tension. NT: Rom 9:20-21 (potter/clay), God's sovereign purposes beyond full human comprehension. Yet also Rom 8:28, works all things for good (Job 42 confirms).
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Sovereignty*

#### 24:1-12 — Why Are Times Not Stored Up By the Almighty?
**Lore:** "Why, seeing times are not hidden from the Almighty, do they that know him not see his days?" (24:1). "Times not hidden" = God aware of all events. "Why don't those who know Him see his days?" = Why doesn't God set appointed judgment days (visible justice)? "Some remove the landmarks; they violently take away flocks, and feed thereof. They drive away the ass of the fatherless, they take the widow's ox for a pledge" (24:2-3). **Catalog of injustices**—removing landmarks (Deut 19:14, 27:17 prohibit), stealing flocks, oppressing orphans/widows (vulnerable). "They turn the needy out of the way: the poor of the earth hide themselves together" (24:4). Poor forced into hiding (social invisibility). "Behold, as wild asses in the desert, go they forth to their work; rising betimes for a prey: the wilderness yieldeth food for them and for their children" (24:5). Poor scavenge like wild donkeys—wilderness subsistence. "They reap every one his corn in the field: and they gather the vintage of the wicked. They cause the naked to lodge without clothing, that they have no covering in the cold" (24:6-7). Harvest others' fields, no warm clothing (violates Exod 22:26-27). "They are wet with the showers of the mountains, and embrace the rock for want of a shelter. They pluck the fatherless from the breast, and take a pledge of the poor" (24:8-9). Exposure to elements, orphans torn away, pledges extracted. "They cause him to go naked without clothing, and they take away the sheaf from the hungry; Which make oil within their walls, and tread their winepresses, and suffer thirst" (24:10-11). Naked laborers, food producers go hungry/thirsty (exploitation). "Men groan from out of the city, and the soul of the wounded crieth out: yet God layeth not folly to them" (24:12). **Climax**—"men groan," "wounded cry out," YET "God lays not folly to them" = doesn't charge oppressors with wrongdoing (no immediate judgment). **Job's empirical theology**: Delayed justice real phenomenon. Oppressors thrive, victims suffer, God silent. Doesn't mean God unjust, means justice timeline exceeds human expectations. NT: 2 Pet 3:9, "Lord not slow... but longsuffering." Rev 6:10, martyrs cry "How long?" Ultimate justice assured (Rev 20:12), but delayed.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Injustice*

#### 24:13-17 — They Rebel Against the Light; The Murderer, Adulterer, and Thief
**Lore:** "They are of those that rebel against the light; they know not the ways thereof, nor abide in the paths thereof" (24:13). "Rebel against light" = moral darkness chosen. "The murderer rising with the light killeth the poor and needy, and in the night is as a thief. The eye also of the adulterer waiteth for the twilight, saying, No eye shall see me: and disguiseth his face" (24:14-15). **Catalog of night crimes**—murderer kills at dawn, thief operates at night, adulterer waits for twilight. All hide from light (cf. John 3:19-20, "men loved darkness... evil deeds"). "In the dark they dig through houses, which they had marked for themselves in the daytime: they know not the light. For the morning is to them even as the shadow of death: if one know them, they are in the terrors of the shadow of death" (24:16-17). Burglars mark houses by day, break in at night. "Morning to them as shadow of death" = light = danger (reversal: normally darkness feared, here light feared). Wicked prefer darkness.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Darkness*

#### 24:18-25 — They Are Swift as Waters; Their Portion Is Cursed in the Earth
**Lore:** "He is swift as the waters; their portion is cursed in the earth: he beholdeth not the way of the vineyards" (24:18). **Textual problem**—sudden shift to describing wicked's punishment (contradicts Ch 21, Job's argument they prosper). Some scholars attribute vv.18-24 to friends' voice (Bildad? Zophar?), or Job sarcastically quoting friends. "Drought and heat consume the snow waters: so doth the grave those which have sinned. The womb shall forget him; the worm shall feed sweetly on him; he shall be no more remembered; and wickedness shall be broken as a tree" (24:19-20). Wicked forgotten, worms consume, wickedness broken—standard retribution theology. "He evil entreateth the barren that beareth not: and doeth not good to the widow" (24:21). Oppressing barren/widows (echoes 24:3). "He draweth also the mighty with his power: he riseth up, and no man is sure of life. Though it be given him to be in safety, whereon he resteth; yet his eyes are upon their ways" (24:22-23). God watches wicked despite apparent safety. "They are exalted for a little while, but are gone and brought low; they are taken out of the way as all other, and cut off as the tops of the ears of corn" (24:24). Temporary exaltation, then cut down. "And if it be not so now, who will make me a liar, and make my speech nothing worth?" (24:25). Challenge: prove me wrong. **Interpretive options**: (1) Job sarcastically quoting friends; (2) Job conceding wicked eventually punished (but still unjustly delayed); (3) Textual corruption/misplaced speech. **Theological point**: Even if wicked eventually punished, doesn't explain righteous suffering NOW.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Punishment*

## Chapter 25: Bildad's Third Speech — Man Cannot Be Righteous

#### 25:1-6 — Dominion and Fear Are With God; How Can Man Be Justified?
**Lore:** "Then answered Bildad the Shuhite, and said, Dominion and fear are with him, he maketh peace in his high places" (25:1-2). **Shortest friend speech**—Bildad running out of arguments. "Dominion and fear" = God's sovereign power. "Makes peace in high places" (angelic realm, cf. Job 1:6, divine council). "Is there any number of his armies? and upon whom doth not his light arise?" (25:3). God's armies innumerable (angelic hosts, cf. 2 Kgs 6:17, Heb 12:22). Light universal. "How then can man be justified with God? or how can he be clean that is born of a woman?" (25:4). **Recycled argument**—echoes Eliphaz 4:17, 15:14. Anthropological pessimism: humans can't be righteous. True as diagnosis (Rom 3:23), but false as explanation for Job's suffering (doesn't address WHY righteous suffer MORE than wicked). "Behold even to the moon, and it shineth not; yea, the stars are not pure in his sight. How much less man, that is a worm? and the son of man, which is a worm?" (25:5-6). Even celestial bodies impure compared to God. "Man = worm" (רִמָּה, rimmah, maggot). Degrading anthropology. **Bildad's failure**: True theology (God holy, man sinful) applied wrongly (therefore Job's suffering deserved). Missing: grace, redemption, Christ's justification (Rom 5:1, "justified by faith"). **Dialogue breakdown signal**—Bildad's brevity shows friends exhausted their arguments. Can only repeat old claims. Job won empirical debate (wicked prosper, Ch 21), friends have no response except "God great, man small."
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Insignificance*

## Chapter 26-27: Job's Response — I Will Maintain My Integrity

#### 26:1-4 — How Have You Helped Him Without Power?
**Lore:** "But Job answered and said, How hast thou helped him that is without power? how savest thou the arm that hath no strength?" (26:1-2). **Sarcasm**—"Great help you are!" Friends claimed to comfort, but crushed instead. "Without power" (לֹא־כֹחַ, lo-koach), "no strength" (לֹא־עֹז, lo-oz) = Job's weakness unaddressed. "How hast thou counselled him that hath no wisdom? and how hast thou plentifully declared the thing as it is?" (26:3). "Counselled him without wisdom" = useless advice. "Plentifully declared the thing" ironic—much talk, no truth. "To whom hast thou uttered words? and whose spirit came from thee?" (26:4). Whose spirit inspired you? (Implied: not God's.)
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Uselessness*

#### 26:5-14 — God's Power in Creation: Dead Things Tremble, He Hangs Earth on Nothing
**Lore:** "Dead things are formed from under the waters, and the inhabitants thereof" (26:5). "Dead things" (רְפָאִים, refa'im, shades/spirits) tremble under waters (Sheol beneath cosmic ocean?). God's power extends even to underworld. "Hell is naked before him, and destruction hath no covering" (26:6). "Hell" (שְׁאוֹל, Sheol), "destruction" (אֲבַדּוֹן, Abaddon). Even death/underworld exposed to God. "He stretcheth out the north over the empty place, and hangeth the earth upon nothing" (26:7). **Cosmology**—"north over empty place" (צָפוֹן עַל־תֹּהוּ, tzafon al-tohu, north over void). "Hangs earth on nothing" (תְלָה אֶרֶץ עַל־בְּלִימָה, talah eretz al-belimah) = earth suspended in space (prescientific text, remarkable insight). "He bindeth up the waters in his thick clouds; and the cloud is not rent under them. He holdeth back the face of his throne, and spreadeth his cloud upon it" (26:8-9). Water cycle—clouds hold water without bursting. Clouds veil God's throne. "He hath compassed the waters with bounds, until the day and night come to an end" (26:10). Horizon = boundary (חֹק, choq, decree) where light meets darkness. "The pillars of heaven tremble and are astonished at his reproof" (26:11). Mountains (cosmic pillars) shake at God's rebuke (theophany imagery). "He divideth the sea with his power, and by his understanding he smiteth through the proud. By his spirit he hath garnished the heavens; his hand hath formed the crooked serpent" (26:12-13). Red Sea Exodus (divided sea), smote Rahab (proud = רַהַב, rahav, chaos monster, cf. Ps 89:10, Isa 51:9). "Crooked serpent" (נָחָשׁ בָּרִחַ, nachash bariach) = Leviathan? (Isa 27:1). Creation as defeating chaos. "Lo, these are parts of his ways: but how little a portion is heard of him? but the thunder of his power who can understand?" (26:14). **Epistemological humility**—"parts of his ways," "little portion," "thunder... who can understand?" Job affirms God's mystery. All our theology = fragments. Contrasts friends' certainty. **Lesson**: Knowing God great/mysterious doesn't answer WHY righteous suffer. Job agrees on God's power (Ch 26), but maintains innocence (Ch 27). Power + mystery ≠ injustice.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Power*

#### 27:1-6 — I Will Not Remove My Integrity From Me
**Lore:** "Moreover Job continued his parable, and said, As God liveth, who hath taken away my judgment; and the Almighty, who hath vexed my soul" (27:1-2). "Continued his parable" (וַיֹּסֶף מְשָׁלוֹ, vayosef meshalo) = resumed discourse. Oath formula: "As God lives" (חַי־אֵל, chai-El). Paradox: swears by God who "taken away judgment" and "vexed soul." Still appeals to God AGAINST God. "All the while my breath is in me, and the spirit of God is in my nostrils; My lips shall not speak wickedness, nor my tongue utter deceit" (27:3-4). Life = breath from God (Gen 2:7). Vow: won't lie (even to ease suffering, won't confess false guilt). "God forbid that I should justify you: till I die I will not remove mine integrity from me. My righteousness I hold fast, and will not let it go: my heart shall not reproach me so long as I live" (27:5-6). **INTEGRITY CLIMAX**—"God forbid I justify you" = won't validate friends' false theology. "Till I die not remove integrity" (תֻמָּה, tummah, blamelessness). "Hold fast righteousness" (מַצְדִּקִי, matzdiqi). "Heart not reproach me" = clear conscience. **Job's invincible fortress**: Internal integrity > external suffering. Friends can't break him because he KNOWS truth about himself. Anticipates Paul: "I know nothing against myself" (1 Cor 4:4). Conscience before God matters more than human opinion. **Theological foundation for resistance**: When systems (religious/political) demand false confession, integrity requires resistance unto death (martyrs, dissidents, Job).
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Integrity*

#### 27:7-23 — Let My Enemy Be as the Wicked; The Portion of the Wicked Is Destruction
**Lore:** "Let mine enemy be as the wicked, and he that riseth up against me as the unrighteous" (27:7). Curse on enemies (= friends now?). "For what is the hope of the hypocrite, though he hath gained, when God taketh away his soul?" (27:8). Hypocrite (חָנֵף, chanef, godless) has no hope at death. "Will God hear his cry when trouble cometh upon him? Will he delight himself in the Almighty? will he call upon God at all times?" (27:9-10). Wicked don't pray, don't delight in God. Implied: I DO (therefore not wicked). "I will teach you by the hand of God: that which is with the Almighty will I not conceal. Behold, all ye yourselves have seen it; why then are ye thus altogether vain?" (27:11-12). "I will teach you about God" (ironic: friends claimed to teach Job). "You've all seen it" = empirical evidence (wicked prosper). "Why vain?" = why deny reality? **Verses 13-23: Problem passage**—Job describes wicked's punishment in detail (sounds like friends' theology). Options: (1) Job quoting/mocking friends; (2) Job conceding wicked eventually punished (but doesn't explain righteous suffering); (3) Misplaced text (belongs to friend speech?). "This is the portion of a wicked man with God, and the heritage of oppressors, which they shall receive of the Almighty" (27:13). "His children are multiplied, it is for the sword: and his offspring shall not be satisfied with bread... He may prepare it, but the just shall put it on, and the innocent shall divide the silver" (27:14,17). Wicked's wealth transferred to righteous. "He buildeth his house as a moth, and as a booth that the keeper maketh. The rich man shall lie down, but he shall not be gathered: he openeth his eyes, and he is not" (27:18-19). House fragile, sudden disappearance. "Terrors take hold on him as waters, a tempest stealeth him away in the night. The east wind carrieth him away, and he departeth: and as a storm hurleth him out of his place" (27:20-21). Violent removal. "For God shall cast upon him, and not spare: he would fain flee out of his hand. Men shall clap their hands at him, and shall hiss him out of his place" (27:22-23). Public mockery of wicked's downfall. **Interpretive challenge**: Why Job speaking friends' language? Possible: Job maintains wicked WILL be punished (eventual justice), but doesn't explain his OWN innocent suffering NOW. Both truths held: (a) wicked eventually punished, (b) righteous presently suffer unjustly. Justice delayed ≠ justice denied.
*Verses: 17 | Theme: Retribution*

## Chapter 28: Wisdom Poem — Where Shall Wisdom Be Found?

#### 28:1-11 — Man Mines Precious Metals From the Earth
**Lore:** **CHAPTER 28 = CHIASTIC CENTER** of book. Distinct from dialogue (neither Job nor friends speak; narrator/divine editorial voice?). Genre shift: hymnic wisdom poem. "Surely there is a vein for the silver, and a place for gold where they fine it. Iron is taken out of the earth, and brass is molten out of the stone" (28:1-2). Mining metaphor begins—humans find silver, gold, iron, brass (copper). "He setteth an end to darkness, and searcheth out all perfection: the stones of darkness, and the shadow of death" (28:3). Miners bring light to darkness (caves), search depths. "The flood breaketh out from the inhabitant; even the waters forgotten of the foot: they are dried up, they are gone away from men" (28:4). Mining shafts penetrate underground water sources, drain them. "As for the earth, out of it cometh bread: and under it is turned up as it were fire. The stones of it are the place of sapphires: and it hath dust of gold" (28:5-6). Earth's surface = bread (agriculture), beneath = gems/gold (mining). "There is a path which no fowl knoweth, and which the vulture's eye hath not seen: The lion's whelps have not trodden it, nor the fierce lion passed by it" (28:7-8). Hidden paths (mine tunnels) unknown to animals. "He putteth forth his hand upon the rock; he overturneth the mountains by the roots. He cutteth out rivers among the rocks; and his eye seeth every precious thing. He bindeth the floods from overflowing; and the thing that is hid bringeth he forth to light" (28:9-11). Human ingenuity—overturns mountains, cuts channels, reveals hidden treasures. **Point**: Humans master earth, find hidden metals/gems through skill and labor. BUT...
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Mining*

#### 28:12-22 — But Where Shall Wisdom Be Found? It Cannot Be Purchased
**Lore:** **"But where shall wisdom be found? and where is the place of understanding?"** (28:12). **CENTRAL QUESTION**—contrasts material wealth (findable) with Wisdom (unfindable). "Man knoweth not the price thereof; neither is it found in the land of the living" (28:13). Wisdom's value incalculable, not in land of living (earthly realm). "The depth saith, It is not in me: and the sea saith, It is not with me. It cannot be gotten for gold, neither shall silver be weighed for the price thereof" (28:14-15). Depth (תְּהוֹם, tehom, abyss) and sea deny possessing it. Not purchasable with gold/silver (mined treasures insufficient). "It cannot be valued with the gold of Ophir, with the precious onyx, or the sapphire. The gold and the crystal cannot equal it: and the exchange of it shall not be for jewels of fine gold" (28:16-17). List of valuables—Ophir gold, onyx, sapphire, crystal, jewels—all insufficient. "No mention shall be made of coral, or of pearls: for the price of wisdom is above rubies. The topaz of Ethiopia shall not equal it, neither shall it be valued with pure gold" (28:18-19). More gems listed—coral, pearls, rubies, topaz—still inadequate. **Economic metaphor**: Wisdom = priceless, beyond all earthly wealth (cf. Prov 3:14-15, 8:11). **Whence then cometh wisdom? and where is the place of understanding? Seeing it is hid from the eyes of all living, and kept close from the fowls of the air** (28:20-21). Repeated question—WHERE? Hidden from living, even birds (symbol of high/distant perspective). "Destruction and death say, We have heard the fame thereof with our ears" (28:22). Abaddon (אֲבַדּוֹן, Abaddon) and Death (מָוֶת, mavet) only HEARD of Wisdom (secondhand), don't possess it. Even death doesn't solve mystery.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Mystery*

#### 28:23-28 — God Understands the Way; The Fear of the Lord Is Wisdom
**Lore:** "God understandeth the way thereof, and he knoweth the place thereof. For he looketh to the ends of the earth, and seeth under the whole heaven" (28:23-24). **ANSWER**: Only God knows Wisdom's location. God's omniscience (sees all earth). "To make the weight for the winds; and he weigheth the waters by measure. When he made a decree for the rain, and a way for the lightning of the thunder: Then did he see it, and declare it; he prepared it, yea, and searched it out" (28:25-27). Creation account—God weighed winds, measured waters, decreed rain patterns, lightning paths. At creation, God "saw, declared, prepared, searched out" Wisdom. Wisdom = embedded in creation order (Prov 8:22-31, Wisdom personified as present at creation). "And unto man he said, Behold, the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom; and to depart from evil is understanding" (28:28). **CONCLUSION**—human access to Wisdom = "fear of Lord" (יִרְאַת אֲדֹנָי, yirat Adonai). Not intellectual mastery, but reverent relationship. "Depart from evil" (סוּר מֵרָע, sur mera) = practical righteousness. Cf. Prov 1:7, 9:10, "fear of Lord = beginning of wisdom." Ps 111:10, "fear of Lord = beginning of wisdom." **Theological message**: Friends claimed to know God's ways (retribution theology = wisdom). Job claimed innocence (integrity = wisdom). Chapter 28 corrects BOTH: True Wisdom transcendent, accessible only through fear of Lord, not human systems. **Implication for Job**: Friends' neat theology ≠ Wisdom. Job's protest ≠ complete Wisdom either. Both must bow before divine mystery. Prepares for God's speeches (Ch 38-41)—God reveals Wisdom not as explanation but as revelation of Himself. Knowing God > understanding suffering. **NT fulfillment**: Christ = Wisdom of God (1 Cor 1:24, 30), in Him are hidden all treasures of wisdom and knowledge (Col 2:3). Inaccessible Wisdom made accessible through Incarnation.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Fear*

**JMP 5 Summary**: Third cycle collapses—Eliphaz resorts to false accusations, Bildad gives brief weak speech, Zophar silent. Job maintains integrity despite friends' pressure, won't falsely confess. Chapter 28 Wisdom Poem = divine pause, editorial comment: Human wisdom (friends' theology, Job's protest) insufficient. True Wisdom = God's alone, accessed through fear of Lord. Dialogue exhausted, prepares for Job's final oath (Ch 29-31) then Elihu's interruption (Ch 32-37) then God's whirlwind answer (Ch 38-41). **Lesson**: When theology fails to explain reality, humble silence better than invented answers. Mystery embraced (Ch 28) prepares for revelation (Ch 38ff). Christ = Wisdom incarnate, makes transcendent wisdom accessible.
