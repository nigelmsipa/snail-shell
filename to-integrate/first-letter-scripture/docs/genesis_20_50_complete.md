### Chapter 20: Abraham and Abimelech

#### 20:1-7 — The Wife-Sister Deception Again
**Lore:** Abraham journeys south to Gerar (between Kadesh and Shur). Again he says Sarah is his sister (second wife-sister episode, first was Gen 12:10-20). Abimelech, king of Gerar, takes her. God comes to Abimelech in a dream by night: "Behold, you are a dead man because of the woman whom you have taken, for she is a man's wife" (20:3). Abimelech pleads innocence: "Lord, will you kill an innocent people? Did he not himself say to me, 'She is my sister'?" (20:4-5). God: "Yes, I know that you have done this in the integrity of your heart, and it was I who kept you from sinning against me. Therefore I did not let you touch her" (20:6). Command: "Now then, return the man's wife, for he is a prophet, and he will pray for you, and you shall live" (20:7). Abraham is called a prophet—he will intercede.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Repeated failure, God's protection*

#### 20:8-18 — Abraham's Excuse and Restoration
**Lore:** Abimelech rises early, tells his servants—they're greatly afraid. Confronts Abraham: "What have you done to us? And how have I sinned against you, that you have brought on me and my kingdom a great sin?" (20:9). Abraham's defense: "I did it because I thought, 'There is no fear of God at all in this place, and they will kill me because of my wife'" (20:11). Plus, "she is indeed my sister, the daughter of my father though not the daughter of my mother" (20:12)—technically half-true but morally deceptive. When God "caused me to wander from my father's house," he asked Sarah to say she's his sister wherever they go. Abimelech restores Sarah, gives sheep, oxen, servants, 1,000 pieces of silver: "It is your vindication before all who are with you" (20:16). Abraham prays; God heals Abimelech, his wife, and female servants so they can bear children again—the LORD had closed all the wombs "because of Sarah" (20:17-18). Irony: Abraham prays for another man's fertility while he and Sarah are still waiting for Isaac. The pagan king acts more righteously than the patriarch.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: God protects His covenant despite human weakness*

### Chapter 21: Isaac Born and Ishmael Sent Away

#### 21:1-7 — The Birth of Isaac
**Lore:** "The LORD visited Sarah as he had said, and the LORD did to Sarah as he had promised" (21:1). Sarah conceives and bears Abraham a son in his old age "at the time of which God had spoken to him" (21:2). Abraham names him Isaac (יִצְחָק, Yitzchak, "he laughs"). Circumcises him on the eighth day (covenant sign). Abraham is 100 years old; Sarah says, "God has made laughter for me; everyone who hears will laugh over me" (21:6). Her earlier laughter of disbelief (Gen 18:12) is transformed into joy. "Who would have said to Abraham that Sarah would nurse children? Yet I have borne him a son in his old age" (21:7). The impossible promise fulfilled.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Laughter of joy*

#### 21:8-21 — Hagar and Ishmael Cast Out
**Lore:** Isaac is weaned (typically 2-3 years old). Great feast. Sarah sees Ishmael (now about 17) "laughing" (21:9, same word as Isaac's name—mocking or playing?). She demands: "Cast out this slave woman with her son, for the son of this slave woman shall not be heir with my son Isaac" (21:10). Abraham is "very displeased because of his son" (21:11). God tells Abraham: "Be not displeased...whatever Sarah says to you, do as she tells you, for through Isaac shall your offspring be named" (21:12). But "I will make a nation of the son of the slave woman also, because he is your offspring" (21:13). Abraham rises early, gives Hagar bread and a skin of water, puts them on her shoulder with the child, sends them away. She wanders in the wilderness of Beersheba. When water is gone, she puts Ishmael under a bush, sits opposite "a good way off," weeps: "Let me not look on the death of the child" (21:16). God hears; the angel of God calls from heaven: "What troubles you, Hagar? Fear not, for God has heard the voice of the boy where he is. Up! Lift up the boy, and hold him fast with your hand, for I will make him into a great nation" (21:17-18). God opens her eyes; she sees a well of water. They drink. God was with Ishmael; he grows up in the wilderness of Paran, becomes an expert with the bow. Hagar gets him a wife from Egypt. Two sons, two mothers, two covenants (Gal 4:21-31).
*Verses: 14 | Theme: Two sons, two destinies*

#### 21:22-34 — Covenant with Abimelech at Beersheba
**Lore:** Abimelech (with Phicol, commander of his army) comes to Abraham: "God is with you in all that you do. Now therefore swear to me here by God that you will not deal falsely with me or with my descendants or with my posterity, but as I have dealt kindly with you, so you will deal with me and with the land where you have sojourned" (21:22-23). Abraham swears. Then Abraham complains: "When Abimelech's servants have seized a well of water" (21:25). Abimelech: "I do not know who has done this thing; you did not tell me" (21:26). They make a covenant. Abraham sets seven ewe lambs apart. "What is the meaning of these seven ewe lambs?" Abraham: "These seven ewe lambs you will take from my hand, that this may be a witness for me that I dug this well" (21:28-30). The place is called Beersheba (בְּאֵר שֶׁבַע, "well of seven" or "well of the oath"). Abraham plants a tamarisk tree, calls on the name of "the LORD, the Everlasting God" (אֵל עוֹלָם, El Olam). Abraham sojourns many days in the land of the Philistines.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Peace through covenant*

### Chapter 22: The Binding of Isaac (Akedah)

**📖 Chapter Note:** Genesis 22 is the theological climax of the Abraham narrative—the ultimate test of faith and the clearest Old Testament type of Christ's sacrifice.

#### 22:1-2 — God Tests Abraham
**Lore:** "After these things God tested Abraham" (22:1). The Hebrew word נָסָה (nasah) means "to test, to prove"—not tempt to evil, but prove faithfulness. Command: "Take your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love, and go to the land of Moriah, and offer him there as a burnt offering on one of the mountains of which I shall tell you" (22:2). "Your only son"—Ishmael is gone; Isaac is the covenant son. "Whom you love"—first mention of love in Scripture. Moriah—later the site of Solomon's temple (2 Chr 3:1), Mount Zion, where God will provide His own sacrifice (2 Chr 3:1). The test: Will Abraham obey even when it seems to contradict God's promise? Hebrews 11:17-19 says Abraham reasoned God could raise Isaac from the dead.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: The ultimate test*

#### 22:3-10 — The Journey to Moriah
**Lore:** Abraham rises early (no hesitation), saddles his donkey, takes two young men and Isaac, cuts wood for the burnt offering, goes to the place God told him. On the third day (three days of anguish—foreshadowing Christ's resurrection) he sees the place. "Stay here with the donkey; I and the boy will go over there and worship and come again to you" (22:5)—faith that they will both return. Abraham takes the wood, lays it on Isaac (Isaac carries the wood of his own sacrifice, as Christ carried the cross). Fire and knife in hand. "They went both of them together" (22:6, 8—repeated for emphasis). Isaac: "Behold, the fire and the wood, but where is the lamb for a burnt offering?" (22:7). Abraham: "God will provide for himself the lamb for a burnt offering, my son" (22:8)—prophetic statement, double meaning (God will provide a lamb; God will provide Himself as the lamb). They arrive. Abraham builds the altar, arranges wood, binds Isaac (עָקַד, akad—thus "Akedah," the binding), lays him on the altar. Stretches out his hand, takes the knife to slaughter his son (22:10). Isaac's silent submission (likely a young man, not a child, able to resist but doesn't)—type of Christ's willing sacrifice.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Obedience unto death*

#### 22:11-14 — The Ram Provided
**Lore:** "But the angel of the LORD called to him from heaven and said, 'Abraham, Abraham!' And he said, 'Here I am.' He said, 'Do not lay your hand on the boy or do anything to him, for now I know that you fear God, seeing you have not withheld your son, your only son, from me'" (22:11-12). Test passed. Abraham lifts his eyes—behold, a ram caught in a thicket by his horns. Offers it as a burnt offering instead of his son. Abraham names the place "The LORD will provide" (יְהוָה יִרְאֶה, YHWH Yireh). "As it is said to this day, 'On the mount of the LORD it shall be provided'" (22:14)—pointing forward to God providing His Son on this very mountain (Moriah = Zion). The ram is a substitute; Christ is the ultimate Substitute. Jewish tradition: the ram was created on the sixth day of creation, waiting for this moment.
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Jehovah-Jireh, the providing God*

#### 22:15-19 — The Covenant Confirmed by Oath
**Lore:** Angel calls a second time from heaven: "By myself I have sworn, declares the LORD, because you have done this and have not withheld your son, your only son, I will surely bless you, and I will surely multiply your offspring as the stars of heaven and as the sand that is on the seashore. And your offspring shall possess the gate of his enemies, and in your offspring shall all the nations of the earth be blessed, because you have obeyed my voice" (22:16-18). God swears by Himself (Heb 6:13-14)—the highest possible oath. The covenant is no longer conditional on Abraham's faith alone; it's sealed by divine oath. "Your offspring" (זֶרַע, zera, singular)—Paul identifies this as Christ (Gal 3:16). Through Christ, all nations are blessed. Abraham returns to his young men; they go to Beersheba.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Covenant oath*

#### 22:20-24 — News of Rebekah's Family
**Lore:** Abraham is told: "Milcah also has borne children to your brother Nahor" (22:20). List includes Bethuel, who fathered Rebekah (22:23). This brief genealogy prepares for chapter 24—God is already orchestrating Isaac's future wife. Providence at work even while Abraham is being tested.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Providence prepares the future*

### Chapter 23: The Death and Burial of Sarah

#### 23:1-2 — Sarah Dies at 127
**Lore:** "Sarah lived 127 years; these were the years of the life of Sarah" (23:1). She is the only woman in Scripture whose age at death is recorded—matriarch, mother of the covenant. She dies in Kiriath-arba (Hebron) in the land of Canaan. "And Abraham went in to mourn for Sarah and to weep for her" (23:2). The partner of his journey, mother of the promise, is gone.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: The matriarch's passing*

#### 23:3-16 — Purchasing the Cave of Machpelah
**Lore:** Abraham rises from before his dead, speaks to the Hittites: "I am a sojourner and foreigner among you; give me property among you for a burying place, that I may bury my dead out of my sight" (23:4). They reply: "You are a prince of God among us. Bury your dead in the choicest of our tombs" (23:6). Abraham bows, requests they entreat Ephron son of Zohar to sell him the cave of Machpelah. Ephron (sitting among the Hittites at the city gate): "No, my lord, hear me: I give you the field, and I give you the cave that is in it. In the sight of my people I give it to you. Bury your dead" (23:11). Abraham bows again: "If you will, hear me: I give the price of the field. Accept it from me, that I may bury my dead there" (23:13). Ephron: "My lord, listen to me: a piece of land worth 400 shekels of silver, what is that between you and me? Bury your dead" (23:15). Abraham weighs out 400 shekels of silver "according to the weights current among the merchants" (23:16)—a significant sum, but Abraham insists on paying full price. He wants legal ownership, not charity.
*Verses: 14 | Theme: Legal claim to the land*

#### 23:17-20 — The Field Deeded to Abraham
**Lore:** "So the field of Ephron in Machpelah, which was to the east of Mamre, the field with the cave that was in it and all the trees that were in the field, throughout its whole area, was made over to Abraham as a possession in the presence of the Hittites, before all who went in at the gate of his city" (23:17-18). Formal, legal transfer. Abraham buries Sarah in the cave. This is the first piece of the Promised Land Abraham actually owns (Acts 7:16; Heb 11:9-10—lived as a sojourner but buried as an owner). Later, Abraham, Isaac, Rebekah, Leah, and Jacob will all be buried here (Gen 25:9-10; 49:29-32; 50:13). A foothold in the promise.
*Verses: 4 | Theme: The patriarchal tomb*

### Chapter 24: A Bride for Isaac

**📖 Chapter Note:** At 67 verses, Genesis 24 is one of the longest chapters—but it's a single, beautiful story of divine providence in finding Isaac's wife. The detailed retelling (servant recounts everything to Laban) emphasizes God's faithful guidance. Consider breaking memorization into: The Mission (v1-9), The Prayer and Sign (v10-27), The Negotiation (v28-61), The Meeting (v62-67).

#### 24:1-9 — Abraham's Charge to His Servant
**Lore:** Abraham is old, "advanced in years" (24:1). "The LORD had blessed Abraham in all things" (24:1). He calls his oldest servant (likely Eliezer, Gen 15:2), "who had charge of all that he had" (24:2). Solemn oath: "Put your hand under my thigh" (24:2)—near the organ of procreation, symbolizing oath sworn by the covenant seed. "I will make you swear by the LORD, the God of heaven and God of the earth, that you will not take a wife for my son from the daughters of the Canaanites, among whom I dwell, but will go to my country and to my kindred, and take a wife for my son Isaac" (24:3-4). Covenant purity is essential—no intermarriage with idolaters. Servant: "Perhaps the woman may not be willing to follow me to this land. Must I then take your son back to the land from which you came?" (24:5). Abraham: "See to it that you do not take my son back there" (24:6). Isaac must remain in the Promised Land. "The LORD, the God of heaven, who took me from my father's house and from the land of my kindred, and who spoke to me and swore to me, 'To your offspring I will give this land,' he will send his angel before you, and you shall take a wife for my son from there" (24:7). Faith that God will provide. "But if the woman is not willing to follow you, then you will be free from this oath of mine; only you must not take my son back there" (24:8). The servant swears the oath.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Covenant faithfulness in marriage*

#### 24:10-27 — The Prayer and the Sign
**Lore:** The servant takes ten camels, all sorts of choice gifts, departs for Mesopotamia, to the city of Nahor (Abraham's brother). Arrives outside the city by the well at evening, "the time when women go out to draw water" (24:11). Camels kneel down. Then he prays: "O LORD, God of my master Abraham, please grant me success today and show steadfast love to my master Abraham. Behold, I am standing by the spring of water, and the daughters of the men of the city are coming out to draw water. Let the young woman to whom I shall say, 'Please let down your jar that I may drink,' and who shall say, 'Drink, and I will water your camels'—let her be the one whom you have appointed for your servant Isaac. By this I shall know that you have shown steadfast love to my master" (24:12-14). Specific, bold prayer—asking for a sign of character (kindness, hospitality, industriousness). "Before he had finished speaking, behold, Rebekah...came out with her water jar on her shoulder" (24:15). Daughter of Bethuel (Abraham's nephew), sister of Laban. "The young woman was very attractive in appearance, a maiden whom no man had known" (24:16). She goes down to the spring, fills her jar, comes up. The servant runs to meet her: "Please give me a little water to drink from your jar" (24:17). She quickly lets down her jar, gives him a drink, then says, "I will draw water for your camels also, until they have finished drinking" (24:19). She quickly empties her jar into the trough, runs again to the well to draw, draws for all ten camels (massive labor—camels can drink 20-30 gallons each after a long journey). The man gazes at her in silence "to learn whether the LORD had prospered his journey or not" (24:21). When the camels finish drinking, he takes a gold ring (half a shekel) and two bracelets (ten gold shekels), says, "Please tell me whose daughter you are. Is there room in your father's house for us to spend the night?" (24:23). She: "I am the daughter of Bethuel the son of Milcah, whom she bore to Nahor" (24:24). "We have plenty of both straw and fodder, and room to spend the night" (24:25). The man bows his head, worships the LORD: "Blessed be the LORD, the God of my master Abraham, who has not forsaken his steadfast love and his faithfulness toward my master. As for me, the LORD has led me in the way to the house of my master's kinsmen" (24:26-27). Prayer answered before he finished praying—divine confirmation.
*Verses: 18 | Theme: Providence in answered prayer*

#### 24:28-49 — The Servant's Testimony
**Lore:** Rebekah runs, tells her mother's household. Laban (her brother) sees the ring and bracelets, hears Rebekah's words, runs out to the man at the spring: "Come in, O blessed of the LORD. Why do you stand outside? For I have prepared the house and a place for the camels" (24:31). Brings him into the house, unloads the camels, gives straw and fodder, water to wash his feet and the feet of the men with him. Food is set before him. But the servant says, "I will not eat until I have said what I have to say" (24:33). Laban: "Speak on." The servant recounts everything: Abraham's wealth ("The LORD has greatly blessed my master, and he has become great"), the birth of Isaac in old age ("to him he has given all that he has"), the oath sworn ("You shall not take a wife for my son from the daughters of the Canaanites"), the journey, the prayer at the well, Rebekah's appearance and actions ("Before I had finished speaking in my heart, behold, Rebekah came out..."), the worship and thanksgiving. Then he appeals: "Now then, if you are going to show steadfast love and faithfulness to my master, tell me; and if not, tell me, that I may turn to the right hand or to the left" (24:49). Straightforward negotiation—he's on a mission.
*Verses: 22 | Theme: Faithful stewardship*

#### 24:50-61 — Rebekah's Decision
**Lore:** Laban and Bethuel answer: "The thing has come from the LORD; we cannot speak to you bad or good. Behold, Rebekah is before you; take her and go, and let her be the wife of your master's son, as the LORD has spoken" (24:50-51). When Abraham's servant hears their words, he bows to the ground before the LORD. Brings out jewelry of silver and gold, garments, gives them to Rebekah; also gives costly ornaments to her brother and mother. They eat, drink, spend the night. In the morning, the servant says, "Send me away to my master" (24:54). Her brother and mother: "Let the young woman remain with us a while, at least ten days; after that she may go" (24:55). He presses: "Do not delay me, since the LORD has prospered my way. Send me away that I may go to my master" (24:56). They say, "Let us call the young woman and ask her" (24:57). They call Rebekah: "Will you go with this man?" She says, "I will go" (24:58). Bold, decisive faith—leaving everything to marry a man she's never met, trusting God's providence. Her family blesses her: "Our sister, may you become thousands of ten thousands, and may your offspring possess the gate of those who hate them!" (24:60)—echoing the blessing of Genesis 22:17. Rebekah and her young women arise, mount the camels, follow the man. The servant takes Rebekah and goes.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Faith's decisive yes*

#### 24:62-67 — Isaac and Rebekah Meet
**Lore:** Isaac has come from Beer-lahai-roi (16:14, the well where Hagar met the Angel of the LORD—a place of divine encounter). He lives in the Negeb. "Isaac went out to meditate in the field toward evening" (24:63)—the only mention of meditation (שׂוּחַ, suach) in the patriarchal narratives; Isaac is contemplative, prayerful. He lifts his eyes—behold, there are camels coming. Rebekah lifts up her eyes; when she sees Isaac, she dismounts from the camel, asks the servant, "Who is that man, walking in the field to meet us?" The servant: "It is my master" (24:65). She takes her veil, covers herself (modesty, anticipation). The servant tells Isaac all the things he has done. "Then Isaac brought her into the tent of Sarah his mother and took Rebekah, and she became his wife, and he loved her. So Isaac was comforted after his mother's death" (24:67). Love follows, not precedes, obedience and commitment. Rebekah fills the void left by Sarah. Beautiful picture of God's providence orchestrating every detail—the prayer, the sign, the journey, the meeting. A love story authored by God.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Divine romance*

### Chapter 25: Abraham's Death and the Twins

#### 25:1-11 — Abraham's Final Years and Death
**Lore:** Abraham takes another wife, Keturah (קְטוּרָה, possibly "incense" or "fragrance"). She bears six sons: Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian, Ishbak, Shuah. These become Arabian peoples, including Midianites (Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, is a Midianite). Abraham gives gifts to the sons of his concubines (Hagar, Keturah), sends them away eastward to the east country, while he is still living, "away from his son Isaac" (25:6)—separating them so Isaac is the sole heir. "Abraham gave all he had to Isaac" (25:5). Abraham lives 175 years—a full, blessed life. "Abraham breathed his last and died in a good old age, an old man and full of years, and was gathered to his people" (25:8). "His sons Isaac and Ishmael buried him in the cave of Machpelah" (25:9)—the two estranged brothers reunite for their father's burial, a moment of reconciliation. Buried with Sarah. "After the death of Abraham, God blessed Isaac his son" (25:11). The covenant passes to the next generation.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: The patriarch's rest*

#### 25:12-18 — The Generations of Ishmael
**Lore:** Brief genealogy of Ishmael's twelve sons (fulfilling Gen 17:20: "I will make him fruitful and multiply him greatly. He shall father twelve princes, and I will make him into a great nation"). They settle "from Havilah to Shur, which is opposite Egypt in the direction of Assyria" (25:18). Ishmael lives 137 years. This closes out Ishmael's account—blessed but outside the covenant line.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: The other son's destiny*

#### 25:19-26 — The Birth of Esau and Jacob
**Lore:** "These are the generations of Isaac, Abraham's son: Abraham fathered Isaac" (25:19—Toledot formula). Isaac is forty years old when he marries Rebekah. "And Isaac prayed to the LORD for his wife, because she was barren. And the LORD granted his prayer, and Rebekah his wife conceived" (25:21). Twenty years of barrenness (Isaac is sixty when the twins are born, 25:26)—testing faith, but Isaac prays. The children "struggled together within her" (25:22). Rebekah inquires of the LORD. Oracle: "Two nations are in your womb, and two peoples from within you shall be divided; the one shall be stronger than the other, the older shall serve the younger" (25:23). Prophecy of reversal—the younger chosen over the elder, contra cultural norms. Paul cites this in Romans 9:10-13 to teach election: "before they were born or had done anything either good or bad...that God's purpose of election might continue...not because of works but because of him who calls." The twins are born. First comes out red (אַדְמוֹנִי, admoni), all his body like a hairy cloak (שֵׂעָר, se'ar); they name him Esau (עֵשָׂו, Esav, possibly "hairy"). Afterward his brother comes out, his hand grasping Esau's heel (עָקֵב, akev); they name him Jacob (יַעֲקֹב, Ya'akov, "he grasps the heel" or "supplanter"). Isaac is sixty. The struggle in the womb foreshadows lifelong rivalry.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Election before birth*

#### 25:27-34 — Esau Sells His Birthright
**Lore:** The boys grow up. Esau becomes "a skillful hunter, a man of the field" (25:27)—outdoorsman, physical, impulsive. Jacob is "a quiet man, dwelling in tents" (25:27)—contemplative, domestic, cunning. "Isaac loved Esau because he ate of his game, but Rebekah loved Jacob" (25:28)—parental favoritism sows discord. One day Jacob is cooking stew (נָזִיד, nazid, lentil stew). Esau comes in from the field, famished: "Let me eat some of that red stew, for I am exhausted!" (25:30). Therefore his name was called Edom (אֱדוֹם, red). Jacob: "Sell me your birthright now" (25:31). The birthright (בְּכֹרָה, bekorah) included double portion of inheritance, family leadership, and covenant blessing. Esau: "I am about to die; of what use is a birthright to me?" (25:32)—hyperbolic, self-centered, short-sighted. Jacob insists: "Swear to me now." Esau swears an oath, sells his birthright. Jacob gives him bread and lentil stew. "Thus Esau despised his birthright" (25:34)—the verdict of Scripture. Hebrews 12:16 calls him "profane" (βέβηλος, bebelos, godless, unholy). He traded eternal privilege for momentary gratification. The birthright carried the covenant promise; Esau valued physical appetite over spiritual inheritance. Jacob is opportunistic but values the blessing. This transaction sets the stage for chapter 27.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Despising the sacred*

### Chapter 26: Isaac's Wells

#### 26:1-6 — Famine; God Appears to Isaac
**Lore:** "Now there was a famine in the land, besides the former famine that was in the days of Abraham" (26:1). Isaac goes to Gerar, to Abimelech king of the Philistines (possibly the son or successor of the Abimelech in Gen 20-21). Isaac intends to go down to Egypt (as Abraham did, Gen 12:10). But "the LORD appeared to him and said, 'Do not go down to Egypt; dwell in the land of which I shall tell you. Sojourn in this land, and I will be with you and will bless you, for to you and to your offspring I will give all these lands, and I will establish the oath that I swore to Abraham your father'" (26:2-3). God reaffirms the Abrahamic covenant to Isaac: land, offspring, blessing to all nations. "I will multiply your offspring as the stars of heaven and will give to your offspring all these lands. And in your offspring all the nations of the earth shall be blessed, because Abraham obeyed my voice and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws" (26:4-5). Isaac is blessed because of Abraham's faithfulness. Isaac dwells in Gerar.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Covenant renewed*

#### 26:7-11 — The Wife-Sister Deception (Third Time)
**Lore:** When the men of the place ask about Rebekah, Isaac says, "She is my sister" (26:7)—repeating Abraham's sin (Gen 12:10-20; 20:1-18). Fear: "I thought, 'Lest I die because of her'" (26:9). Rebekah is beautiful. When Isaac has been there "a long time," Abimelech looks out a window, sees Isaac "laughing with Rebekah his wife" (26:8, same word as Isaac's name, יִצְחָק—intimate, playful behavior). Abimelech calls Isaac: "Behold, she is your wife. How then could you say, 'She is my sister'?" (26:9). Isaac: "Because I thought, 'Lest I die because of her.'" Abimelech rebukes him: "What is this you have done to us? One of the people might easily have lain with your wife, and you would have brought guilt upon us" (26:10). Abimelech commands all the people: "Whoever touches this man or his wife shall surely be put to death" (26:11). Once again, a pagan king is more righteous than the patriarch. Like father, like son—both resort to deception out of fear. Yet God protects His promise despite human failure.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Repeating fathers' failures*

#### 26:12-25 — Isaac's Prosperity and the Wells
**Lore:** Isaac sows in the land, reaps "a hundredfold" (26:12). "The LORD blessed him, and the man became rich, and gained more and more until he became very wealthy. He had possessions of flocks and herds and many servants, so that the Philistines envied him" (26:13-14). The Philistines stop up all the wells Abraham's servants had dug, filling them with earth (26:15)—attacking his water supply, vital in that arid region. Abimelech tells Isaac, "Go away from us, for you are much mightier than we" (26:16). Isaac departs, camps in the Valley of Gerar. He reopens the wells Abraham had dug, calls them by the same names Abraham gave them (26:18)—reclaiming his father's legacy. Isaac's servants dig in the valley, find a well of spring water. But the herdsmen of Gerar quarrel: "The water is ours" (26:20). Isaac names it Esek (עֵשֶׂק, "contention"). They dig another; again they quarrel. He names it Sitnah (שִׂטְנָה, "enmity"). He moves from there, digs another well—no quarrel. He names it Rehoboth (רְחֹבוֹת, "broad places" or "room"): "For now the LORD has made room for us, and we shall be fruitful in the land" (26:22). From Esek (conflict) to Sitnah (hostility) to Rehoboth (space)—progression from strife to peace. Isaac goes up to Beersheba. The LORD appears to him that night: "I am the God of Abraham your father. Fear not, for I am with you and will bless you and multiply your offspring for my servant Abraham's sake" (26:24). Isaac builds an altar, calls upon the name of the LORD, pitches his tent. His servants dig a well.
*Verses: 14 | Theme: From strife to Rehoboth*

#### 26:26-35 — Covenant with Abimelech; Esau's Wives
**Lore:** Abimelech comes from Gerar with Ahuzzath (his adviser) and Phicol (commander of his army). Isaac: "Why have you come to me, seeing that you hate me and have sent me away from you?" (26:27). They reply: "We see plainly that the LORD has been with you...Let there be a sworn pact between us...that you will do us no harm, just as we have not touched you and have done to you nothing but good and have sent you away in peace. You are now the blessed of the LORD" (26:28-29). Isaac makes them a feast; they eat and drink. Early in the morning, they swear an oath to each other. Isaac sends them on their way in peace. That very day, Isaac's servants come, tell him about the well they have dug: "We have found water!" (26:32). He names it Shibah (שִׁבְעָה, "oath"); the city is called Beersheba (בְּאֵר שֶׁבַע, "well of the oath") to this day. Peaceful covenant, divine blessing. But then tragedy: "When Esau was forty years old, he took Judith the daughter of Beeri the Hittite to be his wife, and Basemath the daughter of Elon the Hittite, and they made life bitter for Isaac and Rebekah" (26:34-35). Esau marries Canaanite women—violating the principle of covenant purity (24:3). His parents are grieved. This foreshadows further trouble.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Peace with men, trouble at home*

### Chapter 27: Jacob Steals the Blessing

**📖 Chapter Note:** Genesis 27 is morally complex—deception, favoritism, and family dysfunction. Yet God's sovereign purposes prevail. The blessing, once spoken, is irrevocable (Heb 12:17).

#### 27:1-17 — Rebekah's Plan
**Lore:** Isaac is old, his eyes are dim (27:1). He calls Esau: "Behold, I am old; I do not know the day of my death. Now then, take your weapons, your quiver and your bow, and go out to the field and hunt game for me, and prepare for me delicious food, such as I love, and bring it to me so that I may eat, that my soul may bless you before I die" (27:2-4). Isaac still loves Esau's game (25:28). Rebekah is listening. When Esau goes to hunt, she tells Jacob: "I heard your father speak to your brother Esau, 'Bring me game and prepare for me delicious food, that I may eat it and bless you before the LORD before I die.' Now therefore, my son, obey my voice as I command you. Go to the flock and bring me two good young goats, so that I may prepare from them delicious food for your father, such as he loves. And you shall bring it to your father to eat, so that he may bless you before he dies" (27:6-10). Elaborate deception. Jacob hesitates: "Behold, my brother Esau is a hairy man, and I am a smooth man. Perhaps my father will feel me, and I shall seem to be mocking him and bring a curse upon myself and not a blessing" (27:11-12). Rebekah: "Let your curse be on me, my son; only obey my voice, and go, bring them to me" (27:13). Jacob obeys. Rebekah prepares the food, takes Esau's best garments (kept in the house), puts them on Jacob. Puts goatskins on his hands and smooth part of his neck (27:16). Gives Jacob the food and bread. The stage is set for the great deception.
*Verses: 17 | Theme: Scheming for the blessing*

#### 27:18-29 — Jacob Receives the Blessing
**Lore:** Jacob goes to his father: "My father." Isaac: "Here I am. Who are you, my son?" (27:18). Jacob: "I am Esau your firstborn. I have done as you told me; now sit up and eat of my game, that your soul may bless me" (27:19)—outright lie. Isaac: "How is it that you have found it so quickly, my son?" Jacob: "Because the LORD your God granted me success" (27:20)—invoking God's name in deception. Isaac is suspicious: "Please come near, that I may feel you, my son, to know whether you are really my son Esau or not" (27:21). Jacob goes near. Isaac feels him: "The voice is Jacob's voice, but the hands are the hands of Esau" (27:22). He doesn't recognize him because his hands are hairy like Esau's. Isaac asks again: "Are you really my son Esau?" Jacob: "I am" (27:24). Isaac eats, drinks wine. Then says, "Come near and kiss me, my son" (27:26). Jacob comes near, kisses him. Isaac smells his garments (Esau's), is satisfied. Then the blessing: "See, the smell of my son is as the smell of a field that the LORD has blessed! May God give you of the dew of heaven and of the fatness of the earth and plenty of grain and wine. Let peoples serve you, and nations bow down to you. Be lord over your brothers, and may your mother's sons bow down to you. Cursed be everyone who curses you, and blessed be everyone who blesses you!" (27:27-29). The covenant blessing—echoing Genesis 12:3, 22:17. Once spoken, it's irrevocable. Jacob has secured the blessing through deceit.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Blessing obtained by deception*

#### 27:30-40 — Esau's Anguish and Lesser Blessing
**Lore:** As soon as Isaac finishes blessing Jacob, "Jacob had scarcely gone out from the presence of Isaac his father, when Esau his brother came in from his hunting" (27:30). Esau prepares delicious food, brings it: "Let my father arise and eat of his son's game, that you may bless me" (27:31). Isaac: "Who are you?" Esau: "I am your son, your firstborn, Esau" (27:32). Isaac trembles violently: "Who was it then that hunted game and brought it to me, and I ate it all before you came, and I have blessed him? Yes, and he shall be blessed" (27:33). The blessing cannot be retracted. When Esau hears his father's words, "he cried out with an exceedingly great and bitter cry and said to his father, 'Bless me, even me also, O my father!'" (27:34). Heartbreaking. Isaac: "Your brother came deceitfully, and he has taken away your blessing" (27:35). Esau: "Is he not rightly named Jacob? For he has cheated me these two times. He took away my birthright, and behold, now he has taken away my blessing...Have you not reserved a blessing for me?" (27:36). Isaac: "Behold, I have made him lord over you, and all his brothers I have given to him for servants, and with grain and wine I have sustained him. What then can I do for you, my son?" (27:37). Esau: "Have you but one blessing, my father? Bless me, even me also, O my father." And Esau lifted up his voice and wept (27:38). Isaac gives him a lesser blessing: "Behold, away from the fatness of the earth shall your dwelling be, and away from the dew of heaven on high. By your sword you shall live, and you shall serve your brother; but when you grow restless you shall break his yoke from your neck" (27:39-40). Esau will live by violence, serve Jacob, but eventually break free (historically, Edom rebels against Israel, 2 Kings 8:20-22). Hebrews 12:17 says Esau "found no chance to repent, though he sought it with tears"—not repentance from sin, but reversal of the blessing. It was too late.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Irreversible consequences*

#### 27:41-46 — Esau's Hatred; Jacob Flees
**Lore:** "Now Esau hated Jacob because of the blessing with which his father had blessed him, and Esau said to himself, 'The days of mourning for my father are approaching; then I will kill my brother Jacob'" (27:41). Murder planned. Rebekah hears of Esau's intent, calls Jacob: "Behold, your brother Esau comforts himself about you by planning to kill you. Now therefore, my son, obey my voice. Arise, flee to Laban my brother in Haran and stay with him a while, until your brother's fury turns away—until your brother's anger turns away from you, and he forgets what you have done to him. Then I will send and bring you from there. Why should I be bereft of you both in one day?" (27:42-45). Rebekah thinks it will be a short stay; she never sees Jacob again (she dies while he's away). Then Rebekah tells Isaac: "I loathe my life because of the Hittite women. If Jacob marries one of the Hittite women like these, one of the women of the land, what good will my life be to me?" (27:46). She uses Esau's bad marriages as an excuse to send Jacob away for a wife (true reason: saving his life). The family is fractured by favoritism, deceit, and violence. Yet God's purposes will be accomplished.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Sin's bitter fruit*
