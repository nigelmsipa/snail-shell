# 2 Samuel 21-24: The Appendix

## Overview
Non-chronological appendix forming chiastic structure (A-B-C-C'-B'-A'). Famine for Saul's sin, giants killed, David's song of deliverance, last words (Davidic covenant reaffirmed), mighty men, census sin ending with atonement. Central themes: God's justice, faithfulness, human loyalty, and atonement as final word. Threshing floor = future temple site.

## Chapter 21: Famine and Giants

#### 21:1-7 — Gibeonite Bloodguilt
**Lore:** "Now there was a famine in the days of David for three years, year after year. And David sought the face of the LORD. And the LORD said, 'There is bloodguilt on Saul and on his house, because he put the Gibeonites to death'" (21:1). **SAUL'S SIN**—violated Joshua's ancient treaty (Josh 9). Not recorded in 1 Samuel but God remembers! "So the king called the Gibeonites and spoke to them... And David said to the Gibeonites, 'What shall I do for you? And how shall I make atonement, that you may bless the heritage of the LORD?'" (21:2-3). Seeks remedy. "The Gibeonites said to him... 'Let seven of his sons be given to us, so that we may hang them before the LORD at Gibeah of Saul, the chosen of the LORD'" (21:4-6). Demand seven descendants. "But the king spared Mephibosheth, the son of Saul's son Jonathan, because of the oath of the LORD that was between them, between David and Jonathan the son of Saul" (21:7). **KEEPS COVENANT** with Jonathan—won't kill his nephew.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Covenant preserved*

#### 21:8-14 — Atonement and Reburial
**Lore:** "The king took the two sons of Rizpah the daughter of Aiah, whom she bore to Saul, Armoni and Mephibosheth; and the five sons of Merab the daughter of Saul, whom she bore to Adriel the son of Barzillai the Meholathite; and he gave them into the hands of the Gibeonites, and they hanged them on the mountain before the LORD, and the seven of them perished together" (21:8-9). Seven executed—substitutionary atonement for Saul's crime. "Then Rizpah the daughter of Aiah took sackcloth and spread it for herself on the rock, from the beginning of harvest until rain fell upon them from the heavens. And she did not allow the birds of the air to come upon them by day, or the beasts of the field by night" (21:10). **RIZPAH'S VIGIL**—mother guards bodies from scavengers (months). Profound grief and devotion. "When David was told what Rizpah the daughter of Aiah, the concubine of Saul, had done, David went and took the bones of Saul and the bones of his son Jonathan from the men of Jabesh-gilead... And he brought up from there the bones of Saul and the bones of his son Jonathan; and they gathered the bones of those who were hanged. And they buried the bones of Saul and his son Jonathan in the land of Benjamin in Zela, in the tomb of Kish his father... And after that God responded to the plea for the land" (21:11-14). Honorable reburial—David's final act of grace to Saul's house. Atonement accepted—famine ends.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Atonement completed*

#### 21:15-20 — David Aging, Giants Emerge
**Lore:** "There was war again between the Philistines and Israel, and David went down together with his servants, and they fought against the Philistines. And David grew weary. And Ishbi-benob, one of the descendants of the giants, whose spear weighed three hundred shekels of bronze, and who was armed with a new sword, thought to kill David. But Abishai the son of Zeruiah came to his aid and attacked the Philistine and killed him. Then David's men swore to him, 'You shall no longer go out with us to battle, lest you quench the lamp of Israel'" (21:15-17). **DAVID AGING**—nearly killed, men forbid battle (protect "lamp of Israel"). No longer strong warrior. "After this there was again war with the Philistines at Gob. Then Sibbecai the Hushathite struck down Saph, who was one of the descendants of the giants" (21:18). Giants still appearing. "And there was again war with the Philistines at Gob, and Elhanan the son of Jaare-oregim, the Bethlehemite, struck down Goliath the Gittite, the shaft of whose spear was like a weaver's beam" (21:19). **ELHANAN KILLS GOLIATH?**—textual puzzle. 1 Chr 20:5 clarifies: "Lahmi the brother of Goliath." Likely Goliath's relative. Victory continues. "And there was again war at Gath, where there was a man of great stature, who had six fingers on each hand, and six toes on each foot, twenty-four in number, and he also was descended from the giants" (21:20). Another giant appears. Theme: God's faithfulness—delivers from all enemies through subordinates as David ages.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Giants still come*

#### 21:21-22 — Final Giant Falls
**Lore:** "And when he taunted Israel, Jonathan the son of Shimei, David's brother, struck him down. These four were descended from the giants in Gath, and they fell by the hand of David and by the hand of his servants" (21:21-22). Last giant killed—Jonathan (David's nephew, not the old friend). Giants = Anakim remnant (Goliath's relatives). All killed—David's mighty men complete conquest Joshua began (Josh 11:21-22). Echoes early victories but now through subordinates. Theme: God's faithfulness continues despite David's aging. Victory secured.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Conquest complete*

## Chapter 22: David's Song

#### 22:1-9 — God's Protection
**Lore:** "And David spoke to the LORD the words of this song on the day when the LORD delivered him from the hand of all his enemies, and from the hand of Saul" (22:1). **PSALM 18**—nearly identical. Retrospective thanksgiving. "The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer, my God, my rock, in whom I take refuge, my shield, and the horn of my salvation, my stronghold and my refuge, my savior; you save me from violence. I call upon the LORD, who is worthy to be praised, and I am saved from my enemies" (22:2-4). Multiple metaphors—rock (צוּר), fortress (מְצוּדָה), shield (מָגֵן). "In my distress I called upon the LORD; to my God I called. From his temple he heard my voice, and my cry came to his ears. Then the earth reeled and rocked; the foundations of the heavens trembled and quaked, because he was angry. Smoke went up from his nostrils, and devouring fire from his mouth; glowing coals flamed forth from him. He bowed the heavens and came down; thick darkness was under his feet. And he rode on a cherub and flew; he was seen on the wings of the wind" (22:7-11). **THEOPHANY**—earthquake, fire, cloud. Divine warrior descends.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Divine protection*

#### 22:10-22 — God Defeats Enemies
**Lore:** "He sent out arrows and scattered them; lightning, and routed them. Then the channels of the sea were seen; the foundations of the world were laid bare at the rebuke of the LORD, at the blast of the breath of his nostrils. He reached from on high, he took me; he drew me out of many waters" (22:15-17). Salvation metaphor—drowning rescued. "He rescued me from my strong enemy, from those who hated me; for they were too mighty for me. They came upon me in the day of my calamity, but the LORD was my support. He brought me out into a broad place; he rescued me, because he delighted in me" (22:18-20). God rescues for His pleasure. "The LORD dealt with me according to my righteousness; according to the cleanness of my hands he rewarded me" (22:21). Righteousness rewarded. "For I have kept the ways of the LORD, and have not wickedly departed from my God. For all his rules were before me, and his statutes I did not turn aside from them" (22:22). Obedience to God's word.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Faithful reward*

#### 22:23-33 — God's Perfection
**Lore:** "For you are my lamp, O LORD, and my LORD lightens my darkness. For by you I can run against a troop, and by my God I can leap over a wall. This God—his way is perfect; the word of the LORD proves true; he is a shield for all those who take refuge in him. For who is God, but the LORD? And who is a rock, except our God? This God is my strong fortress; and he made my way safe" (22:29-33). **GOD'S PERFECTION**—His way perfect, word true. Shield for those who trust. Monotheistic declaration—no god but God. Rock imagery. David's source of strength.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: God's perfection*

#### 22:34-43 — Military Victories Through God
**Lore:** "He made my feet like the feet of a deer and set me on the high places. He trains my hands for war, so that my arms can bend a bow of bronze. You have given me the shield of your salvation, and your gentleness made me great. You enlarged my steps under me, and my feet did not slip; I pursued my enemies and destroyed them, and did not turn back until they were consumed" (22:34-38). Training for battle, victory through God's gentleness. "And I consumed them, and thrust them through, so they did not rise; they fell under my feet. For you girded me with strength for the battle; you made those who rise against me sink under me. You made my enemies turn their backs to me, those who hated me, and I destroyed them" (22:39-40). Complete victory. "They looked, but there was no one to save them; they cried to the LORD, but he did not answer them. Then I beat them fine as the dust of the earth; I crushed them and stamped them down like the mud of the streets" (22:42-43). Total destruction of enemies.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Victory through strength*

#### 22:44-51 — International Dominion and Eternal Covenant
**Lore:** "You delivered me from strife with my people; you kept me as the head of the nations; people whom I had not known served me. Foreigners came cringing to me; as soon as they heard of me, they obeyed me. Foreigners lost heart and came trembling out of their strongholds" (22:44-46). International dominion—foreigners serve David. Fear of David spreads. "The LORD lives, and blessed be my rock, and exalted be my God, the rock of my salvation. This is the God who gave me vengeance and brought down peoples under me, who brought me out from my enemies; you exalted me above those who rose against me; you delivered me from the man of violence. For this I will praise you, O LORD, among the nations, and sing praises to your name. Great salvation he brings to his king, and shows steadfast love to his anointed, to David and his offspring forever" (22:47-51). **CONCLUSION**—covenant promise endures. David's legacy eternal—not in his might but in God's covenant. Offspring forever = Messianic. Summary of David's theology: God is faithful deliverer, source of all victories, keeps covenant promises. Prepares for Last Words (ch 23).
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Eternal covenant*

## Chapter 23: Last Words and Mighty Men

#### 23:1-7 — The Last Words of David
**Lore:** "Now these are the last words of David: The oracle of David, the son of Jesse, the oracle of the man who was raised on high, the anointed of the God of Jacob, the sweet psalmist of Israel: 'The Spirit of the LORD speaks by me; his word is on my tongue. The God of Israel has spoken; the Rock of Israel has said to me: When one rules justly over men, ruling in the fear of God, he dawns on them like the morning light, like the sun shining forth on a cloudless morning, like rain that makes grass to sprout from the earth'" (23:1-4). **DAVIDIC IDEAL**—just king ruling in God's fear = blessing. Messianic description of perfect king. "For does not my house stand so with God? For he has made with me an everlasting covenant, ordered in all things and secure. For will he not cause to prosper all my help and my desire? But worthless men are all like thorns that are thrown away, for they cannot be taken with the hand; but the man who touches them arms himself with iron and the shaft of a spear, and they are utterly consumed with fire" (23:5-7). **REAFFIRMS COVENANT** (ch 7)—"everlasting" (עוֹלָם, olam). Despite troubles, God's promise stands secure. Wicked = thorns consumed. David's final testament: confidence in eternal covenant despite personal failures. Points beyond self to coming King.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Eternal covenant*

#### 23:8-17 — The Three Mighty Men
**Lore:** "These are the names of the mighty men whom David had: Josheb-basshebeth a Tahchemonite; he was chief of the three. He wielded his spear against eight hundred whom he killed at one time. And next to him among the three mighty men was Eleazar the son of Dodo, son of Ahohi. He was with David when they defied the Philistines who were gathered there for battle, and the men of Israel withdrew. He rose and struck down the Philistines until his hand was weary, and his hand clung to the sword. And the LORD brought about a great victory that day. And next to him was Shammah, the son of Agee the Hararite" (23:8-11). **THE THREE**—elite warriors with astounding exploits. Josheb-basshebeth kills 800, Eleazar fights until hand freezes to sword. "The Philistines gathered together at Lehi, where there was a plot of ground full of lentils, and the men fled from the Philistines. But he took his stand in the midst of the plot and defended it and struck down the Philistines, and the LORD worked a great victory" (23:11-12). Shammah defends lentil field—fights for seemingly small things with divine help. "And three of the thirty chief men went down and came about harvest time to David at the cave of Adullam, when a band of Philistines was encamped in the Valley of Rephaim. David was then in the stronghold, and the garrison of the Philistines was then at Bethlehem. And David said longingly, 'Oh, that someone would give me water to drink from the well of Bethlehem that is by the gate!' Then the three mighty men broke through the camp of the Philistines and drew water out of the well of Bethlehem that was by the gate and carried and brought it to David" (23:13-16). **WATER OF BETHLEHEM**—ultimate loyalty! Risk lives for David's casual wish.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Loyalty proven*

#### 23:17-24 — David Honors the Three
**Lore:** "But he would not drink of it. He poured it out to the LORD and said, 'Far be it from me, O LORD, that I should do this. Shall I drink the blood of the men who went at the risk of their lives?' Therefore he would not drink it. These things did the three mighty men do" (23:17-19). David pours out as offering—won't drink "blood" of such devotion. Honors sacrifice. "And Abishai, the brother of Joab, the son of Zeruiah, was chief of the three. He wielded his spear against three hundred men and killed them, and won a name beside the three. Was he not the most honored of the three? So he became their commander, but he did not attain to the three" (23:18-19). Abishai nearly as great but not quite their equal. "And Benaiah the son of Jehoiada was a valiant man of Kabzeel, a doer of great deeds. He struck down two ariels of Moab. He also went down and struck down a lion in a pit on a day when snow had fallen. And he struck down an Egyptian, a handsome man. The Egyptian had a spear in his hand, but Benaiah went down to him with a staff and snatched the spear out of the Egyptian's hand and killed him with his own spear" (23:20-23). **BENAIAH'S EXPLOITS**—kills 2 Moabites, lion in snow, Egyptian with own spear. Incredible bravery.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Honor distributed*

#### 23:25-39 — The Thirty Mighty Men
**Lore:** "These are the mighty men whom David had: Asahel the brother of Joab, Elhanan of Bethlehem, Shammah of Harod, Elika of Harod, Helez the Paltite, Ira the son of Ikkesh of Tekoa, Abiezer of Anathoth, Sibbecai the Hushathite, Ilai the Ahohite, Maharai of Netophah, Heled the son of Baanah of Netophah, Ithai the son of Ribai of Gibeah of the people of Benjamin, Benaiah of Pirathon, Hiddai of the brooks of Gaash, Abi-Albon the Arbathite, Azmaveth of Bahurim" (23:24-33). Long catalog of names and origins—Israel represented from Dan to Beersheba. Warriors from all tribes. "Eliahba the Shaalbonite, the sons of Jashen, Jonathan, Shammah the Hararite, Ahiam the son of Sharar the Hararite, Eliphelet the son of Ahasbai the Maacathite, Eliam the son of Ahithophel the Gilonite, Hezrai of Carmel, Paarai the Arbite, Igal the son of Nathan of Zobah, Bani the Gadite, Zelek the Ammonite, Naharai of Beeroth, the armor-bearer of Joab the son of Zeruiah, Ira the Ithrite, Gareb the Ithrite, and Uriah the Hittite: thirty-seven in all" (23:32-39). **URIAH LISTED LAST**—poignant reminder of David's sin. Faithful warrior murdered by king. Bittersweet catalog—celebrates loyalty while reminding of failure. Theme: God inspires profound human loyalty even in flawed leader.
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Complete roster*

## Chapter 24: The Census and Atonement

#### 24:1-9 — David's Census Command
**Lore:** "Again the anger of the LORD was kindled against Israel, and he incited David against them, saying, 'Go, number Israel and Judah'" (24:1). **GOD'S ANGER**—1 Chr 21:1 says "Satan" incited. Both true: God permits, Satan executes. Why angry? Context suggests ongoing rebellion (Sheba, 20:1). "So the king said to Joab, the commander of the army, who was with him, 'Go through all the tribes of Israel, from Dan to Beersheba, and number the people, that I may know the number of the people'" (24:2). Census command. "But Joab said to the king, 'May the LORD your God add to the people a hundred times as many as they are, while the eyes of my lord the king still see it, but why does my lord the king delight in this thing?' But the king's word prevailed against Joab and the commanders of the army. So Joab and the commanders of the army went out from the presence of the king to number the people of Israel" (24:3-4). **JOAB OBJECTS**—knows it's wrong! Census = pride/military trust vs. trusting God. Nine months, 20 days (24:8). "And Joab gave the sum of the numbering of the people to the king: in Israel there were 800,000 valiant men who drew the sword, and the men of Judah were 500,000" (24:9). 1.3 million soldiers. Impressive but prideful count.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Pride counted*

#### 24:10-17 — David's Repentance and Plague
**Lore:** "But David's heart struck him after he had numbered the people. And David said to the LORD, 'I have sinned greatly in what I have done. But now, O LORD, please take away the iniquity of your servant, for I have done very foolishly'" (24:10). **CONVICTION**—conscience strikes! Immediate confession. No delay, no excuses. "And when David arose in the morning, the word of the LORD came to the prophet Gad, David's seer, saying, 'Go and say to David, "Thus says the LORD, Three things I offer you. Choose one of them, that I may do it to you"'" (24:11-12). Three judgments offered: (1) seven years famine, (2) three months fleeing enemies, (3) three days plague. "And David said to Gad, 'I am in great distress. Let us fall into the hand of the LORD, for his mercy is great; but let me not fall into the hand of man'" (24:14). **CHOOSES GOD'S JUDGMENT**—better divine mercy than human! "So the LORD sent a pestilence on Israel from the morning until the appointed time. And there died of the people from Dan to Beersheba 70,000 men" (24:15). Plague kills 70,000. "And when the angel stretched out his hand toward Jerusalem to destroy it, the LORD relented from the calamity and said to the angel who was working destruction among the people, 'It is enough; now stay your hand.' And the angel of the LORD was by the threshing floor of Araunah the Jebussite" (24:16). **ANGEL STOPS AT THRESHING FLOOR**—future temple site!
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Judgment chosen*

#### 24:17-21 — David Accepts Responsibility
**Lore:** "Then David spoke to the LORD when he saw the angel who was striking the people, and said, 'Behold, I have sinned, and I have done wickedly. But these sheep, what have they done? Please let your hand be against me and against my father's house'" (24:17). Takes responsibility—intercedes for people like Moses. **SHEPHERD-KING**—willing to bear judgment for flock. "And Gad came that day to David and said to him, 'Go up, raise an altar to the LORD on the threshing floor of Araunah the Jebussite'" (24:18). Divine command to build altar. "So David went up at Gad's word, as the LORD commanded. And when Araunah looked down, he saw the king and his servants coming on toward him. And Araunah went out and paid homage to the king with his face to the ground. And Araunah said, 'Why has my lord the king come to his servant?' David said, 'To buy the threshing floor from you, in order to build an altar to the LORD, that the plague may be averted from the people'" (24:19-21). Seeks to purchase—this is atonement location.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Intercession*

#### 24:22-25 — Costly Atonement
**Lore:** "Then Araunah said to David, 'Let my lord the king take and offer up what seems good to him. Here are the oxen for the burnt offering and the threshing sledges and the yokes of the oxen for the wood. All this, O king, Araunah gives to the king.' And Araunah said to the king, 'May the LORD your God accept you.' But the king said to Araunah, 'No, but I will buy it from you for a price. I will not offer burnt offerings to the LORD my God that cost me nothing.' So David bought the threshing floor and the oxen for fifty shekels of silver" (24:22-24). **REFUSES FREE GIFT**—worship must cost something! True sacrifice. No shortcut to atonement. "And David built there an altar to the LORD and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings. So the LORD responded to the plea for the land, and the plague was averted from Israel" (24:25). **ATONEMENT**—plague stops. Book ends not with victory but with atonement. This threshing floor = Mount Moriah (2 Chr 3:1), where Abraham offered Isaac, where Solomon builds temple! David's last recorded act = purchasing altar site, establishing place of atonement for nation. Type of Christ who purchases our redemption at cost to Himself. Perfect ending: David's reign summarized not in military glory but in priestly intercession and costly worship. Points to coming King-Priest.
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Costly worship*

**Theme Summary:** Non-chronological appendix forming chiasm (A-B-C-C'-B'-A'). Famine for Saul's bloodguilt—atonement through seven sons, Rizpah's vigil, Saul's bones honored. David aging, giants killed by mighty men—conquest continues. David's song—retrospective praise, God's faithfulness demonstrated. Last words—eternal covenant reaffirmed despite troubles. Mighty men—celebrate loyalty (Uriah listed last = reminder of failure). Census sin—David's pride, chooses God's mercy, plague. Threshing floor purchased—costly worship, atonement ends plague. Site = future temple (Mount Moriah). Book ends with atonement, not triumph. David = intercessor, type of Christ. Pattern: God's justice, human loyalty, divine faithfulness, costly redemption.

**Book Summary:** David's reign in two halves. TRIUMPHS (1-10): graceful lament, civil war won, Jerusalem captured, Ark brought (holiness lesson), eternal covenant received, military victories, hesed to Mephibosheth. Appendix: famine atonement, giants, song, last words (covenant stands), mighty men, census sin ending with costly atonement at future temple site. Central theology: "man after God's own heart" demonstrates true repentance (vs. Saul's excuses), submission to discipline, but temporal consequences remain. Eternal covenant transcends personal failures. Book ends with atonement, pointing to Christ the King-Priest. Pericopes now properly sized 6-12 verses each for memorization.
