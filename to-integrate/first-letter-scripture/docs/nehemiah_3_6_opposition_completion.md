# Nehemiah 3-6: Opposition and Completion

## Overview
The wall rebuilding begins with unified effort—priests, nobles, merchants, families all working "each opposite his own house." Chapter 3's detailed builder list demonstrates corporate unity. Immediately opposition intensifies: mockery (Ch 4), military threats, internal economic crisis from usury (Ch 5), and personal deception attempts against Nehemiah (Ch 6). Yet the wall is completed in miraculous 52 days. Central themes: Corporate unity ("mind to work"), external opposition (sword and trowel), internal corruption (greater threat), perseverance, and divine protection.

---

## Chapter 3: A Mind to Work

#### 3:1-12 — Priests and Nobles Begin: From Sheep Gate to Tower of the Furnaces
**Lore:** "Then Eliashib the high priest rose up with his brethren the priests, and they builded the sheep gate; they sanctified it, and set up the doors of it; even unto the tower of Meah they sanctified it, unto the tower of Hananeel" (3:1). **High priest leads**—Eliashib (אֶלְיָשִׁיב, Elyashiv) = "God restores." Priests build first (like Ezra 3:2, altar before temple). "Sheep gate" (שַׁעַר הַצֹּאן, sha'ar hatzon)—north side, near temple, where sacrificial lambs entered. "Sanctified it" (וַיְקַדְּשֻׁהוּ, vayqadeshuhu) = consecrated, set apart as holy. Wall building = sacred act, not just construction. Lists builders counter-clockwise from Sheep Gate. "And next unto him builded the men of Jericho. And next to them builded Zaccur the son of Imri" (3:2). Pattern: "next unto him," "next to them"—sequential, organized. Jericho's men involved (city cursed by Joshua, Josh 6:26, now rebuilt and participating in God's work—redemption). Lists 42 groups/individuals repairing specific sections. "But the fish gate did the sons of Hassenaah build, who also laid the beams thereof, and set up the doors thereof, the locks thereof, and the bars thereof" (3:3). Technical details: beams (roof structure), doors, locks, bars (security). Not symbolic—actual fortification. "And next unto them repaired Meremoth the son of Urijah, the son of Koz. And next unto them repaired Meshullam the son of Berechiah, the son of Meshezabeel. And next unto them repaired Zadok the son of Baana" (3:4). Names preserved—God honors every worker. "Next unto them the Tekoites repaired; but their nobles put not their necks to the work of their Lord" (3:5). **Exception noted**—Tekoites (from Tekoa, Amos's hometown) work faithfully, but their nobles refuse. "Put not their necks" (לֹא־הֵבִיאוּ צַוָּארָם, lo-hevi'u tzavam) = wouldn't bow, too proud for manual labor. Contrast: common people willing, elites lazy. Leadership failure immortalized in Scripture. Proceeds through Old Gate (3:6), walls repaired by rulers of districts (3:9), Shallum and his daughters (3:12—women participate!). "And next unto him repaired Shallum the son of Halohesh, the ruler of the half part of Jerusalem, he and his daughters" (3:12). Daughters building wall—extraordinary! Women engaged in national defense, not just domestic roles. Corporate effort transcends gender.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Unity*

#### 3:13-22 — Valley Gate to Water Gate: Each Opposite His Own House
**Lore:** "The valley gate repaired Hanun, and the inhabitants of Zanoah; they built it, and set up the doors thereof, the locks thereof, and the bars thereof, and a thousand cubits on the wall unto the dung gate" (3:13). Valley gate = major section (1,000 cubits ≈ 1,500 feet). Massive undertaking. "Malchiah the son of Rechab, the ruler of part of Bethhaccerem, repaired the dung gate; he built it, and set up the doors thereof" (3:14). Even "dung gate" (refuse gate) receives dignified repair—nothing too lowly for God's work. Fountain gate, wall of pool of Siloah, stairs descending from City of David (3:15-16)—detailed topography. Levites listed (3:17), rulers of districts (3:18-19). "After him repaired Baruch the son of Zabbai earnestly another piece" (3:20). "Earnestly" (הֶחֱרָה, hecharah) = zealously, fervently. Some work with exceptional passion—God notices. "After him repaired Meremoth the son of Uriah the son of Koz another piece" (3:21). Meremoth repairs SECOND section (cf. 3:4)—some do double duty. Extra effort honored. **KEY VERSE**: "Next unto them repaired Azariah the son of Maaseiah the son of Ananiah by his house" (3:23 preview). Pattern throughout: **"each one opposite his own house"** (cf. 3:28-29). Why significant? 1) Personal investment (your family's safety); 2) Quality control (won't cut corners protecting own home); 3) Accountability (neighbors see your work); 4) Practical logistics (close to home = minimize travel). **LEADERSHIP PRINCIPLE**: People protect what's theirs. Nehemiah's genius = assign sections by residence. Ownership creates excellence.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Responsibility*

#### 3:23-32 — Horse Gate to Sheep Gate: Full Circuit Completed
**Lore:** "After him repaired Benjamin and Hashub over against their house. After him repaired Azariah the son of Maaseiah the son of Ananiah by his house" (3:23). "Over against their house" (נֶגֶד בֵּיתָם, neged betam)—literally "opposite their house." Repeated 10 times in chapter. Ownership principle. "After him repaired Binnui the son of Henadad another piece" (3:24). Horse gate (3:28)—east side, near temple. "From above the horse gate repaired the priests, every one over against his house" (3:28). Priests protecting temple district—appropriate assignment. "After them repaired Zadok the son of Immer over against his own house" (3:29). Immer = priestly family (3:4, Ezra 2:37). Goldsmith and merchants complete circuit (3:31-32)—everyone contributes, from priests to tradesmen. No class exempt. "And between the going up of the corner unto the sheep gate repaired the goldsmiths and the merchants" (3:32). Returns to Sheep Gate (starting point, 3:1)—**full circle**. Entire perimeter assigned. No gaps, no excuse. Chapter 3 = often skipped as "boring genealogy," but theologically rich: 1) **Corporate unity**—42 groups working simultaneously; 2) **Sacred work**—sanctified by priests; 3) **Personal ownership**—each protects own neighborhood; 4) **Inclusive community**—men, women, priests, merchants, rulers, common folk; 5) **Eternal record**—every name preserved by God. Compared to Ezra 2 registry (who returned), this is registry of who built. Both matter. **Nehemiah 4:6 summarizes**: "So built we the wall... for the people had a mind to work" (וַיְהִי לֵב לָעָם לַעֲשׂוֹת, vayehi lev la'am la'asot)—literally "the people had a heart to work." Unity of purpose, willingness to sacrifice. This chapter = proof of revival. Words mean nothing without action; these people demonstrated faith through works (James 2:18).
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Completion*

## Chapter 4: Sword and Trowel

#### 4:1-6 — Mockery of the Feeble Jews: First Opposition
**Lore:** "But it came to pass, that when Sanballat heard that we builded the wall, he was wroth, and took great indignation, and mocked the Jews" (4:1). **Opposition escalates**—"wroth" (וַיִּחַר, vayichar) = burned with anger; "great indignation" (כַּעַס, ka'as) = vexation, provocation. Can't tolerate God's work succeeding. "And he spake before his brethren and the army of Samaria, and said, What do these feeble Jews? will they fortify themselves? will they sacrifice? will they make an end in a day? will they revive the stones out of the heaps of the rubbish which are burned?" (4:2). **Mockery catalog**—five sarcastic questions: 1) "Feeble Jews" (הַיְּהוּדִים הָאֲמֵלָלִים, hayehudim ha'amelalim) = weak, miserable; 2) Can they fortify? (rhetorical—implies "no"); 3) Will they sacrifice? (mocks their piety); 4) Finish in a day? (ridicules timeline); 5) Revive burned stones? (scorns using rubble). Public humiliation before Samaritan army—psychological warfare. "Now Tobiah the Ammonite was by him, and he said, Even that which they build, if a fox go up, he shall even break down their stone wall" (4:3). Tobiah adds insult: wall so weak a fox could destroy it. Fox (שׁוּעָל, shu'al) = small animal. Implies structural failure. **Nehemiah's response**: "Hear, O our God; for we are despised: and turn their reproach upon their own head, and give them for a prey in the land of captivity: And cover not their iniquity, and let not their sin be blotted out from before thee: for they have provoked thee to anger before the builders" (4:4-5). **Imprecatory prayer** (calling for divine judgment)—controversial today, but biblical pattern (Ps 69, 109, 137). Why harsh? "They have provoked THEE"—not just insulting Jews, but mocking God. Nehemiah doesn't retaliate personally, entrusts vengeance to God (Rom 12:19). "So built we the wall; and all the wall was joined together unto the half thereof: for the people had a mind to work" (4:6). **Victory through persistence**—mockery doesn't stop them. Wall reaches halfway point. "Had a mind to work" (וַיְהִי לֵב לָעָם לַעֲשׂוֹת)—key phrase. Unified determination overcomes discouragement.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Mockery*

#### 4:7-14 — Conspiracy to Fight: Prayer and Posting Guard
**Lore:** "But it came to pass, that when Sanballat, and Tobiah, and the Arabians, and the Ammonites, and the Ashdodites, heard that the walls of Jerusalem were made up, and that the breaches began to be stopped, then they were very wroth, And conspired all of them together to come and to fight against Jerusalem, and to hinder it" (4:7-8). **Escalation**—mockery failed, now military threat. Five enemies listed (north, east, south, west)—surrounded. "Conspired" (קָשַׁר, qashar) = plotted together. "Very wroth" (וַיִּחַר לָהֶם מְאֹד, vayichar lahem me'od)—extreme anger. Progress provokes opposition. "Nevertheless we made our prayer unto our God, and set a watch against them day and night, because of them" (4:9). **DUAL RESPONSE**—1) Prayer (spiritual weapon); 2) Watch (practical defense). Not either/or, both/and. Faith doesn't mean passivity. George Müller: "Work as if everything depends on you; pray as if everything depends on God." "And Judah said, The strength of the bearers of burdens is decayed, and there is much rubbish; so that we are not able to build the wall" (4:10). **Internal discouragement**—enemies without, fatigue within. "Strength decayed" (כָּשַׁל, kashal) = stumbled, weakened. "Much rubbish" (עָפָר, afar) = dust, debris (constant reminder of destruction). "Our adversaries said, They shall not know, neither see, till we come in the midst among them, and slay them, and cause the work to cease" (4:11). Enemy strategy: surprise attack, assassination. "And it came to pass, that when the Jews which dwelt by them came, they said unto us ten times, From all places whence ye shall return unto us they will be upon you" (4:12). Spies report threats repeatedly ("ten times" = many times). Jews living near enemies bring warnings. Fear spreads. "Therefore set I in the lower places behind the wall, and on the higher places, I even set the people after their families with their swords, their spears, and their bows" (4:13). **Nehemiah's tactical response**—stations guards at vulnerable spots. "After their families" (לְמִשְׁפְּחוֹת, lemishpechot)—family units fight together (stronger motivation—protecting relatives). Armed with swords, spears, bows (close-range, mid-range, long-range weapons). "And I looked, and rose up, and said unto the nobles, and to the rulers, and to the rest of the people, Be not ye afraid of them: remember the Lord, which is great and terrible, and fight for your brethren, your sons, and your daughters, your wives, and your houses" (4:14). **Leadership rallying cry**—"Be not afraid" (אַל־תִּירְאוּ, al-tire'u)—common biblical command (Gen 15:1, Josh 1:9). "Remember the LORD"—shift focus from enemy to God. "Great and terrible" (הַגָּדוֹל וְהַנּוֹרָא)—same phrase from Nehemiah's prayer (1:5). Fight for concrete things: family, homes (not abstractions). **LEADERSHIP PRINCIPLE**: Courage comes from remembering God's character + tangible reasons to fight.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Conspiracy*

#### 4:15-23 — Sword and Trowel: Working and Watching
**Lore:** "And it came to pass, when our enemies heard that it was known unto us, and God had brought their counsel to nought, that we returned all of us to the wall, every one unto his work" (4:15). **Plot foiled**—"God brought their counsel to nought" (וַיָּפֶר אֱלֹהִים אֶת־עֲצָתָם, vayafer Elohim et-atzatam)—divine intervention. Enemies' surprise attack exposed. Return to work, but vigilance continues. "And it came to pass from that time forth, that the half of my servants wrought in the work, and the other half of them held both the spears, the shields, and the bows, and the habergeons; and the rulers were behind all the house of Judah" (4:16). **New work arrangement**—50% build, 50% guard. "Habergeons" (שִׁרְיֹנִים, shiryonim) = coats of mail, armor. Rulers provide rear support. "They which builded on the wall, and they that bare burdens, with those that laded, every one with one of his hands wrought in the work, and with the other hand held a weapon" (4:17). **ICONIC IMAGE**—one hand holds trowel, other holds sword. Symbol of Nehemiah's entire approach: work + vigilance, faith + action, trust God + take precautions. Spiritual warfare requires same balance (Eph 6:10-18, put on armor while standing firm in faith). "For the builders, every one had his sword girded by his side, and so builded. And he that sounded the trumpet was by me" (4:18). Swords strapped to waist (hands free for building). Trumpeter stays with Nehemiah (command center). "And I said unto the nobles, and to the rulers, and to the rest of the people, The work is great and large, and we are separated upon the wall, one far from another. In what place therefore ye hear the sound of the trumpet, resort ye thither unto us: our God shall fight for us" (4:19-20). **Emergency protocol**—wall's length creates separation. Trumpet signals location of attack. Defenders rally to threatened point. "Our God shall fight for us"—ultimate confidence. Man's weapon + God's power = victory. Moses: "The LORD shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace" (Ex 14:14). Different context, same principle. "So we laboured in the work: and half of them held the spears from the rising of the morning till the stars appeared" (4:21). Dawn to dusk—exhausting schedule. Total commitment. "Likewise at the same time said I unto the people, Let every one with his servant lodge within Jerusalem, that in the night they may be a guard to us, and labour on the day. So neither I, nor my brethren, nor my servants, nor the men of the guard which followed me, none of us put off our clothes, saving that every one put them off for washing" (4:22-23). **No rest**—sleep in Jerusalem (don't go home to villages, avoid ambush). 24-hour watch rotation. "None of us put off our clothes"—ready for combat at all times. Exception: washing (hygiene, not luxury). Nehemiah models sacrifice—leader lives what he preaches. **PERSEVERANCE LESSON**: God's work requires unrelenting effort when opposed. Comfort sacrificed for mission. Later, wall completed in 52 days (6:15) because of this intensity.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Perseverance*

## Chapter 5: The People's Cry

#### 5:1-13 — Internal Crisis: Usury Among Brothers
**Lore:** "And there was a great cry of the people and of their wives against their brethren the Jews" (5:1). **Internal conflict**—greater threat than Sanballat. "Great cry" (צְעָקָה גְדוֹלָה, tze'aqah gedolah)—same term for Israel's cry in Egypt (Ex 3:9). Now Jews oppress fellow Jews. "For there were that said, We, our sons, and our daughters, are many: therefore we take up corn for them, that we may eat, and live" (5:2). Poor families starving—need grain to survive. "Some also there were that said, We have mortgaged our lands, vineyards, and houses, that we might buy corn, because of the dearth" (5:3). Famine forces land mortgages. Covenant land lost. "There were also that said, We have borrowed money for the king's tribute, and that upon our lands and vineyards. Yet now our flesh is as the flesh of our brethren, our children as their children: and, lo, we bring into bondage our sons and our daughters to be servants, and some of our daughters are brought unto bondage already: neither is it in our power to redeem them; for other men have our lands and vineyards" (5:4-5). **Debt slavery**—borrowing to pay Persian taxes, defaulting, selling children as slaves. "Our flesh is as the flesh of our brethren"—we're same people! Why oppression? Torah forbids charging interest to fellow Israelites (Ex 22:25, Lev 25:35-37, Deut 23:19-20). Can charge foreigners, not kinsmen. Violation = covenant breach. "And I was very angry when I heard their cry and these words" (5:6). Nehemiah's righteous anger (וַיִּחַר לִי מְאֹד, vayichar li me'od)—intensely burned. Not sinful anger (Eph 4:26, "Be angry and sin not"). "Then I consulted with myself, and I rebuked the nobles and the rulers, and said unto them, Ye exact usury, every one of his brother. And I set a great assembly against them" (5:7). **Confrontation**—"consulted with myself" (וַיִּמָּלֵךְ לִבִּי עָלָי, vayimmalekh libbi alai) = took counsel with heart, deliberated before acting. Public rebuke ("great assembly")—sin committed publicly, confronted publicly. "Ye exact usury" (מַשָּׁא אַתֶּם נֹשִׁים, masha'a atem noshim) = charging interest. "And I said unto them, We after our ability have redeemed our brethren the Jews, which were sold unto the heathen; and will ye even sell your brethren? or shall they be sold unto us? Then held they their peace, and found nothing to answer" (5:8). Nehemiah's moral authority—HE redeems Jews sold to foreigners (personal expense). Accusation: you're doing opposite—selling brothers. No defense possible—"held their peace" (וַיַּחֲרִישׁוּ, vayachrishu) = silent, convicted. "Also I said, It is not good that ye do: ought ye not to walk in the fear of our God because of the reproach of the heathen our enemies?" (5:9). "Fear of our God" (יִרְאַת אֱלֹהֵינוּ, yir'at Eloheinu)—covenant relationship demands justice. "Reproach of heathen"—hypocrisy undermines witness. How can pagans respect God if His people oppress each other? "I likewise, and my brethren, and my servants, might exact of them money and corn: I pray you, let us leave off this usury" (5:10). Nehemiah admits he also lent money BUT WITHOUT INTEREST. Models righteousness. Calls for cancellation: "leave off" (נַעַזְבָה־נָא, na'azvah-na) = let us forsake. "Restore, I pray you, to them, even this day, their lands, their vineyards, their oliveyards, and their houses, also the hundredth part of the money, and of the corn, the wine, and the oil, that ye exact of them" (5:11). **Restitution demanded**—return property TODAY. "Hundredth part" = 1% monthly interest (12% annually)—return it. Total debt forgiveness. "Then said they, We will restore them, and will require nothing of them; so will we do as thou sayest. Then I called the priests, and took an oath of them, that they should do according to this promise" (5:12). **Public covenant**—nobles agree. Priests witness oath (binding before God). "Also I shook my lap, and said, So God shake out every man from his house, and from his labour, that performeth not this promise, even thus be he shaken out, and emptied. And all the congregation said, Amen, and praised the LORD. And the people did according to this promise" (5:13). **Symbolic act**—"shook my lap" (חֹצְנִי, chotzni) = fold of garment where money kept. Shakes it empty = curse if break promise. "Amen" = congregation binds itself. Crisis resolved through courageous leadership confronting sin.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Justice*

#### 5:14-19 — Nehemiah's Example: The Governor's Generosity
**Lore:** "Moreover from the time that I was appointed to be their governor in the land of Judah, from the twentieth year even unto the two and thirtieth year of Artaxerxes the king, that is, twelve years, I and my brethren have not eaten the bread of the governor" (5:14). **Nehemiah's tenure**—twelve years as governor (445-433 BC). "Bread of the governor" (לֶחֶם הַפֶּחָה, lechem hapekhah) = governor's salary/food tax. Entitled to tax people for his support, but REFUSED. "But the former governors that had been before me were chargeable unto the people, and had taken of them bread and wine, beside forty shekels of silver; yea, even their servants bare rule over the people: but so did not I, because of the fear of God" (5:15). Previous governors exploited people—heavy taxes PLUS servants abused authority. "Forty shekels of silver" = daily tribute (enormous burden). Nehemiah's contrast: "So did NOT I." Why? "Fear of God" (יִרְאַת אֱלֹהִים, yir'at Elohim)—accountability to God prevents exploitation. Leadership = service, not self-enrichment. "Yea, also I continued in the work of this wall, neither bought we any land: and all my servants were gathered thither unto the work" (5:16). Nehemiah works alongside people (doesn't delegate while living luxuriously). Refuses land acquisition (could've enriched himself during crisis—bought foreclosed properties). Servants also work on wall (not personal profit). "Moreover there were at my table an hundred and fifty of the Jews and rulers, beside those that came unto us from among the heathen that are about us" (5:17). Feeds 150+ daily (hospitality without taxation). "From among the heathen"—includes Gentile visitors (diplomacy). "And that which was prepared for me daily was one ox and six choice sheep; also fowls were prepared for me, and once in ten days store of all sorts of wine: yet for all this required not I the bread of the governor, because the bondage was heavy upon this people" (5:18). **Massive expense**—1 ox + 6 sheep + birds + wine (every 10 days) = feeding small army. Personal cost enormous. "Required not... because bondage was heavy"—considers people's burden, sacrifices entitlement. True leadership: bears cost instead of imposing it. "Think upon me, my God, for good, according to all that I have done for this people" (5:19). **Prayer for remembrance**—not boasting, but covenant appeal. Asks God to remember sacrifice. Pattern: Nehemiah regularly prays brief, spontaneous prayers throughout narrative (flash prayers). Chapter 5's lesson: **Internal sin greater threat than external enemies.** Nehemiah's moral authority (personal integrity + sacrificial generosity) enables him to confront nobles' greed. Cannot demand of others what you don't model yourself.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Integrity*

## Chapter 6: The Unshaken Heart

#### 6:1-9 — Deceptive Diplomacy and Slander: Refusing to Come Down
**Lore:** "Now it came to pass, when Sanballat, and Tobiah, and Geshem the Arabian, and the rest of our enemies, heard that I had builded the wall, and that there was no breach left therein; (though at that time I had not set up the doors upon the gates;) That Sanballat and Geshem sent unto me, saying, Come, let us meet together in some one of the villages in the plain of Ono. But they thought to do me mischief" (6:1-2). **New tactic**—wall nearly complete, only doors remain. Enemies shift to **personal deception**. Mockery (Ch 4), military threat (Ch 4), internal division (Ch 5) all failed. Now: diplomatic trap. "Plain of Ono" (בִּכְפִירִים בְּבִקְעַת אוֹנוֹ, bikhfirim beviq'at Ono)—20 miles northwest of Jerusalem (neutral territory). Pretext: negotiate peace. Reality: "thought to do me mischief" (חֹשְׁבִים לַעֲשׂוֹת לִי רָעָה, choshvim la'asot li ra'ah)—planning harm. Assassination likely. "And I sent messengers unto them, saying, I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down: why should the work cease, whilst I leave it, and come down to you?" (6:3). **ICONIC RESPONSE**—"I am doing a great work" (מְלָאכָה גְדוֹלָה אֲנִי עֹשֶׂה, melakhah gedolah ani oseh). Recognizes priority. "Cannot come down" (וְלֹא אוּכַל לָרֶדֶת, velo ukhal laredet)—physical descent from wall = spiritual/strategic descent from calling. Distraction = enemy tactic. "Why should work cease?"—rhetorical question showing absurdity of leaving mission for meeting. **FOCUS PRINCIPLE**: Leader must discern true priorities, resist distractions disguised as opportunities. "Yet they sent unto me four times after this sort; and I answered them after the same manner" (6:4). **Persistence**—enemies repeat invitation 4x. Nehemiah's consistency—same refusal, same reason. No wavering. "Then sent Sanballat his servant unto me in like manner the fifth time with an open letter in his hand" (6:5). **Fifth attempt**—escalation. "Open letter" (אִגֶּרֶת פְּתוּחָה, iggeret petukhah) = unsealed (normally sealed with wax). Public document, meant to be read by others. Intimidation through gossip. "Wherein was written, It is reported among the heathen, and Gashmu saith it, that thou and the Jews think to rebel: for which cause thou buildest the wall, that thou mayest be their king, according to these words. And thou hast also appointed prophets to preach of thee at Jerusalem, saying, There is a king in Judah: and now shall it be reported to the king according to these words. Come now therefore, and let us take counsel together" (6:6-7). **SLANDER**—three false accusations: 1) Rebellion against Persia (politically dangerous); 2) Nehemiah wants kingship (ambition); 3) False prophets proclaiming Nehemiah king (religious deception). "Now shall it be reported to the king"—threat of denunciation to Artaxerxes. If believed, Nehemiah recalled or executed. Clever trap: meet to "discuss" charges (implies guilt by negotiating). "Then I sent unto him, saying, There are no such things done as thou sayest, but thou feignest them out of thine own heart" (6:8). **Direct denial**—"no such things done" (לֹא נִהְיוּ כַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה, lo nihyu khadvarim ha'eleh). "Thou feignest them" (מִלִּבְּךָ אַתָּה בוֹדָאָם, milibkha attah vodam)—you invented these lies. Doesn't dignify false charges with lengthy defense. Simple, clear denial. "For they all made us afraid, saying, Their hands shall be weakened from the work, that it be not done. Now therefore, O God, strengthen my hands" (6:9). **Enemy's goal revealed**—"made us afraid" (מְיָרְאִים, meyor'im) = trying to intimidate. Purpose: weaken resolve, stop work. Nehemiah's response: **flash prayer**—"O God, strengthen my hands." Doesn't argue with enemy, turns to God. Pattern: Nehemiah prays constantly in crisis (2:4, 4:4-5, 4:9, 5:19, now 6:9). Prayer = reflex, not last resort.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Focus*

#### 6:10-14 — False Prophecy: The Temple Trap
**Lore:** "Afterward I came unto the house of Shemaiah the son of Delaiah the son of Mehetabeel, who was shut up; and he said, Let us meet together in the house of God, within the temple, and let us shut the doors of the temple: for they will come to slay thee; yea, in the night will they come to slay thee" (6:10). **New deception**—Shemaiah (שְׁמַעְיָה, Shema'yah) = "Yahweh has heard," prophetic name. "Shut up" (עָצוּר, atzur)—confined to house (ritually unclean? or feigning fear?). Proposes hiding in temple's Holy Place (sanctuary proper, not just temple courts). "Shut doors"—implies mortal danger. Sounds protective, but... "Then I said, Should such a man as I flee? and who is there, that, being as I am, would go into the temple to save his life? I will not go in" (6:11). **Nehemiah's discernment**—THREE reasons to refuse: 1) **Courage**: "Should such a man as I flee?"—leader doesn't abandon post; 2) **Identity**: "being as I am"—Nehemiah's not a priest, entering sanctuary = sin (Num 18:7); 3) **Priority**: "Save his life" vs. complete mission—martyrdom preferable to disobedience. "And, lo, I perceived that God had not sent him; but that he pronounced this prophecy against me: for Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him" (6:12). **Spiritual discernment**—recognizes false prophecy. "God had not sent him" (לֹא אֱלֹהִים שְׁלָחוֹ, lo Elohim shelakho). How did Nehemiah know? 1) Contradicted Torah (non-priest in sanctuary forbidden); 2) Promoted cowardice (flee vs. trust God); 3) Would discredit Nehemiah (if he violated Law, enemies use it against him). "Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him" (שְׂכָרוֹ, sekharo) = bribed. Mercenary prophet. "Therefore was he hired, that I should be afraid, and do so, and sin, and that they might have matter for an evil report, that they might reproach me" (6:13). **Enemy's strategy**—tempt Nehemiah to sin (entering sanctuary) so they can accuse him of hypocrisy ("claims to obey Law, violates it"). Public "evil report" (לְשֵׁם רָע, leshem ra) = bad reputation. Would destroy moral authority. Clever trap: hide in temple = 1) Sin; 2) Look cowardly; 3) Give enemies ammunition. Refuse = risk assassination (if threat real). Nehemiah chooses obedience over safety. "My God, think thou upon Tobiah and Sanballat according to these their works, and on the prophetess Noadiah, and the rest of the prophets, that would have put me in fear" (6:14). **Prayer for justice**—imprecatory (asks God to remember enemies' evil). "Prophetess Noadiah" (נוֹעַדְיָה, No'adyah)—woman prophet allied with enemies (cf. false prophetess Jezebel, Rev 2:20). "Rest of the prophets" = conspiracy of false prophets. Nehemiah faces spiritual warfare, not just political opposition. **LESSON**: Satan uses religious language, appeals to "safety," even hires prophets to deceive God's servants (2 Cor 11:13-15, false apostles disguise as ministers). Test every prophecy against Scripture (1 John 4:1).

#### 6:15-19 — Wall Completed: 52 Days of Victory
**Lore:** "So the wall was finished in the twenty and fifth day of the month Elul, in fifty and two days" (6:15). **VICTORY**—Elul = 6th month (August/September 445 BC). **52 days**—miraculously fast (ancient construction usually takes years). Josephus claims 2 years 4 months (Antiquities 11.5.8), but biblical text clear: 52 days. How possible? 1) Foundations existed (ruins from 586 BC); 2) Corporate effort (all families working simultaneously); 3) Intense schedule (4:21-23, dawn to dusk, no rest); 4) Divine favor (6:16). "And it came to pass, that when all our enemies heard thereof, and all the heathen that were about us saw these things, they were much cast down in their own eyes: for they perceived that this work was wrought of our God" (6:16). **Enemy's response**—"cast down" (וַיִּפְּלוּ, vayiplu) = fell, discouraged. "Perceived... wrought of our God" (נֶעֶשְׂתָה הַמְּלָאכָה הַזֹּאת מֵאֵת אֱלֹהֵינוּ, ne'esetah hamelakhah hazot me'et Eloheinu) = this work accomplished by our God. Even pagans recognize divine intervention. Impossible speed + opposition overcome = testimony to God's power. Mission: remove "reproach" (1:3, 2:17)—accomplished. "Moreover in those days the nobles of Judah sent many letters unto Tobiah, and the letters of Tobiah came unto them" (6:17). **Continuing intrigue**—even after victory, some Jewish nobles maintain correspondence with enemy. Fifth column. "For there were many in Judah sworn unto him, because he was the son in law of Shechaniah the son of Arah; and his son Johanan had taken the daughter of Meshullam the son of Berechiah" (6:18). **Intermarriage problem** (foreshadows Chapter 13)—Tobiah married into Jewish nobility. "Sworn unto him" (בַּעֲלֵי שְׁבוּעָה לוֹ, ba'alei shevu'ah lo) = oath-bound, allied. Political/family ties create divided loyalties. Meshullam = builder (3:4, 30)—faithful in wall building, but compromised by daughter's marriage to Tobiah's son. "Also they reported his good deeds before me, and uttered my words to him. And Tobiah sent letters to put me in fear" (6:19). **Traitors**—nobles act as Tobiah's PR ("his good deeds") and spies ("uttered my words to him" = reported Nehemiah's plans). "Tobiah sent letters to put me in fear" (לְיָרְאֵנִי, leyar'eni)—continued intimidation. Wall finished, but opposition doesn't end. Spiritual warfare continues. **Chapter 6 summary**: External opposition failed (mockery, military threats), so enemy turns to personal deception (diplomatic trap, slander, false prophecy, insider betrayal). Nehemiah's unshaken focus + spiritual discernment + flash prayers = victory. Wall completed in 52 days = testimony to God's power. BUT internal compromise (intermarriage) = continuing threat (resolved in Ch 13).
