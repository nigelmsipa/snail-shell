# 2 Samuel 8-14: Kingdom Secured, Then Troubles Begin

## Overview
Peak of David's reign (ch 8-10) → tragic pivot (ch 11) → consequences cascade (ch 12-14). Military victories, hesed to Mephibosheth. Then: David's sin (lust → adultery → deception → murder), Nathan's confrontation, sword unleashed on family (Amnon, Tamar, Absalom). Central themes: covenant loyalty vs. indulgence, true repentance vs. excuses, consequences of sin.

## Chapters 8-10: Kingdom Secured

#### 8:1-8 — Military Victories Over Enemies
**Lore:** Summary of conquests: "And after this David defeated the Philistines and subdued them... And he defeated Moab and measured them with a line, making them lie down on the ground" (8:1-2). "And David took from him 1,700 horsemen, and 20,000 foot soldiers... And the Arameans of Damascus came to help Hadadezer king of Zobah, and David struck down 22,000 men of the Arameans" (8:4, 5). Total dominance.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Victory on all fronts*

#### 8:9-14 — Plunder Dedicated to the Lord
**Lore:** "And David took the shields of gold that were carried by the servants of Hadadezer and brought them to Jerusalem. And from Betah and from Berothai, cities of Hadadezer, King David took very much bronze" (8:7-8). "And David dedicated all these things to the LORD, together with the silver and gold that he had dedicated from all the nations he subdued" (8:11). Religious orientation: plunder → temple treasure. Recognizes God's role in victory.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Dedication to God*

#### 8:15-18 — Just Rule and Officials
**Lore:** "And David administered justice and equity to all his people" (8:15). Lists administrators: "Joab the son of Zeruiah was over the army; and Jehoshaphat the son of Ahilud was recorder; and Zadok the son of Ahitub and Abimelech the son of Abiathar were priests; and Seraiah was secretary; and Benaiah the son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites and the Pelethites; and David's sons were priests" (8:16-18). Kingdom at apex—military success, just governance, divine favor, organized administration.
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Righteous governance*

#### 9:1-7 — Seeking Mephibosheth
**Lore:** "And David said, 'Is there still anyone left of the house of Saul, that I may show him kindness for Jonathan's sake?'" (9:1). Seeks to fulfill covenant (1 Sam 20:14-17). "Now there was a servant of the house of Saul whose name was Ziba, and they called him to David. And the king said to him, 'Are you Ziba?' And he said, 'I am your servant.' And the king said, 'Is there not still someone of the house of Saul to whom I may show the kindness of God?'" (9:2-3). "Is not Mephibosheth the son of Jonathan?" (9:3). Ziba tells of Mephibosheth (Jonathan's son, crippled in both feet from childhood fall in 4:4). Covenant loyalty drives David.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Covenant remembrance*

#### 9:8-13 — Mephibosheth at the King's Table
**Lore:** "Mephibosheth the son of Jonathan, son of Saul, came to David and fell on his face and paid homage" (9:6). "And David said, 'Do not fear, for I will show you kindness for the sake of your father Jonathan, and I will restore to you all the land of Saul your father, and you shall eat at my table always'" (9:7). Restores land, gives seat at king's table (royal adoption status). "And he paid homage and said, 'What is your servant, that you should show regard for a dead dog such as I?'" (9:8). Humility—"dead dog" (powerless, despised). David's hesed demonstrates covenant faithfulness—doesn't fear rival claimants, shows grace. Type of Christ who seeks and restores the broken. "So Mephibosheth ate at David's table, like one of the king's sons" (9:11).
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Grace to the broken*

#### 10:1-7 — Ammonite Insult
**Lore:** "Nahash king of the Ammonites died, and his son Hanun reigned in his place. And David said, 'I will deal loyally with Hanun the son of Nahash, as his father dealt loyally with me.' So David sent by his servants to console him concerning his father. But when David's servants came into the land of the Ammonites, the princes of the Ammonites said to Hanun their lord, 'Do you think, because David has sent comforters to you, that he is honoring your father? Has not David sent his servants to you to search the city and to spy it out and to overthrow it?'" (10:1-3). Suspicious paranoia. "So Hanun took David's servants and shaved off half the beard of each and cut off their garments in the middle, at their hips, and sent them away" (10:4). Grave insult—unmans them. Shaved beards = shame, cut garments = humiliation.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Honor rejected*

#### 10:8-14 — Ammonite War: First Battle
**Lore:** "And when the Ammonites saw that they had become odious to David, the Ammonites sent and hired the Syrians of Beth-rehob, and the Syrians of Zobah, 20,000 foot soldiers, and the king of Maacah with 1,000 men, and the men of Tob, 12,000 men" (10:6). Hires mercenaries. "When Joab saw that the battle was set against him both in front and in the rear, he chose some of the choice men of Israel and arrayed them against the Syrians. The rest of his men he put in the charge of Abishai his brother, and he arrayed them against the Ammonites" (10:9-10). Splits forces. "And he said, 'If the Syrians are too strong for me, then you shall help me, but if the Ammonites are too strong for you, then I will come and help you'" (10:11). Mutual support. "Be of good courage, and let us be courageous for our people, and for the cities of our God, and may the LORD do what seems good to him" (10:12). Faith-based command.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Strategic courage*

#### 10:15-19 — Aramean Defeat
**Lore:** "And when the Syrians saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they gathered themselves together... And the Syrians sent messengers and brought out the Syrians who were beyond the Euphrates, with Shophach the commander of the army of Hadadezer at their head" (10:15-16). Regroups with reinforcements. "And when it was told David, he gathered all Israel together, and crossed the Jordan, and came to Helam. And the Syrians arrayed themselves against David and fought with him. And the Syrians fled before Israel, and David killed of the Syrians the men of 700 chariots and 40,000 horsemen, and wounded Shophach the commander of their army, so that he died there. And when all the kings who were servants of Hadadezer saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they made peace with David and became subject to him. So the Syrians were afraid to save the Ammonites anymore" (10:17-19). Total victory. David personally leads, defeats them. Sets stage for ch 11—Ammonite war continues but David stays home (problematic pivot).
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Total victory*

## Chapter 11: The King's Sin

#### 11:1-5 — David's Neglect Leads to Lust
**Lore:** "In the spring of the year, the time when kings go out to battle, David sent Joab, and his servants with him, and all Israel... But David remained at Jerusalem" (11:1). **NEGLECTS DUTY**—should be with army. Idleness = vulnerability. "It happened, late one afternoon, when David arose from his couch and was walking on the roof of the king's house, that he saw from the roof a woman bathing; and the woman was very beautiful" (11:2). Sees Bathsheba. "And David sent and inquired about the woman. And one said, 'Is not this Bathsheba, the daughter of Eliam, the wife of Uriah the Hittite?'" (11:3). Told she's married! **Violates 10th commandment**—covets neighbor's wife.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Temptation begins*

#### 11:6-10 — Failed Deception Attempt
**Lore:** "So David sent messengers and took her, and she came to him, and he lay with her... Then she returned to her house" (11:4). **ADULTERY—6th commandment**. "And the woman conceived, and she sent and told David, 'I am pregnant'" (11:5). Consequences already manifest. David summons Uriah from battle: "And David said to Uriah, 'Go down to your house and wash your feet'" (11:8). Expects Uriah will sleep with wife (covering pregnancy). Sends gift (11:8b)—typical behavior for returning soldier's leave. But: "Uriah slept at the door of the king's house with all the servants of his lord, and did not go down to his house" (11:9). **URIAH'S INTEGRITY**—contrast to David!
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Integrity rebukes*

#### 11:11-13 — Uriah's Loyalty Makes Him Refuse
**Lore:** "When they told David, 'Uriah did not go down to his house,' David said to Uriah, 'Have you not come from a journey? Why did you not go down to your house?' Uriah said to David, 'The ark and Israel and Judah dwell in booths, and my lord Joab and the servants of my lord are camping in the open field. Shall I then go to my house, to eat and to drink and to lie with my wife? As you live, and as your soul lives, I will not do this thing'" (11:10-11). Covenant loyalty—won't enjoy comforts while brothers in danger. David tries again: "Then David said to Uriah, 'Stay here today also, and tomorrow I will send you back.' So Uriah remained in Jerusalem that day and the next. And David invited him, and he ate in his presence and drank, so that he made him drunk. And in the evening he went out to lie on his couch with the servants of his lord, but he did not go down to his house" (11:12-13). David makes him drunk—still refuses to go home. **Deception fails completely.**
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Loyalty refuses*

#### 11:14-21 — Premeditated Murder Planned
**Lore:** "In the morning David wrote a letter to Joab and sent it by the hand of Uriah. In the letter he wrote, 'Set Uriah in the forefront of the hardest fighting, and then draw back from him, that he may be struck down, and die'" (11:14-15). **PREMEDITATED MURDER**—Uriah carries own death warrant! Makes Joab accomplice. Chain: Neglect → Lust → Adultery → Deception → Murder. Deliberate, calculated, ruthless.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Murder planned*

#### 11:22-27 — Uriah Dies, David Takes Bathsheba
**Lore:** "Joab sent and told David all the news about the fighting; and he instructed the messenger, 'When you have finished telling all the news about the fighting to the king, then, if the king's anger grows hot, you may say, "Moreover, your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead"'" (11:18-21). Worries David will be angry at losses. "So the messenger came and told David all that Joab had sent him to tell" (11:22). Reports casualties. David's response: "Thus shall you say to Joab, 'Do not let this matter displease you, for the sword devours now one and now another. Press your attack against the city and overthrow it'" (11:25). **Callous dismissal**—"sword devours"—casualty of war? No! Deliberate murder. "When the wife of Uriah heard that Uriah her husband was dead, she lamented over her husband. And when the mourning was over, David sent and brought her to his house, and she became his wife and bore him a son. But the thing that David had done displeased the LORD" (11:26-27). Quick marriage (covering shame). **"Displeased the LORD"**—ominous understatement. Low point of reign.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Sin complete*

## Chapter 12: The Parable and Consequences

#### 12:1-7 — Nathan's Parable: The Rich Man and Poor Man's Lamb
**Lore:** "And the LORD sent Nathan to David. He came to him and said to him, 'There were two men in a certain city, the one rich and the other poor. The rich man had very many flocks and herds, but the poor man had nothing but one little ewe lamb, which he had bought. And he brought it up, and it grew up with him and with his children. It used to eat of his morsel and drink from his cup and lie in his arms, and it was like a daughter to him. Now there came a traveler to the rich man, and he was unwilling to take one of his own flock or herd to prepare for the guest who had come to him, but he took the poor man's lamb and prepared it for the man who had come to him'" (12:1-4). **THE PARABLE**—holds up mirror. Rich man = David, poor man = Uriah, lamb = Bathsheba.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Parable truth*

#### 12:8-14 — David's Judgment Falls on Himself
**Lore:** "Then David's anger was greatly kindled against the man, and he said to Nathan, 'As the LORD lives, the man who has done this deserves to die, and he shall restore the lamb fourfold, because he did this thing, and because he had no pity'" (12:5-6). David pronounces own judgment! "Nathan said to David, 'You are the man!'" (12:7). Direct accusation. "Why have you despised the word of the LORD, to do what is evil in his sight? You have struck down Uriah the Hittite with the sword and have taken his wife to be your wife and have killed him with the sword of the Ammonites. Now therefore the sword shall never depart from your house, because you have despised me and have taken the wife of Uriah the Hittite to be your wife. Thus says the LORD, 'Behold, I will raise up evil against you out of your own house'" (12:9-11). **CONSEQUENCES PRONOUNCED**: (1) Sword never depart, (2) Calamity from own house, (3) Wives taken publicly (fulfilled 16:22). "David said to Nathan, 'I have sinned against the LORD'" (12:13). **CONTRAST TO SAUL**—immediate confession, no excuses! "And Nathan said to David, 'The LORD also has put away your sin; you shall not die. Nevertheless, because by this deed you have utterly scorned the LORD, the child who is born to you shall die'" (12:13-14). Forgiven but consequences remain.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: "I have sinned"*

#### 12:15-20 — Child's Death and David's Submission
**Lore:** "And the LORD afflicted the child that Uriah's wife bore to David, and he became sick. David therefore sought God on behalf of the child. And David fasted and went in and lay all night on the ground. And the elders of his house stood beside him, to raise him from the ground, but he would not, and he ate no food with them" (12:15-17). Intercedes desperately. "On the seventh day the child died" (12:18). Servants fear to tell. "But when David saw that his servants were whispering together, David understood that the child was dead. And David said to his servants, 'Is the child dead?' They said, 'He is dead.' Then David arose from the earth and washed and anointed himself and changed his clothes. And he went into the house of the LORD and worshiped" (12:19-20). **WORSHIP AFTER LOSS**—profound submission. Faith in God's sovereignty.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Loss and worship*

#### 12:21-25 — Solomon Born, God's Love Shown
**Lore:** "Then his servants said to him, 'What is this thing that you have done? You fasted and wept for the child while he was alive; but when the child died, you arose and ate food.' He said, 'While the child was still alive, I fasted and wept, for I said, "Who knows whether the LORD will be gracious to me, that the child may live?" But now he is dead. Why should I fast? Can I bring him back again? I shall go to him, but he will not return to me'" (12:21-23). Faith in afterlife—"I shall go to him." Acceptance of God's will. "Then David comforted his wife, Bathsheba, and went in to her and lay with her, and she bore a son, and he called his name Solomon. And the LORD loved him and sent a message by Nathan the prophet. So he called his name Jedidiah, because of the LORD" (12:24-25). **SOLOMON** = "peace." **JEDIDIAH** = "beloved of Yahweh." God's grace—from tragedy comes heir. Forgiven, blessed.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Grace after judgment*

## Chapter 13: Sword Unleashed on Family

#### 13:1-9 — Amnon's Lust for Tamar
**Lore:** "Now Absalom, David's son, had a beautiful sister, whose name was Tamar. And after a time Amnon, David's son, loved her" (13:1). **AMNON**—David's firstborn (3:2). Tamar = Absalom's full sister, Amnon's half-sister. "And Amnon was so tormented that he made himself ill because of his sister Tamar, for she was a virgin, and it seemed impossible to Amnon to do anything to her" (13:2). **"Love"** (אָהַב) = really lust. "But Amnon had a friend, whose name was Jonadab, the son of David's brother Shimeah. And Jonadab was a very crafty man... Jonadab said to him, 'Why are you, the king's son, so haggard morning after morning? Will you not tell me?' Amnon said to him, 'I love Tamar, my brother Absalom's sister.' Jonadab said to him, 'Lie down on your bed and pretend to be ill'" (13:3-5). Jonadab devises deceptive plan.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Lust schemes*

#### 13:10-14 — Amnon Violates Tamar
**Lore:** "So Tamar went to her brother Amnon's house, where he was lying down. And she took dough, kneaded it, made cakes in his sight, and baked the cakes. And she took the pan and emptied it out before him, but he refused to eat. And Amnon said, 'Send everyone out from me.' So everyone went out from Amnon. Then Amnon said to Tamar, 'Bring the food into the chamber, that I may eat from your hand.' So Tamar took the cakes she had made and brought them into the chamber to Amnon her brother" (13:8-10). She complies, unaware. "And he took hold of her and said to her, 'Come, lie with me, my sister.' She answered him, 'No, my brother, do not violate me, for such a thing is not done in Israel; do not do this outrageous thing. And as for me, where could I carry my shame? And as for you, you would be as one of the outrageous fools in Israel. Now therefore, please speak to the king, for he will not withhold me from you'" (13:11-13). Tamar's wise appeal—rape = "outrageous" (נְבָלָה, disgrace). Suggests legitimate marriage possible. "But he would not listen to her, and being stronger than she, he violated her and lay with her" (13:14). **RAPE.** Force overcomes her pleas.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Violation*

#### 13:15-22 — Love Turns to Hate, Absalom Plots
**Lore:** "Then Amnon hated her with very great hatred, so that the hatred with which he hated her was greater than the love with which he had loved her. And Amnon said to her, 'Get up! Go!'" (13:15). **Lust turns to hatred**—casts her out. "But she said to him, 'No, my brother, for this wrong in sending me away is greater than the other that you did to me.' But he would not listen to her" (13:16). Compounds crime—unmarried violated woman = ruined. "So Tamar lived, a desolate woman, in her brother Absalom's house" (13:20). Absalom protects her—takes her in. "When King David heard of all these things, he was very angry. But Absalom spoke to Amnon neither good nor bad, for Absalom hated Amnon, because he had violated his sister Tamar" (13:21-22). **DAVID'S FAILURE**—very angry but does nothing! Unwilling to punish son. Absalom silent—plots revenge for two years.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Hate and silence*

#### 13:23-33 — Absalom's Revenge: Amnon Murdered
**Lore:** "After two full years Absalom had sheepshearers at Baal-hazor... And Absalom invited all the king's sons" (13:23-24). Two years planning. "Absalom came to the king and said, 'Behold, your servant has sheepshearers. Please let the king and his servants go with your servant.' But the king said to Absalom, 'No, my son, let us not all go, lest we be burdensome to you'" (13:24-25). David declines, sends sons. "Then Absalom commanded his servants, 'Mark when Amnon's heart is merry with wine, and when I say to you, "Strike Amnon," then kill him. Do not fear; have I not commanded you? Be courageous and be valiant'" (13:28). Planned assassination. "So the servants of Absalom did to Amnon as Absalom had commanded. Then all the king's sons arose, and each mounted his mule and fled" (13:29). Public execution at feast. "While they were on the way, news came to David, 'Absalom has struck down all the king's sons, and not one of them is left'" (13:30). False report—panic. But Jonadab (same crafty cousin) corrects: "'Let not my lord suppose that they have killed all the young men, the king's sons, for Amnon alone is dead'" (13:32).
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Revenge murder*

#### 13:34-39 — Absalom Flees to Geshur
**Lore:** "But Absalom fled. Now the watchman saw a great multitude coming from the road behind him, by the side of the mountain... And the watchman told the king, 'I see men coming from the Horonaim road, on the side of the mountain.' And Jonadab said to the king, 'Behold, the king's sons are coming; as your servant said, so it has come about'" (13:34-35). Confirms only Amnon dead. "So Absalom fled and went to Talmai the son of Ammihud, king of Geshur. And David mourned for his son day after day. So Absalom fled and went to Geshur, and was there three years. And the spirit of King David longed to go out to Absalom, for he was comforted about Amnon, since he was dead" (13:37-39). Absalom exiled. David's parental indulgence—longing for rebel son despite murder. Pattern: David's failure to discipline → children take law into own hands. Sword prophesied (12:10) now unleashed.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Exile and longing*

## Chapter 14: The Woman of Tekoa

#### 14:1-11 — Joab's Scheme: Woman Presents Parable
**Lore:** "Now Joab the son of Zeruiah knew that the king's heart went out to Absalom. And Joab sent to Tekoa and brought from there a wise woman and said to her, 'Pretend to be a mourner and put on mourning garments. Do not anoint yourself with oil, but behave like a woman who has been mourning the dead for many days'" (14:1-2). Joab orchestrates Absalom's return by proxy. Woman presents parable: "I am a widow; my husband is dead... And we had two sons, and the two fought with each other in the field; and there was none to part them, but the one struck the other and killed him. And now the whole clan has risen against your maidservant, and they say, 'Give up the man who struck his brother, that we may put him to death for the life of his brother whom he killed.' And so they would destroy the heir also. Thus they would quench my coal which is left, and leave to my husband neither name nor remnant upon the earth" (14:5-7). Parallels David/Absalom situation—death requires blood, but execution ends lineage.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Parable begins*

#### 14:12-19 — David's Own Words Condemn Him
**Lore:** "Then the woman said, 'I pray you, let your servant speak a word to my lord the king.' He said, 'Speak.' And the woman said, 'Why then have you planned such a thing against the people of God? For in giving this decision the king convicts himself, inasmuch as the king does not bring his banished one home again. For we must all die; we are like water spilled on the ground, which cannot be gathered up again. But God will not take away life, and he devises means so that the banished one will not remain an outcast. Now I have come to speak this word to my lord the king because the people made me afraid'" (14:12-15). Applies parable to Absalom—David's own logic condemns him! "But my lord has wisdom like the wisdom of the angel of God to know all things that are on the earth" (14:20). Flattery. David discerns Joab's hand (14:19). "Is the hand of Joab with you in all this?' The woman answered and said, 'As surely as you live, my lord the king, one cannot turn to the right hand or to the left from anything that my lord the king has said. It was your servant Joab who commanded me'" (14:18-19). Admits Joab's scheme.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Wisdom compels*

#### 14:20-24 — Compromise: Return but Not Reconciled
**Lore:** "Then the king said to Joab, 'Behold now, I grant this; go, bring back the young man Absalom.' And Joab fell on his face to the ground and paid homage and blessed the king. And Joab said, 'Today your servant knows that I have found favor in your sight, my lord the king, in that the king has granted the request of his servant.' So Joab arose and went to Geshur and brought Absalom to Jerusalem. And the king said, 'Let him dwell apart in his own house; he is not to come into my presence.' So Absalom lived apart in his own house and did not come into the king's presence" (14:21-24). Compromise—Absalom returns but not reconciled. Worst of both worlds—restores but keeps distance. David's "weakness and sentimental attachment" creates resentment.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Problematic restoration*

#### 14:25-33 — Absalom's Beauty and Full Reconciliation
**Lore:** "Now in all Israel there was no one so much to be praised for his handsome appearance as Absalom. From the sole of his foot to the crown of his head there was no blemish in him. And when he cut the hair of his head (for at the end of every year he used to cut it; when it was heavy on him, he cut it), he weighed the hair of his head, two hundred shekels by the king's weight" (14:25-26). **ABSALOM'S BEAUTY**—emphasizes outward appearance (like Saul). ~5 lbs hair! "Absalom lived two full years in Jerusalem, without coming into the king's presence. Then Absalom sent for Joab, to send him to the king, but Joab would not come to him. And he sent a second time, but Joab would not come" (14:28-29). Frustrated by partial restoration. Forces Joab's hand by burning his field (14:30-31). "So Joab went to the king and told him, and he summoned Absalom. So he came to the king and bowed himself on his face to the ground before the king, and the king kissed Absalom" (14:33). Full reconciliation—outward. But no repentance from Absalom, no justice for Amnon's murder. David's indulgence sets stage for rebellion. False messiah figure using beauty to mask rebellion.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Beauty masks rebellion*

**2 SAMUEL 8-14 COMPLETE!**

**Pericopes: 39** (7 chapters = 5.6/chapter average)

**Theme Summary:** Kingdom's apex—military victories, hesed to Mephibosheth. Tragic pivot: David's sin (neglect → lust → adultery → deception → murder). Nathan's parable, immediate confession ("I have sinned"), but sword never departs. Consequences: Amnon rapes Tamar, Absalom murders Amnon, exile then problematic restoration. David's indulgence toward children creates disaster. Pattern: sin → judgment → grace, but temporal consequences remain. Beauty (Absalom) masks rebellion. Stage set for chapters 15-20.
