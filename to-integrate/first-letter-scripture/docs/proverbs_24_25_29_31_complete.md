# Proverbs 24:23-34, 25-29, 30-31: PrMP 4-7 — Remaining Collections

## PrMP 4: The Further Sayings (24:23-34)

### Overview
**BRIEF APPENDIX** to "Words of Wise" (24:23, "These also belong to wise"). Only 12 verses, forming mini-collection. **Themes**: Justice, honesty, economic ethics, laziness. **Literary form**: Mixture of sayings + observational wisdom (24:30-34 = first-person field observation). **Connection**: Bridges "Thirty Sayings" (22:17-24:22) and "Hezekiah Collection" (25-29).

#### 24:23-26 — It Is Not Good to Have Respect of Persons in Judgment; A Right Answer Is Like Kisses on the Lips
**Lore:** **"These things also belong unto the wise. It is not good to have respect of persons in judgment"** (24:23). **SUPERSCRIPTION**—"also to wise" (גַּם־אֵלֶּה לַחֲכָמִים, gam-eleh lachakhamim). "Respect of persons" (הַכֵּר־פָּנִים, hakker-panim, show partiality, Lev 19:15; Deut 1:17; 16:19; Jas 2:1-9). Judicial corruption condemned. "He that saith unto the wicked, Thou art righteous; him shall the people curse, nations shall abhor him" (24:24). Acquit guilty → public curse + international disgrace. "But to them that rebuke him shall be delight, and a good blessing shall come upon them" (24:25). Convict wicked → delight + blessing (courage rewarded). "Every man shall kiss his lips that giveth a right answer" (24:26). **HONEST SPEECH**—"kiss lips" (יִשָּׁק שְׂפָתַיִם, yissaq sefatayim, sign of affection/approval, Song 1:2). "Right answer" (דְּבָרִים נְכֹחִים, devarim nekochim, straight words) = truthfulness. Honesty wins hearts.
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Justice*

#### 24:27 — Prepare Your Work Outside, and Make It Fit for Yourself in the Field; Afterwards Build Your House
**Lore:** **"Prepare thy work without, and make it fit for thyself in the field; and afterwards build thine house"** (24:27). **PRIORITIES**—"prepare work outside" (הָכֵן בַּחוּץ מְלַאכְתֶּךָ, hakhen bachutz melakhtekha) = agricultural work first. "Make fit in field" (וְעַתְּדָהּ בַּשָּׂדֶה לָךְ, ve'attedah bassadeh lakh) = establish income source. "Afterwards build house" (אַחַר וּבָנִיתָ בֵיתֶךָ, achar uvanita veitekha) = secure livelihood THEN domestic construction. Don't build dream house before establishing career/income. Prudent sequencing.
*Verses: 1 | Theme: Priorities*

#### 24:28-29 — Be Not a Witness Against Your Neighbor Without Cause; Say Not, I Will Do to Him as He Has Done to Me
**Lore:** "Be not a witness against thy neighbour without cause; and deceive not with thy lips" (24:28). **FALSE TESTIMONY** (19:5, 9). "Without cause" (חִנָּם, chinnam, gratuitously) = unprovoked, malicious accusation. "Deceive not with lips" = perjury. "Say not, I will do so to him as he hath done to me: I will render to the man according to his work" (24:29). **VENGEANCE FORBIDDEN** (20:22; 24:17; Lev 19:18; Rom 12:19). Natural impulse = retaliation. Command: don't repay evil for evil. Leave justice to God.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Vengeance*

#### 24:30-34 — I Went by the Field of the Slothful; Thorns and Nettles Covered the Face Thereof
**Lore:** **"I went by the field of the slothful, and by the vineyard of the man void of understanding; And, lo, it was all grown over with thorns, and nettles had covered the face thereof, and the stone wall thereof was broken down"** (24:30-31). **OBSERVATIONAL WISDOM**—first-person report (אֲנִי עָבַרְתִּי, ani aavarti, I passed by). Sluggard's field = overgrown with thorns (קִמְּשֹׂנִים, qimmesonim, thistles) + nettles (חֲרֻלִּים, charullim, weeds). "Stone wall broken down" (גְּדֵר אֲבָנָיו נֶהֱרָסָה, geder avanav neherasah) = neglected maintenance. Physical decay from laziness. "Then I saw, and considered it well: I looked upon it, and received instruction" (24:32). **LEARNING PROCESS**—"saw" (חָזִיתִי, chaziti), "considered well" (אָשִׁית לִבִּי, ashit libbi, set heart), "received instruction" (לָקַחְתִּי מוּסָר, laqachti musar). Observation → reflection → lesson. "Yet a little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to sleep: So shall thy poverty come as one that travelleth; and thy want as an armed man" (24:33-34). **REPEATED WARNING** (6:10-11, nearly identical). Sleep → poverty's arrival "like traveler" (מִתְהַלֵּךְ, mithalekh, moving steadily) + "want like armed man" (מָגֵן, magen, shielded warrior, unstoppable). Laziness = inevitable destitution.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Laziness*

## PrMP 5: The Hezekiah Collection (25-29)

### Overview
**ROYAL COLLECTION**—Superscription: "These are also proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out" (25:1). **Historical context**: King Hezekiah (715-686 BC) sponsored sapiential editing project (like Josiah's Torah reform, 2 Kgs 22-23). "Men of Hezekiah" (אַנְשֵׁי חִזְקִיָּה, anshei Chizqiyyah) = royal scribes/sages. **Literary shift**: Returns to brief sayings (like 10-22:16) but with more metaphors, nature imagery, and royal themes. **Structure**: Chapters 25-27 = social wisdom, chapters 28-29 = governance/righteousness themes. **137 verses**, requiring ~20-25 pericopes at 6-12 verses each. **Key metaphors**: Apples of gold (25:11), cold water to thirsty soul (25:25), dog returning to vomit (26:11), iron sharpens iron (27:17). **Canonical purpose**: Demonstrates wisdom's ongoing vitality—Solomon's proverbs remained relevant 250+ years later, copied for new generation under reforming king.

### Chapter 25: Metaphors and Relationships

#### 25:1-7 — It Is the Glory of God to Conceal a Thing; The King's Wrath Is as Messengers of Death
**Lore:** **"These are also proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out"** (25:1). **SUPERSCRIPTION + HISTORICAL NOTE**—"also proverbs of Solomon" (גַּם־אֵלֶּה מִשְׁלֵי שְׁלֹמֹה, gam-eleh mishlei Shelomoh). Hezekiah's men (reformers, 2 Kgs 18-20; 2 Chr 29-32) preserved/organized Solomon's sayings (scribal scholarship, transmission). "It is the glory of God to conceal a thing: but the honour of kings is to search out a matter" (25:2). **DIVINE/ROYAL CONTRAST**—God's glory = concealing (mysteries, Dan 2:22; Deut 29:29, "secret things belong to LORD"). Kings' honor = investigating (wisdom in governance, 1 Kgs 3:16-28, Solomon's judgment). "The heaven for height, and the earth for depth, and the heart of kings is unsearchable" (25:3). **ROYAL MYSTERY**—king's heart = unfathomable (like heaven's height, earth's depths). "Take away the dross from the silver, and there shall come forth a vessel for the finer. Take away the wicked from before the king, and his throne shall be established in righteousness" (25:4-5). **REFINING METAPHOR**—remove dross (סִיגִים, sigim, impurities) → pure silver vessel. Remove wicked from court → righteous throne (20:8, 26, 28; 16:12; 29:14). Purging evil = kingdom stability. "Put not forth thyself in the presence of the king, and stand not in the place of great men: For better it is that it be said unto thee, Come up hither; than that thou shouldest be put lower in the presence of the prince whom thine eyes have seen" (25:6-7). **HUMILITY TEACHING**—don't self-promote at court. Better invited up than demoted publicly. **NT PARALLEL** (Luke 14:7-11, parable of wedding feast, "exalt yourself → humbled; humble yourself → exalted").
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Royalty*

#### 25:8-15 — Go Not Forth Hastily to Strive; A Word Fitly Spoken Is Like Apples of Gold in Pictures of Silver
**Lore:** "Go not forth hastily to strive, lest thou know not what to do in the end thereof, when thy neighbour hath put thee to shame" (25:8). **LITIGATION WARNING**—don't rush to court (יָרִיב, yariv, legal strife, Matt 5:25, "agree with adversary quickly"). Might lose, causing public shame. "Debate thy cause with thy neighbour himself; and discover not a secret to another" (25:9). **PRIVATE RESOLUTION**—settle privately first (Matt 18:15-17, rebuke privately). "Discover not secret" (סוֹד, sod) = don't expose confidential matters in court. "Lest he that heareth it put thee to shame, and thine infamy turn not away" (25:10). Breaching confidence → permanent disgrace. "A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in pictures of silver" (25:11). **FAMOUS METAPHOR**—"fitly spoken" (דָּבָר דָּבֻר עַל־אָפְנָיו, davar davur al-ofnav, word spoken on its wheels/circumstances, perfectly timed). "Apples of gold in pictures of silver" (תַּפּוּחֵי זָהָב בְּמַשְׂכִּיּוֹת כָּסֶף, tappuchei zahav bemasikkiyot kasef) = possibly golden fruit in silver filigree baskets OR citrus/apricots in ornate setting (artistic beauty + value, 15:23). "As an earring of gold, and an ornament of fine gold, so is a wise reprover upon an obedient ear" (25:12). **JEWELRY METAPHOR**—wise correction + receptive listener = gold earring (mutual adornment). Teachability beautifies both parties. "As the cold of snow in the time of harvest, so is a faithful messenger to them that send him: for he refresheth the soul of his masters" (25:13). **REFRESHMENT METAPHOR**—"cold of snow" (צִנַּת־שֶׁלֶג, tsinnat-sheleg, coolness from snow) during hot harvest = relief. Reliable messenger (13:17) refreshes employers (satisfying trustworthiness). "Whoso boasteth himself of a false gift is like clouds and wind without rain" (25:14). **EMPTY PROMISE**—brags about giving but doesn't deliver = clouds without rain (Jude 12, "clouds without water"). Deceptive appearance, no substance. "By long forbearing is a prince persuaded, and a soft tongue breaketh the bone" (25:15). **PATIENCE POWER**—prolonged patience (אֹרֶךְ אַפַּיִם, orekh appayim, long nose/slow anger) persuades rulers. "Soft tongue" (לָשׁוֹן רַכָּה, lashon rakkah, gentle speech, 15:1) breaks bone = overcomes strongest resistance (gentleness > force).
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Speech*

#### 25:16-22 — Have You Found Honey? Eat So Much as Is Sufficient; If Your Enemy Be Hungry, Give Him Bread
**Lore:** "Hast thou found honey? eat so much as is sufficient for thee, lest thou be filled therewith, and vomit it" (25:16). **MODERATION**—even good things (honey, wisdom) in excess = sickness (24:13). "Withdraw thy foot from thy neighbour's house; lest he be weary of thee, and so hate thee" (25:17). **SOCIAL RESTRAINT**—don't overstay welcome. Frequent visits → weariness → hatred. Friendship requires boundaries. "A man that beareth false witness against his neighbour is a maul, and a sword, and a sharp arrow" (25:18). **FALSE TESTIMONY** = triple weapon: maul (מֵפִיץ, mefitz, war club), sword (חֶרֶב, cherev), sharp arrow (חֵץ שָׁנוּן, chetz shanun). Devastating harm. "Confidence in an unfaithful man in time of trouble is like a broken tooth, and a foot out of joint" (25:19). **UNRELIABLE ALLY**—trusting faithless person = broken tooth (can't chew) + dislocated foot (can't walk). Useless when needed most. "As he that taketh away a garment in cold weather, and as vinegar upon nitre, so is he that singeth songs to an heavy heart" (25:20). **INAPPROPRIATE CHEER**—taking coat in winter = cruelty. Vinegar on natron (נֶתֶר, neter, alkali, chemical reaction, hissing). Singing to grieving person = offensive (Rom 12:15, "weep with those who weep"). Insensitive timing. "If thine enemy be hungry, give him bread to eat; and if he be thirsty, give him water to drink: For thou shalt heap coals of fire upon his head, and the LORD shall reward thee" (25:21-22). **LOVE ENEMIES**—feed hungry enemy (quoted Rom 12:20). "Heap coals" (גֶּחָלִים, gechalim) = either (1) shame/conviction leading to repentance, OR (2) intensify judgment if unrepentant (debated). LORD rewards kindness to enemies (Matt 5:44-45).
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Relationships*

#### 25:23-28 — The North Wind Drives Away Rain; An Angry Countenance a Backbiting Tongue; Better to Dwell in Corner of Housetop
**Lore:** "The north wind driveth away rain: so doth an angry countenance a backbiting tongue" (25:23). **WEATHER METAPHOR**—north wind (רוּחַ צָפוֹן, ruach tzafon) clears rain (meteorologically questionable, but metaphorically clear face stops gossip). "As cold waters to a thirsty soul, so is good news from a far country" (25:25). **REFRESHMENT**—cold water (מַיִם קָרִים, mayim qarim) to parched (עֲיֵפָה, ayefah, weary) soul. Good report (שְׁמוּעָה טוֹבָה, shemu'ah tovah) from distant land = joy (relief about loved ones). "A righteous man falling down before the wicked is as a troubled fountain, and a corrupt spring" (25:26). **COMPROMISED RIGHTEOUS**—righteous yields/collapses (מָט, mat) before wicked = polluted fountain (מַעְיָן מָרְפָּס, ma'yan morfas, muddied spring) + corrupted source (מָקוֹר מָשְׁחָת, maqor mashchat). Capitulation ruins testimony. "It is not good to eat much honey: so for men to search their own glory is not glory" (25:27). **SELF-GLORY WARNING**—excess honey bad (25:16 repeat). Seeking own honor (דָּרַשׁ כְּבֹדָם כָּבוֹד, darash kefodam kavod) = not glorious (counterproductive, backfires). "He that hath no rule over his own spirit is like a city that is broken down, and without walls" (25:28). **SELF-CONTROL** (16:32). No mastery over spirit (רוּחוֹ, rucho, emotions/temper) = defenseless city (עִיר פְּרוּצָה אֵין חוֹמָה, ir perutzah ein chomah, breached, no walls). Vulnerability to attack.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Self-control*

### Chapter 26: Fools, Sluggards, and Deceivers

#### 26:1-12 — Honor Is Not Seemly for a Fool; Answer a Fool According to His Folly
**Lore:** "As snow in summer, and as rain in harvest, so honour is not seemly for a fool" (26:1). **INCONGRUITY**—snow in summer + rain at harvest = disasters (unseasonable). Honor to fool equally wrong. "As the bird by wandering, as the swallow by flying, so the curse causeless shall not come" (26:2). **UNDESERVED CURSE**—bird/swallow wander aimlessly, don't land. Baseless curse (קִלְלַת חִנָּם, qillat chinnam) won't stick (Num 23:8, Balaam can't curse Israel; David vs. Shimei, 2 Sam 16:12). "A whip for the horse, a bridle for the ass, and a rod for the fool's back" (26:3). **CORRECTION TOOLS**—each requires appropriate instrument (19:29; 10:13). Fool = physical discipline. "Answer not a fool according to his folly, lest thou also be like unto him. Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he be wise in his own conceit" (26:4-5). **APPARENT CONTRADICTION**—(1) Don't answer (descend to his level), (2) DO answer (expose his folly). RESOLUTION: Context determines (sometimes ignore, sometimes rebuke, Matt 7:6 vs. Gal 2:11). Wisdom discerns when. "He that sendeth a message by the hand of a fool cutteth off the feet, and drinketh damage" (26:6). **FOOL AS MESSENGER**—sending fool = self-amputation (ineffective) + drinking violence (self-harm). "The legs of the lame are not equal: so is a parable in the mouth of fools" (26:7). Lame can't walk properly, fool can't use proverbs (misapplied wisdom). "As he that bindeth a stone in a sling, so is he that giveth honour to a fool" (26:8). Tying stone in sling = prevents launching (defeats purpose). Honor to fool = wasted. "As a thorn goeth up into the hand of a drunkard, so is a parable in the mouth of fools" (26:9). Drunk with thorn = dangerous (can't feel, waves around). Fool with proverb = harmful (misuses it). "The great God that formed all things both rewardeth the fool, and rewardeth transgressors" (26:10). **DIVINE JUDGMENT**—God recompenses (שָׂכַר, sakhar, pays) fool + transgressor (eventual justice). "As a dog returneth to his vomit, so a fool returneth to his folly" (26:11). **FAMOUS METAPHOR** (quoted 2 Pet 2:22). Dog eats vomit = disgusting. Fool repeats folly = equally repulsive. "Seest thou a man wise in his own conceit? there is more hope of a fool than of him" (26:12). **WORST CASE**—self-conceited (חָכָם בְּעֵינָיו, chakham be'einav, wise in own eyes, 3:7; 12:15; 28:26) worse than simple fool (at least fool might learn).
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Folly*

#### 26:13-16 — The Slothful Man Says, There Is a Lion in the Way; The Sluggard Is Wiser in His Own Conceit Than Seven Men
**Lore:** "The slothful man saith, There is a lion in the way; a lion is in the streets" (26:13). **ABSURD EXCUSE** (22:13 repeat). Sluggard invents ridiculous reason to avoid work. "As the door turneth upon his hinges, so doth the slothful upon his bed" (26:14). **COMIC IMAGE**—door swings on hinges (צִיר, tzir) but goes nowhere. Sluggard rolls in bed (flips back/forth) but doesn't get up. "The slothful hideth his hand in his bosom; it grieveth him to bring it again to his mouth" (26:15). **EXTREME LAZINESS** (19:24 repeat). Too lazy to feed self. "The sluggard is wiser in his own conceit than seven men that can render a reason" (26:16). **SELF-DELUSION**—lazy person thinks himself wiser than 7 advisors (collective wisdom). Irrational self-assessment.
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Laziness*

#### 26:17-28 — He That Passes By and Meddles with Strife Is Like One That Takes a Dog by the Ears; Where No Wood Is, Fire Goes Out
**Lore:** "He that passeth by, and meddleth with strife belonging not to him, is like one that taketh a dog by the ears" (26:17). **MEDDLING**—interfering in others' quarrel = grabbing dog's ears (gets bitten). "As a mad man who casteth firebrands, arrows, and death, So is the man that deceiveth his neighbour, and saith, Am not I in sport?" (26:18-19). **"JUST JOKING" DEFENSE**—throwing firebrands (זִקִּים, ziqqim) + arrows + death then claiming "just kidding" = madman. Harmful "humor" not excused. "Where no wood is, there the fire goeth out: so where there is no talebearer, the strife ceaseth" (26:20). **GOSSIP = FUEL**—no wood → fire dies. No gossip → conflict ends (16:28; 17:9). Remove talebearer = peace. "As coals are to burning coals, and wood to fire; so is a contentious man to kindle strife" (26:21). **QUARRELSOME PERSON** = fire starter (15:18; Jas 3:5-6). "The words of a talebearer are as wounds, and they go down into the innermost parts of the belly" (26:22). **GOSSIP WOUNDS** (18:8 exact repeat). Slander penetrates deeply. "Burning lips and a wicked heart are like a potsherd covered with silver dross" (26:23). **HYPOCRISY**—fervent speech (שְׂפָתַיִם דֹּלְקוֹת, sefatayim dolqot, burning lips) + evil heart = clay pot with silver slag (cheap pottery coated to look valuable, deceptive appearance). "He that hateth dissembleth with his lips, and layeth up deceit within him" (26:24). Hater disguises with pleasant words, stores treachery internally. "When he speaketh fair, believe him not: for there are seven abominations in his heart" (26:25). **DISTRUST WARNING**—smooth speech (יְחַנֶּן־קוֹלוֹ, yechanen-qolo, makes voice gracious) conceals "seven abominations" (שֶׁבַע תּוֹעֵבוֹת, sheva to'evot, complete evil, 6:16-19 lists seven). "Whose hatred is covered by deceit, his wickedness shall be shewed before the whole congregation" (26:26). Hidden hatred eventually exposed publicly. "Whoso diggeth a pit shall fall therein: and he that rolleth a stone, it will return upon him" (26:27). **SELF-DESTRUCTION**—trap-digger falls in own pit (Ps 7:15; 9:15; 57:6; Eccl 10:8; Est 7:10, Haman). Poetic justice. "A lying tongue hateth those that are afflicted by it; and a flattering mouth worketh ruin" (26:28). Liar hates victims. Flatterer causes ruin (מִדְחֶה, midcheh, slippery ruin).
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Deceit*

### Chapter 27: Relationships and Prudence

#### 27:1-11 — Boast Not Yourself of Tomorrow; Faithful Are the Wounds of a Friend
**Lore:** **"Boast not thyself of tomorrow; for thou knowest not what a day may bring forth"** (27:1). **NO PRESUMPTION** (Jas 4:13-16, "you don't know tomorrow"). God controls future. "Let another man praise thee, and not thine own mouth; a stranger, and not thine own lips" (27:2). **HUMILITY**—let others praise (Prov 31:31; 2 Cor 10:18). Self-praise = vanity. "A stone is heavy, and the sand weighty; but a fool's wrath is heavier than them both" (27:3). **FOOL'S ANGER**—stone + sand heavy, but fool's rage (כַּעַס, ka'as) = heavier burden (dealing with it exhausting). "Wrath is cruel, and anger is outrageous; but who is able to stand before envy?" (27:4). **JEALOUSY WORST**—wrath (חֵמָה, chemah) cruel, anger (אַף, aph) overwhelming, but envy (קִנְאָה, qin'ah) = unstoppable (Song 8:6, "jealousy cruel as grave"). "Open rebuke is better than secret love" (27:5). **HONEST REBUKE** > hidden affection (Matt 18:15; Gal 2:11-14, Paul rebukes Peter). "Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful" (27:6). **FAMOUS PROVERB**—friend's wounds (פְּצָעִים, petza'im, rebukes that hurt) = faithful (נֶאֱמָנִים, ne'emanim, reliable, Ps 141:5). Enemy's kisses (נְשִׁיקוֹת, neshiqot, 2 Sam 20:9-10, Joab kills Amasa with kiss; Matt 26:49, Judas) = deceitful (נַעֲתָרוֹת, na'atarot, abundant but treacherous). Truth from friend > flattery from foe. "The full soul loatheth an honeycomb; but to the hungry soul every bitter thing is sweet" (27:7). **APPETITE PERSPECTIVE**—satisfied person rejects honey (excess). Starving person eats bitter food gladly (necessity transforms taste). "As a bird that wandereth from her nest, so is a man that wandereth from his place" (27:8). **ROOTLESSNESS**—bird leaving nest = vulnerability. Man leaving home/calling = instability. "Ointment and perfume rejoice the heart: so doth the sweetness of a man's friend by hearty counsel" (27:9). **FRIENDSHIP VALUE**—fragrance delights (שֶׁמֶן וּקְטֹרֶת, shemen uqetoret, oil + incense). Friend's counsel (עֲצַת־נֶפֶשׁ, atzat-nefesh, soul counsel) equally sweet. "Thine own friend, and thy father's friend, forsake not; neither go into thy brother's house in the day of thy calamity: for better is a neighbour that is near than a brother far off" (27:10). **PROXIMITY VALUE**—don't abandon family friends (loyalty across generations). In crisis, nearby neighbor > distant brother. "My son, be wise, and make my heart glad, that I may answer him that reproacheth me" (27:11). **PARENTAL DEFENSE**—wise child = parent's vindication against critics (23:15-16, 24-25).
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Friendship*

#### 27:12-22 — A Prudent Man Foresees Evil and Hides Himself; As Iron Sharpens Iron, So a Man Sharpens the Countenance of His Friend
**Lore:** "A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself; but the simple pass on, and are punished" (27:12). **FORESIGHT** (22:3 exact repeat). Wise anticipate danger, avoid. Naive proceed, suffer. "Take his garment that is surety for a stranger, and take a pledge of him for a strange woman" (27:13). **CO-SIGNING** (20:16 repeat). Demand collateral from fool who guarantees stranger's debt. "He that blesseth his friend with a loud voice, rising early in the morning, it shall be counted a curse to him" (27:14). **EXCESSIVE PRAISE**—over-the-top, early morning flattery = suspect (sarcastic? manipulative?). Treated as curse. "A continual dropping in a very rainy day and a contentious woman are alike" (27:15). **QUARRELSOME WIFE** (19:13; 21:9, 19). Perpetual dripping (roof leak) = nagging wife (irritation). "Whosoever hideth her hideth the wind, and the ointment of his right hand, which bewrayeth itself" (27:16). **IMPOSSIBLE TASK**—hiding contentious wife = restraining wind + concealing oil's fragrance (can't be done). "Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend" (27:17). **FAMOUS PROVERB**—mutual sharpening (חָדַד, chadad, hone edge). Friends refine each other (1 Sam 23:16, Jonathan strengthens David; Heb 10:24, "provoke to love and good works"). "Whoso keepeth the fig tree shall eat the fruit thereof: so he that waiteth on his master shall be honoured" (27:18). **REWARD PRINCIPLE**—tend fig tree → eat figs (Deut 20:6; 1 Cor 9:7). Serve master faithfully → honor. "As in water face answereth to face, so the heart of man to man" (27:19). **REFLECTION**—water reflects face. Heart reflects heart (people mirror each other's character/motivations). "Hell and destruction are never full; so the eyes of man are never satisfied" (27:20). **INSATIABLE**—Sheol (שְׁאוֹל) + Abaddon (אֲבַדּוֹן) never say "enough" (30:15-16). Human eyes (greed, lust) equally insatiable (Eccl 1:8; 4:8; 5:10; 1 John 2:16). "As the fining pot for silver, and the furnace for gold; so is a man to his praise" (27:21). **PRAISE TEST**—crucible tests silver, furnace tests gold (17:3). How man handles praise tests character (does he stay humble or become proud?). "Though thou shouldest bray a fool in a mortar among wheat with a pestle, yet will not his foolishness depart from him" (27:22). **INCORRIGIBLE FOOL**—pound fool in mortar (grinding grain) with pestle, folly doesn't leave (29:1). Correction useless on hardened fool.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Prudence*

#### 27:23-27 — Be Diligent to Know the State of Your Flocks; The Lambs Are for Your Clothing
**Lore:** **"Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds"** (27:23). **AGRICULTURAL WISDOM**—"know state" (יָדֹעַ תֵּדַע, yado'a teda', know surely). "Look well" (שִׁית לִבְּךָ, shit libbeka, set heart to). Attend to assets (livestock = ancient wealth). "For riches are not for ever: and doth the crown endure to every generation?" (27:24). **WEALTH TRANSIENCE**—riches temporary (23:5). Even crown (dynasty) doesn't always pass to children (kingdoms fall). Invest in sustainable resources. "The hay appeareth, and the tender grass sheweth itself, and herbs of the mountains are gathered. The lambs are for thy clothing, and the goats are the price of the field. And thou shalt have goats' milk enough for thy food, for the food of thy household, and for the maintenance for thy maidens" (27:25-27). **AGRARIAN CYCLE**—hay appears (חָצִיר, chatzir, grass), new growth shows, mountain herbs gathered. Lambs provide clothing (wool). Goats = field price (sell for land). Goat milk (חֲלֵב עִזִּים, chalev izzim) feeds household + servants (דֵּי חַיֵּי, dei chayei, sufficient life). Picture of sustainable, self-sufficient household economy (vs. chasing get-rich-quick schemes).
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Stewardship*

### Chapters 28-29: Governance and Righteousness

#### 28:1-10 — The Wicked Flee When No Man Pursues; He That Covers His Sins Shall Not Prosper
**Lore:** **"The wicked flee when no man pursueth: but the righteous are bold as a lion"** (28:1). **GUILTY CONSCIENCE**—wicked flee from imagined threats (Lev 26:36, "sound of driven leaf"). Righteous = lion-bold (כְּכְפִיר, kekheir, young lion, fearless, 2 Tim 1:7). "For the transgression of a land many are the princes thereof: but by a man of understanding and knowledge the state thereof shall be prolonged" (28:2). **POLITICAL INSTABILITY**—sin → frequent regime changes (Israel's divided kingdom, rapid dynasties). Wise leader → prolonged stability. "A poor man that oppresseth the poor is like a sweeping rain which leaveth no food" (28:3). **TRAITOR TO CLASS**—poor oppressing poor = devastating rain (סוֹחֵף, socheif, destructive flood washing away crops). Worse than rich oppressor (betrayal). "They that forsake the law praise the wicked: but such as keep the law contend with them" (28:4). **LAW OBSERVANCE**—abandon Torah → praise wicked. Keep Torah → oppose them. Clarity: obedience = opposition to evil. "Evil men understand not judgment: but they that seek the LORD understand all things" (28:5). Wicked don't comprehend justice (מִשְׁפָּט, mishpat). LORD-seekers understand everything (moral/spiritual insight, John 7:17). "Better is the poor that walketh in his uprightness, than he that is perverse in his ways, though he be rich" (28:6). **"BETTER THAN"** (19:1 variant). Poor + integrity > rich + perversion. "Whoso keepeth the law is a wise son: but he that is a companion of riotous men shameth his father" (28:7). **LAW + FAMILY**—Torah keeper = wise son. Glutton's companion = father's shame. "He that by usury and unjust gain increaseth his substance, he shall gather it for him that will pity the poor" (28:8). **INTEREST/OPPRESSION** (Ex 22:25; Lev 25:35-37; Ezek 18:8, 13, 17). Unjust wealth accumulates for one who'll give to poor (wealth transfer, 13:22; Eccl 2:26). "He that turneth away his ear from hearing the law, even his prayer shall be abomination" (28:9). **TORAH REJECTION**—refuse law (תּוֹרָה, Torah) → prayer becomes abomination (תּוֹעֵבָה, to'evah, 15:8, 29). Obedience prerequisite for acceptable worship (1 Sam 15:22; Isa 1:11-17; Amos 5:21-24). "Whoso causeth the righteous to go astray in an evil way, he shall fall himself into his own pit: but the upright shall have good things in possession" (28:10). **MISLEADER JUDGED**—leading righteous astray (הִשְׁגֶּה, hishgeh, cause to err) → self-destruction (fall in own pit, 26:27). Upright inherit good (נָחַל, nachal, possess as inheritance).
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Righteousness*

#### 28:11-20 — The Rich Man Is Wise in His Own Conceit; He That Hastens to Be Rich Has an Evil Eye
**Lore:** "The rich man is wise in his own conceit; but the poor that hath understanding searcheth him out" (28:11). **WEALTH ≠ WISDOM**—rich self-deceived (26:12, 16). Poor with understanding (בִּינָה, binah) sees through him (חָקְרוֹ, choqero, investigates). "When righteous men do rejoice, there is great glory: but when the wicked rise, a man is hidden" (28:12). **RIGHTEOUS RULE** → great glory (תִּפְאֶרֶת, tif'eret, splendor). Wicked rise → people hide (29:2; Est 3:15, Susa perplexed when Haman promoted). "He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy" (28:13). **CONFESSION** (Ps 32:3-5; 1 John 1:9). Cover sins (מְכַסֶּה, mekhasseh) → no prosperity. Confess (מוֹדֶה, modeh) + forsake (עֹזֵב, ozev) → mercy (רֻחָם, rucham, compassion). "Happy is the man that feareth alway: but he that hardeneth his heart shall fall into mischief" (28:14). **CONTINUAL FEAR**—blessed (אַשְׁרֵי, ashrei) who fears always (פַחֵד, pached, reverent caution, Phil 2:12, "work out salvation with fear and trembling"). Hardened heart (מַקְשֶׁה, maqsheh, Ex 7:3, Pharaoh) → calamity. "As a roaring lion, and a ranging bear; so is a wicked ruler over the poor people" (28:15). **OPPRESSIVE RULER**—wicked leader = roaring lion (שֹׁאֵג, sho'eg) + charging bear (דֹּב שֹׁוקֵק, dov shoqeq, 2 Kgs 2:24, bears maul youths). Predators devouring weak. "The prince that wanteth understanding is also a great oppressor: but he that hateth covetousness shall prolong his days" (28:16). **LEADER ETHICS**—foolish prince = oppressor (רַב מַעֲשַׁקּוֹת, rav ma'ashaqqot, many extortions). Hates unjust gain (בֹּצֵעַ בֶּצַע, botze'a betza') → long reign. "A man that doeth violence to the blood of any person shall flee to the pit; let no man stay him" (28:17). **MURDERER**—blood guilt (דַּם־נֶפֶשׁ, dam-nefesh) → flees to pit (Sheol/execution). "Let no one support him" (don't harbor, Num 35:9-34, cities of refuge don't protect murderers). "Whoso walketh uprightly shall be saved: but he that is perverse in his ways shall fall at once" (28:18). **INTEGRITY vs. PERVERSION**—upright (תָּמִים, tamim) saved. Crooked (נֶעְקַשׁ דְּרָכַיִם, ne'qash derakhayim, twisted paths) falls suddenly (בְּאַחַת, be'achat, at once). "He that tilleth his land shall have plenty of bread: but he that followeth after vain persons shall have poverty enough" (28:19). **DILIGENCE** (12:11 variant). Farm → abundance. Chase fantasies → poverty (רֵיקִים, reqim, empty people). "A faithful man shall abound with blessings: but he that maketh haste to be rich shall not be innocent" (28:20). **FAITHFUL vs. HASTY**—reliable (אֱמוּנוֹת, emunot) → blessings. Rush to riches (אָץ לְהוֹן, atz lehon) → not innocent (לֹא יִנָּקֶה, lo yinnaqqeh, 13:11; 20:21; 21:5; 23:4). Get-rich-quick = sin.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Wealth*

#### 28:21-29 — To Have Respect of Persons Is Not Good; He That Rebukes a Man Shall Find More Favor Than He That Flatters
**Lore:** "To have respect of persons is not good: for for a piece of bread that man will transgress" (28:21). **PARTIALITY** (24:23; 18:5). Even for bread (מַת־לֶחֶם, pat-lechem, morsel) partial judge sins. Bribery cheaply bought. "He that hasteth to be rich hath an evil eye, and considereth not that poverty shall come upon him" (28:22). **GREEDY EYE** (רַע עָיִן, ra' ayin, stingy/envious, 23:6; 22:9 opposite). Rush to wealth → poverty (ironic reversal, 28:20). "He that rebuketh a man afterwards shall find more favour than he that flattereth with the tongue" (28:23). **REBUKE > FLATTERY** (27:5-6). Short-term sting, long-term favor (Ps 141:5; Gal 2:11-14). "Whoso robbeth his father or his mother, and saith, It is no transgression; the same is the companion of a destroyer" (28:24). **PARENTAL THEFT** (19:26; Matt 15:5-6, Corban). Rationalizing ("not sin") = partnering with vandal (אִישׁ מַשְׁחִית, ish mashchit). "He that is of a proud heart stirreth up strife: but he that putteth his trust in the LORD shall be made fat" (28:25). **PRIDE vs. TRUST**—arrogant (רְחַב־נֶפֶשׁ, rechav-nefesh, wide of soul, greedy) creates conflict. Trust LORD → prosper (דֻשַּׁן, dushan, fattened, abundance). "He that trusteth in his own heart is a fool: but whoso walketh wisely, he shall be delivered" (28:26). **SELF-TRUST = FOLLY** (3:5-6; Jer 17:9, "heart deceitful above all"). Walk wisely → deliverance. "He that giveth unto the poor shall not lack: but he that hideth his eyes shall have many a curse" (28:27). **GENEROSITY vs. IGNORING** (14:21, 31; 19:17; 22:9). Give to poor → no lack. Hide eyes (close eyes to need) → curses (21:13). "When the wicked rise, men hide themselves: but when they perish, the righteous increase" (28:28). **WICKED RULE** (28:12). Wicked ascend → people hide (fear). Wicked perish → righteous multiply (flourish).
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Justice*

#### 29:1-11 — He That Being Often Reproved Hardens His Neck; When the Righteous Are in Authority, the People Rejoice
**Lore:** **"He, that being often reproved hardeneth his neck, shall suddenly be destroyed, and that without remedy"** (29:1). **FINAL WARNING**—frequent rebuke (אִישׁ תּוֹכָחוֹת, ish tokhachot, man of reproofs) + hardened neck (מַקְשֶׁה־עֹרֶף, maqsheh-oref, stiff-necked, Ex 32:9; Deut 9:6, 13; Acts 7:51) → sudden destruction (פֶּתַע, peta') without remedy (אֵין מַרְפֵּא, ein marpe', no healing, 6:15; 27:22). Point of no return. "When the righteous are in authority, the people rejoice: but when the wicked beareth rule, the people mourn" (29:2). **GOVERNANCE IMPACT**—righteous increase (בִּרְבוֹת צַדִּיקִים, birvot tzaddiqim, when righteous multiply in leadership) → people rejoice. Wicked rule → groaning (28:12, 28; Est 3:15 vs. 8:15). "Whoso loveth wisdom rejoiceth his father: but he that keepeth company with harlots spendeth his substance" (29:3). **FATHER'S JOY vs. GRIEF** (10:1; 15:20; 23:24-25; 27:11). Love wisdom → father rejoices. Prostitutes → squanders wealth (5:9-10; 6:26; Luke 15:13, 30, prodigal son). "The king by judgment establisheth the land: but he that receiveth gifts overthroweth it" (29:4). **JUST KING**—judgment (מִשְׁפָּט, mishpat, justice) stabilizes land. Taking bribes (אִישׁ תְּרוּמוֹת, ish terumot, man of contributions/gifts) destroys kingdom. "A man that flattereth his neighbour spreadeth a net for his feet" (29:5). **FLATTERY TRAP**—smooth talk (מַחֲלִיק, machaliq, makes smooth, 2:16; 7:5; 26:28; 28:23) = net for feet (ensnares). "In the transgression of an evil man there is a snare: but the righteous doth sing and rejoice" (29:6). **WICKED TRAPPED**—sin = snare for evildoer (self-destructive). Righteous sing (יָרוּן, yarun) + rejoice (freedom from trap). "The righteous considereth the cause of the poor: but the wicked regardeth not to know it" (29:7). **POOR ADVOCACY**—righteous knows (יֹדֵעַ, yodea') poor's cause (דִּין דַּלִּים, din dallim, legal rights of weak, Ps 82:3; Jer 22:16). Wicked doesn't understand/care. "Scornful men bring a city into a snare: but wise men turn away wrath" (29:8). **MOCKERS**—scoffers (אַנְשֵׁי לָצוֹן, anshei latzon, 1:22; 3:34; 9:7-8; 13:1; 15:12) inflame city (פּוּחִים קִרְיָה, puchim qiryah, set city on fire, stir riots). Wise turn away wrath (deflect anger, prevent escalation). "If a wise man contendeth with a foolish man, whether he rage or laugh, there is no rest" (29:9). **ARGUING WITH FOOL**—wise vs. fool = no resolution (fool either rages or laughs, no rational engagement). "The bloodthirsty hate the upright: but the just seek his soul" (29:10). **MURDERERS vs. RIGHTEOUS**—violent (אַנְשֵׁי דָמִים, anshei damim, men of blood, Ps 5:6; 26:9; 55:23; 139:19) hate blameless. Just seek to protect his life (נַפְשׁוֹ, nafsho, soul). "A fool uttereth all his mind: but a wise man keepeth it in till afterwards" (29:11). **RESTRAINT** (10:19; 12:23; 13:3; 17:27-28). Fool vents everything (יוֹצִיא כָל־רוּחוֹ, yotzi khol-rucho, lets out all his spirit). Wise withholds, reveals strategically.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Governance*

#### 29:12-18 — If a Ruler Hearken to Lies; Where There Is No Vision, the People Perish
**Lore:** "If a ruler hearken to lies, all his servants are wicked" (29:12). **CORRUPT LEADERSHIP**—ruler believes lies → entire court becomes corrupt (1 Kgs 12:8-14, Rehoboam listens to young advisors). "The poor and the deceitful man meet together: the LORD lighteneth both their eyes" (29:13). **COMMON CREATOR** (22:2; 14:31; 17:5). Poor + oppressor (אִישׁ תְּכָכִים, ish tekhakim, man of oppressions) both created by LORD. God "lights eyes" (gives life, Ps 13:3). "The king that faithfully judgeth the poor, his throne shall be established for ever" (29:14). **POOR JUSTICE** (20:28; 25:5; 16:12; 31:8-9). Ruler judges poor fairly (בֶּאֱמֶת, be'emet, in truth) → eternal throne (dynasty stability, Ps 72). "The rod and reproof give wisdom: but a child left to himself bringeth his mother to shame" (29:15). **PARENTAL DISCIPLINE** (13:24; 19:18; 22:15; 23:13-14; 29:17). Rod (שֵׁבֶט, shevet) + correction → wisdom. Neglected child (מְשֻׁלָּח, meshullach, sent away/abandoned, no discipline) → mother's shame. "When the wicked are multiplied, transgression increaseth: but the righteous shall see their fall" (29:16). **WICKED INCREASE**—evil people multiply → sin increases (28:12, 28). Righteous will witness their downfall (Ps 37:34-36; 58:10; 91:8). "Correct thy son, and he shall give thee rest; yea, he shall give delight unto thy soul" (29:17). **DISCIPLINE REWARD** (13:24; 19:18; 22:6, 15; 23:13-14; 29:15). Correct (יַסֵּר, yasser) → rest (נַחַת, nachat, tranquility) + delight (מַעֲדַנִּים, ma'adannim, delicacies/pleasures). Well-disciplined child = parent's joy. **"Where there is no vision, the people perish: but he that keepeth the law, happy is he"** (29:18). **FAMOUS VERSE**—"no vision" (אֵין חָזוֹן, ein chazon, no prophetic revelation, 1 Sam 3:1) → people perish (יִפָּרַע, yippara', cast off restraint, run wild, Ex 32:25, golden calf). Keep Torah (שֹׁמֵר תּוֹרָה, shomer torah) → blessed (אַשְׁרֵהוּ, ashrehu). Divine guidance essential for social order.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Vision*

#### 29:19-27 — A Servant Will Not Be Corrected by Words; The Fear of Man Brings a Snare
**Lore:** "A servant will not be corrected by words: for though he understand he will not answer" (29:19). **STUBBORN SERVANT**—verbal correction insufficient (understands but won't respond, passive resistance, needs concrete consequences). "Seest thou a man that is hasty in his words? there is more hope of a fool than of him" (29:20). **HASTY SPEECH**—quick talker (אָץ בִּדְבָרָיו, atz bidvarav, rushing in words) worse than fool (26:12; James 1:19). "He that delicately bringeth up his servant from a child shall have him become his son at the length" (29:21). **PAMPERED SERVANT**—spoiling slave from youth (מְפַנֵּק, mefanneq, indulgent) → he'll claim sonship (מָנוֹן, manon, possibly heir, ambiguous term). Blurred boundaries. "An angry man stirreth up strife, and a furious man aboundeth in transgression" (29:22). **ANGER** (15:18; 26:21). Hot-tempered (אִישׁ אַף, ish aph, man of nose) creates conflict. Rage-prone (בַּעַל חֵמָה, ba'al chemah, master of wrath) multiplies sin. "A man's pride shall bring him low: but honour shall uphold the humble in spirit" (29:23). **PRIDE/HUMILITY** (11:2; 15:33; 16:18-19; 18:12; Luke 14:11; 18:14). Pride (גְּאוֹן, ge'on) → humiliation. Humility (שְׁפַל־רוּחַ, shefal-ruach, low of spirit) → honor. "Whoso is partner with a thief hateth his own soul: he heareth cursing, and bewrayeth it not" (29:24). **COMPLICITY**—thief's accomplice (חוֹלֵק עִם־גַּנָּב, choleq im-gannav) = self-hating (שׂוֹנֵא נַפְשׁוֹ, sone nafsho). Hears oath (אָלָה, alah, curse formula adjuring witnesses to testify, Lev 5:1; Judg 17:2) but won't tell (self-destruction via silence). **"The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe"** (29:25). **FAMOUS VERSE**—fearing people (חֶרְדַּת אָדָם, cherdat adam, dread of man) = trap (מוֹקֵשׁ, moqesh, snare, John 12:42-43, fear Pharisees more than God; Gal 1:10). Trust LORD (בֹּטֵחַ בַּיהוָה, boteach ba-YHWH) → safe (יְשֻׂגָּב, yesuggav, set on high, secure, Ps 118:6; Heb 13:6). "Many seek the ruler's favour; but every man's judgment cometh from the LORD" (29:26). **DIVINE JUSTICE**—many court ruler's favor (פְּנֵי־מוֹשֵׁל, fenei-moshel, face of leader). But true justice (מִשְׁפַּט, mishpat) comes from LORD (Ps 75:7, "God is judge"). "An unjust man is an abomination to the just: and he that is upright in the way is abomination to the wicked" (29:27). **MUTUAL ABOMINATION**—righteous loathe (תּוֹעֲבַת, to'avat) unjust. Wicked loathe upright (יְשַׁר־דָּרֶךְ, yeshar-derekh, straight in way). Moral incompatibility (2 Cor 6:14-15; 1 John 3:13).
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Trust*

## PrMP 6: The Oracle of Agur (30:1-33)

### Overview
**NON-SOLOMONIC COLLECTION**—Superscription: "The words of Agur the son of Jakeh, even the prophecy" (30:1). **Agur** (אָגוּר, Agur, "gatherer") = otherwise unknown sage (possibly non-Israelite? Massa = Arabian tribe, Gen 25:14). **Literary form**: "Numerical sayings" (X...X+1 pattern, e.g., "three things...four"). **Themes**: Humility before God's transcendence, ethical warnings, observations about creation. **Tone**: Self-deprecating opening ("I am more brutish than any man") contrasts Solomon's confidence. **33 verses**, requiring ~5-6 pericopes.

#### 30:1-6 — I Am More Brutish Than Any Man; Every Word of God Is Pure
**Lore:** **"The words of Agur the son of Jakeh, even the prophecy: the man spake unto Ithiel, even unto Ithiel and Ucal"** (30:1). **SUPERSCRIPTION**—"Agur son of Jakeh" (אָגוּר בִּן־יָקֶה, Agur bin-Yaqeh). "Prophecy" (מַשָּׂא, massa', oracle/burden, also name of Arabian tribe). Ithiel (אִיתִיאֵל, Iti'el, "God is with me") + Ucal (אוּכָל, Ukhal, "I can/am able") = recipients OR poetic device (declarations: "God with me, I can prevail"?). "Surely I am more brutish than any man, and have not the understanding of a man. I neither learned wisdom, nor have the knowledge of the holy" (30:2-3). **HUMILITY**—"brutish" (בַּעַר, ba'ar, stupid, 12:1) more than anyone. No wisdom (חָכְמָה, chokhmah) or knowledge of Holy One (קְדֹשִׁים, qedoshim, possibly plural of majesty OR angelic beings). Socratic confession of ignorance (vs. Solomon's confident wisdom, 1 Kgs 4:29-34). **"Who hath ascended up into heaven, or descended? who hath gathered the wind in his fists? who hath bound the waters in a garment? who hath established all the ends of the earth? what is his name, and what is his son's name, if thou canst tell?"** (30:4). **DIVINE INTERROGATION**—five questions re: God's transcendent activity (Job 38-41 parallel). Ascended/descended (John 3:13, "No one ascended but He who descended, Son of Man"). Gathered wind (Ps 135:7). Bound waters (Job 26:8; 38:8-11). Established earth (Isa 40:12). "His name...son's name" = possibly Christological foreshadowing (Ps 2:7, 12; Prov 8:22-31, Wisdom personified). "Every word of God is pure: he is a shield unto them that put their trust in him. Add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar" (30:5-6). **SCRIPTURE PURITY** (Ps 12:6; 18:30; 119:140; 2 Sam 22:31). God's words = refined (צְרוּפָה, tzerufah, tested/smelted). Don't add (Deut 4:2; 12:32; Rev 22:18-19). Divine reproof for tampering.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Humility*

#### 30:7-9 — Two Things Have I Required of Thee; Give Me Neither Poverty Nor Riches
**Lore:** **"Two things have I required of thee; deny me them not before I die: Remove far from me vanity and lies: give me neither poverty nor riches; feed me with food convenient for me: Lest I be full, and deny thee, and say, Who is the LORD? or lest I be poor, and steal, and take the name of my God in vain"** (30:7-9). **AGUR'S PRAYER**—two requests: (1) Remove falsehood (שָׁוְא וּדְבַר־כָזָב, shav udvar-kazav, vanity + lying word). (2) Give neither poverty nor riches (לֹא־עֹשֶׁר וָרָשׁ, lo-osher varash), just "food of my portion" (לֶחֶם חֻקִּי, lechem chuqqi, allotted bread, daily provision). Reasons: (a) Wealth → forget God, say "Who is LORD?" (apostasy via prosperity, Deut 8:10-14; 32:15; Hos 13:6). (b) Poverty → steal, profane God's name (desperation tempts to crime, violates 3rd/8th commandments). **GOLDEN MEAN**—contentment between extremes (Phil 4:11-13; 1 Tim 6:6-8; Heb 13:5). Rare prayer for moderation (vs. prosperity gospel).
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Contentment*

#### 30:10-17 — There Is a Generation That Curses Their Father; The Eye That Mocks at His Father, the Ravens Shall Pick It Out
**Lore:** "Accuse not a servant unto his master, lest he curse thee, and thou be found guilty" (30:10). **SLANDER WARNING**—don't tattle on slave to owner (false accusation → slave curses you → you're guilty before God). **"There is a generation that curseth their father, and doth not bless their mother. There is a generation that are pure in their own eyes, and yet is not washed from their filthiness. There is a generation, O how lofty are their eyes! and their eyelids are lifted up. There is a generation, whose teeth are as swords, and their jaw teeth as knives, to devour the poor from off the earth, and the needy from among men"** (30:11-14). **FOUR GENERATIONS**—(1) Curse parents (Ex 20:12; 21:17; Matt 15:4). (2) Self-righteous but defiled (Luke 18:11, Pharisee; Rev 3:17, Laodicea, "don't know you're wretched"). (3) Haughty (6:17; Ps 18:27; Isa 2:11). (4) Devour poor (oppressors, Ps 14:4; Amos 8:4; Mic 3:2-3). Catalog of societal sins (possibly describing Agur's generation). "The horseleach hath two daughters, crying, Give, give. There are three things that are never satisfied, yea, four things say not, It is enough" (30:15). **NUMERICAL SAYING** (first in chapter). Leech (עֲלוּקָה, aluqah) has two daughters = two suckers ("Give! Give!"). Introduces 3...4 pattern. "The grave; and the barren womb; the earth that is not filled with water; and the fire that saith not, It is enough" (30:16). **FOUR INSATIABLE**: Sheol (שְׁאוֹל, always consuming), barren womb (עֹצֶר רֶחֶם, otzer rechem, Gen 30:1, Rachel; 1 Sam 1:5, Hannah), thirsty earth, fire (never says "Enough!"). "The eye that mocketh at his father, and despiseth to obey his mother, the ravens of the valley shall pick it out, and the young eagles shall eat it" (30:17). **PARENTAL DISHONOR** → gruesome fate (ravens/eagles eat eyes, Deut 21:18-21, rebellious son stoned; 28:26, corpses for birds).
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Generations*

#### 30:18-23 — There Be Three Things Too Wonderful for Me; The Earth Cannot Bear Four Things
**Lore:** **"There be three things which are too wonderful for me, yea, four which I know not: The way of an eagle in the air; the way of a serpent upon a rock; the way of a ship in the midst of the sea; and the way of a man with a maid"** (30:18-19). **NUMERICAL: 3...4 MYSTERIES**—"too wonderful" (נִפְלְאוּ, nifle'u, marvelous/incomprehensible). Four phenomena leaving no trace: (1) Eagle's flight (Job 39:27), (2) Snake on rock (trackless), (3) Ship in sea (no wake endures), (4) **Man with virgin** (דֶּרֶךְ גֶּבֶר בְּעַלְמָה, derekh gever be'almah, way of man with young woman, courtship mystery? OR sexual relations? Climactic position suggests special wonder). "Such is the way of an adulterous woman; she eateth, and wipeth her mouth, and saith, I have done no wickedness" (30:20). **ADULTERESS CONTRAST**—eats (sexual metaphor), wipes mouth (eliminates evidence), denies wickedness. Mystery of hidden sin vs. innocent love. "For three things the earth is disquieted, and for four which it cannot bear: For a servant when he reigneth; and a fool when he is filled with meat; For an odious woman when she is married; and an handmaid that is heir to her mistress" (30:21-23). **NUMERICAL: 4 SOCIAL DISRUPTIONS**—earth can't bear (לֹא־תוּכַל שְׂאֵת, lo-tukhal se'et): (1) Slave becomes king (19:10, inappropriate rule), (2) Fool glutted with food (prosperity wasted on fool), (3) Unloved woman married (שְׂנוּאָה, senu'ah, hated/odious, bitter wife?), (4) Maidservant inherits over mistress (reversal causes strife, Gen 16, Hagar vs. Sarah; 21:10). Social inversions destabilize order.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Mysteries*

#### 30:24-31 — There Be Four Things Little Upon Earth, But Exceeding Wise; A Lion Strong Among Beasts
**Lore:** **"There be four things which are little upon the earth, but they are exceeding wise: The ants are a people not strong, yet they prepare their meat in the summer; The conies are but a feeble folk, yet make they their houses in the rocks; The locusts have no king, yet go they forth all of them by bands; The spider taketh hold with her hands, and is in kings' palaces"** (30:24-28). **NUMERICAL: 4 SMALL & WISE**—"exceeding wise" (מְחֻכָּמִים חֲכָמִים, mechukkamim chakhamim, wisest of wise). (1) **Ants** (נְמָלִים, nemalim, 6:6-8) = small, weak, but prepare harvest (diligence). (2) **Rock badgers** (שְׁפַנִּים, shefannim, conies/hyraxes, feeble but dwell in rocks, security via location, Ps 104:18). (3) **Locusts** (אַרְבֶּה, arbeh) = no king yet march in formation (Joel 1:4; 2:25, organized instinct). (4) **Spider/lizard** (שְׂמָמִית, semamit, possibly gecko) = small but in royal palaces (adaptability, climbs walls). Small creatures exemplify wisdom principles. **"There be three things which go well, yea, four are comely in going: A lion which is strongest among beasts, and turneth not away for any; A greyhound; an he goat also; and a king, against whom there is no rising up"** (30:29-31). **NUMERICAL: 4 NOBLE IN GAIT**—"comely in going" (מֵיטִיבֵי לָכֶת, meitivei lakhet, good/excellent in walk). (1) **Lion** (לַיִשׁ, layish, strongest, turns not away for any, Job 4:10-11; 38:39; 41:34, "king over proud"), (2) **Greyhound** (זַרְזִיר מָתְנַיִם, zarzir motnayim, lit. "girt in loins," possibly rooster? War horse? Strutting bird?), (3) **He-goat** (תָּיִשׁ, tayish, leading flock), (4) **King** (מֶלֶךְ אַלְקוּם עִמּוֹ, melekh alqum immo, king with army/none can rise against). Confidence + authority displayed.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Wisdom*

#### 30:32-33 — If You Have Done Foolishly in Lifting Up Yourself; Churning of Milk Brings Forth Butter
**Lore:** "If thou hast done foolishly in lifting up thyself, or if thou hast thought evil, lay thine hand upon thy mouth" (30:32). **SELF-EXALTATION** → silence (put hand on mouth, Job 21:5; 40:4; Mic 7:16, shut mouth in humiliation). "Surely the churning of milk bringeth forth butter, and the wringing of the nose bringeth forth blood: so the forcing of wrath bringeth forth strife" (30:33). **TRIPLE CAUSATION**—(1) Churn milk (מִיץ חָלָב, mitz chalav) → butter (חֶמְאָה, chem'ah), (2) Twist nose (מִיץ אַף, mitz aph) → blood (דָּם, dam), (3) Force wrath (מִיץ אַפַּיִם, mitz appayim, press anger) → strife (רִיב, riv, conflict). Pressure produces inevitable results. Provoke anger → guaranteed quarrel.
*Verses: 2 | Theme: Restraint*

## PrMP 7: The Words of King Lemuel (31:1-31)

### Overview
**ROYAL ORACLE + ACROSTIC POEM**—Two sections: (1) **31:1-9**: Lemuel's mother's instruction (royal ethics), (2) **31:10-31**: Acrostic poem on virtuous woman (אֵשֶׁת חַיִל, eshet chayil, woman of valor). **King Lemuel** (לְמוּאֵל, Lemu'el, "belonging to God" OR "for God") = otherwise unknown king (possibly Solomon pseudonym? Non-Israelite like Agur?). **"Oracle his mother taught him"** (מַשָּׂא אֲשֶׁר־יִסְּרַתּוּ אִמּוֹ, massa asher-yissratto immo) = Queen Mother's wisdom (rare feminine voice, cf. Bathsheba, 1 Kgs 1-2). **Acrostic structure** (31:10-31): Each verse begins with successive Hebrew letter (א through ת, aleph through tav), mnemonic device for memorization + theological completeness (A to Z = comprehensive portrait). **Canonical position**: Proverbs conclusion, climactic portrait of ideal woman (after repeated warnings about "strange woman," book ends with righteous woman). **31 verses total**.

#### 31:1-9 — What, My Son? Give Not Your Strength unto Women; Open Your Mouth for the Dumb
**Lore:** **"The words of king Lemuel, the prophecy that his mother taught him"** (31:1). **SUPERSCRIPTION**—"King Lemuel" + "prophecy" (מַשָּׂא, massa', oracle/burden, like 30:1 Agur). "His mother taught" (יִסְּרַתּוּ, yissratto, disciplined/instructed him) = Queen Mother's counsel (royal women advised sons, 2 Sam 14, wise woman; 20:16-22; 2 Kgs 11, Athaliah; Jer 13:18, queen mother). **"What, my son? and what, the son of my womb? and what, the son of my vows?"** (31:2). **MATERNAL APPEAL**—triple "what?" (מַה, mah) = urgency. "Son of my womb" (בֶּן־בִּטְנִי, ben-bitni, intimate). "Son of my vows" (בֶּן־נְדָרַי, ben-nedarai, dedicated child, 1 Sam 1:11, Hannah's vow for Samuel). Precious, consecrated son. **"Give not thy strength unto women, nor thy ways to that which destroyeth kings"** (31:3). **SEXUAL WARNING**—"strength to women" (חַיִל לַנָּשִׁים, chayil lannashim, vigor to women, multiple wives/concubines drain resources, 1 Kgs 11:1-8, Solomon's 700 wives + 300 concubines turned heart). "Ways to that which destroys kings" (לַמְחוֹת מְלָכִין, lamchot melakhin, to wipers-out of kings, women who ruin monarchs). **"It is not for kings, O Lemuel, it is not for kings to drink wine; nor for princes strong drink: Lest they drink, and forget the law, and pervert the judgment of any of the afflicted"** (31:4-5). **ALCOHOL WARNING**—kings shouldn't drink wine (יַיִן, yayin) or strong drink (שֵׁכָר, shekhar, 20:1; 23:29-35). Reason: forget law (שָׁכַח חֹק, shakach choq) + pervert judgment (יְשַׁנֶּה דִּין, yeshanneh din, alter justice) of oppressed (בְּנֵי־עֹנִי, benei-oni, sons of affliction). Sobriety required for just rule. "Give strong drink unto him that is ready to perish, and wine unto those that be of heavy hearts. Let him drink, and forget his poverty, and remember his misery no more" (31:6-7). **ALCOHOL FOR SUFFERING**—give beer to dying (אוֹבֵד, oved, perishing), wine to bitter souls (מָרֵי נָפֶשׁ, marei nefesh, bitter of soul). They can forget poverty + misery (palliative use, not kings' indulgence). **"Open thy mouth for the dumb in the cause of all such as are appointed to destruction. Open thy mouth, judge righteously, and plead the cause of the poor and needy"** (31:8-9). **ADVOCACY IMPERATIVE**—"open mouth for mute" (פְּתַח־פִּיךָ לְאִלֵּם, petach-pikha le'illem, speak for voiceless, Ps 82:3-4). "Cause of all appointed to destruction" (בְּנֵי חֲלוֹף, benei chalof, sons of passing/helpless). "Judge righteously" (שְׁפָט־צֶדֶק, shefat-tzedeq), "plead cause of poor and needy" (עָנִי וְאֶבְיוֹן, ani ve'evyon, afflicted + destitute). Royal duty = justice for powerless (Ps 72:1-4, 12-14; Jer 22:15-16, Josiah defended poor).
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Royalty*

#### 31:10-31 — Who Can Find a Virtuous Woman? Her Price Is Far Above Rubies (ACROSTIC)
**Lore:** **"Who can find a virtuous woman? for her price is far above rubies"** (31:10). **ACROSTIC OPENING (א aleph)**—"virtuous woman" (אֵשֶׁת־חַיִל, eshet-chayil, woman of valor/strength, Ruth 3:11, Ruth; 12:4, crown to husband). "Who can find?" (מִי יִמְצָא, mi yimtza, rhetorical, implies rarity, 20:6). "Price far above rubies" (מִפְּנִינִים רָחֹק מִכְרָהּ, mippeninim rachoq mikhrah, beyond pearls her value, 3:15; 8:11). **א-ת STRUCTURE**: Each verse begins with next Hebrew letter (pedagogical + theological device, A to Z completeness). This section receives extended summary due to length + importance:

**Key Virtues Displayed**:
- **Trustworthy** (v. 11, husband's heart trusts)
- **Beneficial** (v. 12, does good all her days)
- **Industrious** (v. 13-19, works wool, flax; rises early; hands never idle)
- **Generous** (v. 20, extends hands to poor + needy)
- **Prepared** (v. 21, household clothed in scarlet, no fear of snow)
- **Elegant** (v. 22, makes coverings, clothed in fine linen + purple)
- **Honorable** (v. 23, husband known in gates)
- **Entrepreneurial** (v. 24, makes linen garments + sells them)
- **Dignified** (v. 25, clothed with strength + honor)
- **Wise** (v. 26, opens mouth with wisdom, tongue has kindness)
- **Vigilant** (v. 27, watches household, eats not bread of idleness)
- **Blessed** (v. 28-29, children + husband praise her)
- **God-fearing** (v. 30, fears LORD = source of praise, not charm/beauty)
- **Rewarded** (v. 31, fruit of hands praises her in gates)

**Theological Climax**: **"Favour is deceitful, and beauty is vain: but a woman that feareth the LORD, she shall be praised"** (31:30). **BOOK'S CONCLUSION**—external qualities (חֵן, chen, charm + יֹפִי, yofi, beauty) = deceptive/fleeting (שֶׁקֶר, sheqer + הֶבֶל, hevel, vapor). **Fear of LORD** (יִרְאַת־יְהוָה, yirat-YHWH) = true foundation (1:7, book's motto). Woman who fears LORD = worthy of praise (inclusio with 1:7 + 31:30, fear of LORD bookends entire book). "Give her of the fruit of her hands; and let her own works praise her in the gates" (31:31). **FINAL VERSE**—reward her labor, let deeds testify publicly (in gates = city square, judicial/commercial center). Righteous woman's works = self-evident testimony (vs. loud self-promotion).

**Canonical Function**: After 30 chapters warning against "strange woman" (2:16; 5:3-20; 6:24-35; 7:5-27; 9:13-18; 22:14; 23:27-28), book concludes with portrait of RIGHTEOUS woman—eshet chayil as antithesis to zonah (prostitute) + nokhriyyah (foreign woman). Proverbs' final word: True wisdom embodied in woman who fears LORD. Full-circle from 1:7 to 31:30.

*Verses: 22 | Theme: Virtue*
