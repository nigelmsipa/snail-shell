# Judges 1-21: The Cycle of Apostasy

## Overview
Judges covers ~350 years of Israel's darkest period between Joshua and the monarchy. The book reveals the consequences of incomplete obedience (Joshua's generation failed to drive out Canaanites). Central theme: **"Everyone did what was right in his own eyes"** (17:6; 21:25). Structure: Introduction (1-2), Cycles of Judges (3-16), Appendix showing moral collapse (17-21). The cycle repeats: Israel sins → God sends oppressor → Israel cries out → God raises judge → temporary peace → repeat. Progressive decline from Othniel to Samson to civil war.

## Part I: Foundation of Failure (Chapters 1-2)

### Chapter 1: Incomplete Conquest

#### 1:1-20 — Judah's Partial Success
**Lore:** "After the death of Joshua, the people of Israel inquired of the LORD, 'Who shall go up first for us against the Canaanites, to fight against them?'" (1:1). Joshua dead, leadership vacuum. "The LORD said, 'Judah shall go up; behold, I have given the land into his hand'" (1:2). Judah leads. "And Judah went with Simeon his brother, and they defeated the Canaanites and the Perizzites at Bezek, and they defeated 10,000 of them. They found Adoni-bezek at Bezek and fought against him and defeated the Canaanites and the Perizzites. Adoni-bezek fled, but they pursued him and caught him and cut off his thumbs and his big toes" (1:4-6). **Poetic justice:** "And Adoni-bezek said, 'Seventy kings with their thumbs and their big toes cut off used to pick up scraps under my table. As I have done, so God has repaid me'" (1:7). Reaps what he sowed. Judah captures Jerusalem, Hebron (Caleb drives out three sons of Anak), Debir (1:8-15). "But the people of Benjamin did not drive out the Jebusites who lived in Jerusalem, so the Jebusites have lived with the people of Benjamin in Jerusalem to this day" (1:21). **First failure noted**—Jerusalem unconquered until David.
*Verses: 20 | Theme: Partial obedience*

#### 1:21-36 — Other Tribes Compromise
**Lore:** Benjamin fails at Jerusalem (1:21). "Manasseh did not drive out the inhabitants of Beth-shean and its villages, or Taanach and its villages, or the inhabitants of Dor and its villages, or the inhabitants of Ibleam and its villages, or the inhabitants of Megiddo and its villages, for the Canaanites persisted in dwelling in that land. When Israel grew strong, they put the Canaanites to forced labor, but did not drive them out completely" (1:27-28). **Pattern: forced labor instead of expulsion**. Ephraim, Zebulun, Asher, Naphtali all fail similarly (1:29-33). "The Amorites pressed the people of Dan back into the hill country, for they did not allow them to come down to the plain" (1:34). Dan driven from territory (later migrate north, ch 18). "But the hand of the house of Joseph rested heavily on them, and they became subject to forced labor" (1:35). Compromise everywhere—Joshua 23:12-13 warning fulfilled.
*Verses: 16 | Theme: Compromise brings trouble*

### Chapter 2: The Cycle Explained

#### 2:1-5 — Angel's Rebuke at Bochim
**Lore:** "Now the angel of the LORD went up from Gilgal to Bochim. And he said, 'I brought you up from Egypt and brought you into the land that I swore to give to your fathers. I said, "I will never break my covenant with you, and you shall make no covenant with the inhabitants of this land; you shall break down their altars." But you have not obeyed my voice. What is this you have done?'" (2:1-2). **Theophany**—angel of LORD rebukes. "So now I say, I will not drive them out before you, but they shall become thorns in your sides, and their gods shall be a snare to you" (2:3). Consequence: enemies remain as thorns (Num 33:55). "As soon as the angel of the LORD spoke these words to all the people of Israel, the people lifted up their voices and wept. And they called the name of that place Bochim (weepers). And they sacrificed there to the LORD" (2:4-5). Tears but no lasting repentance.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Covenant broken*

#### 2:6-15 — Joshua's Generation Passes
**Lore:** "When Joshua dismissed the people, the people of Israel went each to his inheritance to take possession of the land. And the people served the LORD all the days of Joshua, and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua, who had seen all the great work that the LORD had done for Israel" (2:6-7). Faithful generation. "And Joshua the son of Nun, the servant of the LORD, died at the age of 110 years. And they buried him within the boundaries of his inheritance in Timnath-heres, in the hill country of Ephraim, north of the mountain of Gaash" (2:8-9). Joshua's death. "And all that generation also were gathered to their fathers. And there arose another generation after them who did not know the LORD or the work that he had done for Israel" (2:10). **Catastrophic transition**—new generation doesn't know God! "And the people of Israel did what was evil in the sight of the LORD and served the Baals. And they abandoned the LORD, the God of their fathers, who had brought them out of the land of Egypt. They went after other gods, from among the gods of the peoples who were around them, and bowed down to them. And they provoked the LORD to anger" (2:11-12). Baal worship begins. "They abandoned the LORD and served the Baals and the Ashtaroth. So the anger of the LORD was kindled against Israel, and he gave them over to plunderers, who plundered them. And he sold them into the hand of their surrounding enemies, so that they could no longer withstand their enemies" (2:13-14). Divine judgment: military defeat. "Whenever they marched out, the hand of the LORD was against them for harm, as the LORD had warned, and as the LORD had sworn to them. And they were in terrible distress" (2:15). Deuteronomy 28 curses activated.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: New generation apostatizes*

#### 2:16-23 — The Judges Pattern
**Lore:** "Then the LORD raised up judges, who saved them out of the hand of those who plundered them. Yet they did not listen to their judges, for they whored after other gods and bowed down to them. They soon turned aside from the way in which their fathers had walked, who had obeyed the commandments of the LORD, and they did not do so" (2:16-17). Judges = temporary deliverers. "Whenever the LORD raised up judges for them, the LORD was with the judge, and he saved them from the hand of their enemies all the days of the judge. For the LORD was moved to pity by their groaning because of those who afflicted and oppressed them" (2:18). Divine compassion. "But whenever the judge died, they turned back and were more corrupt than their fathers, going after other gods, serving them and bowing down to them. They did not drop any of their practices or their stubborn ways" (2:19). Worse after each judge! "So the anger of the LORD was kindled against Israel, and he said, 'Because this people have transgressed my covenant that I commanded their fathers and have not obeyed my voice, I will no longer drive out before them any of the nations that Joshua left when he died, in order to test Israel by them, whether they will take care to walk in the way of the LORD as their fathers did, or not'" (2:20-22). Remaining nations = test of faithfulness. "So the LORD left those nations, not driving them out quickly, and he did not give them into the hand of Joshua" (2:23). Stage set for judges cycles.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: The cycle explained*

## Part II: Early Judges (Chapters 3-5)

### Chapter 3: Othniel, Ehud, Shamgar

#### 3:1-6 — Nations Left to Test Israel
**Lore:** "Now these are the nations that the LORD left, to test Israel by them" (3:1). Five Philistine lords, all Canaanites, Sidonians, Hivites (3:3). "They were for the testing of Israel, to know whether Israel would obey the commandments of the LORD, which he commanded their fathers by the hand of Moses" (3:4). Purpose: test obedience. "So the people of Israel lived among the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites. And their daughters they took to themselves for wives, and their own daughters they gave to their sons, and they served their gods" (3:5-6). Intermarriage forbidden (Deut 7:3-4), yet they do it—spiritual adultery follows.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Test of faithfulness*

#### 3:7-11 — Othniel: First Judge
**Lore:** "And the people of Israel did what was evil in the sight of the LORD. They forgot the LORD their God and served the Baals and the Asheroth" (3:7). **Cycle begins**. "Therefore the anger of the LORD was kindled against Israel, and he sold them into the hand of Cushan-rishathaim king of Mesopotamia. And the people of Israel served Cushan-rishathaim eight years" (3:8). First oppression. "But when the people of Israel cried out to the LORD, the LORD raised up a deliverer for the people of Israel, who saved them, Othniel the son of Kenaz, Caleb's younger brother" (3:9). Othniel = Caleb's nephew (Josh 15:17). "The Spirit of the LORD was upon him, and he judged Israel. He went out to war, and the LORD gave Cushan-rishathaim king of Mesopotamia into his hand. And his hand prevailed over Cushan-rishathaim" (3:10). Spirit-empowered victory. "So the land had rest forty years. Then Othniel the son of Kenaz died" (3:11). Peace during judge's life—pattern established.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Spirit-empowered deliverance*

#### 3:12-30 — Ehud: Left-Handed Deliverer
**Lore:** "And the people of Israel again did what was evil in the sight of the LORD, and the LORD strengthened Eglon the king of Moab against Israel, because they had done what was evil in the sight of the LORD" (3:12). God empowers enemy as judgment. "He gathered to himself the Ammonites and the Amalekites, and went and defeated Israel. And they took possession of the city of palms" (3:13). Jericho occupied. "And the people of Israel served Eglon the king of Moab eighteen years" (3:14). "Then the people of Israel cried out to the LORD, and the LORD raised up for them a deliverer, Ehud, the son of Gera, the Benjaminite, a left-handed man" (3:15). Left-handed (אִטֵּר, itter) = "bound/restricted in right hand"—possible disability becomes advantage. "The people of Israel sent tribute by him to Eglon the king of Moab. And Ehud made for himself a sword with two edges, a cubit in length, and he bound it on his right thigh under his clothes" (3:16-17). Hidden weapon. "And he presented the tribute to Eglon king of Moab. Now Eglon was a very fat man" (3:17). Physical detail foreshadows. Ehud returns, says "I have a secret message for you, O king" (3:19). Gets private audience. "And Ehud reached with his left hand, took the sword from his right thigh, and thrust it into his belly. And the hilt also went in after the blade, and the fat closed over the blade, for he did not pull the sword out of his belly; and the dung came out" (3:21-22). Graphic assassination! Ehud escapes while servants hesitate (3:23-25). "When he arrived, he sounded the trumpet in the hill country of Ephraim. Then the people of Israel went down with him from the hill country, and he was their leader. And he said to them, 'Follow after me, for the LORD has given your enemies the Moabites into your hand.' So they went down after him and seized the fords of the Jordan against the Moabites and did not allow anyone to pass over. And they killed at that time about 10,000 of the Moabites, all strong, able-bodied men; not a man escaped. So Moab was subdued that day under the hand of Israel. And the land had rest for eighty years" (3:27-30). Longest peace period.
*Verses: 19 | Theme: Unlikely hero*

#### 3:31 — Shamgar: Oxgoad Warrior
**Lore:** "After him was Shamgar the son of Anath, who killed 600 of the Philistines with an oxgoad, and he also saved Israel" (3:31). One verse judge! Oxgoad = farming implement. God uses weak to confound strong (1 Cor 1:27).
*Verses: 1 | Theme: Weak defeats mighty*

### Chapter 4: Deborah and Barak

#### 4:1-11 — Deborah Summons Barak
**Lore:** "And the people of Israel again did what was evil in the sight of the LORD after Ehud died. And the LORD sold them into the hand of Jabin king of Canaan, who reigned in Hazor. The commander of his army was Sisera, who lived in Harosheth-hagoyim. Then the people of Israel cried out to the LORD for help, for he had 900 chariots of iron and he oppressed the people of Israel cruelly for twenty years" (4:1-3). Iron chariots = superior military technology. "Now Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth, was judging Israel at that time. She used to sit under the palm of Deborah between Ramah and Bethel in the hill country of Ephraim, and the people of Israel came up to her for judgment" (4:4-5). **Only female judge!** Prophet and judge. "She sent and summoned Barak the son of Abinoam from Kedesh-naphtali and said to him, 'Has not the LORD, the God of Israel, commanded you, "Go, gather your men at Mount Tabor, taking 10,000 from the people of Naphtali and the people of Zebulun. And I will draw out Sisera, the general of Jabin's army, to meet you by the river Kishon with his chariots and his troops, and I will give him into your hand"?'" (4:6-7). Divine battle plan. "Barak said to her, 'If you will go with me, I will go, but if you will not go with me, I will not go.' And she said, 'I will surely go with you. Nevertheless, the road on which you are going will not lead to your glory, for the LORD will sell Sisera into the hand of a woman.' Then Deborah arose and went with Barak to Kedesh" (4:8-9). Barak's hesitation = loses honor; woman will get victory.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Woman leads Israel*

#### 4:12-24 — Jael Kills Sisera
**Lore:** "When Sisera was told that Barak the son of Abinoam had gone up to Mount Tabor, Sisera called out all his chariots, 900 chariots of iron, and all the men who were with him, from Harosheth-hagoyim to the river Kishon" (4:12-13). "And Deborah said to Barak, 'Up! For this is the day in which the LORD has given Sisera into your hand. Does not the LORD go out before you?' So Barak went down from Mount Tabor with 10,000 men following him. And the LORD routed Sisera and all his chariots and all his army before Barak by the edge of the sword. And Sisera got down from his chariot and fled away on foot" (4:14-15). God's panic. "But Sisera fled away on foot to the tent of Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite, for there was peace between Jabin the king of Hazor and the house of Heber the Kenite" (4:17). Sisera seeks refuge. "And Jael came out to meet Sisera and said to him, 'Turn aside, my lord; turn aside to me; do not be afraid.' So he turned aside to her into the tent, and she covered him with a rug" (4:18). False hospitality. "And he said to her, 'Please give me a little water to drink, for I am thirsty.' So she opened a skin of milk and gave him a drink and covered him" (4:19). Milk = sedative? "And he said to her, 'Stand at the opening of the tent, and if any man comes and asks you, "Is anyone here?" say, "No."' But Jael the wife of Heber took a tent peg, and took a hammer in her hand. Then she went softly to him and drove the peg into his temple until it went down into the ground while he was lying fast asleep from weariness. So he died" (4:20-21). **Shocking execution!** Tent peg through temple. "And behold, as Barak was pursuing Sisera, Jael went out to meet him and said to him, 'Come, and I will show you the man whom you are seeking.' So he went in to her tent, and there lay Sisera dead, with the tent peg in his temple" (4:22). Deborah's prophecy fulfilled—woman gets glory. "So on that day God subdued Jabin the king of Canaan before the people of Israel. And the hand of the people of Israel pressed harder and harder against Jabin the king of Canaan, until they destroyed Jabin king of Canaan" (4:23-24).
*Verses: 13 | Theme: God uses unlikely*

### Chapter 5: The Song of Deborah

#### 5:1-31 — Victory Song
**Lore:** "Then sang Deborah and Barak the son of Abinoam on that day" (5:1). Ancient Hebrew poetry. "Hear, O kings; give ear, O princes; to the LORD I will sing; I will make melody to the LORD, the God of Israel" (5:3). "LORD, when you went out from Seir, when you marched from the region of Edom, the earth trembled and the heavens dropped, yes, the clouds dropped water. The mountains quaked before the LORD, even Sinai before the LORD, the God of Israel" (5:4-5). Theophanic imagery—God as warrior. "In the days of Shamgar, son of Anath, in the days of Jael, the highways were abandoned, and travelers kept to the byways. The villagers ceased in Israel; they ceased to be until I arose; I, Deborah, arose as a mother in Israel" (5:6-7). Deborah = mother of Israel. "When new gods were chosen, then war was in the gates. Was shield or spear to be seen among forty thousand in Israel?" (5:8). Disarmed, defenseless. "Awake, awake, Deborah! Awake, awake, break out in a song! Arise, Barak, lead away your captives, O son of Abinoam" (5:12). "From the heavens the stars fought, from their courses they fought against Sisera. The torrent Kishon swept them away, the ancient torrent, the torrent Kishon. March on, my soul, with might!" (5:20-21). **Cosmic warfare**—stars fight, river sweeps enemy. "Most blessed of women be Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite, of tent-dwelling women most blessed. He asked for water and she gave him milk; she brought him curds in a noble's bowl. She sent her hand to the tent peg and her right hand to the workmen's mallet; she struck Sisera; she crushed his head; she shattered and pierced his temple" (5:24-26). Jael celebrated. "So may all your enemies perish, O LORD! But your friends be like the sun as he rises in his might.' And the land had rest for forty years" (5:31). Typical rest period.
*Verses: 31 | Theme: Victory celebrated*
