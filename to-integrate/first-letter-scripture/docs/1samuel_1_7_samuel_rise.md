# 1 Samuel 1-7: The Rise of Samuel

## Overview
1 Samuel bridges Judges' chaos to monarchy. Corrupt priesthood (Eli's sons) creates vacuum. God raises Samuel through Hannah's vow—"consecrated offering." Ark captured (Ichabod = "glory departed"), proves unconquerable among Philistines. Samuel leads revival at Mizpah, defeats Philistines, sets up Ebenezer ("stone of help"). Central theme: God bypasses corrupt establishment to raise up faithful. Key terms: שְׁמוּאֵל (Shemuel = "heard by God"), אִי־כָבוֹד (Ichabod = "no glory"), אֶבֶן הָעֶזֶר (Ebenezer = "stone of help").

---

## Chapter 1: Hannah's Faithful Offering

#### 1:1-8 — Hannah's Barrenness and Peninnah's Provocation
**Lore:** "There was a certain man of Ramathaim-zophim of the hill country of Ephraim whose name was Elkanah" (1:1). Ramathaim = "two heights," later Ramah. "He had two wives. The name of the one was Hannah, and the name of the other, Peninnah. And Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children" (1:2). **Polygamy**—always causes strife. **Hannah** (חַנָּה) = "grace." Barrenness = profound shame. "This man used to go up year by year from his city to worship and to sacrifice to the LORD of hosts at Shiloh, where the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were priests of the LORD" (1:3). Annual pilgrimage to Shiloh (tabernacle location). Eli's corrupt sons foreshadowed. "And her rival used to provoke her grievously to irritate her, because the LORD had closed her womb. So it went on year by year" (1:6-7). **Peninnah** = Satan's agent, tormenting faithful woman. Annual harassment at worship. Hannah weeps, fasts. Doesn't retaliate. Echoes Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel—God uses barrenness → miraculous birth to set apart special children (Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Samson, Samuel, John Baptist).
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Affliction and provocation*

#### 1:9-18 — Hannah's Vow and Eli's Blessing
**Lore:** "She was deeply distressed and prayed to the LORD and wept bitterly. And she vowed a vow and said, 'O LORD of hosts, if you will indeed look on the affliction of your servant and remember me and not forget your servant, but will give to your servant a son, then I will give him to the LORD all the days of his life, and no razor shall come upon his head'" (1:10-11). **NAZARITE VOW**—Numbers 6: no wine, no unclean food, no haircut. "Great self-denial"—promises firstborn back to God's exclusive service. Lifelong dedication like Samson but voluntary. "As she continued praying before the LORD, Eli the priest was sitting on the seat beside the doorpost of the temple of the LORD. She was speaking in her heart; only her lips moved, and her voice was not heard. Therefore Eli thought she was drunk" (1:12-13). **Silent prayer**—unusual in ancient worship. Eli's **spiritual dullness**—can't discern genuine prayer from drunkenness! High priest should have sensitivity. "But Hannah answered, 'No, my lord, I am a woman troubled in spirit. I have drunk neither wine nor strong drink, but I have been pouring out my soul before the LORD'" (1:15). "Pouring out my soul" (שָׁפַךְ, shaphak) = complete emotional emptying. Respectful but firm defense. "Then Eli answered, 'Go in peace, and the God of Israel grant your petition that you have made to him.'" (1:17). Priestly blessing. Hannah believes before seeing—faith demonstrated: "her face was no longer sad" (1:18). Type of justification by faith.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Vow of consecration*

#### 1:19-28 — Samuel's Birth and Presentation
**Lore:** "And the LORD remembered her. And in due time Hannah conceived and bore a son, and she called his name Samuel, for she said, 'I have asked for him from the LORD'" (1:19-20). **SAMUEL** (שְׁמוּאֵל, Shemuel) = "heard by God" or "asked of God." "LORD remembered" (זָכַר, zakar) = divine attention, covenant faithfulness (Gen 8:1 Noah, 30:22 Rachel). "But Hannah did not go up, for she said to her husband, 'As soon as the child is weaned, I will bring him'" (1:22). ~3 years nursing period (ancient Near East custom). "And when she had weaned him, she took him up with her, along with a three-year-old bull, an ephah of flour, and a skin of wine, and she brought him to the house of the LORD at Shiloh. And the child was young" (1:24). Generous offerings. "The child was young" (נַעַר, na'ar) emphatic—very young, ~3 years. "And she said, 'Oh, my lord! As you live, my lord, I am the woman who was standing here in your presence, praying to the LORD. For this child I prayed, and the LORD has granted me my petition that I made to him. Therefore I have lent him to the LORD. As long as he lives, he is lent to the LORD'" (1:26-28). **Fulfills vow**—ultimate faith and self-denial. "I have lent" (הִשְׁאִלְתִּיהוּ) = wordplay on שָׁאַל (sha'al = ask). Asked from LORD, returns to LORD. Hannah gives up raising son for God's purposes. "Consecrated offering" God will use to reform Israel. Pattern: faithful prayers of righteous women (Hannah, Mary) bring deliverers (Samuel, Jesus).
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Faithful fulfillment*

## Chapter 2: Contrasting Songs and Sons

#### 2:1-11 — Hannah's Prophetic Song
**Lore:** "And Hannah prayed and said, 'My heart exults in the LORD; my horn is exalted in the LORD. My mouth derides my enemies, because I rejoice in your salvation'" (2:1). **HANNAH'S SONG**—prophetic praise, theological depth beyond personal circumstance. "Horn" (קֶרֶן, qeren) = strength/power. "Salvation" (יְשׁוּעָה, yeshuah) shares root with Joshua/Jesus. "There is none holy like the LORD: for there is none besides you; there is no rock like our God" (2:2). Monotheistic declaration. "The bows of the mighty are broken, but the feeble bind on strength... The barren has borne seven, but she who has many children is forlorn" (2:4-5). **REVERSAL THEME**—God inverts human expectations. "Seven" = perfect number (Hannah had 6 total). Principle: God exalts humble, humbles proud (Luke 1:52-53, Mary's Magnificat echoes). "The LORD kills and brings to life; he brings down to Sheol and raises up. The LORD makes poor and makes rich; he brings low and he exalts. He raises up the poor from the dust; he lifts the needy from the ash heap to make them sit with princes" (2:6-8). Divine sovereignty over life/death, wealth/poverty. Foreshadows resurrection. "He will guard the feet of his faithful ones, but the wicked shall be cut off in darkness... The LORD will judge the ends of the earth; he will give strength to his king and exalt the horn of his anointed" (2:9-10). **FIRST "ANOINTED"** (מָשִׁיחַ, mashiach = messiah) in Prophets! "His king"—prophetic, no king yet. Points to Davidic monarchy and ultimately Messiah. Hannah's personal song becomes eschatological prophecy. Sets book's theme: transition to monarchy under God's anointed.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: God's reversal principle*

#### 2:12-26 — Corrupt Sons vs. Faithful Samuel
**Lore:** "Now the sons of Eli were worthless men. They did not know the LORD" (2:12). "Worthless" (בְּלִיַּעַל, beliya'al = sons of Belial). "Did not know the LORD"—relational/covenantal rejection. Priests who don't know God = ultimate hypocrisy. "The custom of the priests with the people was that when any man offered sacrifice, the priest's servant would come, while the meat was boiling, with a three-pronged fork" (2:13). Took more than allowed (Lev 7:28-36 specifies breast and right thigh). Random forking = greed, violated law. "Moreover, before the fat was burned, the priest's servant would come and say to the man who was sacrificing, 'Give meat for the priest to roast'" (2:15). **WORSE SIN**—demanded raw meat before fat burned to God. Lev 3:16, 7:23-25 required fat burned first as God's portion. Putting appetite before God's due = sacrilege. "By force" if refused. "Thus the sin of the young men was very great in the sight of the LORD, for the men treated the offering of the LORD with contempt" (2:17). "Contempt" (נָאַץ, na'ats) = despise, blaspheme. "Samuel was ministering before the LORD, a boy clothed with a linen ephod. And his mother used to make for him a little robe" (2:18-19). **CONTRAST**—Samuel faithful, growing spiritually. "Little robe" (מְעִיל קָטֹן) = priestly garment, Hannah's continued care. "Indeed the LORD visited Hannah, and she conceived and bore three sons and two daughters" (2:21). Reward: 5 more children (total 6). "Now Eli was very old, and he kept hearing all that his sons were doing to all Israel, and how they lay with the women who were serving at the entrance to the tent of meeting. And he said to them, 'Why do you do such things?'" (2:22-23). **ELI'S CRIMINAL NEGLECT**—hears reports, weak rebuke, no action. Sexual sin at tabernacle (like pagan temple prostitution). "But they would not listen to the voice of their father, for it was the will of the LORD to put them to death" (2:25). Hardened hearts. Divine judgment coming. "Now the young man Samuel continued to grow both in stature and in favor with the LORD and also with man" (2:26). **Echoes Luke 2:52** (Jesus). Samuel = type of Christ, faithful in corrupt system.
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Corruption vs. faithfulness*

#### 2:27-36 — Prophecy Against Eli's House
**Lore:** "And there came a man of God to Eli and said to him, 'Thus says the LORD, "Did I indeed reveal myself to the house of your father when they were in Egypt... And I chose him out of all the tribes of Israel to be my priest"'" (2:27-28). Unnamed prophet recalls priestly privilege given to Aaron's line. "Why then do you scorn my sacrifices and my offerings that I commanded for my dwelling, and honor your sons above me by fattening yourselves on the choicest parts?" (2:29). **ACCUSATION**—Eli honors sons above God! "Scorn" (בָּעַט, ba'at) = kick at, contempt. Eli complicit through negligence. "Therefore the LORD declares: 'I promised that your house and the house of your father should go in and out before me forever,' but now the LORD declares: 'Far be it from me, for those who honor me I will honor, and those who despise me shall be lightly esteemed'" (2:30). **CONDITIONAL COVENANT**—priesthood promised "forever" (לְעוֹלָם) but conditional on faithfulness. Key axiom: "those who honor me I will honor, those who despise me shall be lightly esteemed." "Behold, the days are coming when I will cut off your strength and the strength of your father's house, so that there will not be an old man in your house" (2:31). No old age in Eli's line. "And this that shall come upon your two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, shall be the sign to you: both of them shall die on the same day" (2:34). **VERIFIABLE SIGN**—specific prophecy. Fulfilled ch 4:11. "And I will raise up for myself a faithful priest, who shall do according to what is in my heart and in my mind. And I will build him a sure house, and he shall go in and out before my anointed forever" (2:35). Points to Samuel immediately, ultimately Christ (Heb 4:14-16). "Sure house" (בַּיִת נֶאֱמָן) = established line. Establishes crisis: corrupt priesthood judged, God raises faithful child.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Judgment on corruption*

## Chapter 3: Samuel's Call

#### 3:1-10 — The LORD Calls Samuel
**Lore:** "Now the young man Samuel was ministering to the LORD under Eli. And the word of the LORD was rare in those days; there was no frequent vision" (3:1). **Spiritual darkness**—"word of the LORD was rare" (יָקָר, yaqar = precious/scarce). Prophetic silence due to corruption. "Now Eli's eyes had begun to grow dim so that he could not see" (3:2). Physical blindness = spiritual blindness. "Samuel was lying down in the temple of the LORD, where the ark of God was" (3:3). Privileged position near ark. "Then the LORD called Samuel, and he said, 'Here I am!' and ran to Eli and said, 'Here I am, for you called me.' But he said, 'I did not call; lie down again'" (3:4-5). **FIRST CALL**—doesn't recognize God's voice. "And the LORD called again, 'Samuel!'" (3:6). **SECOND CALL**—still doesn't recognize. "Now Samuel did not yet know the LORD, and the word of the LORD had not yet been revealed to him" (3:7). "Did not yet know the LORD"—no personal revelation yet, only external service. "And the LORD called Samuel again the third time... Then Eli perceived that the LORD was calling the young man" (3:8). **THIRD CALL**—Eli finally discerns! Took three times to recognize. "Therefore Eli said to Samuel, 'Go, lie down, and if he calls you, you shall say, "Speak, LORD, for your servant hears"'" (3:9). Teaches proper response. Irony: corrupt priest instructs faithful child. "And the LORD came and stood, and called as at other times, 'Samuel! Samuel!' And Samuel said, 'Speak, for your servant hears'" (3:10). **FOURTH CALL**—double name (Abraham, Moses, Saul pattern). "LORD came and stood"—personal presence. Ready to receive.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Learning God's voice*

#### 3:11-21 — Judgment Confirmed, Prophet Established
**Lore:** "Then the LORD said to Samuel, 'Behold, I am about to do a thing in Israel at which the two ears of everyone who hears it will tingle'" (3:11). "Ears will tingle" (צָלַל, tsalal) = ring—shocking news (used of Jerusalem's destruction, 2 Kings 21:12). "On that day I will fulfill against Eli all that I have spoken concerning his house, from beginning to end. And I declare to him that I am about to punish his house forever, for the iniquity that he knew, because his sons were blaspheming God, and he did not restrain them. Therefore I swear to the house of Eli that the iniquity of Eli's house shall not be atoned for by sacrifice or offering forever" (3:12-14). **CONFIRMS CHAPTER 2 PROPHECY**—Eli's house doomed. "Iniquity that he knew"—Eli culpable for knowing, not acting. "Did not restrain" (כָּהָה, kahah) = rebuke/restrain—weak rebuke insufficient. "Shall not be atoned for by sacrifice or offering"—no ritual remedy for deliberate, unrepented sin. Only Christ's blood can atone (Heb 10:4). "Samuel lay until morning; then he opened the doors of the house of the LORD. And Samuel was afraid to tell the vision to Eli" (3:15). Young boy must deliver judgment to mentor. "But Eli called Samuel and said, 'Samuel, my son.' And Eli said, 'What was it that he told you? Do not hide it from me. May God do so to you and more also if you hide anything from me'" (3:17). Oath formula—invokes divine punishment if disobedient. "So Samuel told him everything and hid nothing from him. And he said, 'It is the LORD. Let him do what seems good to him'" (3:18). **SUBMISSION**—Eli accepts judgment. "It is the LORD" = acknowledges divine authority. Yet too late—no change toward sons. "And Samuel grew, and the LORD was with him and let none of his words fall to the ground. And all Israel from Dan to Beersheba knew that Samuel was established as a prophet of the LORD" (3:19-20). **PROPHET CONFIRMED**—"none of his words fall" = all prophecies fulfilled (Deut 18:22 test). "Dan to Beersheba" = entire nation. "Established" (נֶאֱמָן, ne'eman) = trustworthy, faithful—fulfills 2:35. "And the LORD appeared again at Shiloh, for the LORD revealed himself to Samuel at Shiloh by the word of the LORD" (3:21). Repeated revelations—God's word no longer rare. Samuel replaces corrupt priesthood as channel. God raises faithful from youth when elders fail.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Prophet established*

## Chapters 4-7: The Ark and National Revival

#### 4:1-11 — Presumptuous Use of the Ark
**Lore:** "Now Israel went out to battle against the Philistines. They encamped at Ebenezer, and the Philistines encamped at Aphek" (4:1). Recurring Philistine threat. **Ebenezer** (אֶבֶן הָעֶזֶר) = "stone of help" but won't help here (ironic, renamed ch 7). "The Philistines drew up in line against Israel, and when the battle spread, Israel was defeated before the Philistines, who killed about four thousand men" (4:2). Initial defeat—4,000 casualties. "And when the people came to the camp, the elders of Israel said, 'Why has the LORD defeated us today before the Philistines? Let us bring the ark of the covenant of the LORD here from Shiloh, that it may come among us and save us'" (4:3). **FATAL ERROR**—treat ark as magic talisman! Right question ("Why has LORD defeated us?") but wrong answer. Don't address sin, just want divine power. No repentance. Misunderstand covenant: blessing conditional on obedience. "So the people sent to Shiloh and brought from there the ark of the covenant of the LORD of hosts, who is enthroned on the cherubim. And the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were there with the ark" (4:4). **Corrupt priests handle holy ark**—presumption! "As soon as the ark of the covenant of the LORD came into the camp, all Israel gave a mighty shout" (4:5). False confidence. "When the Philistines heard the noise... they said, 'A god has come into the camp... These are the gods who struck the Egyptians with every sort of plague'" (4:6-8). Philistines remember Exodus! Fear ark's reputation. "Take courage, and be men, O Philistines... Be men and fight'" (4:9). Rally for desperate battle. "So the Philistines fought, and Israel was defeated, and they fled, every man to his home. And there was a very great slaughter, for thirty thousand foot soldiers of Israel fell. And the ark of God was captured, and the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, died" (4:10-11). **CATASTROPHE**—30,000 dead (vs. 4,000 first battle), ark captured, Eli's sons dead! Fulfills 2:34 prophecy. Ark's presence couldn't save because hearts weren't right. Mistook ark's sacredness for automatic protection—ignored that sacredness tied to obedience to law within it!
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Presumption brings disaster*

#### 4:12-22 — Ichabod: The Glory Departed
**Lore:** "A man of Benjamin ran from the battle line and came to Shiloh the same day, with his clothes torn and with dirt on his head" (4:12). Mourning signs. "When he arrived, Eli was sitting on his seat by the road watching, for his heart trembled for the ark of God" (4:13). Eli's concern—knows ark's importance. "When the man came into the city and told the news, all the city cried out. When Eli heard the sound of the outcry, he said, 'What is this uproar?' Then the man hurried and came and told Eli" (4:14-15). Bad news coming. "Eli was ninety-eight years old and his eyes were set so that he could not see" (4:15). Age and blindness emphasized. "And the man said to Eli, 'I am he who has come from the battle; I fled from the battle today.' And he said, 'How did it go, my son?' He who brought the news answered and said, 'Israel has fled before the Philistines, and there has also been a great defeat among the people. Your two sons also, Hophni and Phinehas, are dead, and the ark of God has been captured'" (4:16-17). Four-part bad news: (1) Israel fled, (2) great slaughter, (3) sons dead, (4) ark captured. "As soon as he mentioned the ark of God, Eli fell over backward from his seat by the side of the gate, and his neck was broken and he died, for the man was old and heavy. He had judged Israel forty years" (4:18). **ARK'S CAPTURE KILLS ELI**—not sons' deaths but ark's loss. Physical weight + shock = broken neck. Judged 40 years but failed household. "Now his daughter-in-law, the wife of Phinehas, was pregnant, about to give birth. And when she heard the news that the ark of God was captured, and that her father-in-law and her husband were dead, she bowed and gave birth, for her pains came upon her" (4:19). Triple trauma induces labor. "And about the time of her death the women attending her said to her, 'Do not be afraid, for you have borne a son.' But she did not answer or pay attention. And she named the child Ichabod, saying, 'The glory has departed from Israel!' because the ark of God had been captured and because of her father-in-law and her husband. And she said, 'The glory has departed from Israel, for the ark of God has been captured'" (4:20-22). **ICHABOD** (אִי־כָבוֹד, 'i-kavod) = "no glory" or "where is the glory?" Book's lowest point—glory departed from Israel. Dying woman's testimony. Glory (כָּבוֹד, kavod) = God's manifest presence, tied to ark. Tragic cry defining spiritual bankruptcy.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Glory departed*

#### 5:1-12 — The Unconquerable Ark
**Lore:** "When the Philistines captured the ark of God, they brought it from Ebenezer to Ashdod. Then the Philistines took the ark of God and brought it into the house of Dagon and set it up beside Dagon" (5:1-2). **Dagon** = Philistine grain/fish god. Place ark as trophy in temple. "And when the people of Ashdod rose early the next day, behold, Dagon had fallen face downward on the ground before the ark of the LORD. So they took Dagon and put him back in his place" (5:3). **Idol falls in submission** before ark—God vindicates honor! "But when they rose early on the next morning, behold, Dagon had fallen face downward on the ground before the ark of the LORD, and the head of Dagon and both his hands were lying cut off on the threshold. Only the trunk of Dagon was left to him" (5:4). **SECOND FALL**—idol decapitated, hands severed! Utterly defeated. Dagon worshipers still avoid threshold (5:5). "The hand of the LORD was heavy against the people of Ashdod, and he terrified and afflicted them with tumors" (5:6). Plagues—possibly bubonic plague (tumors/boils). God's "hand" = judgment. "So they sent and gathered together all the lords of the Philistines and said, 'What shall we do with the ark of the God of Israel?' They answered, 'Let the ark of the God of Israel be brought around to Gath'" (5:8). Move to Gath. "But after they had brought it around, the hand of the LORD was against the city, causing a very great panic, and he afflicted the men of the city, both young and old, so that tumors broke out on them" (5:9). Same plague follows! "So they sent the ark of God to Ekron. But as soon as the ark of God came to Ekron, the people of Ekron cried out, 'They have brought around to us the ark of the God of Israel to kill us and our people'... For there was a deathly panic throughout the whole city. The hand of God was very heavy there" (5:10-11). Third city—same result! "Deathly panic" (מְהוּמַת־מָוֶת, mehumat-mavet). God proves sovereign even among enemies. Ark = unconquerable. No pagan god can stand before Yahweh.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: God's sovereignty vindicated*

#### 6:1-12 — The Ark's Return Journey
**Lore:** "The ark of the LORD was in the country of the Philistines seven months" (6:1). Seven months of plagues. "And the Philistines called for the priests and the diviners and said, 'What shall we do with the ark of the LORD? Tell us with what we shall send it to its place'" (6:2). Desperate for solution. "They said, 'If you send away the ark of the God of Israel, do not send it empty, but by all means return him a guilt offering. Then you will be healed'" (6:3). Recognize need for atonement! "And they said, 'What is the guilt offering that we shall return to him?' They answered, 'Five golden tumors and five golden mice, according to the number of the lords of the Philistines'" (6:4). Five = Philistine cities. Golden representations of afflictions (like modern ex-voto offerings). "So you must make images of your tumors and images of your mice that ravage the land, and give glory to the God of Israel. Perhaps he will lighten his hand from off you and your gods and your land" (6:5). Pagans recognize need to "give glory"! "Now then, take and prepare a new cart and two milk cows on which there has never come a yoke, and yoke the cows to the cart, but take their calves home, away from them. And take the ark of the LORD and place it on the cart... If it goes up on the way to its own land, to Beth-shemesh, then it is he who has done us this great harm, but if not, then we shall know that it is not his hand that struck us; it happened to us by chance'" (6:7-9). **TEST**—nursing cows would naturally return to calves, not pull cart uphill away. If go to Beth-shemesh = divine intervention vs. chance. "And the cows went straight in the direction of Beth-shemesh along one highway, lowing as they went. They turned neither to the right nor to the left, and the lords of the Philistines went after them as far as the border of Beth-shemesh" (6:12). **MIRACLE**—cows go against nature, straight path! "Lowing" = distressed moos for calves. Lords follow to confirm. God's sovereignty demonstrated even over animals.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Divine compulsion*

#### 6:13-21 — Irreverent Curiosity Judged
**Lore:** "Now the people of Beth-shemesh were reaping their wheat harvest in the valley. And when they lifted up their eyes and saw the ark, they rejoiced to see it" (6:13). Israelites rejoice at return! Wheat harvest = May/June, dry season. "The cart came into the field of Joshua of Beth-shemesh and stopped there. A great stone was there. And they split up the wood of the cart and offered the cows as a burnt offering to the LORD" (6:14). Proper sacrifice—cows that brought ark burned as offering. "Great stone" = altar. "And the Levites took down the ark of the LORD and the box that was beside it, in which were the golden figures, and set them upon the great stone" (6:15). Levites handle ark properly (Num 4:15). "And the men of Beth-shemesh offered burnt offerings and sacrificed sacrifices on that day to the LORD" (6:16). Celebration. "And when the five lords of the Philistines saw it, they returned that day to Ekron" (6:17). Witnesses confirm. "But he struck some of the men of Beth-shemesh, because they looked upon the ark of the LORD. He struck seventy men of them, and the people mourned because the LORD had struck the people with a great blow" (6:19). **JUDGMENT**—irreverent curiosity! "Looked upon" (רָאָה, ra'ah) may mean looked inside ark (violation, Num 4:20). 70 men died (some texts say 50,070—likely copyist error). God's holiness cannot be treated casually even by own people. "Then the men of Beth-shemesh said, 'Who is able to stand before the LORD, this holy God? And to whom shall he go up away from us?' So they sent messengers to the inhabitants of Kiriath-jearim, saying, 'The Philistines have returned the ark of the LORD. Come down and take it up to you'" (6:20-21). Recognize ark's dangerous holiness. Send away—can't bear weight of God's presence. Ark unconquerable by enemies, untouchable by presumptuous allies.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: God's holiness dangerous*

#### 7:1-17 — National Revival and Ebenezer
**Lore:** "And the men of Kiriath-jearim came and took up the ark of the LORD and brought it to the house of Abinadab on the hill. And they consecrated his son Eleazar to have charge of the ark of the LORD. From the day that the ark was lodged at Kiriath-jearim, a long time passed, some twenty years, and all the house of Israel lamented after the LORD" (7:1-2). Ark stored 20 years. "Lamented" (נָהָה, nahah) = mourned, yearned for God. Spiritual longing develops. "And Samuel said to all the house of Israel, 'If you are returning to the LORD with all your heart, then put away the foreign gods and the Ashtaroth from among you and direct your heart to the LORD and serve him only, and he will deliver you out of the hand of the Philistines'" (7:3). **SAMUEL'S CALL TO REPENTANCE**—conditions: (1) put away idols, (2) direct heart to LORD, (3) serve Him only. True repentance requires action. "So the people of Israel put away the Baals and the Ashtaroth, and they served the LORD only" (7:4). **Actual obedience**! Contrast to Judges cycle. "Then Samuel said, 'Gather all Israel at Mizpah, and I will pray to the LORD for you.' So they gathered at Mizpah and drew water and poured it out before the LORD and fasted on that day and said there, 'We have sinned against the LORD.' And Samuel judged the people of Israel at Mizpah" (7:5-6). **MIZPAH GATHERING**—water poured out = symbol of repentance, pouring out hearts (2 Sam 23:16, Lam 2:19). Public confession. Samuel judges. "Now when the Philistines heard that the people of Israel had gathered at Mizpah, the lords of the Philistines went up against Israel" (7:7). Philistines attack during vulnerable moment (fasting, unarmed worship). "And when the people of Israel heard of it, they were afraid of the Philistines. And the people of Israel said to Samuel, 'Do not cease to cry out to the LORD our God for us, that he may save us from the hand of the Philistines'" (7:8). Trust Samuel's intercession, not weapons! "So Samuel took a nursing lamb and offered it as a whole burnt offering to the LORD. And Samuel cried out to the LORD for Israel, and the LORD answered him" (7:9). Whole burnt offering = complete consecration. "As Samuel was offering up the burnt offering, the Philistines drew near to attack Israel. But the LORD thundered with a mighty sound that day against the Philistines and threw them into confusion, and they were defeated before Israel" (7:10). **DIVINE INTERVENTION**—God thunders! Like Sinai (Exod 19:16), confirms presence. Supernatural defeat. "And the men of Israel went out from Mizpah and pursued the Philistines and struck them, as far as below Beth-car" (7:11). Rout enemy. "Then Samuel took a stone and set it up between Mizpah and Shen and called its name Ebenezer; for he said, 'Till now the LORD has helped us'" (7:12). **EBENEZER** (אֶבֶן הָעֶזֶר, 'even ha-'ezer) = "stone of help." IRONY—same place as defeat (4:1)! Now site of victory through repentance. Samuel creates "diary" (memorial stone) of God's faithfulness. "Hitherto" (עַד־הֵנָּה, ad-henah) = "up to here" the LORD helped. Testimony monument. "So the Philistines were subdued and did not again enter the territory of Israel. And the hand of the LORD was against the Philistines all the days of Samuel" (7:13). Complete victory—Philistines subdued throughout Samuel's judgeship. Contrast to partial victories under other judges. "And Samuel judged Israel all the days of his life. And he went on a circuit year by year to Bethel, Gilgal, and Mizpah. And he judged Israel in all these places. Then he would return to Ramah, for his home was there, and there also he judged Israel. And he built there an altar to the LORD" (7:15-17). Circuit judge—brings justice to people. Ramah = home base with altar. Samuel = last judge, faithful leader. **HIGH POINT OF THEOCRACY** under final judge—sets stage for monarchy's beginning.
