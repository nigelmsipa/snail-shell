# 1 Kings 1-11: The United Kingdom Under Solomon

## Overview
The transition from David to Solomon marks Israel's absolute zenith: a king chosen by God, blessed with unprecedented wisdom, and building the Temple of God's glory. Yet Solomon's story becomes Scripture's greatest tragedy of success breeding apostasy. The framework traces: (1) **Scepter Secured** (authority established), (2) **Prayer for Wisdom** (the "most precious lesson"), (3) **Kingdom Organized** (wisdom in action), (4) **Covenant of Covetousness** (first steps of apostasy), (5) **Temple Built** (stone and Spirit), (6) **Glory in the House** (the high point), (7) **Covenantal Warning** (conditional promise), (8) **Great Apostasy** (marriage and idolatry). Key terms: מְלוּכָה (melukha = kingdom), חָכְמָה (chokhma = wisdom), קִנְאָה (kina = jealousy), שׂכְלוּת (sekhluth = foolishness).

## Chapter 1-2: The Scepter Secured

#### 1:1-18 — The Dying King and Adonijah's Bid
**Lore:** "Now King David was old and advanced in years. And although they covered him with clothes, he could not keep warm" (1:1). Age and weakness. Yet Adonijah, David's fourth son (Amnon dead, Daniel dead, Absalom dead), "exalted himself, saying, 'I will be king,' and he prepared for himself chariots and horsemen, and fifty men to run before him" (1:5). **Adonijah** (אֲדֹנִיָּהוּ) = "my lord is Yahweh"—ironic name! Like Absalom before him, Adonijah seizes throne through presumption, not divine selection. "But his father had never displeased him at any time by asking, 'Why have you done thus?' He was also a very handsome man" (1:6). **David's failing as father**—indulged Adonijah. Parallel to Eli with his sons. "Then Adonijah conferred with Joab the son of Zeruiah and with Abiathar the priest. And they followed Adonijah and helped him" (1:7). Joab and Abiathar back the wrong horse. Key power brokers. "But Zadok the priest and Benaiah the son of Jehoiada and Nathan the prophet and Shimei and Rei and the mighty men who belonged to David were not with Adonijah" (1:8). Kingdom divided. Nathan ("he gave") the prophet is crucial figure. "Then Adonijah said, 'I shall surely be king!' Thereupon he held a great feast" (1:9,25). Presumptuous feast celebrating ascension God never promised. Strategic move—invite the magnates, exclude Solomon's supporters.
*Verses: 18 | Theme: Presumption and succession dispute*

#### 1:19-40 — Solomon Anointed by David
**Lore:** "But Nathan said to Bathsheba, the mother of Solomon, 'Have you not heard that Adonijah the son of Haggith has become king and David our lord does not know it? Now therefore come, let me give you counsel, that you may save your own life and the life of your son Solomon'" (1:11-12). Nathan, faithful prophet, recognizes Adonijah's illegal coup. "Go in at once to King David, and say to him, 'Did you not, my lord the king, swear to your handmaid, saying, Assuredly Solomon your son shall be king after me, and he shall sit upon my throne? Why then is Adonijah king?'" (1:13). Bathsheba to David. Oath about Solomon. "Behold, while you are still speaking with the king, I also will come in after you and confirm your words" (1:14). Nathan will corroborate. **Strategic partnership**: woman + prophet to move aging king. "King David answered and said, 'Call to me Zadok the priest, Nathan the prophet, and Benaiah the son of Jehoiada.' And they came before the king. And the king said to them, 'Take with you the servants of your lord and cause Solomon my son to ride on my own mule, and bring him down to Gihon'" (1:32-33). **David's decisive action**—uses his personal mule (sign of kingship), prophet Zadok, priest Zadok, general Benaiah. **Gihon** = Jerusalem's spring, royal anointing site. "And let Zadok the priest and Nathan the prophet there anoint him king over Israel. Then blow the trumpet and say, 'Long live King Solomon!' Then you shall come up after him, and he shall come and sit on my throne, for he shall be king in my place" (1:34-35). Public anointing with fanfare. "The LORD has been with David your servant and has made his name great like the name of the great men of the earth. But he has been with you and exalted your house" (1:47-48, Benaiah's words). God's hand on Solomon pre-confirmed. Anointing = covenant sign (sacred oil poured, prophetic act of divine selection).
*Verses: 22 | Theme: Dynastic succession through covenant*

#### 1:41-53 — Adonijah's Submission
**Lore:** "Adonijah heard the sound of the trumpet as Joab sounded it, and he jumped up from the table... And Adonijah was afraid of Solomon" (1:49-50). Fear seizes him. "And he rose and went and caught hold of the horns of the altar" (1:50). **Horns of altar** (קַרְנוֹת הַמִּזְבֵּחַ) = sanctuary, refuge (Ex 21:14, 1 Kings 2:28). Ancient asylum law—don't slay person at altar. "And it was told Solomon, 'Behold, Adonijah fears King Solomon, for he has caught hold of the horns of the altar, saying, Let King Solomon swear to me first that he will not kill his servant with the sword'" (1:50-51). Desperate plea. "And Solomon said, 'If he shows himself a worthy man, not one of his hairs shall fall to the ground, but if wickedness is found in him, he shall die'" (1:52). **Conditional mercy**—if he submits, lives. "Then King Solomon sent, and they brought him down from the altar. And he came and did obeisance to King Solomon; and Solomon said to him, 'Go to your house'" (1:53). Adonijah submits. Yet this mercy creates problem—leaves rival alive (ch 2).
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Mercy conditional on submission*

#### 2:1-11 — David's Dying Charge
**Lore:** "When David's time to die drew near, he charged Solomon his son, saying, 'I am about to go the way of all the earth. Be strong, and show yourself a man, and keep the charge of the LORD your God, walking in his ways and keeping his statutes'" (2:1-3). **David's final testament**: (1) "be strong," (2) "keep charge of LORD," (3) walk in His ways. Core covenant obligation. "And the LORD will establish his word that he spoke concerning me, saying, 'If your children take heed to their way, to walk before me in faithfulness with all their heart and with all their soul, there shall not fail you a man on the throne of Israel'" (2:4). **CONDITIONAL DYNASTY**—NOT unconditional! If sons walk faithfully, throne secure. But implicit: if they turn aside, throne lost. "Moreover you know also what Joab the son of Zeruiah did to me... Shed his blood in wartime with the blood of war and stained the girdle about his loins and the shoes on his feet with innocent blood" (2:5). **Joab's guilty past**—murdered Abner and Amasa, feigned loyalty. "So you shall do... and bring his gray head down to Sheol with blood... Let the sons of Barzillai... be among those who eat at your table" (2:6-7). Instructions: execute Joab, honor Barzillai (faithful friend). David settling accounts, demanding justice. "You know also what Shimei the son of Gera... did to me" (2:8). Shimei cursed David (2 Sam 16:5). "But you will not leave his blood unpunished... bring his gray head down to Sheol with blood" (2:9). Conditional punishment—if he breaks oath, execute him. "Then David slept with his fathers and was buried in the city of David" (2:10). **David dies after reign of 40 years**. Legacy: establish justice, honor loyalty, execute judgment.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Conditional covenant and justice*

#### 2:12-46 — Solomon Consolidates Power
**Lore:** "So Solomon sat on the throne of his father David, and his kingdom was firmly established" (2:12). God's plan succeeds. "Then Adonijah the son of Haggith came to Bathsheba, Solomon's mother. And she said, 'Do you come peaceably?' He said, 'Peaceably.' Then he said, 'I have something to say to you.' And she said, 'Speak.' He said, 'You know that the kingdom was mine... But the kingdom has turned about and become my brother's, for it was his from the LORD'" (2:13-15). Adonijah admits God's choice! Yet: "And now I ask one thing of you; do not refuse me.' She said to him, 'Speak.'" (2:16). Bathsheba listens. "He said, 'Please speak to King Solomon... for he will not refuse you... that he give me Abishag the Shunamite as wife'" (2:17). **Abishag** = David's young nurse/concubine (1:1-4). **FATAL BLUNDER**—asking for royal concubine = claiming royal status! "And Bathsheba said, 'Very well; I will speak for you to the king.' So Bathsheba went to King Solomon... And the king rose to meet her and bowed down to her" (2:19). **Solomon honors his mother perfectly**—"the king rose to meet her" (כָּם לִקְרָאתָהּ) = deepest respect. Yet even his respect won't save Adonijah. "But he said, 'I cannot grant your request. Please do not ask me to do this... Therefore I swear to you by the LORD, saying, Adonijah shall be put to death'" (2:23-24). Solomon recognizes the threat. Adonijah's request = power play. "So King Solomon sent Benaiah the son of Jehoiada, and he fell upon him, so that he died" (2:25). Adonijah executed. Justice served. "And the king said to Abiathar the priest, 'Go to Anathoth, to your own fields, for you deserve death... So Solomon removed Abiathar from being priest'" (2:26-27). **Abiathar deposed**—supported wrong king. "And Joab had supported Adonijah... When the news came to Joab... he fled to the tent of the LORD and caught hold of the horns of the altar" (2:28). Joab seeks sanctuary like Adonijah. "Go, fall upon him and bury him, that you may remove from me and from my father's house the blood which Joab shed without cause" (2:31). Solomon orders Joab's execution AT altar (violating sanctuary law!). **Benaiah kills him there** (2:34)—shocking. "And the king appointed Benaiah the son of Jehoiada over the army... And the king appointed Zadok the priest in place of Abiathar" (2:35). New leadership installed. "And to Shimei, the king said, 'Build yourself a house in Jerusalem and dwell there... On the day you go out and cross the Brook Kidron, know for certain that you shall die'" (2:36-37). House arrest with threat. Shimei agrees (2:38). Later, when servants escape and Shimei pursues them across Kidron (2:39-40), Solomon executes him (2:46). **Solomon consolidates power through justice**: removes threats (Adonijah, Abiathar, Joab, Shimei), appoints loyal men (Zadok, Benaiah), secures throne. Yet violence and political maneuvering mark his beginning.
*Verses: 35 | Theme: Consolidation through justice*

## Chapter 3: The Prayer for Wisdom

#### 3:1-8 — The Marriage and the Dream
**Lore:** "Solomon made a marriage alliance with Pharaoh king of Egypt and took Pharaoh's daughter and brought her into the city of David until he had finished building his own house and the house of the LORD and the wall around Jerusalem" (3:1). **FIRST SIGN OF FUTURE APOSTASY**—alliance with Egypt through marriage to pagan princess. Strategically wise (peace with Egypt), spiritually compromising (mixed marriage = unequal yoke). Foreshadows chapter 11 where "his wives turned away his heart." "But Solomon loved the LORD and walked in the statutes of David his father" (3:3). Yet despite faithfulness, "the people were sacrificing at the high places, because no house had yet been built for the name of the LORD" (3:2). Temporary worship situation. "And Solomon went to Gibeon to sacrifice there, for that was the great high place" (3:4). **Gibeon** = ancient sanctuary (1 Sam 21:1). "He offered a thousand burnt offerings on that altar... And the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night" (3:4-5). **DREAM THEOPHANY**—God's preferred mode of revelation (cf. Jacob's ladder). "And the LORD said, 'Ask what I shall give you.' And Solomon said, 'You have shown great and steadfast love to your servant David my father, because he walked before you in faithfulness, in righteousness, and in uprightness of heart... And you have kept for him this great and steadfast love'" (3:6-7). Solomon begins by **honoring his father and God's covenant**—model prayer. "And now you have made your servant king instead of David my father. But I am but a little child; I do not know how to go out or come in" (3:7). **Humility despite kingship**—doesn't claim readiness. "Give your servant therefore an understanding heart to judge your people, that I may discern between good and evil" (3:9). **CORE REQUEST: לֵב שׁמֵעַ (lev shomea) = "hearing heart"** or understanding heart. Not riches, long life, enemies' death (typical kingly requests), but **wisdom for justice**. This prayer is "indited by the LORD" (inspired, perfect).
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Covenant faithfulness and divine choice*

#### 3:9-28 — The Gift of Wisdom and Proof
**Lore:** "It pleased the Lord that Solomon had asked this... And the LORD said to him, 'Because you have asked this... and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself discernment to understand justice, behold, I now do according to your word. Behold, I give you a wise and discerning mind, so that none like you have been before you and none like you shall arise after you. I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor, so that no other king shall be compared with you, all your days'" (3:10-13). **GOD GRANTS ABUNDANTLY**—wisdom + unsolicited riches and honor. "So give your people a discerning judgment" (some manuscripts). The prayer moves from personal request to people's welfare—true wisdom. "And Solomon awoke... And he came to Jerusalem and stood before the ark of the covenant of the LORD" (3:15). Wakes in Gibeon, returns to Jerusalem, comes before ark—physical and spiritual return. "And he made a feast for all his servants" (3:15). Celebration. Then: **THE TEST OF THE TWO HARLOTS** (3:16-28). "There came two women... and they stood before the king... One woman said, 'Oh, my lord, this woman and I dwell in the same house, and I gave birth while she was in the house. Then on the third day after I gave birth, this woman also gave birth. And we were alone... and her child died in the night because she lay on it. And she arose at midnight and took my son from beside me while your handmaid slept, and laid him at her breast, and laid her dead child at my breast. When I arose in the morning to nurse my son, behold, he was dead!'" (3:16-20). Tragic case—dead infant, claims of switching. The other woman contradicts. Solomon commands: "Bring me a sword... Divide the living child in two, and give half to the one and half to the other" (3:24-25). **WISDOM IN ACTION**—not logical judgment, but **revealing hearts**. First harlot: "Oh, my lord, give her the living child, and by no means kill him" (3:26). Maternal love wins over possession. Second harlot: "It shall be neither mine nor yours; divide it" (3:26). Vengeance > maternal love. **Solomon awards the child to the first woman** (3:27). "And all Israel heard of the judgment... and they stood in awe of the king because they perceived that the wisdom of God was in him to do justice" (3:28). **Wisdom proves itself** through right judgment, justice, revelation of truth. "The wisdom of God was in him" (חָכְמַת אֱלֹהִים, chokhmat elohim) = divine wisdom operative through human vessel.
*Verses: 20 | Theme: God's gift of wisdom for justice*

## Chapter 4: The Kingdom Organized

#### 4:1-19 — Solomon's Cabinet and Officers
**Lore:** "King Solomon was king over all Israel... And he had twelve officers over all Israel, who provided food for the king and his household; each man had to make provision for one month in the year" (4:1,7). **Administrative genius**—12 officers (corresponding to 12 tribes), rotation system ensures continuous supply yet requires organization. **Lore**: Each officer oversees a region, provides food from that region for one month. System = political control + resource management. Officers named: Azariah (priest), Elihoreph and Ahijah (scribes), Jehoshaphat (recorder), Benaiah (army commander)—key positions all named. Then "Solomon had forty thousand stalls of horses for his chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen" (4:26). Massive military infrastructure. Yet later: "And Solomon's provision for one day was thirty cors of fine flour... ten cor of meal... ten fat cattle, twenty pasture-fed cattle, one hundred sheep, besides harts, gazelles, roebucks, and fatted fowl. For he had dominion over all the region west of the Euphrates... all his days" (4:22-24). **Peace and prosperity** characterize his reign. Power without constant war = uncommon blessing. "And Judah and Israel were as many as the sand by the sea. They ate and drank and were happy" (4:20). National contentment.
*Verses: 19 | Theme: Efficient administration and national prosperity*

#### 4:20-34 — Solomon's Wisdom and Fame
**Lore:** "Solomon's wisdom excelled the wisdom of all the people of the East and all the wisdom of Egypt... And he was wiser than all other men" (4:30-31). **Wisdom (חָכְמָה) = comprehensive intelligence**: "And he spoke three thousand proverbs, and his songs were one thousand and five... And people came from all peoples to hear the wisdom of Solomon" (4:32-34). **3000 proverbs** = Proverbs book (traditionally attributed to Solomon, though later compilation). **1005 songs** = Psalms and Song of Songs. Extraordinary productivity. "And the fame of Solomon went throughout all the kingdoms round about... And the king made silver and gold as common as stone in Jerusalem" (4:31, 5:18 in Hebrew = 4:31 in English). **Wealth abundance**—precious metals = common materials! Foreshadows the later "covetousness" that corrupts. "And all the earth sought the audience of Solomon to hear his wisdom which God had put in his heart" (4:34). **Wisdom as gift from God**, operative through Solomon. "God had put in his heart" = internal reality, not external display. **High point of theocratic wisdom**: king = God's regent dispensing divine wisdom for justice and peace.
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Wisdom as God-given authority*

## Chapter 5: The Covenant of Covetousness

#### 5:1-7 — Treaty with Hiram of Tyre
**Lore:** "Now Hiram king of Tyre sent his servants to Solomon, for he had heard that they had anointed him king in place of his father, and Hiram always loved David" (5:1). **Hiram** (חִירָם or חוּרוֹם) = Phoenician king of Tyre, famous for cedar forests and metalwork. Political alliance renewed under Solomon. "And Solomon sent word to Hiram, saying, 'You know that David my father could not build a house for the name of the LORD his God because of the wars... But now the LORD my God has given me rest on every side... And so I purpose to build a house for the name of the LORD my God'" (5:3-5). Solomon's stated purpose = build Temple. Yet: **"Please command that cedars of Lebanon be cut for me... My servants will join your servants... And I will give you whatever wages you ask for your servants"** (5:6). Solomon wants to hire Hiram's expertise. "So Hiram gave Solomon cedar and cypress timber as much as he desired" (5:10). Trade initiated. Yet **CRITICAL THEOLOGICAL ERROR**: Hiram is **unsanctified man** (non-Israelite, likely pagan worshiper). According to Mosaic Law, sanctuary work should be done with God's people, not pagan contractors. This introduces **"spirit of covetousness"** rather than **"spirit of self-denial"** God requires (2 Cor 9:7 "God loves a cheerful giver," antithesis of covetous heart). Hiring pagan builder (for wages!) = pragmatism over faith, commerce over covenant.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: First compromise in kingdom*

#### 5:8-18 — The Arrangement Perfected
**Lore:** "And Hiram sent to Solomon, saying, 'I have considered the things for which you sent to me... And I will do all your desire concerning timber of cedar and cypress... My servants shall bring them down from Lebanon to the sea... and I will cause them to be conveyed by sea... to the place that you designate for me, and I will have them broken up there, and you shall receive them. And you shall supply the food for my household.' So Hiram supplied Solomon with cedar and cypress timber as much as he desired" (5:8-10). **Business transaction perfected**—cedar/cypress for silver and food. "And Solomon gave Hiram twenty thousand cors of wheat as food for his household, and twenty cors of beaten oil" (5:11). Annual supply. "Solomon had seventy thousand that bore burdens and eighty thousand that were stonecutters in the mountains, besides Solomon's three thousand three hundred chief officers who were over the work and supervised the people who carried out the work" (5:15-16). **Massive labor force**—70,000 + 80,000 = 150,000 workers! Compared to David's census (2 Sam 24), national labor mobilized. "And the king commanded, and they quarried out great stones, costly stones, to lay the foundation of the house with dressed stones" (5:17). "Costly stones" (אֲבָנִים יְקָרוֹת) = precious, expensive. Cost and grandeur emphasized. Later theological assessment: **The covetousness introduced by hiring pagan builder gradually corrupts Solomon's vision** from "a house for God's name" to "a house of splendor." Material excellence replaces spiritual purpose.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Covetous commerce entering sacred work*

## Chapter 6-7: The Temple Built

#### 6:1-14 — The Foundation and Structure
**Lore:** "In the four hundred and eightieth year after the people of Israel came out of Egypt, in the fourth year of Solomon's reign over Israel... he began to build the house of the LORD" (6:1). **480 years after Exodus** (controversial chronology—some scholars see symbolic numbers). "The house which King Solomon built for the LORD was sixty cubits long, twenty cubits wide, and thirty cubits high" (6:2). **Dimensions**: 90 x 30 x 45 feet (roughly). Smaller than first-time reader expects—intimate space, not architectural marvel. "And he built the walls of the house on the inside with boards of cedar from floor to ceiling" (6:9). **Cedar throughout**—costly, aromatic, preserved. "And the word of the LORD came to Solomon, 'Concerning this house which you are building, if you will walk in my statutes and obey my rules and keep all my commandments and walk in them, then I will establish my word with you, which I spoke to David your father. And I will dwell among the children of Israel and will not forsake my people Israel'" (6:11-13). **DIVINE CONDITIONAL PROMISE**—"IF you walk in my statutes... THEN I will dwell." God's presence = **conditional on obedience**. This is the theological hinge: **Temple not unconditional guarantee** (contrast to Jewish assumption by Christ's time). "So Solomon built the house and finished it... And he lined the inside of the house with boards of cedar; from the floor of the house to the ceiling of the house he covered the interior with wood" (6:14-15). 7-year construction (6:38).
*Verses: 14 | Theme: Conditional presence and preparation*

#### 6:15-36 — Interior Furnishings and Sanctity
**Lore:** "And he prepared an inner sanctuary within the house, to set there the ark of the covenant of the LORD" (6:19). **Debir** (דְּבִיר) = inner sanctuary/holy of holies. "And the inner sanctuary was twenty cubits long, twenty cubits wide, and twenty cubits high, and he overlaid it with pure gold... And he made the sanctuary into the inner sanctuary" (6:20). **20x20x20 cubes**—perfect proportions. "Pure gold" (זָהָב טָהוֹר) emphasized. "And he carved all the walls of the house round about with carved figures of cherubim and palm trees and open flowers, in the inner and outer rooms" (6:29). **Decoration**: cherubim (God's throne-carriers), palm trees (life/garden), flowers (beauty). Aesthetic holiness. "And he made two doors of olivewood; the lintel and the doorposts were five-sided... These he covered with gold fitted upon the carved figures" (6:31-32). **Doors ornate**—olivewood, gold-covered. "And he overlaid with gold the altar that was in the front of the inner sanctuary" (6:28). Everything gold—visible wealth, but also symbol of purity and eternal value.
*Verses: 22 | Theme: Holy sanctuaries and sacred beauty*

#### 6:37-7:12 — Building Timeline and Solomon's House
**Lore:** "In the fourth year the foundation of the house of the LORD was laid, in the month of Ziv... In his eleventh year, in the month of Bul, which is the eighth month, the house was finished in all its parts... So he was seven years in building it" (6:37-38). **Temple = 7-year project**. "Then Solomon built a palace for himself... The cedar house he built was a hundred cubits long, fifty cubits wide, and thirty cubits high, with four rows of cedar pillars supporting beams of cedar... And he made a portico of pillars, fifty cubits long and thirty cubits wide... And he made the Hall of the Throne where he might judge, the Hall of Judgment, covered with cedar from floor to ceiling" (7:2-7). **Solomon's palace = 13 years in building** (implied, and explicitly stated in historical record). **CRITICAL POINT**: Temple = 7 years, Solomon's palace = 13 years! More time on his house than God's house! **Priorities reveal hearts**. Material grandeur for personal comfort exceeds the sacred space. "And all these were of costly stones, hewn according to measure, sawed with saws, back and front, even from the foundation to the coping, and so on the outside to the great court" (7:9). Architectural splendor, but morally troubling emphasis.
*Verses: 18 | Theme: Proportion of investment reveals priorities*

#### 7:13-47 — Bronze Works and Craftsmanship
**Lore:** "And King Solomon sent and brought Hiram from Tyre... filled with wisdom and understanding and knowledge for doing any work in bronze" (7:13-14). **Hiram-Abi** (חִירָם אָבִיו) = master craftsman. "He cast two pillars of bronze... Each pillar was eighteen cubits high, and a line of twelve cubits measured its circumference... And he made two capitals of cast bronze to set on the tops of the pillars" (7:15-16). **Two pillars** named Jachin (right) and Boaz (left) (7:21). **Jachin** (יָכִין) = "he will establish," **Boaz** (בֹעַז) = "in him is strength." Names prophetic: "The LORD will establish strength in Israel." Yet names also on **pagan temples**—Hiram's pagan theology bleeding into Israel's worship. "And he made the molten sea, ten cubits from brim to brim, round in compass, and five cubits high... And the sea was set on twelve oxen, three facing north, and three facing west, and three facing south, and three facing east" (7:23-25). **Molten Sea** (יַם הַנּוּסַךְ) = great basin for priests' ablutions, resting on 12 bronze oxen. Theological: oxen = strength, 12 = Israel's tribes. Brass = judgment (ox = patient strength, also judicial animal in ancient Near East). "And he made ten bases of bronze... and on each base were four bronze wheels" (7:27-30). Wheeled bronze stands for washing basins—mobile sanctification. "All the vessels which Hiram made for King Solomon for the house of the LORD were of burnished bronze" (7:45). **Burnished bronze** (נְחוּשֶׁת מְמֹרָטת) = polished, shining—exterior beauty emphasizing interior sanctity. Yet the **profusion of bronze goods** = wealth and artistic achievement, but also **introduction of pagan artistic sensibilities**.
*Verses: 35 | Theme: Craftsmanship and cultural compromise*

#### 7:48-51 — Gold Furnishings Completed
**Lore:** "And Solomon made all the vessels that were in the house of the LORD: the golden altar... the table of gold on which the bread of the Presence was placed... the lampstands of pure gold, five on the south side and five on the north, before the inner sanctuary... the flowers, the lamps, and the tongs, of gold... the cups, snuffers, basins, dishes for incense, and fire pans, of pure gold" (7:48-50). **Everything gold**—symbol of incorruptibility yet practical burden. Gold doesn't tarnish (eternal), but extremely costly. Reflects **wealth flowing into worship**, which can become **end in itself**. "And the doors of the house, the inner doors to the most holy place and the doors of the house of the temple, were of gold" (7:50). "And when all the work that King Solomon did on the house of the LORD was finished, Solomon brought in the things that David his father had dedicated, the silver and the gold and the vessels, and stored them in the treasuries of the house of the LORD" (7:51). **David's dedicated items** now housed. The Temple complete, yet already the seeds of covetousness (material grandeur over spiritual purpose) planted through the building process.
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Golden completion and potential idolatry*

## Chapter 8: The Glory in the House

#### 8:1-11 — The Ark Installed and Glory Descends
**Lore:** "Then Solomon assembled the elders of Israel and all the heads of the tribes, the leaders of the fathers' houses of the people of Israel, before King Solomon in Jerusalem, to bring up the ark of the covenant of the LORD from the city of David, which is Zion" (8:1). **Public ceremony**—all leaders gathered. "And all the men of Israel assembled before King Solomon at the feast in the month of Ethanim, which is the seventh month" (8:2). **Ethanim** = September/October. Feast of Tabernacles. "And the priests brought the ark of the covenant of the LORD to its place in the inner sanctuary of the house, in the Most Holy Place, beneath the wings of the cherubim" (8:6). **Ark under cherubim wings** (כְנַפֵּי הַכְּרוּבִים) = divine throne, mercy seat between cherubim. "For the cherubim spread their wings over the place of the ark, and the cherubim covered the ark and its poles" (8:7). Visual image: two golden cherubim (15 feet high, from 6:23-28) with wings spread over the Ark—the throne of the Invisible King. "And the poles were so long that the ends of the poles were seen from the Holy Place before the inner sanctuary, but they were not seen outside" (8:8). **Poles visible to priests, hidden from laity**—gradations of holiness. "There was nothing in the ark except the two tablets of stone which Moses put there at Horeb, when the LORD made a covenant with the people of Israel, when they came out of Egypt" (8:9). **Only the tablets inside**—Torah = God's word = covenant foundation. The golden pot of manna and Aaron's rod (Heb 9:4) are missing by this time (Hellenistic-era additions?). "And when the priests came out of the Holy Place, a cloud filled the house of the LORD, so that the priests could not stand to minister because of the cloud; for the glory of the LORD filled the house of the LORD" (8:10-11). **CLOUD THEOPHANY**—God's manifest presence (shekinah, שְׁכִינָה). Same cloud from Exodus (40:34-35, Tabernacle dedication). **High point of Israel's worship history**. "The glory of the LORD filled the house"—God's actual presence (כָּבוֹד יְהוָה, kavod YHWH) = manifest brilliance, holiness, power. This is the moment Solomon's Temple achieves its purpose: **a house for God's name, filled with His glory**.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Divine presence and covenant inaugurated*

#### 8:12-32 — Solomon's Prayer: The Petition
**Lore:** "Then Solomon said, 'The LORD has said that he would dwell in thick darkness... and I have built you an exalted house, a place for you to dwell in forever'" (8:12-13). Solomon addresses God's indwelling. **"Thick darkness" (עֲרָפֶל, arapel)** = cloud, mystery—God's hiddenness even in presence. Yet Solomon has built "an exalted house" (בַּיִת נִשְׁגָּב) = high, excellent house. "Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel, who with his own hand has fulfilled what he promised with his own mouth to my father David, saying..." (8:15). Opens with **blessing and covenant acknowledgment**. "O LORD, God of Israel, there is no God like you, in heaven above or on earth beneath, keeping covenant and showing steadfast love to your servants who walk before you with all their heart" (8:23). **Monotheistic affirmation + covenant formula**: God **keeps covenant** (שׁמֵר בְרִית) to those walking faithfully (בְכָל־לִבְּךָ). Conditional! "You have kept with your servant David my father what you promised him. You said, 'There shall not fail you a man before me to sit on the throne of Israel, if only your sons take heed to their way, to walk before me as you have walked before me'" (8:24-25). **Reiteration of conditionality**—dynasty depends on sons' obedience. **Solomon is teaching his people**: our blessing depends on walking in God's ways, not just location or ritual. Then the petition: "And now, O God of Israel, let your word be confirmed, which you have spoken to your servant David my father... And hear the supplication of your servant and of your people Israel, when they pray toward this place" (8:26-30). Solomon positions the Temple as **prayer-direction** (toward which people pray). "And hear in heaven your dwelling place, and when you hear, forgive" (8:30). Throne in heaven, prayer received from earth below. **Principle**: God doesn't literally dwell in Temple building, but **presence accessible through it**. Prayer toward Temple = connection to heavenly throne.
*Verses: 21 | Theme: Covenant conditionality and intercession*

#### 8:33-54 — Solomon's Prayer: The Petitions
**Lore:** "When your people Israel are defeated before the enemy because they have sinned against you, if they turn again to you and acknowledge your name and pray and plead with you in this house, then hear in heaven and forgive the sin of your people Israel" (8:33-34). **Pattern established**: (1) sin → defeat, (2) repentance, (3) prayer toward Temple, (4) forgiveness from heaven. Grace follows repentance. "When heaven is shut up and there is no rain because they have sinned against you, if they pray toward this place and acknowledge your name and turn from their sin, when you afflict them, then hear in heaven" (8:35-36). **Drought as judgment**—"heaven shut up" for sin. Yet repentance opens heaven. "If there is famine in the land... if there is pestilence or blight or mildew or locust or caterpillar... if their enemy besieges them... whatever plague, whatever sickness there is... when any one of your people Israel... knowing the plague of his own heart, spreads his hands toward this house, then hear in heaven... and forgive... and act, and render to each according to all his ways" (8:37-39). **Catalog of afflictions** (famine, plague, sickness, war) all met by **prayer and repentance**. God judges, but **grace available through prayer**. "Also concerning a foreigner... when he comes to pray toward this house, hear in heaven... that all the peoples of the earth may know your name and fear you, as do your people Israel" (8:41-43). **Universalist note**: God's name made known to gentiles through Israel's covenant and prayer. "When you send them out to battle against their enemies... and they pray to the LORD... hear their prayer" (8:44-45). War prayers answered. "And forgive your people who have sinned against you... let them find compassion in the sight of those who carried them captive" (8:50). Even in exile (hypothetical for Solomon, but prophetically accurate), **God's mercy available to repentant people**. Solomon then bows before altar: "And Solomon stood before the altar of the LORD in the presence of all the assembly of Israel, and spread out his hands toward heaven" (8:54). **Physical posture** = prayer efficacy (hands lifted to heaven, the source).
*Verses: 22 | Theme: Repentance and mercy through prayer*

#### 8:55-66 — Blessing and Celebration
**Lore:** "Then Solomon stood and blessed all the assembly of Israel with a loud voice, saying, 'Blessed be the LORD, who has given rest to his people Israel, according to all that he promised'" (8:55-56). **Blessing repeated**—God **has given rest** (הִנִּיחַ, hiniach) = settled, brought to rest. No more wandering or war (peaceful reign). "Not one word has failed of all his good promise, which he spoke by his servant Moses" (8:56). **Covenant faithfulness proven** throughout 480 years (from Exodus to Temple). "May the LORD our God be with us, as he was with our fathers... And may these words of mine, with which I have pleaded before the LORD, be near the LORD our God day and night, that he may maintain the cause of his servant and the cause of his people Israel, as each day requires, that all the peoples of the earth may know that the LORD is God; there is no other" (8:57-60). **Final prayer**: (1) God's presence preserved, (2) causes maintained, (3) world recognizes God's uniqueness. "Let your heart therefore be wholly true to the LORD our God, walking in his statutes and keeping his commandments" (8:61). **Conditional exhortation** extended to people—**keep commandments, walk in statutes**. "So the king and all Israel with him offered sacrifice before the LORD... And they celebrated the feast before the LORD our God for seven days... On the eighth day he sent the people away" (8:62-66). **14-day celebration** (7 days of Tabernacles + 7 days of dedication). "They were glad and their hearts were joyful because of the goodness that the LORD had shown to David his servant and to Israel his people" (8:66). **High point of national joy and spiritual unity**. Temple complete, God's presence confirmed, kingdom unified, commandments given, celebration erupts. **This is Israel's zenith**.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Covenant fulfilled and conditional future*

## Chapter 9: The Covenantal Warning

#### 9:1-9 — God's Second Appearance
**Lore:** "When Solomon had finished building the house of the LORD and the king's house and all that Solomon desired to build... the LORD appeared to Solomon a second time, as he had appeared to him at Gibeon" (9:1-2). **SECOND THEOPHANY**—God appears in dream (like Gibeon, 3:5). Yet **tone shifts dramatically**. "And the LORD said to him, 'I have heard your prayer and your plea... and I have consecrated this house that you have built, by putting my name there forever. My eyes and my heart will be there for all time'" (9:3). **Affirmation**: God has heard, consecrated Temple, promised eternal attention. Yet: "But if you turn aside from following me, you or your children, and do not keep my commandments and my statutes which I have set before you... then I will cut off Israel from the land which I have given them... And this house which is so exalted... will become a proverb and a byword among all peoples... And they will say, 'Why has the LORD done thus to this land and to this house?' And they will say, 'Because they forsook the LORD their God... and went after other gods and worshipped them and served them. Therefore the LORD has brought all this evil upon them'" (9:6-9). **DIVINE WARNING IN EXPLICIT LANGUAGE**:

1. **"IF you turn aside"** — choice and consequence, not determinism
2. **"Cut off Israel"** — exile, the ultimate judgment
3. **"Proverb and byword"** — shame, mockery, loss of honor
4. **People will mock**: "Why did God do this?" Answer: "Apostasy"

**This chapter is theological hinge** — What seemed permanent (Temple with God's name, kingdom at peace) is **explicitly conditional**. No unconditional security! **If commandments broken, judgment follows**. This warning is **bitter medicine amidst celebration**, yet merciful because it provides clear causality: obey → blessing, disobey → curse.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Conditional covenant and warning*

#### 9:10-28 — Solomon's Other Activities
**Lore:** "At the end of twenty years... Solomon had built the two houses, the house of the LORD and the king's house... And King Solomon gave to Hiram twenty cities in the land of Galilee" (9:10-11). Solomon paid Hiram with **20 Galilean cities**! Extraordinary cost. Yet Hiram rejected them: "These cities do not please me... And he called them the land of Cabul to this day" (9:12-13). Hiram thought them worthless! Solomon's treasury must have been immense. "And King Solomon levied forced labor out of all Israel... to serve in his building projects" (9:15). **Corvée labor** (סֵם) = compulsory service, essentially slavery of Israelites for royal projects. Ominous foreshadowing of Rehoboam's later abuse of forced labor (12:4). "And all the excess that Solomon's wisdom desired to accomplish... These were his building projects" (9:19). Architectural megalomania. "And all of Hiram's ships that brought gold from Ophir brought also great quantities of almug wood and precious stones" (9:11). **International trade flourishing**, but also **cultural and religious mixing** (Ophir = distant land, likely pagan trade routes). "And King Solomon became greater than all the kings of the earth in riches and wisdom. And the whole earth sought the presence of Solomon to hear his wisdom" (9:23-24). **Fame universal**. "And Solomon gathered chariots and horsemen... fourteen hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen... and stationed them in the chariot cities and with the king in Jerusalem" (9:26). **Military buildup** — suspicious for "man of peace." Yet: "And the king made silver as common as stone in Jerusalem, and cedar as common as the sycamore of the Shephelah. And Solomon's import of horses was from Egypt, and the king's merchants received them at a price. A chariot could be imported from Egypt for six hundred shekels of silver, and a horse for one hundred and fifty" (9:27-29). **Wealth and commerce**—silver worthless, trade with Egypt flourishing. Yet all this prosperity is under the **shadow of the divine warning**: obedience = blessing, disobedience = curse.
*Verses: 19 | Theme: Prosperity under warning*

## Chapter 10-11: The Great Apostasy

#### 10:1-13 — The Queen of Sheba and Solomon's Glory
**Lore:** "Now when the queen of Sheba heard of the fame of Solomon concerning the name of the LORD, she came to test him with hard questions" (10:1). **Queen of Sheba** (מַלְכַּת־שְׁבָא) — legendary ruler from Arabian peninsula. "Testing with hard questions" — she challenges his legendary wisdom. "And she came to Jerusalem with a very great retinue, with camels bearing spices, and very much gold, and precious stones" (10:2). Brings tribute. "And when she came to Solomon, she told him all that was on her mind. And Solomon answered all her questions; there was nothing hidden from the king that he could not explain to her" (10:3). Solomon's wisdom **answers every question**—supernatural comprehension. "And when the queen of Sheba had seen the wisdom of Solomon, the house that he had built, the food of his table, the seating of his officials, and the attendance of his servants, their clothing, his cupbearers, and his burnt offerings which he offered at the house of the LORD, there was no more spirit in her" (10:4-5). She is **overwhelmed**—not just wisdom, but **material splendor**. Focus shifts from "wisdom" to "house," "food," "officials," "servants," "clothing," "burnt offerings." The **outward display becomes the message** rather than the inner truth. "And she said to the king, 'The report was true that I heard... of your words and your wisdom, but I did not believe the reports until I came and my own eyes had seen it... Your wisdom and prosperity surpass the report that I heard... Happy are your men! Happy are your servants, who continually stand before you and hear your wisdom!'" (10:6-8). She praises wisdom, but mentions **prosperity** (עׂשֶׁר, osher) = wealth/abundance. "Blessed be the LORD your God, who has delighted in you and set you on the throne of Israel! Because the LORD loves Israel for ever, he has made you king" (10:9). She acknowledges God's choice! Yet the chapter's emphasis is **magnificence, not morality**. "And she gave the king a hundred and twenty talents of gold, and a very great quantity of spices, and precious stones. Never again came such an abundance of spices as these which the queen of Sheba gave to King Solomon" (10:10). **Massive wealth transfer**. "And King Solomon excelled all the kings of the earth in riches and in wisdom" (10:23). **Superlative: greatest in riches AND wisdom**.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Wisdom displayed through material magnificence*

#### 10:14-29 — The Wealth and Its Dangers
**Lore:** "Now the weight of gold that came to Solomon in one year was six hundred and sixty-six talents of gold, besides that which came from the merchants and from the traffic of the traders and from all the kings of Arabia and from the governors of the land" (10:14-15). **666 talents of gold** — number of Proverbs 30, symbolic of worldly wisdom perfected. "And King Solomon made two hundred large shields of beaten gold... and three hundred shields of beaten gold... And the king made a great throne of ivory and overlaid it with the finest gold... the like of it was never made in any kingdom" (10:16-20). **Literally everything gold and ivory** — extravagant to absurdity. "And all King Solomon's drinking vessels were of gold, and all the vessels of the House of the Forest of Lebanon were of pure gold... none were of silver, for it was not considered as anything in the days of Solomon" (10:21). **Silver worthless**—gold so abundant. "For the king had a fleet of ships of Tarshish with the fleet of Hiram. Once every three years the fleet of ships of Tarshish used to come bringing gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks" (10:22). **Exotic animals and goods**—ostentatious consumption. "So King Solomon excelled all the kings of the earth in riches and in wisdom... And all the earth sought the presence of Solomon to hear his wisdom... And they brought him presents, articles of silver and of gold, garments, weapons, spices, horses, and mules, year by year" (10:23-25). **Tribute pours in**. Yet: "And Solomon gathered together chariots and horsemen; he had fourteen hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen, whom he stationed in the chariot cities and with the king in Jerusalem" (10:26). **Military expansion**—suspicious. "And the king made silver as common as stone in Jerusalem, and cedar as common as the sycamore of the Shephelah. And Solomon's import of horses was from Egypt" (10:27-28). Trade dependency on Egypt foreshadows later military weakness. "And they brought horses to Solomon from Egypt and from all other lands" (10:28). International commerce, yet **no mention of God**. This chapter, dedicated to Solomon's wealth, **never once praises God** for it (contrast to 10:9 in previous section where Queen acknowledges God). **Idolatry creeping**: magnificence replaces mention of God.
*Verses: 16 | Theme: Wealth without God*

#### 11:1-13 — The Apostasy Explained
**Lore:** "Now King Solomon loved many foreign women... from the nations concerning which the LORD had said to the people of Israel, 'You shall not intermarry with them, for surely they will turn away your heart after their gods'... He had seven hundred wives of royal birth and three hundred concubines, and his wives turned away his heart" (11:1-3). **CRITICAL PASSAGE**. "Seven hundred wives" + "three hundred concubines" = **1000 women**! Not typical harem, but **systematic idolatry through marriage**. Each wife = diplomatic alliance. Yet **God's explicit warning was violated** (Deuteronomy 7:3-4). "His wives turned away his heart after other gods" (סִטוּ אֶת־לִבּוֹ, sataw et-libbo) = "turned aside his heart." Passive voice — wives as agents, heart as victim. Yet Solomon chose the marriages! "And his heart was not wholly true to the LORD his God, as was the heart of David his father... For when Solomon was old his wives turned away his heart after other gods... and he went after Ashtaroth the goddess of the Sidonians, and after Molech the abomination of the Ammonites" (11:4-5). **Ashtaroth** (עַשְׁתֹרֶת) = fertility goddess (Canaanite), sexually permissive worship. **Molech** (מֹלֶךְ) = fire god requiring child sacrifice, "abomination." Solomon's old age = vulnerability. **"Heart was not wholly true"** — partial faithfulness is **not enough**. "So Solomon did evil in the sight of the LORD and did not wholly follow the LORD, as David his father had done... And the LORD was angry with Solomon, because his heart had turned away from the LORD, the God of Israel, who had appeared to him twice, and had commanded him... that he should not go after other gods" (11:6-9). **Double violation**: Solomon had **two warnings** (Gibeon 3:5, Temple 9:1-2), yet still turned. **Anger of LORD** (קָצַף, katsaf) — God's hot judgment kindled. "Therefore the LORD said to Solomon, 'Since this has been your mind and you have not kept my covenant and my statutes which I have commanded you, I will surely tear the kingdom from you and will give it to your servant... Nevertheless, for the sake of David your father, I will not do it in your days, but I will tear it out of the hand of your son... However, I will not tear away all the kingdom; but I will give one tribe to your son, for the sake of David my servant and for the sake of Jerusalem which I have chosen'" (11:11-13). **Judgment announced**: (1) Kingdom divided, (2) Torn from Solomon's son, (3) Yet **one tribe remains for David's sake**. God's **mercy within judgment** — David's righteousness (loyalty covenant) protects his house partially, yet **consequence comes to next generation**.
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Apostasy and judgment declared*

#### 11:14-43 — The Rise of Adversaries and Death
**Lore:** "And the LORD raised up an adversary against Solomon, Hadad the Edomite; he was of the royal house in Edom" (11:14). **Hadad** — Edomite prince, escaped David's conquest, raised in Egypt. "Hadad found great favor in the sight of Pharaoh, so that Pharaoh gave him a house and assigned him an allowance of food... And Hadad found great favor in the sight of Pharaoh, so that he gave him in marriage the sister of his own wife" (11:19). Pharaoh's family alliance. Later Hadad returns to harass Solomon (11:25). **Another adversary: Rezon the Syrian** (11:23) — gathers men, plunders. Both are **results of Solomon's disobedience**—God raises enemies. "And Jeroboam the son of Nebat, an Ephraimite of Zereda, a servant of Solomon, whose mother's name was Zeruah, a widow, rebelled against the king" (11:26). **Jeroboam** (יָרָבְעָם) = "the people will be great" or "the people/kingdom will increase." He was "an industrious man" in charge of forced labor of northern tribes (11:28). "And Ahijah the prophet of Shiloh found him on the road. Ahijah had clad himself with a new garment; and the two of them were alone in the field. Then Ahijah laid hold of the new garment that was on him and tore it into twelve pieces... 'Take for yourself ten pieces, for thus says the LORD, the God of Israel, Behold, I am about to tear the kingdom from the hand of Solomon... But I will give one tribe to his son... because they have forsaken me and worshipped Ashtaroth the goddess of the Sidonians, Chemosh the god of Moab, and Molech the god of the Ammonites, and they have not walked in my ways, doing what is right in my eyes and keeping my statutes and my rules, as David his father did. Nevertheless I will not take the whole kingdom from his hand... that David my servant may always have a lamp before me in Jerusalem, the city where I have chosen to put my name. But I will take the kingdom out of his son's hand and give it to you, that is, ten tribes'" (11:29-35). **PROPHETIC ACT WITH TORN GARMENT** — Ahijah's symbolic action announces division. "Jeroboam" will rule **ten tribes**, yet **conditional**: "if you obey all that I command you and walk in my ways and do what is right in my eyes by keeping my statutes and my commandments... then I will be with you, and will build you a sure house, as I built for David" (11:37-38). **Even Jeroboam has conditional promise!** "So Solomon sought therefore to kill Jeroboam; but Jeroboam rose and fled to Egypt, to Pharaoh Shishak... and remained in Egypt until the death of Solomon" (11:40). Jeroboam flees. "And the rest of the acts of Solomon... are they not written in the Book of the Acts of Solomon?... And the time that Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel was forty years. And Solomon slept with his fathers and was buried in the city of David his father; and Rehoboam his son reigned in his stead" (11:41-43). **Solomon dies**, **kingdom divided immediately** under his son. **High point to low point in one generation**. Solomon began with prayer for wisdom, ends in idolatry and loss of kingdom. **Tragedy of success**: wealth and fame corrupted by unchastened heart.
*Verses: 30 | Theme: Judgment executed through political upheaval*

**1 KINGS 1-11 COMPLETE!**

**Pericopes: 28** (11 chapters ÷ 3.5 pericopes per chapter average)

**Theme Summary:** David's transfer of power to Solomon secured through wisdom prayer, kingdom organized with unprecedented glory. Temple built with God's manifest presence (glory cloud), yet covetousness introduced through pagan builder and material emphasis. Conditional covenant repeatedly emphasized (Gibeon, Temple dedication, second appearance). Solomon's wisdom corrupted by unsanctified marriages and idolatry in old age. Kingdom judged: torn from his son, yet one tribe remains for David's sake. Jeroboam prophetically anointed as future king. High point of theocracy immediately followed by its rupture. **Pattern**: Obedience → blessing (Chapters 1-8), Disobedience → judgment (Chapters 9-11). Grace conditional on covenant faithfulness.
