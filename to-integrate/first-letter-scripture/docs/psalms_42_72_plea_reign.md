# Psalms 42-72: Plea & Reign (Book 2)

## Overview
Book 2 (Psalms 42-72) shifts from the predominantly individual laments of Book 1 to a broader focus on communal laments, the Sanctuary, and the Messianic King. Opening with the Sons of Korah's yearning for God's presence (Pss 42-49), progressing through Asaph's single psalm (Ps 50) and David's great penitential prayer (Ps 51), and culminating in the royal psalms, particularly the magnificent Psalm 72 portraying the Messiah's righteous global reign. This book ends with the doxology: "Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel, who alone does wondrous things. Blessed be his glorious name forever; may the whole earth be filled with his glory\! Amen and Amen\!" (72:18-19).

## Psalm 42: My Soul Thirsts for God

#### 42:1-5 — As a Deer Pants for Water
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster. A Maskil of the Sons of Korah" (superscription). **SONS OF KORAH**—Levitical singers descended from Korah who rebelled (Num 16), yet his sons spared (Num 26:11). Authors of Pss 42-49, 84-85, 87-88. **MASKIL** = instruction/contemplation. Psalms 42-43 form single poem (no superscription for 43, shared refrain). "As a deer pants for flowing streams, so pants my soul for you, O God. My soul thirsts for God, for the living God. When shall I come and appear before God?" (42:1-2). **FAMOUS OPENING**—deer imagery expresses spiritual longing. **LIVING GOD** (אֵל חָי, El Chai) vs. dead idols. "My tears have been my food day and night, while they say to me all the day long, 'Where is your God?'" (42:3). Taunt of enemies questions God's presence. "These things I remember, as I pour out my soul: how I would go with the throng and lead them in procession to the house of God with glad shouts and songs of praise, a multitude keeping festival" (42:4). **Memory of worship**—past temple experience now lost (exile?). "Why are you cast down, O my soul, and why are you in turmoil within me? Hope in God; for I shall again praise him, my salvation" (42:5). **REFRAIN** (repeated 42:11, 43:5)—self-exhortation to hope.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Thirsting for God's presence*

#### 42:6-11 — Deep Calls to Deep
**Lore:** "My soul is cast down within me; therefore I remember you from the land of Jordan and of Hermon, from Mount Mizar. Deep calls to deep at the roar of your waterfalls; all your breakers and your waves have gone over me" (42:6-7). **Geographic markers**—Jordan, Hermon, Mizar (northern Israel, far from Jerusalem). **DEEP CALLS TO DEEP** (תְּהוֹם אֶל־תְּהוֹם, tehom el-tehom)—chaos waters, overwhelming flood of trouble. "By day the LORD commands his steadfast love, and at night his song is with me, a prayer to the God of my life. I say to God, my rock: 'Why have you forgotten me? Why do I go mourning because of the oppression of the enemy?'" (42:8-9). Day/night cycle of God's **steadfast love** (חֶסֶד, chesed). Yet feels forgotten. "As with a deadly wound in my bones, my adversaries taunt me, while they say to me all the day long, 'Where is your God?' Why are you cast down, O my soul, and why are you in turmoil within me? Hope in God; for I shall again praise him, my salvation and my God" (42:10-11). **Refrain repeated**—hope declared despite circumstances.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Overwhelmed yet hoping*

## Psalm 43: Send Out Your Light

#### 43:1-5 — Vindicate Me, O God
**Lore:** No superscription—continues Ps 42. "Vindicate me, O God, and defend my cause against an ungodly nation; from the deceitful and unjust man deliver me\! For you are the God in whom I take refuge; why have you rejected me? Why do I go about mourning because of the oppression of the enemy?" (43:1-2). Prayer for vindication and deliverance. "Send out your light and your truth; let them lead me; let them bring me to your holy hill and to your dwelling\! Then I will go to the altar of God, to God my exceeding joy, and I will praise you with the lyre, O God, my God" (43:3-4). **SEND OUT YOUR LIGHT AND TRUTH**—divine guides back to Sanctuary. **Holy hill** = Zion, temple mount. **Exceeding joy** (שִׂמְחַת גִּילִי, simchat gili) = joy of my joy. "Why are you cast down, O my soul, and why are you in turmoil within me? Hope in God; for I shall again praise him, my salvation and my God" (43:5). **Third refrain**—trilogy of hope (42:5, 11, 43:5). Psalm pair moves from despair to anticipated worship.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Light and truth lead to worship*

## Psalm 44: Why Do You Sleep, O Lord?

#### 44:1-8 — Our Fathers Told Us
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster. A Maskil of the Sons of Korah" (superscription). **Communal lament**. "O God, we have heard with our ears, our fathers have told us, what deeds you performed in their days, in the days of old: you with your own hand drove out the nations, but them you planted; you afflicted the peoples, but them you set free" (44:1-2). Appeal to **salvation history**—conquest of Canaan (Josh 1-12). "For not by their own sword did they win the land, nor did their own arm save them, but your right hand and your arm, and the light of your face, for you delighted in them" (44:3). Victory attributed to God, not military might. "You are my King, O God; ordain salvation for Jacob\! Through you we push down our foes; through your name we tread down those who rise up against us" (44:4-5). God as **King**—source of victory. "For not in my bow do I trust, nor can my sword save me. But you have saved us from our foes and have put to shame those who hate us. In God we have boasted continually, and we will give thanks to your name forever. Selah" (44:6-8). Trust in God, not weapons (cf. 20:7, 33:16-17).
*Verses: 8 | Theme: God's past deliverance*

#### 44:9-16 — Yet You Have Rejected Us
**Lore:** "But you have rejected us and disgraced us and have not gone out with our armies. You have made us turn back from the foe, and those who hate us have gotten spoil. You have made us like sheep for slaughter and have scattered us among the nations" (44:9-11). **Current defeat**—military loss, exile. God seems absent. "You have sold your people for a trifle, demanding no high price for them. You have made us the taunt of our neighbors, the derision and scorn of those around us" (44:12-13). Sold cheaply, mocked by neighbors. "You have made us a byword among the nations, a laughingstock among the peoples. All day long my disgrace is before me, and shame has covered my face" (44:14-15). National humiliation.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Divine rejection and disgrace*

#### 44:17-26 — Awake\! Why Do You Sleep?
**Lore:** "All this has come upon us, though we have not forgotten you, and we have not been false to your covenant. Our heart has not turned back, nor have our steps departed from your way" (44:17-18). **Protest of innocence**—suffering despite faithfulness (cf. Job). "yet you have broken us in the place of jackals and covered us with the shadow of death. If we had forgotten the name of our God or spread out our hands to a foreign god, would not God discover this? For he knows the secrets of the heart" (44:19-21). Appeal to God's omniscience. "Yet for your sake we are killed all the day long; we are regarded as sheep to be slaughtered" (44:22, quoted Rom 8:36—Paul applies to Christian suffering). **Martyrdom for God**. "Awake\! Why are you sleeping, O Lord? Rouse yourself\! Do not reject us forever\! Why do you hide your face? Why do you forget our affliction and oppression? For our soul is bowed down to the dust; our belly clings to the ground. Rise up; come to our aid\! Redeem us for the sake of your steadfast love\!" (44:23-26). **IMPERATIVE PRAYERS**—wake up\! Anthropomorphic—God doesn't literally sleep, but seems inactive. Final appeal to **chesed**. **Psalm ends without resolution**—unusual, reflects genuine perplexity.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Innocent suffering and God's hiddenness*

## Psalm 45: Your Throne, O God, Forever

#### 45:1-9 — The King in His Beauty
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: according to Lilies. A Maskil of the Sons of Korah; a love song" (superscription). **LILIES** (שֹׁשַׁנִּים, shoshannim) = tune name. **Royal wedding psalm**—celebrates earthly king's marriage, yet interpreted messianically. "My heart overflows with a pleasing theme; I address my verses to the king; my tongue is like the pen of a ready scribe. You are the most handsome of the sons of men; grace is poured upon your lips; therefore God has blessed you forever" (45:1-2). **Gracious lips**—eloquent king (cf. Luke 4:22, "gracious words" of Jesus). "Gird your sword on your thigh, O mighty one, in your splendor and majesty\! In your majesty ride out victoriously for the cause of truth and meekness and righteousness; let your right hand teach you awesome deeds\!" (45:3-4). **Divine warrior**—defends truth, meekness, righteousness. "Your arrows are sharp in the heart of the king's enemies; the peoples fall under you. Your throne, O God, is forever and ever. The scepter of your kingdom is a scepter of uprightness" (45:5-6, quoted Heb 1:8—applies to Christ as God). **YOUR THRONE, O GOD** (כִּסְאֲךָ אֱלֹהִים, kis'akha elohim)—king addressed as **elohim** (God/divine). Messianic interpretation: eternal throne. "you have loved righteousness and hated wickedness. Therefore God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness beyond your companions" (45:7, quoted Heb 1:9). **Anointed**—messiah language. Oil of gladness. "your robes are all fragrant with myrrh and aloes and cassia. From ivory palaces stringed instruments make you glad; daughters of kings are among your ladies of honor; at your right hand stands the queen in gold of Ophir" (45:8-9). Royal splendor—fragrant robes, ivory palaces, music, queen in finest gold.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: The divine king in majesty*

#### 45:10-17 — The Bride and Her Glory
**Lore:** "Hear, O daughter, and consider, and incline your ear: forget your people and your father's house, and the king will desire your beauty. Since he is your lord, bow to him" (45:10-11). **Advice to bride**—leave old life, submit to king. **Messianic/Ecclesial interpretation**—church as bride of Christ (Eph 5:25-32, Rev 19:7-9). "The people of Tyre will seek your favor with gifts, the richest of the people" (45:12). International homage. "All glorious is the princess in her chamber, with robes interwoven with gold. In many-colored robes she is led to the king, with her virgin companions following behind her" (45:13-14). Bride's beauty—gold-woven robes. "With joy and gladness they are led along as they enter the palace of the king. In place of your fathers shall be your sons; you will make them princes in all the earth. I will cause your name to be remembered in all generations; therefore nations will praise you forever and ever" (45:15-17). **Posterity promise**—sons become princes. Eternal remembrance. **Royal/Messianic psalm**—earthly wedding pictures Christ and church.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: The glorious bride*

## Psalm 46: God is Our Refuge

#### 46:1-7 — God is Our Refuge and Strength
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster. Of the Sons of Korah. According to Alamoth. A Song" (superscription). **ALAMOTH** (עֲלָמוֹת) = maidens, soprano voices? **BASIS FOR LUTHER'S "A MIGHTY FORTRESS"**. "God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble. Therefore we will not fear though the earth gives way, though the mountains be moved into the heart of the sea, though its waters roar and foam, though the mountains tremble at its swelling. Selah" (46:1-3). **FAMOUS OPENING**—cosmic chaos (earth, mountains, sea) doesn't shake confidence. "There is a river whose streams make glad the city of God, the holy habitation of the Most High. God is in the midst of her; she shall not be moved; God will help her when morning dawns" (46:4-5). **River in city of God**—Jerusalem has no river (spiritual imagery, cf. Ezek 47, Rev 22). "The nations rage, the kingdoms totter; he utters his voice, the earth melts. The LORD of hosts is with us; the stronghold of the God of Jacob. Selah" (46:6-7). **REFRAIN** (repeated v.11)—LORD of hosts (צְבָאוֹת, tzeva'ot) with us. **God of Jacob**—covenant name.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: God our refuge amid chaos*

#### 46:8-11 — Be Still and Know
**Lore:** "Come, behold the works of the LORD, how he has brought desolations on the earth. He makes wars cease to the end of the earth; he breaks the bow and shatters the spear; he burns the chariots with fire" (46:8-9). God ends warfare—bow broken, spear shattered, chariots burned. Messianic peace (Isa 2:4, Mic 4:3). "Be still, and know that I am God. I will be exalted among the nations, I will be exalted in the earth\!" (46:10). **FAMOUS VERSE**—**BE STILL** (הַרְפוּ, harpu) = cease striving, let go, relax. Know God's sovereignty. "The LORD of hosts is with us; the stronghold of the God of Jacob. Selah" (46:11). **Refrain repeated**—Immanuel theology (God with us).
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Be still, God reigns*

## Psalm 47: God Reigns Over the Nations

#### 47:1-9 — Clap Your Hands, All Peoples
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster. A Psalm of the Sons of Korah" (superscription). **Enthronement psalm**—celebrates God's kingship. "Clap your hands, all peoples\! Shout to God with loud songs of joy\! For the LORD, the Most High, is to be feared, a great king over all the earth" (47:1-2). **Universal call**—all peoples worship. **Most High** (עֶלְיוֹן, Elyon) = supreme God. "He subdued peoples under us, and nations under our feet. He chose our heritage for us, the pride of Jacob whom he loves. Selah" (47:3-4). God's victories for Israel. "God has gone up with a shout, the LORD with the sound of a trumpet. Sing praises to God, sing praises\! Sing praises to our King, sing praises\!" (47:5-6). **Liturgical procession**—God ascends (ark entering temple?). Trumpet blast. "For God is the King of all the earth; sing praises with a psalm\! God reigns over the nations; God sits on his holy throne. The princes of the peoples gather as the people of the God of Abraham. For the shields of the earth belong to God; he is highly exalted\!" (47:7-9). **GOD REIGNS**—theme of Book 4 (Pss 93, 96-99). All nations gather to worship God of Abraham. Universal sovereignty.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: God's universal reign*

## Psalm 48: Great is the LORD in Zion

#### 48:1-8 — The City of Our God
**Lore:** "A Song. A Psalm of the Sons of Korah" (superscription). **Zion psalm**—celebrates Jerusalem. "Great is the LORD and greatly to be praised in the city of our God\! His holy mountain, beautiful in elevation, is the joy of all the earth, Mount Zion, in the far north, the city of the great King" (48:1-2). **Holy mountain** = Zion/temple mount. **Far north** (יַרְכְּתֵי צָפוֹן, yarketei tzafon)—mythic language, God's dwelling (cf. Isa 14:13, Canaanite myth of gods on Mt. Zaphon). "Within her citadels God has made himself known as a fortress. For behold, the kings assembled; they came on together. As soon as they saw it, they were astounded; they were in panic; they took to flight" (48:3-5). Enemy kings flee at sight of God's city. "trembling took hold of them there, anguish as of a woman in labor, as when you shatter the ships of Tarshish with the east wind" (48:6-7). Panic like childbirth pain. **Ships of Tarshish**—large trading vessels, destroyed by storm. "As we have heard, so have we seen in the city of the LORD of hosts, in the city of our God, which God will establish forever. Selah" (48:8). Heard tradition, now witnessed—God establishes city forever.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Zion, city of God*

#### 48:9-14 — Walk About Zion
**Lore:** "We have thought on your steadfast love, O God, in the midst of your temple. As your name, O God, so your praise reaches to the ends of the earth. Your right hand is filled with righteousness" (48:9-10). **Steadfast love** (חֶסֶד, chesed) meditated in temple. "Let Mount Zion be glad\! Let the daughters of Judah rejoice because of your judgments\! Walk about Zion, go around her, number her towers, consider well her ramparts, go through her citadels, that you may tell the next generation that this is God, our God forever and ever. He will guide us forever" (48:11-14). **PILGRIMAGE INSTRUCTIONS**—walk Zion's walls, count towers, tell next generation. God forever guides. Anticipates Songs of Ascent (Pss 120-134).
*Verses: 6 | Theme: God's eternal presence in Zion*

## Psalm 49: Why Should I Fear in Times of Trouble?

#### 49:1-12 — No Ransom for Life
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster. A Psalm of the Sons of Korah" (superscription). **Wisdom psalm** on mortality and wealth. "Hear this, all peoples\! Give ear, all inhabitants of the world, both low and high, rich and poor together\! My mouth shall speak wisdom; the meditation of my heart shall be understanding" (49:1-3). **Universal address**—all classes, all peoples. "I will incline my ear to a proverb; I will solve my riddle to the music of the lyre. Why should I fear in times of trouble, when the iniquity of those who cheat me surrounds me, those who trust in their wealth and boast of the abundance of their riches?" (49:4-6). **Riddle/proverb**—why fear rich oppressors? "Truly no man can ransom another, or give to God the price of his life, for the ransom of their life is costly and can never suffice, that he should live on forever and never see the pit" (49:7-9). **No human ransom**—can't buy eternal life (contrast Christ's ransom, Mark 10:45, 1 Tim 2:6). "For he sees that even the wise die; the fool and the stupid alike must perish and leave their wealth to others. Their graves are their homes forever, their dwelling places to all generations, though they called lands by their own names. Man in his pomp will not remain; he is like the beasts that perish" (49:10-12). **Death equalizes**—wise and fool both die. Wealth doesn't transfer. Humans = beasts (mortality, cf. Eccl 3:19-20).
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Wealth cannot ransom from death*

#### 49:13-20 — But God Will Ransom My Soul
**Lore:** "This is the path of those who have foolish confidence; yet after them people approve of their boasts. Selah. Like sheep they are appointed for Sheol; death shall be their shepherd, and the upright shall rule over them in the morning. Their form shall be consumed in Sheol, with no place to dwell" (49:13-14). Foolish trust wealth. **Death their shepherd**—grim reversal of Ps 23. **Morning**—resurrection/vindication for upright? "But God will ransom my soul from the power of Sheol, for he will receive me. Selah" (49:15). **HOPE OF REDEMPTION**—God ransoms from Sheol. **Receive me** (יִקָּחֵנִי, yiqqacheni)—same word as Enoch (Gen 5:24) and Elijah (2 Kings 2:3) taken to heaven. Resurrection hope? "Be not afraid when a man becomes rich, when the glory of his house increases. For when he dies he will carry nothing away; his glory will not go down after him" (49:16-17). Don't fear/envy rich—wealth doesn't follow to grave. "Though, while he lives, he counts himself blessed—and though you get praise when you do well for yourself—his soul will go to the generation of his fathers, who will never again see light. Man in his pomp yet without understanding is like the beasts that perish" (49:18-20). **Refrain** (cf. v.12)—human without understanding = animal. **Wisdom**: fear God, not wealth; God ransoms soul.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: God ransoms from death*

## Psalm 50: The Mighty One Summons the Earth

#### 50:1-6 — God Comes to Judge
**Lore:** "A Psalm of Asaph" (superscription). **ASAPH**—Levitical musician (1 Chr 15:17, 16:5). Author of Pss 50, 73-83. **Covenant lawsuit**—God judges His people. "The Mighty One, God the LORD, speaks and summons the earth from the rising of the sun to its setting. Out of Zion, the perfection of beauty, God shines forth" (50:1-2). **Three divine names**: El, Elohim, Yahweh—emphasizes majesty. God summons all earth from Zion. "Our God comes; he does not keep silence; before him is a devouring fire, around him a mighty tempest. He calls to the heavens above and to the earth, that he may judge his people: 'Gather to me my faithful ones, who made a covenant with me by sacrifice\!'" (50:3-5). **Theophany**—fire, tempest. Covenant people summoned. "The heavens declare his righteousness, for God himself is judge\! Selah" (50:6). **Heavens witness**—cosmic courtroom.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: God summons His people to judgment*

#### 50:7-15 — I Do Not Need Your Sacrifices
**Lore:** "Hear, O my people, and I will speak; O Israel, I will testify against you. I am God, your God" (50:7). **God speaks directly**—rare in Psalms. "Not for your sacrifices do I rebuke you; your burnt offerings are continually before me. I will not accept a bull from your house or goats from your folds. For every beast of the forest is mine, the cattle on a thousand hills" (50:8-10). God doesn't need sacrifices—owns all animals. "I know all the birds of the hills, and all that moves in the field is mine. If I were hungry, I would not tell you, for the world and its fullness are mine" (50:11-12). **Divine self-sufficiency**—God not dependent on offerings (cf. Isa 40:16, Mic 6:6-8). "Do I eat the flesh of bulls or drink the blood of goats? Offer to God a sacrifice of thanksgiving, and perform your vows to the Most High, and call upon me in the day of trouble; I will deliver you, and you shall glorify me" (50:13-15). God desires **thanksgiving** (תּוֹדָה, todah), **vows kept**, **prayer** in trouble—not empty ritual. Heart worship over external form.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: God desires heart, not ritual*

#### 50:16-23 — But to the Wicked God Says
**Lore:** "But to the wicked God says: 'What right have you to recite my statutes or take my covenant on your lips? For you hate discipline, and you cast my words behind you. If you see a thief, you are pleased with him, and you keep company with adulterers'" (50:16-18). **Indictment of hypocrites**—recite law but violate it. Ally with thieves and adulterers. "You give your mouth free rein for evil, and your tongue frames deceit. You sit and speak against your brother; you slander your own mother's son. These things you have done, and I have been silent; you thought that I was one like yourself. But now I rebuke you and lay the charge before you" (50:19-21). **Slander** even of family. God's silence misinterpreted as approval. "Mark this, then, you who forget God, lest I tear you apart, and there be none to deliver\! The one who offers thanksgiving as his sacrifice glorifies me; to one who orders his way rightly I will show the salvation of God\!" (50:22-23). **Warning**—forgetting God brings judgment. **Thanksgiving sacrifice** + **rightly ordered life** = salvation shown. **Prophetic psalm**—emphasizes covenant obedience over ritual.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Rebuke of hypocrisy*

## Psalm 51: Create in Me a Clean Heart

#### 51:1-6 — Have Mercy on Me, O God
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster. A Psalm of David, when Nathan the prophet went to him, after he had gone in to Bathsheba" (superscription). Links to 2 Sam 11-12 (David's adultery, murder, Nathan's confrontation). **MOST FAMOUS PENITENTIAL PSALM** (4th of 7). "Have mercy on me, O God, according to your steadfast love; according to your abundant mercy blot out my transgressions. Wash me thoroughly from my iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin\!" (51:1-2). **Three-fold plea**: mercy (חָנֵּנִי, channeni), steadfast love (חֶסֶד, chesed), abundant mercy (רַחֲמִים, rachamim). **Three terms for sin**: transgressions (פֶּשַׁע, pesha), iniquity (עָוֹן, avon), sin (חַטָּאָה, chata'ah). **Three actions**: blot out, wash, cleanse. "For I know my transgressions, and my sin is ever before me. Against you, you only, have I sinned and done what is evil in your sight, so that you may be justified in your words and blameless in your judgment" (51:3-4, quoted Rom 3:4). **Confession**—sin ultimately against God. God justified in judging. "Behold, I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin did my mother conceive me" (51:5). **Original sin**—sinful from conception (not that conception is sinful, but that sin nature inherited). "Behold, you delight in truth in the inward being, and you teach me wisdom in the secret heart" (51:6). God desires internal truth, not external piety.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Confession and plea for mercy*

#### 51:7-12 — Purge Me with Hyssop
**Lore:** "Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean; wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow" (51:7). **HYSSOP** (אֵזוֹב, ezov)—plant used in purification rituals (Exod 12:22, Lev 14:4, Num 19:18). Cleansing from leprosy, death. "Let me hear joy and gladness; let the bones that you have broken rejoice. Hide your face from my sins, and blot out all my iniquities" (51:8-9). **Broken bones**—metaphor for divine discipline (cf. 32:3-4). Prayer for joy restored. "Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me. Cast me not away from your presence, and take not your Holy Spirit from me. Restore to me the joy of your salvation, and uphold me with a willing spirit" (51:10-12). **FAMOUS VERSES**—**CREATE** (בְּרָא, bara) = only God creates (Gen 1:1 same verb). New creation needed. **Clean heart** (לֵב טָהוֹר, lev tahor). **Right spirit** (רוּחַ נָכוֹן, ruach nakon) = steadfast spirit. **Holy Spirit**—rare OT reference (also Isa 63:10-11). **Joy of salvation** restored.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Prayer for new creation*

#### 51:13-19 — Then I Will Teach Transgressors
**Lore:** "Then I will teach transgressors your ways, and sinners will return to you. Deliver me from bloodguiltiness, O God, O God of my salvation, and my tongue will sing aloud of your righteousness" (51:13-14). **Vow**—if restored, will teach others. **Bloodguiltiness** (דָּמִים, damim)—Uriah's murder (2 Sam 11:15-17). "O Lord, open my lips, and my mouth will declare your praise. For you will not delight in sacrifice, or I would give it; you will not be pleased with a burnt offering" (51:15-16, quoted Heb 10:5-6—Christ's fulfillment). **Sacrifice insufficient**—God desires more. "The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit; a broken and contrite heart, O God, you will not despise. Do good to Zion in your good pleasure; build up the walls of Jerusalem; then will you delight in right sacrifices, in burnt offerings and whole burnt offerings; then bulls will be offered on your altar" (51:17-19). **BROKEN SPIRIT, CONTRITE HEART** (רוּחַ נִשְׁבָּרָה לֵב־נִשְׁבָּר, ruach nishbarah lev-nishbar)—what God requires. Once heart right, then sacrifices acceptable. Prayer for Jerusalem (added during/after exile?). **Psalm of deep repentance**—model for all penitents.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Broken heart God accepts*

## Psalm 52: The Steadfast Love of God

#### 52:1-5 — Why Do You Boast of Evil?
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster. A Maskil of David, when Doeg, the Edomite, came and told Saul, 'David has come to the house of Ahimelech'" (superscription). Links to 1 Sam 22:9-19 (Doeg betrays David, slaughters priests). "Why do you boast of evil, O mighty man? The steadfast love of God endures all the day. Your tongue plots destruction, like a sharp razor, you worker of deceit" (52:1-2). **Tongue as weapon**—razor sharp. "You love evil more than good, and lying more than speaking what is right. Selah. You love all words that devour, O deceitful tongue. But God will break you down forever; he will snatch and tear you from your tent; he will uproot you from the land of the living. Selah" (52:3-5). **Five judgments**: break down, snatch, tear, uproot. Wicked removed from living.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Judgment on deceitful tongue*

#### 52:6-9 — I Am Like a Green Olive Tree
**Lore:** "The righteous shall see and fear, and shall laugh at him, saying, 'See the man who would not make God his refuge, but trusted in the abundance of his riches and sought refuge in his own destruction\!'" (52:6-7). Righteous **laugh** at wicked's downfall (cf. 37:13)—not cruel mockery but vindication. "But I am like a green olive tree in the house of God. I trust in the steadfast love of God forever and ever. I will thank you forever, because you have done it. I will wait for your name, for it is good, in the presence of the godly" (52:8-9). **GREEN OLIVE TREE**—flourishing in God's house (contrast Ps 1 tree, 37:35 wicked's temporary tree). Trust in **chesed**. Perpetual thanksgiving.
*Verses: 4 | Theme: Flourishing in God's steadfast love*

## Psalm 53: The Fool Says There Is No God

#### 53:1-6 — Universal Corruption (Duplicate of Ps 14)
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: according to Mahalath. A Maskil of David" (superscription). **MAHALATH** (מָחֲלַת) = tune name, possibly "sickness" or "dancing." **Duplicate of Ps 14** with minor variations (uses "Elohim" instead of "Yahweh"). "The fool says in his heart, 'There is no God.' They are corrupt, doing abominable iniquity; there is none who does good" (53:1). **FOOL** (נָבָל, naval) = morally deficient (practical atheism). "God looks down from heaven on the children of man to see if there are any who understand, who seek after God. They have all fallen away; together they have become corrupt; there is none who does good, not even one" (53:2-3, quoted Rom 3:10-12). Universal corruption. "Have those who work evil no knowledge, who eat up my people as they eat bread, and do not call upon God?" (53:4). Wicked consume God's people. "There they are, in great terror, where there is no terror\! For God scatters the bones of him who encamps against you; you put them to shame, for God has rejected them. Oh, that salvation for Israel would come out of Zion\! When God restores the fortunes of his people, let Jacob rejoice, let Israel be glad\!" (53:5-6). **Terror without cause**—God strikes enemies. Hope for Zion's salvation.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: The fool denies God*

## Psalm 54: God is My Helper

#### 54:1-7 — Save Me by Your Name
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: with stringed instruments. A Maskil of David, when the Ziphites went and told Saul, 'Is not David hiding among us?'" (superscription). Links to 1 Sam 23:19, 26:1 (Ziphites betray David's location). "O God, save me by your name, and vindicate me by your might. O God, hear my prayer; give ear to the words of my mouth. For strangers have risen against me; ruthless men seek my life; they do not set God before themselves. Selah" (54:1-3). **By your name**—God's reputation/character. Strangers/ruthless = those who ignore God. "Behold, God is my helper; the Lord is the upholder of my life. He will return the evil to my enemies; in your faithfulness put an end to them. With a freewill offering I will sacrifice to you; I will give thanks to your name, O LORD, for it is good. For he has delivered me from every trouble, and my eye has looked in triumph on my enemies" (54:4-7). **God as helper**—confidence despite danger. **Freewill offering** (נְדָבָה, nedavah)—voluntary sacrifice of thanksgiving. Past tense ("has delivered")—anticipatory confidence.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Salvation by God's name*

## Psalm 55: Cast Your Burden on the LORD

#### 55:1-8 — Oh, That I Had Wings Like a Dove
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: with stringed instruments. A Maskil of David" (superscription). "Give ear to my prayer, O God, and hide not yourself from my plea for mercy\! Attend to me, and answer me; I am restless in my complaint and I moan, because of the noise of the enemy, because of the oppression of the wicked" (55:1-3). **Restless complaint**—mental anguish. "My heart is in anguish within me; the terrors of death have fallen upon me. Fear and trembling come upon me, and horror overwhelms me" (55:4-5). Physical symptoms of dread. "And I say, 'Oh, that I had wings like a dove\! I would fly away and be at rest; yes, I would wander far away; I would lodge in the wilderness; Selah. I would hurry to find a shelter from the raging wind and tempest'" (55:6-8). **FAMOUS WISH**—escape like dove to wilderness. Desire for flight from trouble.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Desire to escape*

#### 55:9-15 — Violence and Strife in the City
**Lore:** "Destroy, O Lord, divide their tongues; for I see violence and strife in the city. Day and night they go around it on its walls, and iniquity and trouble are within it; ruin is in its midst; oppression and fraud do not depart from its marketplace" (55:9-11). **Urban chaos**—violence, strife on walls, oppression in marketplace. Prayer to confuse enemies' speech (cf. Babel, Gen 11:7-9). "For it is not an enemy who taunts me—then I could bear it; it is not an adversary who deals insolently with me—then I could hide from him. But it is you, a man, my equal, my companion, my familiar friend. We used to take sweet counsel together; within God's house we walked in the throng" (55:12-14). **Betrayal by close friend**—most painful (cf. 41:9, Judas). Shared worship, sweet fellowship now violated. "Let death steal over them; let them go down to Sheol alive; for evil is in their dwelling place and in their heart" (55:15). **Imprecatory prayer**—like Korah's rebellion (Num 16:30-33).
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Betrayal by friend*

#### 55:16-23 — Cast Your Burden on the LORD
**Lore:** "But I call to God, and the LORD will save me. Evening and morning and at noon I utter my complaint and moan, and he hears my voice" (55:16-17). **Three daily prayers** (cf. Dan 6:10). God hears. "He redeems my soul in safety from the battle that I wage, for many are arrayed against me. God will give ear and humble them, he who is enthroned from of old, Selah, because they do not change and do not fear God" (55:18-19). God's eternal throne guarantees justice. Enemies unchanging in wickedness. "My companion stretched out his hand against his friends; he violated his covenant. His speech was smooth as butter, yet war was in his heart; his words were softer than oil, yet they were drawn swords" (55:20-21). **Betrayer's deceit**—smooth speech, violent intent. "Cast your burden on the LORD, and he will sustain you; he will never permit the righteous to be moved. But you, O God, will cast them down into the pit of destruction; men of blood and treachery shall not live out half their days. But I will trust in you" (55:22-23). **FAMOUS VERSE** (v.22, quoted 1 Pet 5:7)—**CAST YOUR BURDEN** (הַשְׁלֵךְ, hashlekh) = throw, hurl. God sustains. Wicked's short life. Trust declared.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Cast burdens on God*

## Psalm 56: In God I Trust; I Shall Not Be Afraid

#### 56:1-7 — Be Gracious to Me, O God
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: according to The Dove on Far-off Terebinths. A Miktam of David, when the Philistines seized him in Gath" (superscription). **DOVE ON FAR-OFF TEREBINTHS** (יוֹנַת אֵלֶם רְחֹקִים, yonat elem rechoqim) = tune name (uncertain). Links to 1 Sam 21:10-15 (David captured in Gath, feigned madness). **MIKTAM** (cf. Ps 16). "Be gracious to me, O God, for man tramples on me; all day long an attacker oppresses me; my enemies trample on me all day long, for many attack me proudly" (56:1-2). Constant oppression—all day. "When I am afraid, I put my trust in you. In God, whose word I praise, in God I trust; I shall not be afraid. What can flesh do to me?" (56:3-4). **REFRAIN** (vv.4, 10-11)—trust removes fear. **What can flesh do?** (rhetorical—nothing ultimate). "All day long they injure my cause; all their thoughts are against me for evil. They stir up strife, they lurk; they watch my steps, as they have waited for my life" (56:5-6). Enemies plot constantly. "For their crime will they escape? In wrath cast down the peoples, O God\!" (56:7). Prayer for justice.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Trust banishes fear*

#### 56:8-13 — You Have Kept Count of My Tossings
**Lore:** "You have kept count of my tossings; put my tears in your bottle. Are they not in your book?" (56:8). **GOD COUNTS TEARS**—records suffering. **Bottle** (נֹאד, no'd) = wineskin/flask. God preserves each tear (cf. Mal 3:16, "book of remembrance"). "Then my enemies will turn back in the day when I call. This I know, that God is for me. In God, whose word I praise, in the LORD, whose word I praise, in God I trust; I shall not be afraid. What can man do to me?" (56:9-11). **Refrain expanded**—God for me. Parallel: God/LORD. "I must perform my vows to you, O God; I will render thank offerings to you. For you have delivered my soul from death, yes, my feet from falling, that I may walk before God in the light of life" (56:12-13). **Vows kept**—thanksgiving offerings. Deliverance from death to walk in **light of life** (אוֹר הַחַיִּים, or hachayyim)—continued existence, God's presence.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: God records every tear*

## Psalm 57: Be Exalted, O God, Above the Heavens

#### 57:1-6 — My Soul Takes Refuge in the Shadow of Your Wings
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: according to Do Not Destroy. A Miktam of David, when he fled from Saul, in the cave" (superscription). **DO NOT DESTROY** (אַל־תַּשְׁחֵת, al-tashchet) = tune name (also Pss 58-59, 75). Links to 1 Sam 22:1 (Adullam cave) or 24:3 (En-gedi cave). "Be merciful to me, O God, be merciful to me, for in you my soul takes refuge; in the shadow of your wings I will take refuge, till the storms of destruction pass by" (57:1). **Shadow of wings**—cherubim imagery, divine protection (cf. Ruth 2:12). Storms pass. "I cry out to God Most High, to God who fulfills his purpose for me. He will send from heaven and save me; he will put to shame him who tramples on me. Selah. God will send out his steadfast love and his faithfulness\!" (57:2-3). **God Most High** (אֵל עֶלְיוֹן, El Elyon). God fulfills purpose, sends **chesed** and **faithfulness**. "My soul is in the midst of lions; I lie down amid fiery beasts—the children of man, whose teeth are spears and arrows, whose tongues are sharp swords" (57:4). **Lion imagery**—enemies as predators. Tongues = weapons. "They set a net for my steps; my soul was bowed down. They dug a pit in my way, but they have fallen into it themselves. Selah" (57:6). **Retributive justice**—trapped in own snare (cf. 7:15, 9:15, 35:8).
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Refuge under God's wings*

#### 57:7-11 — My Heart is Steadfast
**Lore:** "My heart is steadfast, O God, my heart is steadfast\! I will sing and make melody\! Awake, my glory\! Awake, O harp and lyre\! I will awake the dawn\!" (57:7-8). **STEADFAST HEART** (נָכוֹן לִבִּי, nakon libbi) repeated—firm resolve. **Awake the dawn**—wake up early to praise (anticipate sunrise). "I will give thanks to you, O Lord, among the peoples; I will sing praises to you among the nations. For your steadfast love is great to the heavens, your faithfulness to the clouds. Be exalted, O God, above the heavens\! Let your glory be over all the earth\!" (57:9-11). **International praise**—nations will hear. **Cosmic attributes** (chesed to heavens, faithfulness to clouds). **REFRAIN** (v.5 and v.11)—"Be exalted... glory over earth." Moves from hiding in cave to global worship. **Psalm duplicated** partially in Ps 108:1-5.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Steadfast heart praises*

## Psalm 58: Do You Indeed Speak Justice?

#### 58:1-5 — The Wicked Go Astray from Birth
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: according to Do Not Destroy. A Miktam of David" (superscription). "Do you indeed decree what is right, you gods? Do you judge the children of man uprightly?" (58:1). **GODS** (אֵלִים, elim) = rulers/judges (cf. Ps 82, Exod 21:6, 22:8-9) or divine council. Rhetorical question—accusation of unjust judges. "No, in your hearts you devise wrongs; your hands deal out violence on earth. The wicked are estranged from the womb; they go astray from birth, speaking lies" (58:2-3). **Original sin**—wickedness from birth. "They have venom like the venom of a serpent, like the deaf adder that stops its ear, so that it does not hear the voice of charmers or of the cunning enchanter" (58:4-5). **Serpent imagery**—deaf to correction. Snake charmer can't control.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Unjust rulers*

#### 58:6-11 — Let Them Vanish Like Water
**Lore:** "O God, break the teeth in their mouths; tear out the fangs of the young lions, O LORD\! Let them vanish like water that runs away; when he aims his arrows, let them be blunted. Let them be like the snail that dissolves into slime, like the stillborn child who never sees the sun" (58:6-8). **IMPRECATORY PSALM**—six violent metaphors: broken teeth, vanishing water, blunted arrows, dissolving snail, stillborn child, thorns burned. "Sooner than your pots can feel the heat of thorns, whether green or ablaze, may he sweep them away\! The righteous will rejoice when he sees the vengeance; he will bathe his feet in the blood of the wicked. Mankind will say, 'Surely there is a reward for the righteous; surely there is a God who judges on earth'" (58:9-11). **Vindication**—righteous rejoice at justice (not cruelty but relief). Blood-bathing = complete victory. **Theodicy affirmed**—God judges earth, rewards righteous.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Judgment on unjust judges*

## Psalm 59: Deliver Me from My Enemies

#### 59:1-5 — My Strength, I Will Watch for You
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: according to Do Not Destroy. A Miktam of David, when Saul sent men to watch his house in order to kill him" (superscription). Links to 1 Sam 19:11-17 (Michal helps David escape through window). "Deliver me from my enemies, O my God; protect me from those who rise up against me; deliver me from those who work evil, and save me from bloodthirsty men" (59:1-2). Three-fold petition: deliver, protect, save. "For behold, they lie in wait for my life; fierce men stir up strife against me. For no transgression or sin of mine, O LORD, for no fault of mine, they run and make ready. Awake, come to meet me, and see\!" (59:3-4). **Protest of innocence**—unprovoked attack. "You, LORD God of hosts, are God of Israel. Rouse yourself to punish all the nations; spare none of those who treacherously plot evil. Selah" (59:5). **LORD God of hosts**—military title.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Protection from ambush*

#### 59:6-10 — They Howl Like Dogs
**Lore:** "Each evening they come back, howling like dogs and prowling around the city. There they are, bellowing with their mouths with swords in their lips—for 'Who,' they think, 'will hear us?'" (59:6-7). **Dog imagery**—enemies prowl, bark. Arrogant speech ("who hears?"). "But you, O LORD, laugh at them; you hold all the nations in derision. O my Strength, I will watch for you, for you, O God, are my fortress. My God in his steadfast love will meet me; God will let me look in triumph on my enemies" (59:8-10). God **laughs** at enemies (cf. 2:4, 37:13). **REFRAIN**—"O my Strength" (vv.9, 17). God as fortress. Chesed meets psalmist.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: God laughs at enemies*

#### 59:11-17 — But I Will Sing of Your Strength
**Lore:** "Kill them not, lest my people forget; make them totter by your power and bring them down, O Lord, our shield\!" (59:11). Prayer for prolonged judgment—not immediate death but visible downfall so people remember. "For the sin of their mouths, the words of their lips, let them be trapped in their pride. For the cursing and lies that they utter, consume them in wrath; consume them till they are no more, that they may know that God rules over Jacob to the ends of the earth. Selah" (59:12-13). **Sin of speech**—cursing, lies. Complete consumption teaches God's sovereignty. "Each evening they come back, howling like dogs and prowling around the city. They wander about for food and growl if they are not satisfied. But I will sing of your strength; I will sing aloud of your steadfast love in the morning. For you have been to me a fortress and a refuge in the day of my distress. O my Strength, I will sing praises to you, for you, O God, are my fortress, the God who shows me steadfast love" (59:14-17). **Dog refrain** (vv.6, 14). **Morning song**—praise at dawn. **Refrain** concluded—strength, fortress, chesed. Lament becomes praise.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Morning praise for God's strength*

## Psalm 60: You Have Made the Land Quake

#### 60:1-5 — O God, You Have Rejected Us
**Lore:** "To the choirmaster: according to Shushan Eduth. A Miktam of David; for instruction; when he strove with Aram-naharaim and with Aram-zobah, and when Joab on his return struck down twelve thousand of Edom in the Valley of Salt" (superscription). **SHUSHAN EDUTH** (שׁוּשַׁן עֵדוּת) = "Lily of Testimony," tune name. Links to 2 Sam 8:3-14, 10:6-19, 1 Chr 18:3-13 (David's Aramean and Edomite wars). "O God, you have rejected us, broken our defenses; you have been angry; oh, restore us. You have made the land to quake; you have torn it open; repair its breaches, for it totters. You have made your people see hard things; you have given us wine to drink that made us reel" (60:1-3). **National lament**—military defeat, earthquake metaphor. Wine of God's wrath (cf. Isa 51:17, Jer 25:15). "You have set up a banner for those who fear you, that they may flee to it from the bow. Selah. That your beloved ones may be delivered, give salvation by your right hand and answer us\!" (60:4-5). **Banner**—rallying point. Prayer for deliverance of beloved (Israel).
*Verses: 5 | Theme: National defeat and prayer*

#### 60:6-12 — With God We Shall Do Valiantly
**Lore:** "God has spoken in his holiness: 'With exultation I will divide up Shechem and portion out the Vale of Succoth. Gilead is mine; Manasseh is mine; Ephraim is my helmet; Judah is my scepter. Moab is my washbasin; upon Edom I cast my shoe; over Philistia I shout in triumph'" (60:6-8). **DIVINE ORACLE**—God declares ownership. **Shechem, Succoth, Gilead, Manasseh, Ephraim, Judah** = Israel's territory. **Moab** = washbasin (servant). **Edom** = sandal (conquest, cf. Ruth 4:7-8). **Philistia** = defeated. "Who will bring me to the fortified city? Who will lead me to Edom? Have you not rejected us, O God? You do not go out, O God, with our armies. Oh, grant us help against the foe, for vain is the salvation of man\! With God we shall do valiantly; it is he who will tread down our foes" (60:9-12). **Petition**—lead us to victory. **Vain human salvation** (cf. 146:3). **With God, valiant**—confidence in divine help. **Psalm duplicated** partially in Ps 108:6-13.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Confidence in God for victory*

## Psalm 71: Do Not Cast Me Off in Old Age

#### 71:1-8 — You Are My Hope from My Youth
**Lore:** No superscription. "In you, O LORD, do I take refuge; let me never be put to shame\!" (71:1). Refuge plea. "For you, O Lord, are my hope, my trust, O LORD, from my youth. Upon you I have leaned from before my birth; you are he who took me from my mother's womb" (71:5-6). **Lifelong trust**—from youth, from birth.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Lifelong trust*

#### 71:9-18 — Do Not Forsake Me in Old Age
**Lore:** "Do not cast me off in the time of old age; forsake me not when my strength is spent" (71:9). **Prayer of the aged**. "But I will hope continually and will praise you yet more and more. My mouth will tell of your righteous acts, of your deeds of salvation all the day" (71:14-15). Continual hope and praise. "So even to old age and gray hairs, O God, do not forsake me, until I proclaim your might to another generation, your power to all those to come" (71:18). **Testament to next generation**—witness to God's power.
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Prayer not to be abandoned in old age*

#### 71:19-24 — You Will Revive Me Again
**Lore:** "Your righteousness, O God, reaches the high heavens. You who have done great things, O God, who is like you? You who have made me see many troubles and calamities will revive me again; from the depths of the earth you will bring me up again" (71:19-20). **Resurrection hope**—brought up from depths. "My lips will shout for joy, when I sing praises to you; my soul also, which you have redeemed. And my tongue will talk of your righteous help all the day long" (71:23-24). Perpetual praise for redemption.
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Hope in revival and redemption*

## Psalm 72: The Messianic King's Righteous Reign

#### 72:1-7 — Give the King Your Justice
**Lore:** "Of Solomon" (superscription). **ROYAL/MESSIANIC PSALM**—final psalm of Book 2, describes ideal king. "Give the king your justice, O God, and your righteousness to the royal son\! May he judge your people with righteousness, and your poor with justice\!" (72:1-2). Prayer for king's righteous rule. "Let the mountains bear prosperity for the people, and the hills, in righteousness\! May he defend the cause of the poor of the people, give deliverance to the children of the needy, and crush the oppressor\!" (72:3-4). **Social justice**—defends poor, crushes oppressors. "May they fear you while the sun endures, and as long as the moon, throughout all generations\! May he be like rain that falls on the mown grass, like showers that water the earth\! In his days may the righteous flourish, and peace abound, till the moon be no more\!" (72:5-7). **Eternal reign**—while sun/moon endure. King as **rain**—brings flourishing, peace.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Prayer for righteous king*

#### 72:8-14 — He Shall Have Dominion
**Lore:** "May he have dominion from sea to sea, and from the River to the ends of the earth\! May desert tribes bow down before him, and his enemies lick the dust\! May the kings of Tarshish and of the coastlands render him tribute; may the kings of Sheba and Seba bring gifts\! May all kings fall down before him, all nations serve him\!" (72:8-11). **UNIVERSAL DOMINION**—sea to sea (Mediterranean to Persian Gulf? Or global?). **River** = Euphrates. International homage. **Tarshish** (Spain?), **Sheba** (Yemen/Ethiopia), **Seba** bring gifts (cf. Matt 2:1-11, Magi bring gifts). "For he delivers the needy when he calls, the poor and him who has no helper. He has pity on the weak and the needy, and saves the lives of the needy. From oppression and violence he redeems their life, and precious is their blood in his sight" (72:12-14). **Compassionate king**—delivers needy, redeems from violence. **Precious blood**—values human life.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Universal dominion and compassion*

#### 72:15-20 — Blessed Be His Glorious Name Forever
**Lore:** "Long may he live; may gold of Sheba be given to him\! May prayer be made for him continually, and blessings invoked for him all the day\! May there be abundance of grain in the land; on the tops of the mountains may it wave; may its fruit be like Lebanon; and may people blossom in the cities like the grass of the field\!" (72:15-16). Prosperity—gold, grain, population growth. "May his name endure forever, his fame continue as long as the sun\! May people be blessed in him, all nations call him blessed\!" (72:17). **Abrahamic promise** fulfilled (Gen 12:3, "all nations blessed"). **Eternal name**. "Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel, who alone does wondrous things. Blessed be his glorious name forever; may the whole earth be filled with his glory\! Amen and Amen\!" (72:18-19). **DOXOLOGY ENDING BOOK 2**—echoes Rev 5:13. **Double Amen**—emphatic. "The prayers of David, the son of Jesse, are ended" (72:20). **Colophon**—marks end of Davidic collection (though more Davidic psalms in Books 3-5).
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Eternal blessing and glory*

Total Pericopes in Book 2: 68 pericopes covering 31 psalms, featuring rich lore on communal lament, Messianic kingship, and culminating in Psalm 72's vision of righteous global reign.
