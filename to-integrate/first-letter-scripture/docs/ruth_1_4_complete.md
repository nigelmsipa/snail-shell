# Ruth 1-4: Covenant Loyalty and Redemption

## Overview
Ruth is set "in the days when the judges ruled" (1:1)—the dark period of Judges. Yet it shines as a beacon of **hesed** (חֶסֶד, covenant loyalty/lovingkindness) amidst moral chaos. The book demonstrates that even during national apostasy, individuals can still walk in faithful covenant relationship with God. Central theme: God's providence orchestrates redemption through loyal human agents. Ruth = Moabite convert who becomes great-grandmother of David, ancestor of Christ. Perfect 4-act structure: Tragedy (ch 1) → Providence (ch 2) → Plea (ch 3) → Redemption (ch 4). Contrast to Judges: "everyone did right in own eyes" vs. covenant faithfulness.

## Act I: The Loyalty of the Sojourner (Chapter 1)

### Chapter 1: From Fullness to Emptiness

#### 1:1-5 — Tragedy in Moab
**Lore:** "In the days when the judges ruled there was a famine in the land, and a man of Bethlehem in Judah went to sojourn in the country of Moab, he and his wife and his two sons" (1:1). Irony: **Bethlehem** = "house of bread," yet famine forces departure. Judges period = spiritual/physical hunger. "The name of the man was Elimelech and the name of his wife Naomi, and the names of his two sons were Mahlon and Chilion. They were Ephrathites from Bethlehem in Judah. They went into the country of Moab and remained there" (1:2). Names significant: Elimelech = "my God is king," Naomi = "pleasant," Mahlon = "sickly," Chilion = "failing." Moab = descended from Lot's incest (Gen 19:37), historical enemy (Num 22-25), excluded from assembly (Deut 23:3-6). "But Elimelech, the husband of Naomi, died, and she was left with her two sons. These took Moabite wives; the name of the one was Orpah and the name of the other Ruth. They lived there about ten years, and both Mahlon and Chilion died, so that the woman was left without her two sons and her husband" (1:3-5). **Triple tragedy**—husband, two sons dead. Naomi = utterly vulnerable widow in foreign land. No heir, no security. Emptied.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Famine and death*

#### 1:6-14 — Decision to Return
**Lore:** "Then she arose with her daughters-in-law to return from the country of Moab, for she had heard in the fields of Moab that the LORD had visited his people and given them food" (1:6). God ends famine—time to return. "So she set out from the place where she was with her two daughters-in-law, and they went on the way to return to the land of Judah" (1:7). All three start journey. "But Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, 'Go, return each of you to her mother's house. May the LORD deal kindly with you, as you have dealt with the dead and with me. The LORD grant that you may find rest, each of you in the house of her husband!' Then she kissed them, and they lifted up their voices and wept" (1:8-9). Naomi releases them—seeks their security in remarriage. First mention of **hesed** (חֶסֶד) = "deal kindly" = covenant loyalty. "And they said to her, 'No, we will return with you to your people.' But Naomi said, 'Turn back, my daughters; why will you go with me? Have I yet sons in my womb that they may become your husbands?'" (1:10-11). Allusion to levirate marriage law (Deut 25:5-10). "Turn back, my daughters; go your way, for I am too old to have a husband. If I should say I have hope, even if I should have a husband this night and should bear sons, would you therefore wait till they were grown? Would you therefore refrain from marrying? No, my daughters, for it is exceedingly bitter to me for your sake that the hand of the LORD has gone out against me'" (1:12-13). Naomi sees God's hand against her—theological interpretation of suffering. "Then they lifted up their voices and wept again. And Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth clung to her" (1:14). Orpah leaves (sensible choice), Ruth clings (דָּבַק, dabaq = same word for marriage bond, Gen 2:24).
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Choose to return*

#### 1:15-22 — Ruth's Vow and Bitter Return
**Lore:** "And she said, 'See, your sister-in-law has gone back to her people and to her gods; return after your sister-in-law.' But Ruth said, 'Do not urge me to leave you or to return from following you. For where you go I will go, and where you lodge I will lodge. Your people shall be my people, and your God my God. Where you die I will die, and there will I be buried. May the LORD do so to me and more also if anything but death parts me from you'" (1:15-17). **COVENANT VOW**—one of most beautiful passages in Scripture! Ruth chooses: (1) Naomi's destination, (2) Naomi's people (Israel), (3) Naomi's God (Yahweh), (4) Naomi's burial place. Converts from Moabite paganism to Israelite monotheism. Oath formula = binding covenant. "And when Naomi saw that she was determined to go with her, she said no more" (1:18). Accepts Ruth's loyalty. "So the two of them went on until they came to Bethlehem. And when they came to Bethlehem, the whole town was stirred because of them. And the women said, 'Is this Naomi?'" (1:19). After 10+ years, town remembers. "She said to them, 'Do not call me Naomi; call me Mara, for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me. I went away full, and the LORD has brought me back empty. Why call me Naomi, when the LORD has testified against me and the Almighty has brought calamity upon me?'" (1:20-21). Name change: Naomi = "pleasant" → **Mara** (מָרָה) = "bitter." Echoes Exodus 15:23 (bitter waters). Theology: sees God's sovereign hand in tragedy. "So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabite her daughter-in-law with her, who returned from the country of Moab. And they came to Bethlehem at the beginning of barley harvest" (1:22). **Key detail**—barley harvest = spring (April/May), Passover season. God's providence timing. Ruth = "Moabite" emphasized (foreigner), yet God includes her.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Covenant loyalty*

## Act II: The Wings of the Redeemer (Chapter 2)

### Chapter 2: Providence in the Field

#### 2:1-7 — Ruth Gleans in Boaz's Field
**Lore:** "Now Naomi had a relative of her husband's, a worthy man of the clan of Elimelech, whose name was Boaz" (2:1). **Boaz** (בֹּעַז) = "in him is strength." "Worthy man" = אִישׁ גִּבּוֹר חַיִל (ish gibbor chayil) = man of valor/wealth. Sets up go'el. "And Ruth the Moabite said to Naomi, 'Let me go to the field and glean among the ears of grain after him in whose sight I shall find favor.' And she said to her, 'Go, my daughter'" (2:2). **Gleaning**—Levitical law for poor/foreigner (Lev 19:9-10; 23:22; Deut 24:19). God's provision system. Ruth = industrious, humble. "So she set out and went and gleaned in the field after the reapers, and she happened to come to the part of the field belonging to Boaz, who was of the clan of Elimelech" (2:3). **KEY PHRASE**: "she happened" (וַיִּקֶר מִקְרֶהָ, vayiqer miqreh) = "her hap was" = chance/accident. BUT—divine providence! No accident—God orchestrates. "And behold, Boaz came from Bethlehem. And he said to the reapers, 'The LORD be with you!' And they answered, 'The LORD bless you'" (2:4). Godly greeting—contrast to Judges chaos. "Then Boaz said to his young man who was in charge of the reapers, 'Whose young woman is this?' And the servant who was in charge of the reapers answered, 'She is the young Moabite woman, who came back with Naomi from the country of Moab. She said, "Please let me glean and gather among the sheaves after the reapers." So she came, and she has continued from early morning until now, except for a short rest'" (2:5-7). Ruth's reputation precedes her—industrious, respectful. "Moabite" noted again, yet Boaz interested.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Divine providence*

#### 2:8-16 — Boaz Shows Extraordinary Kindness
**Lore:** "Then Boaz said to Ruth, 'Now, listen, my daughter, do not go to glean in another field or leave this one, but keep close to my young women. Let your eyes be on the field that they are reaping, and go after them. Have I not charged the young men not to touch you? And when you are thirsty, go to the vessels and drink what the young men have drawn'" (2:8-9). Extraordinary protection and provision! Commands men not to harass her. "Then she fell on her face, bowing to the ground, and said to him, 'Why have I found favor in your eyes, that you should take notice of me, since I am a foreigner?'" (2:10). Ruth recognizes grace—she's Moabite outsider. "But Boaz answered her, 'All that you have done for your mother-in-law since the death of your husband has been fully told to me, and how you left your father and mother and your native land and came to a people that you did not know before. The LORD repay you for what you have done, and a full reward be given you by the LORD, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge!'" (2:11-12). **THEOLOGICAL BLESSING**—"under whose wings" (תַּחַת כְּנָפָיו, tachat kenafav) = like Psalm 91:4. Wings = protection, refuge. Ruth = converted, seeking shelter under Yahweh's wings. Foreshadows 3:9. "Then she said, 'I have found favor in your eyes, my lord, for you have comforted me and spoken kindly to your servant, though I am not one of your servants'" (2:13). Humble gratitude. "And at mealtime Boaz said to her, 'Come here and eat some bread and dip your morsel in the wine.' So she sat beside the reapers, and he passed to her roasted grain. And she ate until she was satisfied, and she had some left over" (2:14). Shared meal—social inclusion, abundance. "When she rose to glean, Boaz instructed his young men, saying, 'Let her glean even among the sheaves, and do not reproach her. And also pull out some from the bundles for her and leave it for her to glean, and do not rebuke her'" (2:15-16). **Extra grace**—deliberately leaves grain! Goes beyond law. Hesed embodied.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Grace and refuge*

#### 2:17-23 — Naomi's Hope Renewed
**Lore:** "So she gleaned in the field until evening. Then she beat out what she had gleaned, and it was about an ephah of barley" (2:17). Ephah = ~22 liters/half bushel. Extraordinary amount for one day—Boaz's generosity! "And she took it up and went into the city. Her mother-in-law saw what she had gleaned. She also brought out and gave her what food she had left over after being satisfied. And her mother-in-law said to her, 'Where did you glean today? And where have you worked? Blessed be the man who took notice of you.' So she told her mother-in-law with whom she had worked and said, 'The man's name with whom I worked today is Boaz'" (2:18-19). Naomi's interest piqued. "Then Naomi said to her daughter-in-law, 'May he be blessed by the LORD, whose kindness (חֶסֶד, hesed) has not forsaken the living or the dead!' Naomi also said to her, 'The man is a close relative of ours, one of our redeemers (גֹּאֵל, go'el)'" (2:20). **PIVOTAL MOMENT**—Naomi recognizes: (1) God's hesed still operative, (2) Boaz is kinsman-redeemer! **Go'el** = kinsman with right/duty to redeem family member from slavery, buy back land, marry widow to raise heir (Lev 25:25-55; Deut 25:5-10). Type of Christ the Redeemer. "And Ruth the Moabite said, 'Besides, he said to me, "You shall keep close by my young men until they have finished all my harvest"'" (2:21). Ongoing provision. "And Naomi said to Ruth, her daughter-in-law, 'It is good, my daughter, that you go out with his young women, lest in another field you be assaulted.' So she kept close to the young women of Boaz, gleaning until the end of the barley and wheat harvests. And she lived with her mother-in-law" (2:22-23). Safety and continued provision for ~7 weeks (barley = April/May, wheat = May/June).
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Kinsman-redeemer revealed*

## Act III: The Plea at the Threshing Floor (Chapter 3)

### Chapter 3: The Bold Request

#### 3:1-9 — Naomi's Plan
**Lore:** "Then Naomi her mother-in-law said to her, 'My daughter, should I not seek rest (מָנוֹחַ, manoach) for you, that it may be well with you? Is not Boaz our relative, with whose young women you were? See, he is winnowing barley tonight at the threshing floor. Wash therefore and anoint yourself, and put on your cloak and go down to the threshing floor, but do not make yourself known to the man until he has finished eating and drinking'" (3:1-3). Naomi's bold plan! "Rest" = security in marriage (1:9). Threshing floor = community space, harvest celebration. Instructions: clean up, go at night. Risky! "And when he lies down, observe the place where he lies. Then go and uncover his feet and lie down, and he will tell you what to do.' And she replied, 'All that you say I will do'" (3:4-5). "Uncover his feet" (גָּלָה מַרְגְּלֹתָיו, galah margelotav) = reveal/uncover, feet/legs. Ambiguous! Some see sexual overtones, but text presents as virtuous legal claim. Ruth trusts Naomi, obeys completely. "So she went down to the threshing floor and did just as her mother-in-law had commanded her. And when Boaz had eaten and drunk, and his heart was merry, he went to lie down at the end of the heap of grain. Then she came softly and uncovered his feet and lay down" (3:6-7). Executes plan—vulnerable position! "At midnight the man was startled and turned over, and behold, a woman lay at his feet! He said, 'Who are you?' And she answered, 'I am Ruth, your servant. Spread your wings (כְּנָפֶךָ, kenafekha) over your servant, for you are a redeemer (גֹּאֵל, go'el)'" (3:8-9). **CLIMACTIC REQUEST**! "Spread your wings" = idiom for marriage (Ezek 16:8). Echoes 2:12—Boaz prayed she find refuge under God's wings; now she asks him to BE those wings! Legal claim on go'el. Not seduction but covenant request.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Covenant claim*

#### 3:10-18 — Boaz Responds with Integrity
**Lore:** "And he said, 'May you be blessed by the LORD, my daughter. You have made this last kindness (חֶסֶד, hesed) greater than the first in that you have not gone after young men, whether poor or rich'" (3:10). Boaz recognizes Ruth's SECOND act of hesed! First = loyalty to Naomi (ch 1). Second = seeking to raise heir for Elimelech's line rather than marrying younger man for herself. Counter-intuitive loyalty! "And now, my daughter, do not fear. I will do for you all that you ask, for all my fellow townsmen know that you are a worthy woman (אֵשֶׁת חַיִל, eshet chayil)" (3:11). "Worthy woman" = woman of valor/noble character. Same phrase as Prov 31:10! Matches Boaz as "worthy man" (2:1). "And now it is true that I am a redeemer (גֹּאֵל, go'el). Yet there is a redeemer nearer than I. Remain tonight, and in the morning, if he will redeem you, good; let him do it. But if he is not willing to redeem you, then, as the LORD lives, I will redeem you. Lie down until the morning'" (3:12-13). **Integrity**—Boaz acknowledges closer relative has first rights! Won't bypass law even though he wants to marry Ruth. Swears oath. "So she lay at his feet until the morning, but arose before one could recognize another. And he said, 'Let it not be known that the woman came to the threshing floor.' And he said, 'Bring the garment you are wearing and hold it out.' So she held it, and he measured out six measures of barley and put it on her. Then she went into the city" (3:14-15). Protects her reputation—leaves before dawn. Six measures barley = generous gift, pledge of intent. "And when she came to her mother-in-law, she said, 'How did you fare, my daughter?' Then she told her all that the man had done for her, saying, 'These six measures of barley he gave to me, for he said to me, "You must not go back empty-handed to your mother-in-law"'" (3:16-17). Irony—Naomi returned "empty" (1:21), now being filled! "She replied, 'Wait, my daughter, until you learn how the matter turns out, for the man will not rest but will settle the matter today'" (3:18). Naomi confident in Boaz's character.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Integrity and hesed*

## Act IV: The Redemption at the Gate (Chapter 4)

### Chapter 4: Legal Redemption and Messianic Line

#### 4:1-12 — Transaction at the Gate
**Lore:** "Now Boaz had gone up to the gate and sat down there. And behold, the redeemer (גֹּאֵל, go'el), of whom Boaz had spoken, came by. So Boaz said, 'Turn aside, friend; sit down here.' And he turned aside and sat down" (4:1). City gate = legal court, public assembly place. Boaz acts immediately. "And he took ten men of the elders of the city and said, 'Sit down here.' So they sat down" (4:2). Ten witnesses—proper legal proceeding. "Then he said to the redeemer, 'Naomi, who has come back from the country of Moab, is selling the parcel of land that belonged to our relative Elimelech. So I thought I would tell you of it and say, "Buy it in the presence of those sitting here and in the presence of the elders of my people." If you will redeem it, redeem it. But if you will not, tell me, that I may know, for there is no one besides you to redeem it, and I come after you'" (4:3-4). Offers land first. Clever! "And he said, 'I will redeem it'" (4:4). Closer relative accepts—wants land! "Then Boaz said, 'The day you buy the field from the hand of Naomi, you also acquire Ruth the Moabite, the widow of the dead, in order to perpetuate the name of the dead in his inheritance'" (4:5). **REVEAL**: Package deal—land + Moabite widow + obligation to raise heir! "Then the redeemer said, 'I cannot redeem it for myself, lest I impair my own inheritance. Take my right of redemption yourself, for I cannot redeem it'" (4:6). Refuses! Reasons: (1) Cost of marrying Ruth, (2) Raised heir would inherit land (wouldn't go to his existing sons), (3) Ruth = Moabite foreigner. Prejudice shows. "Now this was the custom in former times in Israel concerning redeeming and exchanging: to confirm a transaction, the one drew off his sandal and gave it to the other, and this was the manner of attesting in Israel. So when the redeemer said to Boaz, 'Buy it for yourself,' he drew off his sandal" (4:7-8). **Sandal ceremony**—validates transfer of redemption rights. Echoes Deut 25:9-10 (refusing levirate duty = shame). "Then Boaz said to the elders and all the people, 'You are witnesses this day that I have bought from the hand of Naomi all that belonged to Elimelech and all that belonged to Chilion and to Mahlon. Also Ruth the Moabite, the widow of Mahlon, I have bought to be my wife, to perpetuate the name of the dead in his inheritance, that the name of the dead may not be cut off from among his brothers and from the gate of his native place. You are witnesses this day'" (4:9-10). **PUBLIC DECLARATION**—Boaz redeems land AND marries Ruth! Legal, honorable. "Then all the people who were at the gate and the elders said, 'We are witnesses. May the LORD make the woman, who is coming into your house, like Rachel and Leah, who together built up the house of Israel. May you act worthily in Ephrathah and be renowned in Bethlehem, and may your house be like the house of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah, because of the offspring that the LORD will give you by this young woman'" (4:11-12). **Prophetic blessing**! Comparison to Rachel, Leah (matriarchs), and Tamar (another foreigner in messianic line—Gen 38). Perez = ancestor of Boaz, born through levirate-type situation.
*Verses: 12 | Theme: Public redemption*

#### 4:13-17 — Birth of Obed
**Lore:** "So Boaz took Ruth, and she became his wife. And he went in to her, and the LORD gave her conception, and she bore a son" (4:13). God opens womb—sovereign grace. "Then the women said to Naomi, 'Blessed be the LORD, who has not left you this day without a redeemer, and may his name be renowned in Israel! He shall be to you a restorer of life and a nourisher of your old age, for your daughter-in-law who loves you, who is more to you than seven sons, has given birth to him'" (4:14-15). Women's chorus—bookends with 1:19-21. Go'el now refers to grandson! "More than seven sons" = highest praise (seven = perfect number). Ruth's hesed > biological sons. "Then Naomi took the child and laid him on her lap and became his nurse. And the women of the neighborhood gave him a name, saying, 'A son has been born to Naomi.' They named him Obed. He was the father of Jesse, the father of David" (4:16-17). **OBED** (עוֹבֵד) = "servant/worshiper." Naomi's emptiness FILLED! Grandfather of David—messianic line secured! Ruth the Moabite becomes David's great-grandmother.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Emptiness to fullness*

#### 4:18-22 — Genealogy: Perez to David
**Lore:** "Now these are the generations of Perez: Perez fathered Hezron, Hezron fathered Ram, Ram fathered Amminadab, Amminadab fathered Nahshon, Nahshon fathered Salmon, Salmon fathered Boaz, Boaz fathered Obed, Obed fathered Jesse, and Jesse fathered David" (4:18-22). **TEN GENERATIONS** from Perez to David. Bracketed genealogy: begins book's purpose. Places Ruth's story in redemptive history. Matthew 1:5 includes Ruth (and Tamar, Rahab, Bathsheba) in Jesus's genealogy—God uses unlikely people, even Gentiles/foreigners! Perez (Tamar) → Boaz (Rahab) → Obed (Ruth) → David → Jesus. Theme: God's sovereign grace includes outsiders, works through hesed, orchestrates redemption through faithful remnant even in darkest times.
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Messianic genealogy*

**RUTH 1-4 COMPLETE!**

**Pericopes: 10** (4 chapters = 2.5/chapter)

**Book Summary:** From emptiness (Mara) to fullness (Obed). Ruth's hesed toward Naomi, Boaz's hesed toward Ruth, God's hesed toward all. Divine providence ("her hap") orchestrates redemption. Go'el (kinsman-redeemer) typology points to Christ. Moabite outsider becomes David's ancestor, Christ's lineage. Beautiful contrast to Judges chaos—individuals can walk faithfully even when nation fails. Key Hebrew terms: hesed (covenant loyalty), go'el (redeemer), manoach (rest/security), kenafekha (wings of protection). Themes: Providence, redemption, inclusion of Gentiles, faithful remnant, messianic hope.
