# Job 32-37: Elihu — The Angry Young Man

## Overview
**Sudden new character**: Elihu ben Barachel the Buzite (32:2), silent during dialogue, now erupts. **Elihu's anger**—at Job for self-justification, at three friends for condemning without answering. **Elihu's claim**—younger but wiser (spirit gives understanding, not age). **Elihu's speeches** (6 uninterrupted chapters): (1) Preamble explaining anger (Ch 32); (2) Refutation of Job (Ch 33); (3) God's justice defended (Ch 34); (4) Pride addressed (Ch 35); (5) God's greatness in nature (Ch 36-37). **Key theological contributions**: God speaks through suffering (disciplinary pedagogy), through dreams, through nature's wonders. Suffering = megaphone (C.S. Lewis). **Elihu's role disputed**: Some scholars = later addition (not mentioned in Prologue/Epilogue, verbose, repetitive). Others = bridge to God's speeches (introduces themes God expands). **Literary function**: Delays God's answer, builds tension, represents human theology's best attempt before divine revelation. Central themes: Youth vs. age, revelation vs. tradition, pedagogical suffering, God's transcendence yet communicativeness, nature as theophany, preparation for Whirlwind.

## Chapter 32: Elihu's Anger — I Will Answer Also My Part

#### 32:1-5 — Job's Three Friends Ceased to Answer Because He Was Righteous in His Own Eyes
**Lore:** "So these three men ceased to answer Job, because he was righteous in his own eyes" (32:1). **Dialogue ends**—friends gave up. "Righteous in his own eyes" (צַדִּיק בְּעֵינָיו, tzadik be'einav) = self-justified. Narrator's comment or friends' perception? "Then was kindled the wrath of Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite, of the kindred of Ram: against Job was his wrath kindled, because he justified himself rather than God" (32:2). **NEW CHARACTER**—Elihu (אֱלִיהוּא, Elihu, "He is my God") son of Barachel (בָּרַכְאֵל, Barakhel, "God blesses") the Buzite (בּוּזִי, Buzi, from Buz, Gen 22:21, Abraham's nephew). "Of kindred of Ram" (מִמִּשְׁפַּחַת רָם, mimishpachat Ram). Lineage = authentic (not Satan figure, human participant). **Elihu's first anger**—"justified himself rather than God" (מִצַּדֵּק נַפְשׁוֹ מֵאֱלֹהִים, mitzaddeq nafsho me'Elohim). Job defended innocence vs. defending God's justice. "Also against his three friends was his wrath kindled, because they had found no answer, and yet had condemned Job" (32:3). **Elihu's second anger**—friends condemned without refuting. Logical failure. "Now Elihu had waited till Job had spoken, because they were elder than he" (32:4). Elihu silent during dialogue (respect for age). "When Elihu saw that there was no answer in the mouth of these three men, then his wrath was kindled" (32:5). Silence broken when friends exhausted arguments. **Elihu's critique legitimate**: Job over-focused on self-vindication, friends condemned without answering. But will Elihu do better?
*Verses: 5 | Theme: Anger*

#### 32:6-14 — I Am Young, and You Are Very Old; Therefore I Was Afraid
**Lore:** "And Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite answered and said, I am young, and ye are very old; wherefore I was afraid, and durst not shew you mine opinion" (32:6). **Age vs. youth tension**—"young" (צָעִיר, tza'ir), "very old" (יָשִׁשׁ, yashish). Cultural deference to elders (Lev 19:32). "I said, Days should speak, and multitude of years should teach wisdom" (32:7). Proverb: age = wisdom (Prov 16:31). "But there is a spirit in man: and the inspiration of the Almighty giveth them understanding" (32:8). **KEY VERSE**—"spirit in man" (רוּחַ הִיא בֶאֱנוֹשׁ, ruach hi be'enosh), "inspiration of Almighty" (וְנִשְׁמַת שַׁדַּי, venishmat Shaddai, breath of Almighty). Wisdom source = divine spirit, not mere age. Job 33:4, "spirit of God made me, breath of Almighty gives life." Gen 2:7, "breathed into nostrils breath of life." **Elihu's claim**: Spirit-inspired wisdom transcends age. "Great men are not always wise: neither do the aged understand judgment" (32:9). Challenges gerontocracy. True wisdom requires divine inspiration. Cf. 1 Cor 1:20, "Where is the wise? where is the scribe?" God's wisdom vs. human. "Therefore I said, Hearken to me; I also will shew mine opinion" (32:10). Demands hearing despite youth. "Behold, I waited for your words; I gave ear to your reasons, whilst ye searched out what to say. Yea, I attended unto you, and, behold, there was none of you that convinced Job, or that answered his words: Lest ye should say, We have found out wisdom: God thrusteth him down, not man" (32:11-13). **Diagnostic**—friends failed to convince Job. Wisest response would be: "Only God can answer" (which Elihu almost says, but then talks for 6 chapters). "Now he hath not directed his words against me: neither will I answer him with your speeches" (32:14). Elihu claims fresh approach (not repeating friends).
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Youth*

#### 32:15-22 — I Am Full of Matter; the Spirit Within Me Constrains Me
**Lore:** "They were amazed, they answered no more: they left off speaking. When I had waited, (for they spake not, but stood still, and answered no more;) I said, I will answer also my part, I also will shew mine opinion" (32:15-17). Friends silent, Elihu fills void. "For I am full of matter, the spirit within me constraineth me. Behold, my belly is as wine which hath no vent; it is ready to burst like new bottles" (32:18-19). **Bursting compulsion**—"full of matter" (מָלֵתִי מִלִּין, maleti millin, filled with words). "Spirit within constrains me" (רוּחַ בִּטְנִי, ruach bitni). "Belly as wine without vent" = fermentation pressure. "New bottles" (אֹבוֹת חֲדָשִׁים, ovot chadashim, new wineskins). Jesus used similar metaphor (Matt 9:17, new wine in old wineskins bursts). Elihu = new wine needing expression. "I will speak, that I may be refreshed: I will open my lips and answer. Let me not, I pray you, accept any man's person, neither let me give flattering titles unto man. For I know not to give flattering titles; in so doing my maker would soon take me away" (32:20-22). Vow of impartiality—no favoritism, no flattery. "My maker would soon take me away" = God would judge dishonesty. **Elihu's self-presentation**: Spirit-compelled, impartial, fresh perspective. **Question**: Does Elihu deliver? Or just verbose? (Readers divided—some see insight, others hot air.)
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Compulsion*

## Chapter 33: Elihu's First Speech — Behold, God Speaks in Many Ways

#### 33:1-7 — Hear My Speech, O Job; I Am According to Your Wish in God's Stead
**Lore:** "Wherefore, Job, I pray thee, hear my speeches, and hearken to all my words. Behold, now I have opened my mouth, my tongue hath spoken in my mouth" (33:1-2). Formal opening. "My words shall be of the uprightness of my heart: and my lips shall utter knowledge clearly" (33:3). Claims sincerity + clarity. "The spirit of God hath made me, and the breath of the Almighty hath given me life" (33:4). Echoes 32:8. Common humanity—both Job and Elihu created by God's breath (cf. 31:15, Job's argument for servant equality). "Behold, I am according to thy wish in God's stead: I also am formed out of the clay" (33:5-6). **Key claim**—"according to your wish in God's stead" (לְאֵל אֶרֶךְ־פִּי, le-El erekh-pi, "to God I am as you"). Job wanted mediator (9:33), someone to argue with God (23:3-7). Elihu offers: "I'm your peer (clay like you), can represent God without terrifying you." "Behold, my terror shall not make thee afraid, neither shall my hand be heavy upon thee" (33:7). Won't intimidate like God would (cf. 23:15-16, Job afraid of God). **Elihu's proposal**: Safe proxy for divine encounter. Noble intention, but will he succeed?
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Mediator*

#### 33:8-13 — Surely You Have Spoken in My Hearing: 'I Am Clean Without Transgression'
**Lore:** "Surely thou hast spoken in mine hearing, and I have heard the voice of thy words, saying, I am clean without transgression, I am innocent; neither is there iniquity in me. Behold, he findeth occasions against me, he counteth me for his enemy, He putteth my feet in the stocks, he marketh all my paths" (33:8-11). **Job's position summarized**—"clean," "innocent," "no iniquity." God finds pretexts ("occasions," תְּנוּאוֹת, tenu'ot), counts Job as enemy, imprisons (stocks), watches. Accurate summary of Job's complaints (13:24-27). "Behold, in this thou art not just: I will answer thee, that God is greater than man" (33:12). **Elihu's thesis**—"God is greater than man" (אֱלוֹהַּ יִרְבֶּה מֵאֱנוֹשׁ, Eloah yirbeh me'enosh). True but insufficient (Job knows this). "Why dost thou strive against him? for he giveth not account of any of his matters" (33:13). God not obligated to explain. True (Rom 9:20, "who art thou that repliest against God?"), but doesn't address Job's core issue—innocent suffering's meaning. **Elihu's limitation**: Restates divine transcendence (like friends) without answering "why righteous suffer."
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Transcendence*

#### 33:14-22 — God Speaks Once, Twice, Though Man Perceives It Not
**Lore:** **"For God speaketh once, yea twice, yet man perceiveth it not"** (33:14). **KEY VERSE**—God DOES communicate, but humans miss it. Two modes follow: (1) Dreams, (2) Suffering. "In a dream, in a vision of the night, when deep sleep falleth upon men, in slumberings upon the bed; Then he openeth the ears of men, and sealeth their instruction, That he may withdraw man from his purpose, and hide pride from man" (33:15-17). **First mode: Dreams**—"opens ears" (יִגְלֶה אֹזֶן, yigleh ozen), "seals instruction" (חָתַם תַּסְרָם, chatam tasram, seals/authenticates warning). Purpose: "withdraw from purpose" (turn from sin), "hide pride" (prevent arrogance). Examples: Joseph's dreams (Gen 37), Pharaoh's dreams (Gen 41), Nebuchadnezzar's dreams (Dan 2, 4). Dreams = divine communication mode. "He keepeth back his soul from the pit, and his life from perishing by the sword" (33:18). Dreams save from death (warning function). "He is chastened also with pain upon his bed, and the multitude of his bones with strong pain: So that his life abhorreth bread, and his soul dainty meat. His flesh is consumed away, that it cannot be seen; and his bones that were not seen stick out" (33:19-21). **Second mode: Suffering**—physical pain as divine pedagogy. "Chastened" (יוּסַר, yusar, disciplined, Heb 12:6). Sickness = teacher. "Yea, his soul draweth near unto the grave, and his life to the destroyers" (33:22). Near death experiences as wake-up calls. **Elihu's contribution**: Suffering not always punishment, sometimes education. More nuanced than friends (who saw only retribution). Closer to Heb 12:7-11 (discipline for sons). **Limitation**: Job's suffering excessive for pedagogy (children dead, not just sick). Elihu's category doesn't fully fit Job's case.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Communication*

#### 33:23-30 — If There Is a Messenger, an Interpreter, One Among a Thousand
**Lore:** "If there be a messenger with him, an interpreter, one among a thousand, to shew unto man his uprightness: Then he is gracious unto him, and saith, Deliver him from going down to the pit: I have found a ransom" (33:23-24). **Angel-mediator**—"messenger" (מַלְאָךְ, malakh, angel), "interpreter" (מֵלִיץ, melitz), "one among thousand" (rare, special). Function: "show man his uprightness" (declare God's way). **RANSOM**—"I have found a ransom" (מָצָאתִי כֹפֶר, matzati kofer). Who provides ransom? Angel? God? Ambiguous. **Christological foreshadowing**—Christ = mediator (1 Tim 2:5), interpreter of God (John 1:18), ransom (Matt 20:28, Mark 10:45, 1 Tim 2:6). "His flesh shall be fresher than a child's: he shall return to the days of his youth: He shall pray unto God, and he will be favourable unto him: and he shall see his face with joy: for he will render unto man his righteousness" (33:25-26). Restoration after repentance—"flesh fresher than child's" (rejuvenation), "see God's face with joy," "render righteousness" (imputed or restored?). "He looketh upon men, and if any say, I have sinned, and perverted that which was right, and it profited me not; He will deliver his soul from going into the pit, and his life shall see the light" (33:27-28). **Confession leads to deliverance**—"I have sinned" required. Problem for Job: he HASN'T sinned (God confirms 1:8, 2:3, 42:7). Elihu's formula doesn't fit. "Lo, all these things worketh God oftentimes with man, To bring back his soul from the pit, to be enlightened with the light of the living" (33:29-30). Pedagogical cycle—God works repeatedly to save. **Elihu's theology**: Suffering → messenger → confession → ransom → restoration. **Gap**: Assumes sin requiring confession. Job's innocence (vindicated by God) breaks this model.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Ransom*

#### 33:31-33 — Mark Well, O Job, Hearken Unto Me; Hold Thy Peace, and I Will Speak
**Lore:** "Mark well, O Job, hearken unto me: hold thy peace, and I will speak. If thou hast anything to say, answer me: speak, for I desire to justify thee. If not, hearken unto me: hold thy peace, and I shall teach thee wisdom" (33:31-33). Invitation to respond. "I desire to justify thee" (חָפַצְתִּי צַדְּקֶךָּ, chafatzti tzaddekka) = wants Job vindicated IF Job repents. "I will teach thee wisdom"—assumes Elihu has it to teach. **Rhetorical posture**: Silences Job (like friends did). Will Elihu truly listen, or just monologue? (Spoiler: monologues for 6 chapters.)
*Verses: 3 | Theme: Teaching*

## Chapter 34: Elihu's Second Speech — God Cannot Do Wickedness

#### 34:1-9 — Job Has Said, 'I Am Righteous, and God Has Taken Away My Judgment'
**Lore:** "Furthermore Elihu answered and said, Hear my words, O ye wise men; and give ear unto me, ye that have knowledge" (34:1-2). Addresses broader audience (not just Job). "For the ear trieth words, as the mouth tasteth meat. Let us choose to us judgment: let us know among ourselves what is good" (34:3-4). Proverbial wisdom—discernment required. "For Job hath said, I am righteous: and God hath taken away my judgment. Should I lie against my right? my wound is incurable without transgression" (34:5-6). Job's position restated—righteous, God removed justice, incurably wounded despite innocence. Accurate (Job 27:2-6). "What man is like Job, who drinketh up scorning like water? Which goeth in company with the workers of iniquity, and walketh with wicked men" (34:7-8). **Accusation**—Job "drinks scorning like water" (mocking God?), "company with workers of iniquity." Unfair (Job didn't join wicked, protested injustice). "For he hath said, It profiteth a man nothing that he should delight himself with God" (34:9). Claims Job said serving God profitless. Misquote—Job said WICKED say this (21:15), Job never endorsed it. **Elihu's error**: Strawman argument, misrepresents Job.
*Verses: 9 | Theme: Misrepresentation*

#### 34:10-15 — Far Be It From God That He Should Do Wickedness
**Lore:** "Therefore hearken unto me, ye men of understanding: far be it from God, that he should do wickedness; and from the Almighty, that he should commit iniquity" (34:10). **Thesis**—God cannot do evil (חָלִלָה לָאֵל מֵרֶשַׁע, chalilah la-El mereshا, "profanity for God from wickedness"). True (Jas 1:13, Deut 32:4, "His work perfect, all his ways judgment"). "For the work of a man shall he render unto him, and cause every man to find according to his ways. Yea, surely God will not do wickedly, neither will the Almighty pervert judgment" (34:11-12). Retribution theology restated—deeds matched by consequences. Cf. Gal 6:7, "whatsoever a man soweth." **Problem**: Observable reality contradicts (Job Ch 21, wicked prosper). "Who hath given him a charge over the earth? or who hath disposed the whole world? If he set his heart upon man, if he gather unto himself his spirit and his breath; All flesh shall perish together, and man shall turn again unto dust" (34:13-15). God's sovereignty—He rules earth, controls life/death. True but doesn't address Job's complaint (innocent suffer unjustly). **Elihu's limitation**: Reaffirms divine justice abstractly, ignores empirical exceptions (like Job).
*Verses: 6 | Theme: Justice*

#### 34:16-30 — Shall Even He That Hates Right Govern?
**Lore:** "If now thou hast understanding, hear this: hearken to the voice of my words. Shall even he that hateth right govern? and wilt thou condemn him that is most just?" (34:16-17). Rhetorical question—"Will you condemn the Just One?" Implies condemning God's justice = absurd. "Is it fit to say to a king, Thou art wicked? and to princes, Ye are ungodly? How much less to him that accepteth not the persons of princes, nor regardeth the rich more than the poor? for they all are the work of his hands" (34:18-19). God's impartiality—doesn't favor rich/powerful (cf. Deut 10:17, Acts 10:34, Rom 2:11). "In a moment shall they die, and the people shall be troubled at midnight, and pass away: and the mighty shall be taken away without hand" (34:20). Sudden divine judgment (cf. Exod 12:29, Passover; Dan 5:30, Belshazzar). "For his eyes are upon the ways of man, and he seeth all his goings. There is no darkness, nor shadow of death, where the workers of iniquity may hide themselves" (34:21-22). Omniscience—God sees all (Ps 139:7-12, Jer 23:24). "For he will not lay upon man more than right; that he should enter into judgment with God" (34:23). **Key claim**—God doesn't over-burden. **Problem**: Job's case seems EXACTLY that—more than right. Elihu's principle contradicted by Job's experience. "He shall break in pieces mighty men without number, and set others in their stead. Therefore he knoweth their works, and he overturneth them in the night, so that they are destroyed" (34:24-25). Divine judgment swift. "He striketh them as wicked men in the open sight of others; Because they turned back from him, and would not consider any of his ways: So that they cause the cry of the poor to come unto him, and he heareth the cry of the afflicted" (34:26-28). Public judgment for oppressing poor. Ironically, Job DEFENDED poor (29:12-17), yet suffers. Elihu's categories don't fit. "When he giveth quietness, who then can make trouble? and when he hideth his face, who then can behold him? whether it be done against a nation, or against a man only: That the hypocrite reign not, lest the people be ensnared" (34:29-30). God's sovereign timing. **Elihu's argument**: God just, all-seeing, punishes wicked eventually. **Missing**: Explanation for innocent suffering NOW (Job's case).
*Verses: 15 | Theme: Omniscience*

#### 34:31-37 — Should God Recompense According to Your Mind?
**Lore:** "Surely it is meet to be said unto God, I have borne chastisement, I will not offend any more: That which I see not teach thou me: if I have done iniquity, I will do no more" (34:31-32). Ideal response to suffering—accept chastisement, ask for teaching, pledge amendment. Standard piety. "Should it be according to thy mind? he will recompense it, whether thou refuse, or whether thou choose; and not I: therefore speak what thou knowest" (34:33). Rhetorical—should God adjust to your preference? You choose, not Elihu. "Let men of understanding tell me, and let a wise man hearken unto me. Job hath spoken without knowledge, and his words were without wisdom. My desire is that Job may be tried unto the end because of his answers for wicked men" (34:34-36). **Harsh judgment**—"Job speaks without knowledge," "answers for wicked men" (allies with wicked by protesting). Unfair accusation (Job protested FOR righteousness, not against it). "For he addeth rebellion unto his sin, he clappeth his hands among us, and multiplieth his words against God" (34:37). "Adds rebellion to sin" (compounds offense), "claps hands" (mocking gesture?), "multiplies words against God" (verbosity as blasphemy). **Elihu's error**: Judges Job harshly for honest protest. Ironically, God vindicates Job's speech (42:7, "Job spoken right"), condemns friends' (not Elihu mentioned, but similar errors).
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Judgment*

## Chapter 35: Elihu's Third Speech — What Advantage From Righteousness?

#### 35:1-8 — You Say, 'What Advantage Will It Be to You? What Profit Shall I Have?'
**Lore:** "Elihu spake moreover, and said, Thinkest thou this to be right, that thou saidst, My righteousness is more than God's?" (35:1-2). Misquote—Job said his righteousness genuine, not superior to God's. Elihu distorts. "For thou saidst, What advantage will it be unto thee? and, What profit shall I have, if I be cleansed from my sin?" (35:3). Job questioned profit of piety (similar to 21:15, but reporting wicked's claim, not endorsing). Elihu conflates Job with wicked again. "I will answer thee, and thy companions with thee. Look unto the heavens, and see; and behold the clouds which are higher than thou. If thou sinnest, what doest thou against him? or if thy transgressions be multiplied, what doest thou unto him? If thou be righteous, what givest thou him? or what receiveth he of thine hand?" (35:4-6). **Divine transcendence**—God unaffected by human sin or righteousness (cf. Ps 50:12, "If I were hungry, I would not tell thee"; Acts 17:25, "neither is worshipped with men's hands, as though he needed any thing"). True theology. "Thy wickedness may hurt a man as thou art; and thy righteousness may profit the son of man" (35:8). Human actions affect humans, not God. **Elihu's point**: God above pragmatic calculation. **Problem**: Job never claimed righteousness to manipulate God, claimed righteousness to defend integrity. Elihu attacks strawman.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Transcendence*

#### 35:9-16 — They Cry Out, But He Answers Not Because of the Pride of Evil Men
**Lore:** "By reason of the multitude of oppressions they make the oppressed to cry: they cry out by reason of the arm of the mighty. But none saith, Where is God my maker, who giveth songs in the night; Who teacheth us more than the beasts of the earth, and maketh us wiser than the fowls of heaven?" (35:9-11). Oppressed cry but don't seek God ("Where is God my maker?"). "Songs in the night" (זְמִרוֹת בַּלַּיְלָה, zemirot balaylah)—God gives joy even in darkness (cf. Ps 42:8, Acts 16:25, Paul/Silas singing in prison). "There they cry, but none giveth answer, because of the pride of evil men" (35:12). Unanswered prayers due to pride. "Surely God will not hear vanity, neither will the Almighty regard it. Although thou sayest thou shalt not see him, yet judgment is before him; therefore trust thou in him" (35:13-14). God ignores vain cries. Job should trust despite not seeing God. "But now, because it is not so, he hath visited in his anger; yet he knoweth it not in great extremity: Therefore doth Job open his mouth in vain; he multiplieth words without knowledge" (35:15-16). Conclusion: Job's words vain, without knowledge. **Elihu's failure**: Doesn't distinguish righteous lament (Job, Psalms) from proud complaint. Job's protest = covenant lawsuit, not vain babbling.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Pride*

## Chapters 36-37: Elihu's Fourth Speech — God's Greatness in Nature

#### 36:1-7 — Behold, God Is Mighty, He Preserves Not the Life of the Wicked
**Lore:** "Elihu also proceeded, and said, Suffer me a little, and I will shew thee that I have yet to speak on God's behalf. I will fetch my knowledge from afar, and will ascribe righteousness to my Maker" (36:1-3). "Speak on God's behalf" (לֵאֱלוֹהַּ מִלִּין, le-Eloah millin) = apologist role. "For truly my words shall not be false: he that is perfect in knowledge is with thee" (36:4). Claims perfection in knowledge (hubris?). "Behold, God is mighty, and despiseth not any: he is mighty in strength and wisdom. He preserveth not the life of the wicked: but giveth right to the poor" (36:5-6). God's justice—destroys wicked, defends poor. Standard theology. "He withdraweth not his eyes from the righteous: but with kings are they on the throne; yea, he doth establish them for ever, and they are exalted" (36:7). Righteous exalted. **Problem**: Job = righteous, not exalted but afflicted. Elihu's principle contradicted.
*Verses: 7 | Theme: Might*

#### 36:8-15 — If They Are Bound in Fetters, He Shows Them Their Transgression
**Lore:** "And if they be bound in fetters, and be holden in cords of affliction; Then he sheweth them their work, and their transgressions that they have exceeded. He openeth also their ear to discipline, and commandeth that they return from iniquity" (36:8-10). **Pedagogical suffering**—fetters/affliction = teaching tools. God shows transgressions, commands repentance. Elihu's key contribution (beyond friends). "If they obey and serve him, they shall spend their days in prosperity, and their years in pleasures. But if they obey not, they shall perish by the sword, and they shall die without knowledge" (36:11-12). Choice: obey = prosperity, disobey = death. "But the hypocrites in heart heap up wrath: they cry not when he bindeth them. They die in youth, and their life is among the unclean" (36:13-14). Hypocrites don't cry to God, die young. "He delivereth the poor in his affliction, and openeth their ears in oppression" (36:15). Affliction = divine pedagogy for deliverance. **Elihu's theology**: Suffering school, not just punishment. More sophisticated than friends. **Limitation**: Job's suffering still excessive for pedagogy (children dead, not just disciplined). Category doesn't fully fit.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Discipline*

#### 36:16-25 — He Would Have Brought You Out of Distress Into a Broad Place
**Lore:** "Even so would he have removed thee out of the strait into a broad place, where there is no straitness; and that which should be set on thy table should be full of fatness. But thou hast fulfilled the judgment of the wicked: judgment and justice take hold on thee" (36:16-17). God wanted to deliver Job, but Job "fulfilled judgment of wicked" (how?). Unclear accusation. "Because there is wrath, beware lest he take thee away with his stroke: then a great ransom cannot deliver thee" (36:18). Warning—God's wrath requires caution. "Will he esteem thy riches? no, not gold, nor all the forces of strength" (36:19). Wealth can't save. "Desire not the night, when people are cut off in their place. Take heed, regard not iniquity: for this hast thou chosen rather than affliction" (36:20-21). "Chosen iniquity rather than affliction"—implies Job preferring sin to suffering's lesson. Unfair. "Behold, God exalteth by his power: who teacheth like him? Who hath enjoined him his way? or who can say, Thou hast wrought iniquity?" (36:22-23). God = supreme teacher, no one directs Him. "Remember that thou magnify his work, which men behold. Every man may see it; man may behold it afar off" (36:24-25). Exhortation: praise God's visible works (nature). **Transitions to nature hymn** (36:26-37:24).
*Verses: 10 | Theme: Deliverance*

#### 36:26-33 — Behold, God Is Great; He Makes Small the Drops of Water
**Lore:** "Behold, God is great, and we know him not, neither can the number of his years be searched out" (36:26). Divine inscrutability. "For he maketh small the drops of water: they pour down rain according to the vapour thereof: Which the clouds do drop and distil upon man abundantly" (36:27-28). **Water cycle**—God evaporates water ("makes small drops"), clouds pour rain. Prescientific observation. "Also can any understand the spreadings of the clouds, or the noise of his tabernacle? Behold, he spreadeth his light upon it, and covereth the bottom of the sea" (36:29-30). Lightning ("light") and ocean depths—divine control. "For by them judgeth he the people; he giveth meat in abundance. With clouds he covereth the light; and commandeth it not to shine by the cloud that cometh betwixt. The noise thereof sheweth concerning it, the cattle also concerning the vapour" (36:31-33). Thunder ("noise") signals storm. Animals sense weather changes. Nature = divine communication.
*Verses: 8 | Theme: Nature*

#### 37:1-13 — At This Also My Heart Trembles; Hear the Noise of His Voice
**Lore:** "At this also my heart trembleth, and is moved out of his place. Hear attentively the noise of his voice, and the sound that goeth out of his mouth. He directeth it under the whole heaven, and his lightning unto the ends of the earth. After it a voice roareth: he thundereth with the voice of his excellency; and he will not stay them when his voice is heard" (37:1-4). **Thunder = God's voice**—Elihu's awe. Ps 29, "voice of Lord upon waters... breaks cedars." "God thundereth marvellously with his voice; great things doeth he, which we cannot comprehend. For he saith to the snow, Be thou on the earth; likewise to the small rain, and to the great rain of his strength" (37:5-6). Snow + rain commanded by God. "He sealeth up the hand of every man; that all men may know his work. Then the beasts go into dens, and remain in their places. Out of the south cometh the whirlwind: and cold out of the north" (37:7-9). Winter = divine seal on human activity, animals hibernate. "By the breath of God frost is given: and the breadth of the waters is straitened. Also by watering he wearieth the thick cloud: he scattereth his bright cloud: And it is turned round about by his counsels: that they may do whatsoever he commandeth them upon the face of the world in the earth. He causeth it to come, whether for correction, or for his land, or for mercy" (37:10-13). Frost, clouds, rain—all divine tools for correction OR mercy. Weather = God's pedagogy. **Elihu's climax**: Nature theophany. Prepares for God's whirlwind speeches (Ch 38-41, God uses same nature imagery).
*Verses: 13 | Theme: Thunder*

#### 37:14-24 — Can You, Like Him, Spread Out the Skies? The Almighty We Cannot Find Out
**Lore:** "Hearken unto this, O Job: stand still, and consider the wondrous works of God. Dost thou know when God disposed them, and caused the light of his cloud to shine?" (37:14-15). Questions Job—understand God's works? Anticipates God's questions (Ch 38ff). "Dost thou know the balancings of the clouds, the wondrous works of him which is perfect in knowledge? How thy garments are warm, when he quieteth the earth by the south wind? Hast thou with him spread out the sky, which is strong, and as a molten looking glass?" (37:16-18). "Spread out sky" (רָקַע, raqa, hammered firmament, cf. Gen 1:6). Rhetorical questions = No, you can't. "Teach us what we shall say unto him; for we cannot order our speech by reason of darkness. Shall it be told him that I speak? if a man speak, surely he shall be swallowed up" (37:19-20). Human speech inadequate before God. "And now men see not the bright light which is in the clouds: but the wind passeth, and cleanseth them. Fair weather cometh out of the north: with God is terrible majesty" (37:21-22). Sun breaks through clouds (God's glory breaks through mystery). **"Touching the Almighty, we cannot find him out: he is excellent in power, and in judgment, and in plenty of justice: he will not afflict"** (37:23). **Key verse**—God unfindable (inaccessible), excellent in power/judgment/justice, "will not afflict" (לֹא־יְעַנֶּה, lo-ye'aneh). Contradicted by Job's experience (God DID afflict, by permission if not directly). Elihu's limitation showing. "Men do therefore fear him: he respecteth not any that are wise of heart" (37:24). Conclusion: Fear God. Wisdom insufficient. **Elihu's speeches end**—no response from Job (unlike friends' speeches). God speaks next (Ch 38), implicitly bypassing Elihu. **Elihu's evaluation**: Some insights (pedagogical suffering, nature theophany, God communicates), but ultimately verbose repetition of friends' errors (retribution theology, Job implicitly sinful). God doesn't rebuke Elihu (42:7, only friends), but doesn't validate him either (not mentioned). Theological limbo—better than friends, worse than God's answer.
*Verses: 11 | Theme: Majesty*

---

**JMP 7 Summary**: Elihu's 6-chapter monologue = angry young man's intervention. Criticizes Job for self-justification, friends for condemning without answering. Theological contributions: (1) Suffering as pedagogy (not just retribution); (2) God communicates via dreams, suffering, nature; (3) Nature theophany (thunder, lightning, clouds as divine speech). Limitations: (1) Verbose, repetitive; (2) Misrepresents Job's position; (3) Retribution theology persists; (4) Categories don't fit Job's innocent suffering. Literary function: Delays God's answer, builds tension, shows best human theology inadequate. Prepares for Whirlwind (Ch 38-41)—God uses Elihu's nature themes but transcends Elihu's explanations. Elihu = bridge between dialogue and theophany, but ultimately another human voice requiring divine correction (by omission—God ignores him).
